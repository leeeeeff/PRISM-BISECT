# Sequence-first isoform-resolution function prediction maps the limits of protein language models and reveals a Complex I enzyme–substrate cascade disrupted in Alzheimer's disease

**Seungwon Lee**, [co-authors TBD]

*Draft for Nature Communications — v0 (2026-06-26)*

---

## Abstract

Alternative splicing generates functionally distinct protein isoforms from single genes, yet current annotation tools assign identical functions to all isoforms, masking the biological consequences of disease-associated splicing changes. Here we present PRISM (Protein-isoform Resolution via Intrinsic Sequence Modeling), a protein language model framework that predicts isoform-specific Biological Process GO terms from ESM-2 sequence embeddings, and BISECT (Biological Isoform-Switch Evidence Characterization Tool), a multi-evidence pipeline that evaluates functional consequences of isoform switches in disease. We report two central findings. First, a representational boundary: ESM-2 embeddings already encode isoform-level functional distinctions that domain- and homology-based annotation cannot access — a within-gene ranking signal retrievable even by a training-free embedding-distance baseline. Yet this encoding saturates at a representational ceiling that neither added model capacity, explicit within-gene supervision, nor per-term layer selection can exceed. We map where that ceiling lies: the domain-describable majority of splices is captured, while a bounded ~30% of non-domain targeting, NMD, and disorder-region edits forms the residual label-describability gap. Second, orthogonal convergence in a real disease application: deployed zero-shot to an Alzheimer's-disease cohort, PRISM's sequence-only functional scores — computed with no access to BISECT's structural, evolutionary, or transcript-usage evidence — converge locus-by-locus with BISECT's independently-derived Complex I isoform disruption cascade. This cascade is anchored by a genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and a Bonferroni-confirmed DOCK11 switch, with further Complex I components nominated but requiring independent replication, demonstrating that a characterised, bounded representational signal still delivers genuine biological corroboration through a methodologically orthogonal route.

Applied to 36,748 isoforms from human skeletal muscle long-read single-cell RNA-seq, PRISM achieves macro AUPRC 0.7022 across 18 GO terms — substantially exceeding domain-based annotation (AUPRC 0.108) — and demonstrates genuine within-gene isoform discrimination: PRISM's learned 18-dimensional representation shows within-gene prediction variance (0.00126) exceeding between-gene variance (0.00070) — a reversal of raw ESM-2's gene-dominated structure (within/between = 0.23) — with individual isoform pairs differing by 27- to 50-fold in predicted function.

Because macro AUPRC on gene-level-labelled test sets is dominated by gene-family identification — a gene-mean oracle that collapses every isoform to its parent gene's mean embedding, exploiting gene-identity information unavailable to PRISM at inference, achieves AUPRC 0.803 (muscle) and 0.795 (brain zero-shot), exceeding PRISM's own headline numbers, yet the identical oracle is forced to chance (AUC 0.500) on within-gene ranking — we report two metrics constructed to be structurally immune to this gene-identity confound as the paper's primary isoform-resolution evidence. First, per-locus domain-ranking AUC (whether PRISM ranks domain-complete isoforms above domain-truncated variants of the same gene) reaches 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] on held-out muscle and 0.775 in true-brain zero-shot transfer, versus a mathematically-guaranteed gene-mean-oracle null of 0.500 [0.500, 0.500] (deterministic under B=500 gene-level bootstrap, since collapsing every isoform to its within-gene mean score ties all rankings in every resample; +0.130/+0.275 gap) — a gap no gene-level classifier can produce regardless of its macro AUPRC. Second, on a curated benchmark of 51 UniProt/Swiss-Prot reviewed isoform pairs spanning kinase domain loss, large truncations, transcription factor TAD loss, and E3 ubiquitin ligase domain architecture (48 evaluable), PRISM achieves 12/13 (92.3%) direction accuracy on pre-specified complete-domain-loss pairs versus 4/13 (30.8%) on regulatory pairs (Fisher odds ratio 27, p = 0.0018) — a categorical contrast defined by structural mechanism rather than by any prediction-score threshold. Equivalently, stratifying by prediction confidence, all 11 pairs with prediction gap ≥ 0.10 are correctly directed (permutation p < 0.001, n=10,000) and every one involves complete structural domain loss or major truncation, whereas 34 pairs with gap < 0.05 show near-chance accuracy, consistent with quantitative regulatory isoform differences that ESM-2 mean-pooling cannot resolve rather than model failure. Sequence length difference between isoform pairs shows no correlation with direction accuracy (Spearman ρ = +0.019, p = 0.91; n = 45 pairs with length data), ruling out length bias as a driver of the gap stratification.

To recover isoform-specific signal suppressed by ESM-2 mean-pooling, we introduce a layer-contrast delta architecture (δ_layer = L30 − L15) that achieves macro AUPRC **0.734** (95% CI: 0.723–0.747) across 82 Molecular Function GO terms on held-out muscle isoforms (gene-disjoint split; +0.138 over PRISM baseline; gene-family-identification ceiling: gene-mean oracle 0.803). In true brain zero-shot the same δ_layer architecture reaches 0.647 (95% CI: 0.637–0.658) — a drop from muscle that reflects loss of macro-visible isoform signal rather than gene-level accuracy, since the gene-mean oracle itself falls only 0.803 → 0.795 across the identical transfer while the within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC rises from 0.630 to 0.775 (§6). On brain, the layer contrast confers no advantage over a single ESM-2 layer, because the muscle-optimal L15/L30 pair is misaligned with brain's deeper Fisher peaks (see §6). In muscle training, the largest gains are concentrated on structurally complex binding terms (L2_Structural: 0.313 → 0.637). Extended to the full 279-term GO ontology, the same architecture achieves 0.664 Biological Process and 0.617 Cellular Component AUPRC, recovering 94% of previously intractable GO terms (H2 category) to AUPRC ≥ 0.50 when trained within the same GO domain (held-out gene validation). Within-gene isoform discrimination (pos_bias above shuffled-label null) is significant in 277/278 evaluated GO terms in true brain zero-shot (82 MF, 103 BP, 93 CC; B=1,000 bootstrap, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR < 5%), confirming that the discrimination mechanism generalises across the full GO ontology and across tissues.

For the disease application we deployed the original 18-term Biological-Process PRISM model (§2; the model benchmarked against DIFFUSE, distinct from the 82-term δ_layer architecture above) zero-shot to 63,994 Alzheimer's disease prefrontal cortex isoforms without retraining, where it achieves AUPRC 0.600 — 74% of that model's own gene-identity oracle ceiling of 0.811 (§Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC), i.e. genuine sequence-based transfer that nonetheless remains below the gene-membership prior it cannot access at inference. Independently — donor-level DRIMSeq DTU testing and targeted donor-label permutation testing (n=10,000) take no PRISM score as input — this evidence reveals a Complex I isoform disruption axis with three levels of statistical confidence. **Confirmed** (Bonferroni-corrected): NDUFAF5 — a hydroxylase assembly factor that obligatorily hydroxylates NDUFS7 at Arg73 during early Q-module intermediate formation — reaches genome-wide DRIMSeq significance in excitatory neurons (Tier A-DR), and DOCK11, a GEF nominated in inhibitory neurons, achieves Bonferroni-corrected permutation significance (p_adj = 0.004; 5 pre-specified tests; Tier A-BPC). **Preliminary, replication-required**: NDUFS7 shows a nominal isoform switch in the same excitatory neuron population as NDUFAF5 (permutation p = 0.048, uncorrected), nominated primarily by its direct enzyme–substrate relationship to NDUFAF5 rather than by statistical correction; and NDUFS8 shows a Q-module TYKY subunit switch in inhibitory neurons (permutation p_adj = 0.022, above the Bonferroni threshold of 0.010; 37% effect; batch-concordant) (both Tier A-BPP). **Exploratory, replication-required**: NDUFS4 carries BISECT-confirmed MTS exon loss (M1+M9 PASS), but its Samsung donor-permutation signal (p = 0.024–0.041) is individually underpowered (post-hoc power 8.6%, n=13/8), and its apparently strong Stage-1 nomination p-value (dtu_p = 3.62×10⁻⁶) reflects cell-level pseudoreplication rather than donor-level evidence. An independent Ebbert cohort shows the same direction (p = 0.041), but a post-hoc Fisher combination of the two individually-underpowered cohorts (combined p = 0.0124) does not cross the Bonferroni-corrected threshold for this 5-test pre-specified panel (α_corrected = 0.010), so NDUFS4 remains **Tier C** pending an adequately-powered independent cohort (§8). KIF21B motor domain loss is independently replicated in an external cohort. At each locus in this cascade, PRISM's independently-computed zero-shot score differential converges with BISECT's structural verdict without either informing the other — orthogonal convergence rather than PRISM-driven discovery — and together the two methods provide a sequence-first, evidence-based framework for isoform-resolution functional annotation and disease switch characterisation.

*Keywords*: protein language model, ESM-2, alternative splicing, isoform function prediction, Alzheimer's disease, long-read single-cell RNA-seq, Complex I, BISECT

## Introduction

Alternative splicing affects ~95% of human multi-exon genes and produces protein isoforms with distinct domain compositions, interaction surfaces, and subcellular localisation patterns [1]. The resulting proteomic diversity is functionally consequential: disease-causing mutations are enriched at alternatively spliced exon boundaries [2], splicing factor dysregulation is a hallmark of neurodegeneration [3], and isoform-switching events in Alzheimer's disease (AD) brain have been documented across multiple cohorts using long-read sequencing technologies [4,5]. Yet the tools available to interpret these events remain anchored at the gene level. Pfam domain databases, InterProScan, and pfam2go — the field standard for functional annotation — annotate canonical protein sequences and assign identical Gene Ontology (GO) terms to all isoforms of a gene [6]. When a splicing event removes an interaction domain, alters a mitochondrial targeting sequence, or introduces a premature termination codon, no existing tool propagates this structural change into a revised functional prediction for the resulting isoform.

The core difficulty is not that isoform-level differences are biologically subtle; they are often structurally stark. The obstacle has been representational: domain-based tools require annotatable domain matches, which are absent for ~80% of all isoforms [our data: 19.1% carry any Pfam domain] and universally absent for novel isoforms uncatalogued in RefSeq or Ensembl. Protein language models (PLMs) offer a fundamentally different entry point. ESM-2, trained by masked language modelling on evolutionary sequence context across 250 million proteins, generates continuous 640-dimensional embeddings that encode structural and functional features at sub-domain resolution without requiring any database match [7]. We hypothesised that these embeddings encode the isoform-level differences invisible to discrete domain boundaries — that exon-skipping events altering protein hydrophobic cores, interaction interfaces, or disordered linker regions produce measurable embedding shifts, and that a task-specific classifier trained on gene-level GO labels can learn to exploit these shifts for isoform-level prediction.

Here we test this hypothesis systematically. We train PRISM, a lightweight multi-label classifier on top of frozen ESM-2 embeddings, on 36,748 isoforms from long-read single-cell skeletal muscle RNA-seq. We show that, despite training exclusively on gene-level GO labels, PRISM spontaneously learns to discriminate isoforms within the same gene: within-gene prediction variance exceeds between-gene variance, 11 of 13 GO terms show isoform-specific signal significantly above shuffled-label controls (bootstrapped p < 0.05), and individual isoform pairs differ by 27–50-fold in predicted functional score. This isoform discrimination is absent from raw ESM-2 embeddings (where PRISM's 18-dim output outperforms 640-dim ESM-2 by up to 10.6-fold on brain GO terms) and from a splice-feature-only model (within-gene discrimination 0.027 vs 0.094 for PRISM). We then deploy PRISM zero-shot to 63,994 prefrontal cortex isoforms from an AD single-cell long-read cohort, where it achieves AUPRC 0.600 without any retraining. Independently of PRISM — using donor-level differential transcript usage and structural evidence alone, with no PRISM score as input to any module — BISECT (a 15-module multi-evidence pipeline performing domain, PPI, structural confidence, evolutionary conservation, NMD, and localization signal analysis) identifies and mechanistically characterises functionally consequential isoform switches in AD brain. A Complex I disruption cascade — anchored by a genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and its NDUFS7 enzyme–substrate partner (nominally significant, replication-required) in excitatory neurons, with additional Q-module subunits (NDUFS8, NDUFS4) similarly nominated but requiring replication — together with a Bonferroni-confirmed DOCK11 switch and independently replicated KIF21B motor domain loss, illustrates BISECT's discovery capability. At each of these loci, PRISM's independently-computed zero-shot score differential converges with BISECT's structural verdict — two methodologically orthogonal routes to the same functional conclusion, providing corroboration rather than a case of one method discovering targets for the other.

To recover isoform-specific information suppressed by ESM-2 mean-pooling, we introduce the layer-contrast delta architecture (v17f*): the difference vector δ_layer = φ_{L30} − φ_{L15} between ESM-2's final and mid transformer layers captures how each individual sequence resolves from local structural to global functional encoding — a per-isoform, inductive signal that requires no gene-sibling sequences at inference time. Concatenated with the standard L30 embedding and trained on 82 Molecular Function GO terms, v17f* achieves macro AUPRC 0.734 (95% CI: 0.723–0.747) in brain zero-shot evaluation — exceeding frozen-embedding training (+0.074), ESM-2 fine-tuning (+0.074), and ESM-2 k-NN retrieval; it also exceeds DeepFRI [14] and DeepGoPlus [15], though these two pre-trained tools were evaluated out-of-distribution (trained on canonical PDB/Swiss-Prot proteins; tested on novel long-read isoforms) and a matched-training comparison was computationally infeasible (see §Comparative analysis). The gains are concentrated on structurally complex L2_Structural binding terms (PRISM 0.313 → v17f* 0.637; +0.324), where isoform-level domain composition variation is most functionally consequential. Extended to the full 279-term GO ontology, the same architecture achieves AUPRC 0.664 (BP) and 0.617 (CC), recovering 94% of previously intractable GO terms to AUPRC ≥ 0.50 under domain-matched training. Beyond demonstrating that this isoform-resolution signal exists, our central result is to map its limit. We show that the within-gene signal is intrinsic to ESM-2 rather than manufactured by training (a training-free embedding-distance baseline matches PRISM on it), that three orthogonal interventions — added model capacity, explicit within-gene supervision, and per-term layer selection — all fail to push it past a ceiling set by the ESM-2 representation, and that this ceiling is itself describable: the domain-defined majority of splices is captured and saturated, while a bounded minority of non-domain (targeting, NMD, disorder-region) edits is structurally encoded yet undescribable by any gene-level label. It is this boundary — what protein language models can and cannot resolve about isoform function — that our analysis maps, and against which the Alzheimer's-disease application operates.

## Results

### 1. ESM-2 embeddings encode isoform-discriminating sequence features

We first asked whether ESM-2 embeddings intrinsically separate isoforms of the same gene — a prerequisite for any classifier trained on gene-level labels to achieve isoform-specific predictions. For each multi-isoform gene in the training set, we computed the ratio of within-gene embedding variance (variation among isoforms of the same gene) to between-gene embedding variance (variation across genes). Mean-pooled ESM-2 embeddings show a within/between variance ratio of 0.23, consistent with prior evidence that raw PLM embeddings primarily encode gene identity. Following PRISM training, however, the learned 18-dimensional functional representation exhibits within-gene prediction variance of **0.00126**, exceeding between-gene variance of **0.00070** — a reversal demonstrating that task-specific training reorganises the representational space to amplify isoform-level functional differences.

The mechanism by which gene-level supervision produces isoform-level discrimination is informational rather than architectural. Formally, let φ(i) ∈ ℝ^640 denote the frozen ESM-2 embedding of isoform i, and let g(i) denote its gene of origin with gene-level GO label y_{g(i)} ∈ {0,1}^K. PRISM's training objective is:

> **L(θ) = Σ_{i ∈ I} Σ_{k=1}^{K} FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k , y_{g(i),k} )**

where FL(p, y) = −α_y(1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t) is the focal loss with γ=2 and p_t = p if y=1, 1−p otherwise. The critical feature of this formulation is that f_θ receives no gene-membership signal: isoforms of the same gene are presented as independent examples, and the MLP learns a gene-agnostic mapping from sequence embedding geometry to GO term probability. At convergence, f_θ(φ(i))_k ≈ P(y_k = 1 | φ(i)) — the posterior probability of function k given sequence embedding. If two isoforms i_a, i_b of the same gene produce embeddings in different regions of ℝ^640 (because their sequences encode distinct structural features), f_θ necessarily assigns them different scores, despite both carrying the gene-level label y_{g} = 1. Spontaneous isoform discrimination is therefore a predictable consequence of three properties simultaneously satisfied: (i) ESM-2 preserves structural consequences of exon-skipping in continuous embedding space; (ii) f_θ learns a cross-gene universal mapping; (iii) focal loss (γ=2) concentrates training gradient on hard boundary cases — isoforms whose embedding-based estimate P(y_k|φ(i)) is uncertain — rather than driving all positive-gene isoforms uniformly to score 1.0. The γ=2 gradient weight (1 − p_t)^2 reduces gradient from a confident isoform (p_t=0.9) by 25-fold relative to a boundary isoform (p_t=0.5), forcing the learned w_k to discriminate the uncertain cases. This mechanism also explains why an explicit within-gene discriminator objective (v15d_splice) destroys performance: adding splice_delta δ_i = φ(i) − E_{j∈G}[φ(j)] as input and jointly maximising within-gene score variance introduces an objective that conflicts with gene-level supervision (Supplementary Note S1). The model cannot determine from gene-level labels which isoform should receive the positive score — the within-gene discriminator gradient is therefore directionally random, disrupting the global sequence-function mapping and collapsing AUPRC from 0.7022 to 0.4573.

To quantify this isoform discrimination signal term-by-term, we defined **pos_bias** as the bootstrapped ratio of within-gene positive-class score variance to the label-shuffled null level. Of 13 GO terms tested with 1,000-resample bootstrap CIs, **11 show pos_bias significantly above the shuffled baseline** (Benjamini-Hochberg corrected p < 0.05), with pos_bias values of 1.902 (muscle contraction), 1.711 (skeletal muscle development), 1.497 (motor activity), 1.340 (sarcomere organization), and 1.037 (muscle cell differentiation). The two non-significant terms (Ca²⁺ signalling, glycolysis) have wide bootstrap CIs, consistent with insufficient positive-class sample size rather than absence of signal. Crucially, this signal is absent from a gene-mean baseline (pos_bias = 0 by construction), a splice-feature-only model (pos_bias 0.027), and a logistic regression on binary Pfam domain presence (pos_bias 0.467 on muscle contraction vs PRISM's 1.902), establishing that the learned discrimination emerges from the continuous PLM embedding and not from any discrete feature. The gene-mean baseline is the critical control for the gene-grouped cross-validation design: because no training gene appears in the test set, and all isoforms of a test gene are evaluated together, the only source of within-gene score variance is isoform-level sequence differences. A model that assigns each isoform its gene's mean prediction (collapsing all isoforms of the same gene to an identical score) achieves pos_bias = 0 by construction — confirmed empirically across 8,569 multi-isoform test genes (within-gene variance: PRISM 5.0×10⁻⁴, gene-mean 0.0; ratio ~5×10⁸). Non-zero PRISM pos_bias therefore cannot arise from gene identity memorisation, training set gene membership, or gene-level label leakage; it reflects exclusively the structural information encoded per-isoform in ESM-2.

### 2. PRISM achieves isoform-level GO prediction with substantial gains over domain-based tools

We trained PRISM on 36,748 isoforms from 12,706 human skeletal muscle genes (Samsung long-read single-cell RNA-seq; Samsung Medical Center and Asan Medical Center; sarcopenia vs healthy controls), using frozen ESM-2 (esm2_t30_150M_UR50D; 640-dim) embeddings as input to a three-layer MLP with binary focal loss (γ=2.0) over 18 GO Biological Process terms. In gene-level held-out evaluation (isoform-level stratified cross-validation), PRISM achieves **macro AUPRC 0.7022** across 18 GO BP terms (median 0.699, range 0.527–0.772; 5-seed ensemble). Two complementary evaluation frameworks apply throughout this work. *Gene-level AUPRC* (§§2–5) measures gene-family identification capacity: because GO labels are assigned at the gene level and shared by all isoforms, a gene-mean oracle that assigns each isoform its parent gene's mean score achieves AUPRC 0.803, and a cross-gene shuffle test attributes **97.7%** of v17f*'s above-random macro AUPRC signal to gene identity rather than isoform-specific discrimination (cross-gene shuffle: 0.734 → 0.048; within-gene shuffle: 0.734 → 0.734, no drop; see §Methods: Gene-level versus isoform-level AUPRC decomposition; §Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC). *Within-gene discrimination* (Domain-Ranking AUC, §§4–5; UniProt isoform-pair direction accuracy, §Results) tests whether PRISM correctly orders isoforms of the same gene — a test structurally immune to gene-identity confounds because gene identity is identical for both members of each pair. All macro AUPRC figures reported below should be interpreted in the gene-level evaluation context; isoform-resolution evidence is assessed through within-gene metrics.

To establish the advantage over existing annotation methods, we trained a logistic regression classifier on binary Pfam domain presence (512 domains; hmmscan; same train/test split) as an upper bound on domain-based pfam2go performance — this data-driven classifier systematically outperforms fixed pfam2go mappings. The domain-only classifier achieves macro AUPRC 0.108, a gap of **+0.605** relative to PRISM (Fig. 2D). This gap persists across the 8 GO terms where pfam2go has direct BP mapping entries (e.g., glycolytic process: 0.079 vs 0.839; microtubule-based movement: 0.058 vs 0.690; Ca²⁺ homeostasis: 0.054 vs 0.698) and is particularly pronounced for the 10 terms with no pfam2go BP entry, where domain tools achieve near-prevalence AUPRC while PRISM achieves mean 0.703. Ablation of ESM-2 input reduces AUPRC to 0.108; ablation of focal loss to 0.524 (mode collapse on rare GO terms); ablation of isoform-level stratification to 0.648 (gene-level memorisation). A public multi-organism benchmark (SwissProt 91% sequence identity split; 96 GO slim terms) yields PRISM AUPRC 0.271, consistent with a muscle-specialised model evaluated on non-tissue-specific GO annotations.

To assess whether PRISM performance generalises beyond the 18 muscle-biology GO terms, we extended training to 279 GO terms spanning Biological Process (n=104), Molecular Function (n=82), and Cellular Component (n=93), using the same 31,668 skeletal muscle isoforms with identical hyperparameters (focal γ=2, 5-seed ensemble). All 279 terms retained ≥2 positive test examples in true brain zero-shot evaluation (63,994 isoforms). Molecular Function terms achieve the strongest transfer performance (macro AUPRC 0.4671 in true brain zero-shot; 44% of MF terms exceeding AUPRC 0.5), consistent with MF-level GO terms encoding molecular activities that map directly to conserved protein structural motifs (motor activity, ATPase activity, binding). BP (brain macro AUPRC 0.3256; 17% of terms > 0.5) and CC (0.3288; 15% > 0.5) terms encode cellular and organismal context beyond sequence-encoded features, and accordingly transfer less well. Across all 279 terms, the MLP matches or exceeds logistic regression on raw ESM-2 features for every term (MLP_hurts = 0/279), establishing that task-specific training uniformly extracts additional discriminative signal beyond what is recoverable by a linear classifier on frozen embeddings alone.

### 3. Mean-pooling suppresses isoform-specific signal: four diagnostic lines converge on a representational bottleneck

Despite PRISM's overall AUPRC of 0.702 on muscle and 0.600 on brain, a consistent deficit emerged for the structurally complex L2_Structural subset of Molecular Function GO terms (n=33; PRISM AUPRC = 0.313), where isoform-to-isoform differences in binding interface composition should be the primary discriminating signal. This localised underperformance, specifically in the term class most directly determined by per-isoform structural variation, indicated a representational bottleneck rather than a general capacity limitation.

We conducted four diagnostic experiments to identify the root cause. **First**, we tested whether providing within-gene differential signal directly could recover L2_Structural performance. Adding the splice delta δ_i = φ(i) − μ_{gene(i)} as prediction input (v15d_splice) collapsed AUPRC from 0.702 to 0.457. The failure is mechanistic: gene-level GO labels carry no signal about which isoform should receive the higher score — the gradient from δ_i is directionally random, disrupting the global sequence-function mapping. **Second**, we tested whether a two-stage triplet encoder on binary Pfam domain features (v17a; T_ψ Dense(640+512→256→64, ℓ₂-norm), GO-label cross-gene triplets) could organise the embedding space before GO supervision. Stage 1 training collapsed within 15 epochs (active triplet ratio: 27% → 0.03% → 0.00%), because 80.9% of isoform pairs share zero Pfam domain overlap, providing no triplet signal. **Third**, multimodal extension with RNA and localisation features (v18a: RNA_delta + LOC_delta; v18b: brain cell-type expression context) degraded All MF AUPRC by −0.009 and −0.110 respectively, confirming that sequence-derived handcrafted features are partially redundant with ESM-2, while cross-tissue expression features introduce training-domain mismatch. **Fourth**, ESM-2 fine-tuning of the last 5 layers (D1; 24.62M parameters, 5 epochs) produced no measurable gain relative to frozen embeddings (D1: 0.660 vs. D0: 0.660; 95% CIs fully overlapping), ruling out "frozen embeddings are suboptimal" as the explanation.

All four experiments converge on the same mechanistic diagnosis: ESM-2 mean-pooling uniformly averages residue representations from early structural layers (local secondary structure, residue chemistry) and late functional layers (global evolutionary topology, long-range co-evolutionary patterns), suppressing the per-isoform structural-to-functional depth progression. This layer-progression signal varies between isoforms of the same gene according to their domain composition — precisely the information most relevant to L2_Structural GO term prediction — but is invisible to any classifier operating on the mean-pooled final-layer representation alone. The full experimental development path leading to this diagnosis is documented in Supplementary Note S_arch.

### 4. Layer-contrast delta architecture (v17f*) overcomes the mean-pooling bottleneck

The diagnostic synthesis yielded explicit design requirements: the differential signal must be (i) inductive — computable per-isoform without gene siblings at inference time; (ii) gradient-conflict-free under gene-level supervision; (iii) directly encoding the structural-to-functional depth progression suppressed by mean-pooling; (iv) independent of Pfam domain annotations, absent for 80.9% of isoforms.

The layer-contrast delta δ_layer(i) = φ_{L30}(i) − φ_{L15}(i) satisfies all four requirements. It is non-zero for every isoform in the training set (compare 26.9% zero-delta isoforms in the transductive gene-mean splice_delta), requires no gene siblings, and captures how each individual sequence resolves from local structural representation (L15) to global functional context (L30) — amplifying dimensions where the depth progression is largest while cancelling background structure shared across both layers. This within-sequence layer contrast encodes the isoform-level structural differences that mean-pooling erases.

We evaluated whether triplet-trained dimensionality reduction of δ_layer (v17f: T_ψ 640→64-dim, 23,837 GO-label cross-gene triplets, active ratio 80–85%) improves performance. Ablation of T_ψ — concatenating δ_layer at full 640-dim directly with φ_{L30} (MaxAbsScaler-normalised) — achieves All MF AUPRC **0.734** (95% CI: 0.723–0.747) and L2_Structural **0.637** (95% CI: 0.617–0.654), *exceeding* v17f by +0.017/+0.015. The T_ψ 640→64 bottleneck reduces δ_layer's Stage 2 input weight from 50% to 9%, limiting its contribution. We therefore report **v17f*** (no-T_ψ: concat[δ_layer(640), φ_{L30}(640)] → Dense(256→128→82, sigmoid); focal γ=2; 5-seed ensemble) as the preferred architecture: highest performance with the simplest training pipeline (+0.138 over PRISM baseline, +0.074 over ESM-2 fine-tuning D1, +0.098 over k-NN retrieval; see §5 for full benchmark comparison).

Three ablations confirm the mechanistic attribution. **(i) Random-δ control**: replacing δ_layer with isotropic random vectors matched in L₂ norm yields 0.642 All MF and 0.501 L2_Structural — both substantially below v17f* (+0.092/+0.136 gap), confirming that the directional content of δ_layer, not architectural capacity, drives the gain. **(ii) Scale dependence**: v17f* applied to ESM-2 8M (δ = L6−L3) and 35M (δ = L12−L6) yields AUPRC improvement of Δ = −0.001 and 0.000 over PRISM respectively, confirming that the layer-contrast signal requires ≥30 transformer layers to develop distinct structural-to-functional layer specialisation. Extended to ESM-2 650M (33 layers; 4.3× parameters; δ = L33−L16), the concat[φ_{L33} ‖ δ] architecture achieves All MF AUPRC **0.7339** (plain L33: 0.6793; Δ_gain = +0.0546), versus 150M concat **0.6693** (Δ_gain = +0.0611). To test whether the δ_layer benefit extends beyond ESM-2's encoder-only architecture, we additionally evaluated ProtT5-XL (encoder-decoder T5; 24 encoder layers; 1,024-dim; δ = L24−L12): concat[φ_{L24} ‖ δ] achieves AUPRC **0.7288** (plain L24: 0.6922; Δ_gain = +0.0366). Notably, for ProtT5-XL the δ_only representation (0.6744) falls below plain-last, unlike in ESM-2; yet the concat gains +0.037, confirming that the mechanism is not identical but produces consistent benefit — the layer-contrast signal enriches the final representation even when it cannot substitute for it. We further evaluated a second, independent T5-family model, Ankh-base (encoder-decoder; 48 layers; 768-dim; δ = L48−L24): concat[φ_{L48} ‖ δ] achieves AUPRC **0.7126** (plain L48: 0.6734; Δ_gain = +0.0392), reproducing both the positive concat gain and the T5-specific pattern in which δ_only (0.6638) falls below plain-last — establishing the ESM-2/T5 architecture-family distinction across two independent T5 models rather than a single one. Across all four larger PLMs evaluated (ESM-2 150M/650M, ProtT5-XL, Ankh-base), spanning the two dominant sequence-encoder families (masked-LM ESM-2 and span-corruption T5), all Δ_gain values are positive (mean +0.048), the layer midpoint ratio is fixed at 50%, and larger models consistently outperform smaller ones. This generality also holds on the within-gene isoform-resolution axis: the primary Domain-Ranking AUC (§Interpreting macro AUPRC) is near-identical across the three 5-seed models — 0.629 (ESM-2 150M), 0.626 (ProtT5-XL), and 0.630 (Ankh-base), each far above the gene-mean mathematical null of 0.500. Together these indicate that the layer-contrast mechanism captures a depth-dependent representational property of sequence protein language models that is neither specific to a single model nor cancelled at larger model size (Supplementary Figure S5; Table S_PLM; `reports/exp_f_plm_scale/dr_auc.json`). **(iii) Layer insensitivity**: all L_mid ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 25} achieve All MF 0.717–0.725 (range narrower than run-to-run variance), confirming the signal is a stable property of the ESM-2 representation manifold rather than an artefact of a specific layer pair.

To directly establish that v17f*'s within-gene score variation reflects genuine isoform-level structural differences rather than noise, we computed the genome-wide correlation between within-gene v17f* score variance and within-gene δ_layer norm variance across 6,106 multi-isoform genes in the brain test set. The Spearman ρ = **0.707** (p < 10⁻³⁰⁰), with partial ρ > 0.82 after controlling for isoform count per gene — confirming that genes for which v17f* assigns diverse functional scores are genes whose ESM-2 representations are structurally isoform-diverse. By contrast, within-gene variance in localisation features (ρ = 0.031, p = 0.005) and RNA features (ρ = 0.063, p = 6×10⁻⁹) shows negligible correlation, establishing that ESM-2 sequence representation — not secondary structural annotation — is the source of the discrimination signal.

Extended to the full 279-term GO ontology with identical hyperparameters, v17f* achieves BP 0.664 (95% CI: 0.649–0.680; Δ = +0.292 over PRISM) and CC 0.617 (95% CI: 0.600–0.634; Δ = +0.250), confirming that the layer-contrast depth signal is a domain-general property of ESM-2 150M. Under domain-matched training (279 terms trained within the same GO domain), 158/168 (94%) of previously intractable H2 terms recover to AUPRC ≥ 0.50; the 10 residually hard terms uniformly correspond to functions of rare cell types (olfactory neurons, sperm, endothelial cells) underrepresented in the training corpus, establishing corpus coverage — not fundamental sequence-encodability — as the binding constraint.

### 4b. Within-gene isoform-resolution evidence: the metric structurally immune to gene-identity confounds

Macro AUPRC — the headline metric reported above — is dominated by gene-family identification rather than isoform-specific discrimination: a gene-mean oracle — a classifier trained and tested on gene-mean embeddings (each isoform's ESM-2 embedding replaced by its parent gene's mean before training), the identical recipe applied to both tissues — achieves AUPRC 0.803 (held-out muscle) and 0.795 (true brain zero-shot), exceeding v17f*'s 0.734 (muscle) and 0.647 (brain) respectively (§Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC). These gene-mean-of-representation figures collapse the model's within-gene variation entirely and are near-identical across tissues (0.803 vs 0.795), whereas v17f* falls further behind on the harder zero-shot brain fold. The GO labels themselves are gene-level in both tissues (every isoform inherits its parent gene's gene2go annotation; on brain we confirmed label-constancy across all 996,874 term × multi-isoform-gene blocks), so a still simpler label-identity lookup — assigning each isoform its parent gene's annotation directly — attains a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 on either tissue. Crucially, this same perfect-scoring lookup is forced to exactly chance (Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.500) on the within-gene task below, because it assigns every isoform of a gene the identical score: the two metrics are orthogonal, and macro AUPRC's gene-level ceiling carries no isoform-resolution information. Because a gene-identity oracle is mathematically guaranteed to win on any gene-level-labelled test set, macro AUPRC cannot by itself demonstrate isoform-level discrimination. We therefore report two metrics constructed to be structurally immune to the gene-identity confound, since both contrast isoforms that share the same gene label and so cannot be solved by gene recognition alone.

**Domain-Ranking AUC.** For every multi-isoform gene, we tested whether v17f* ranks the domain-complete isoform above domain-truncated isoforms of the same gene. A gene-mean classifier is mathematically forced to a tie (AUC = 0.500 by construction, since both isoforms receive an identical score) — this is not an empirically weak baseline but a provable null. v17f* achieves Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] (B = 500 gene-level bootstrap) on the held-out muscle fold, a +0.130 gap over the gene-mean null that cannot be produced by any gene-level classifier regardless of its macro AUPRC. The same metric computed directly on the true-brain zero-shot set (n = 6,565 within-gene domain-differentiated pairs) is higher still, Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.775, against the identical 0.500 gene-mean null — so the within-gene domain-ranking signal not only survives but strengthens under genuine cross-tissue transfer. (We report the brain figure as a point estimate: unlike the muscle fold, its bootstrap is computationally prohibitive at brain scale, but the comparison null is 0.500 *exactly and by construction* — a gene-mean classifier assigns tied within-gene scores — not an empirically sampled baseline, so rejecting it does not depend on a sampled interval; the brain estimate moreover rests on more evaluation pairs than muscle's, 6,565 vs 3,542, so is no less stable.)

**UniProt isoform-pair direction accuracy.** On a curated benchmark of 51 UniProt/Swiss-Prot reviewed isoform pairs (48 evaluable) spanning kinase domain loss, large truncations, transcription-factor TAD loss, and E3 ubiquitin ligase domain architecture, v17f* achieves 11/11 (100%) direction accuracy for pairs with a prediction gap ≥ 0.10 (permutation p < 0.001, n = 10,000); all eleven involve complete structural domain loss or major truncation. The 34 pairs with gap < 0.05 show near-chance accuracy, consistent with quantitative regulatory differences that ESM-2 mean-pooling does not resolve rather than with model failure on an arbitrary subset. Sequence-length difference between pair members shows no correlation with direction accuracy (Spearman ρ = +0.019, p = 0.91, n = 45 pairs with length data), ruling out length as a confound for the gap-stratified accuracy pattern.

Together, Domain-Ranking AUC and UniProt direction accuracy constitute the paper's primary isoform-resolution evidence: both are constructed so that a gene-identity classifier is provably at chance, and both show v17f* substantially exceeds that chance level. Macro AUPRC (0.734 muscle / 0.647 brain) should be read as the gene-family-identification context in which this isoform-resolution signal is embedded, not as the isoform-resolution claim itself.

### 4c. Anatomical dissection of ESM-2's isoform representation: what is encoded, what mean-pooling suppresses, and what is absent

The results above establish *that* PRISM resolves isoforms at the domain level; this section dissects *why*, by decomposing where in ESM-2's 30-layer trajectory the isoform-discriminating signal resides, what biological properties its principal axes encode, and where the representation reaches its ceiling. These are post-hoc anatomical analyses performed after the δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 architecture was already empirically validated by ablation (§4): they are offered as a mechanistic account of why the architecture works, not as a prospective derivation that predicted the L15 contrast layer in advance (layer selection was an empirical sweep over L_mid ∈ {5,10,15,20,25}, all of which performed within run-to-run variance; §Methods). Three analyses converge on a three-tier account of ESM-2's isoform representation: information that is encoded and surfaced in the final embedding, information that is encoded but suppressed by mean-pooling, and information that is not encoded at all.

The full anatomical dissection underlying this synthesis — the per-layer Fisher-peak distribution, the within-gene layer-divergence analysis, the eight joint-PCA axis-biology interpretation and its gene-family conditioning control, the causal-occlusion double dissociation and axis-0 leakage account, the capacity/supervision bracketing of the Domain-Ranking ceiling, and a per-residue dissection of the tier-iii motif ceiling — is provided in **Supplementary Note S_dissection**; the three-tier synthesis below states its conclusions.

**Synthesis: a three-tier account of what ESM-2 encodes at isoform resolution.** These analyses, together with the motif-level boundary quantified in the Discussion (§Scope), delineate three tiers of ESM-2's isoform representation. **(i) Encoded and surfaced at the final layer** — domain-level architecture, which PRISM reads directly from φ_L30 to achieve the Domain-Ranking AUC 0.630 and UniProt 11/11 direction accuracy of §4b. **(ii) Encoded but suppressed by mean-pooling** — the mid-layer-concentrated discriminative signal that the L30 mean-pool integrates over, surfaced by the δ_layer layer-contrast (Fisher-peak distribution; within-gene mid-layer divergence peak). **(iii) Not resolved by PRISM's mean-pooled architecture** — the functional direction of short-linear-motif differences (§Scope: same-length isoform pairs differing only by SLiMs receive statistically indistinguishable PRISM scores). A per-residue dissection (Supplementary Note S_dissection) refines the source of this ceiling: the underlying sequence perturbation is locally encoded in per-residue ESM-2 embeddings near the splice junction, but whole-sequence mean-pooling dilutes it, and — unlike domain completeness, which has a single learnable common anchor that a local-pooling architecture can partially recover (preliminary, n=14) — motif-level function is mechanism-heterogeneous and currently lacks any common reference direction to pool toward. The same-length contrast still makes tiers (i)/(ii) and (iii) complementary for PRISM's current architecture: at fixed sequence length, isoforms differing in domain content diverge strongly in the pooled ESM-2 trajectory, whereas isoforms differing only in motif content do not — but tier (iii)'s ceiling is now attributed to pooling-and-anchor availability rather than to an absence of encoding.

### 4d. The representational boundary of isoform resolution: an intrinsic ESM-2 ceiling, and where it lies

The preceding sections establish that ESM-2 encodes isoform-discriminating signal (§1, §4c) and that PRISM extracts it into calibrated GO predictions (§2, §4) with genuine within-gene resolution (§4b). We now state the paper's central result, which the remaining sections and the Discussion substantiate in detail: this isoform-resolution capability is bounded by a ceiling intrinsic to the ESM-2 representation, and that ceiling is itself describable.

Five convergent lines fix its nature. **First, the within-gene signal is intrinsic to ESM-2, not manufactured by training:** a training-free per-GO-term embedding-centroid baseline attains the same within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC as the trained model (0.638 vs. 0.630), while the identical centroid method collapses to macro AUPRC 0.099 (chance prevalence 0.032) — an order of magnitude below PRISM's 0.734. What task training buys is therefore calibrated macro prediction, itself a gene-identity measure (the gene-mean oracle reaches 0.803, and a label-identity oracle scores a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 while being forced to exactly chance, Domain-Ranking AUC 0.500, on within-gene ranking; §4b), not isoform resolution. **Second, the ceiling cannot be lifted by modelling:** three mechanistically distinct interventions — added MLP head capacity, an explicit within-gene domain-ranking auxiliary loss, and per-GO Fisher-peak layer selection (including a label-leakage oracle) — each fail to raise the within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC (§6, §6b, §Discussion), a triangulation that localizes the limit to the representation rather than to the readout. **Third, the ceiling is describable in domain terms:** mapping every within-gene splice onto Pfam domain envelopes, the majority of within-gene variation (69.8% in brain, 53.5% in muscle) overlaps a structured domain and is already saturated by a single ESM-2 layer, which is why the layer contrast and the interventions above add nothing to it. **Fourth, the residual is a label limit, not a representational one:** the non-domain minority (30.2% in brain) — smaller, more disordered, N-terminally-enriched targeting/NMD edits — remains structurally encoded (the inter-isoform embedding difference decodes splice disorder at AUROC 0.79) and, unlike the domain signal, is *not* single-layer-saturated (a later ESM-2 layer significantly improves its decoding across splice size), so the representation retains untapped headroom there that no gene-level label can supervise toward a function. **Fifth, adding predicted three-dimensional structure does not obviously bypass the ceiling:** in a pilot structural audit (ESMFold single-sequence prediction; 40 non-domain and 30 domain-affecting brain within-gene edits, proteins ≤ 350 aa), the isoform-distinguishing edited (spliced) regions themselves fold at only low-to-moderate confidence — and, decisively, do so *regardless* of domain overlap (median per-residue pLDDT 58.0 for non-domain versus 55.3 for domain-affecting edits; Mann–Whitney p = 0.79; only 30–40% of edits exceed pLDDT 70), even though the surrounding proteins frequently fold confidently (whole-protein pLDDT often > 80). Because structure-aware protein language models depend on confident structural input, this indicates they would receive unreliable structure precisely at the splice junctions where isoform discrimination occurs — so the ceiling is unlikely to be an artefact of ESM-2's lack of explicit structural input, and is not obviously bypassable by structure-aware encoders (`hMuscle/model/exp_stage0_esmfold_pldct_audit.py`; a pilot, not a full-scale structural benchmark).

**A fifth line makes the residual's headroom concrete and, in the same stroke, shows why it cannot cross into functional labelling.** Recomputing per-residue ESM-2 embeddings for the equal-domain-count within-gene pairs and pooling the inter-isoform difference over the splice-*edited* interval rather than the whole protein recovers a cross-gene-consistent direction that whole-protein mean-pooling destroys: for internal edits, gene-disjoint direction-consistency rises from chance (0.481 ± 0.004) to 0.739 ± 0.005 (fallback-free subset, 30 fold-seeds), and this recovery is specific to the edited residues — length-matched scrambled-region pools reach only 0.56–0.63 (a domain-differing positive control rises in parallel, 0.708 → 0.844). The non-domain residual is thus *encoded but mean-pool-diluted*, not absent, and it decomposes by edit location: the N-terminally-enriched targeting subclass (the NDUFAF5/NDUFS8 class) carries, atop a length-dominated axis, a weak but robust mitochondrial-presequence signature — its recovered direction aligns with an ESM-2-independent presequence score after partialling out length and hydrophobicity (partial Spearman ρ = 0.126, incremental R² = 1.2%, p = 9 × 10⁻⁴; the net-charge and acidic-depletion components survive Bonferroni correction), an alignment wholly absent for internal edits (ρ = 0.008, n.s.), whose recovered direction aligns with no sequence-computable functional proxy tested (disorder, net charge, hydrophobicity; all n.s. after correction). Critically, this recovered coherence is embedding-space self-consistency, and it is empirically *anti-correlated* with functional labelling rather than convertible into it: the very region-pooling that raises within-gene direction coherence *lowers* the per-GO within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC relative to mean-pooling (paired Δ = −0.11, 95% CI [−0.18, −0.03], gene-cluster bootstrap on a training-free centroid protocol), because concentrating the local edit discards the whole-protein context a functional centroid requires. A global–local linear blend does not rescue this trade-off either: sweeping the mixing weight between whole-protein and edited-region pooling (α ∈ {0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0}, same domain-differing population and centroid protocol) traces a Domain-Ranking AUC that is highest near pure mean-pooling, with the best interior blend statistically indistinguishable from mean-pooling alone (α = 0.9: 0.823 vs. α = 1.0: 0.815, Δ = +0.008, 95% CI [−0.036, +0.066], gene-cluster bootstrap) — and this DR-AUC-optimal blend does not covertly retain the region-pool's coherence advantage either: its within-pair direction accuracy on the same domain-differing pairs (0.740) recovers only 22% of the gap between mean-pooling (0.715) and pure region-pooling (0.831), confirming the blend trades away most of the coherence gain for a labelling gain it does not obtain. No linear combination of the two poolings therefore recovers both properties at once, though non-linear fusion (e.g. a learned gate) is not excluded by this test. The residual therefore supplies a coherent embedding direction but no isoform-resolved label to supervise it — a sharper statement of the same limit, with a concrete pooling recipe (splice-interval region-pool) that retrieves within-gene *direction* where curated *function* remains out of reach.

The boundary is therefore two-sided: macro AUPRC is capped by gene-constant labels, and within-gene resolution is capped by representational saturation of the domain-describable signal, leaving a bounded, label-undescribable non-domain remainder. §6 shows that this boundary transfers across tissues — the splice-size mechanism predicts the muscle-to-brain non-domain contrast — and §§6–8 show that the biologically consequential isoform switches in Alzheimer's disease brain fall squarely in the label-undescribable class the boundary identifies, which is precisely why a sequence-first framework is required to detect them.

### 5. PRISM predicts non-overlapping functional space: 92.3% of predictions unavailable from InterProScan

A critical question is whether PRISM simply recapitulates what domain-based tools already annotate. We compared PRISM predictions with InterProScan+pfam2go across 26 BISECT-validated isoform switch cases. InterProScan+pfam2go maps domain matches (Pfam, PRINTS, ProSite) to **Molecular Function (MF)** GO terms: motor activity (kinesin domain), cytoskeletal binding (spectrin domain), PDZ-domain binding. PRISM predicts **Biological Process (BP)** terms: microtubule-based movement, synaptic transmission, actin-based movement. These two annotation layers address complementary facets of protein function (domain-level activity vs. cellular process context), and **24 of 26 BISECT-validated cases (92.3%)** carry a PRISM BP prediction not covered by any pfam2go BP mapping for that isoform, establishing non-redundant rather than competing annotation. This comparison is not confounded by ontological differences: we tested pfam2go BP entries specifically (not only MF), and 10 of the 18 PRISM training GO terms lack any pfam2go BP entry, confirming that the non-overlap reflects a genuine coverage gap rather than an ontological mismatch. Furthermore, PRISM covers all isoforms including the 7,899 brain NIC/NNIC sequences (12.3%) that carry no recognisable Pfam domains and thus receive no InterProScan annotation; among these, 541 (6.8%) score >0.5 for at least one of 73 brain-relevant BP GO terms. These findings motivate a three-layer annotation framework: InterProScan (domain-level MF) → PRISM (sequence-encoded BP) → BISECT (multi-evidence causal support).

**Beyond gene-level annotation: isoforms acquiring GO terms absent from their parent gene's label set.** The analyses above concern PRISM's ability to discriminate isoforms of the same gene on GO terms the gene is already annotated with, or terms absent from domain-based tools. A logically distinct and stronger question is whether any isoform's PRISM score ever crosses the positive threshold for a GO term that gene2go does not assign to that gene at all — i.e., whether PRISM's sequence-based prediction ever extends beyond the label set its own training gene provides. Using the true brain zero-shot score matrix (63,994 isoforms; 18 GO BP terms; §Methods), we identified two structurally distinct cases. **Case A** (fully unannotated gene): a gene negative for all 18 trained GO terms has an isoform whose score exceeds 0.5 for at least one term. This occurs for 378/15,858 fully-unannotated genes (2.38%; 706 distinct isoforms, 814 isoform–term instances). **Case B** (partially annotated gene, foreign-term dominance): a gene positive for at least one term has an isoform whose score on a term the gene is *not* annotated with both exceeds 0.5 and exceeds that same isoform's own best score among the gene's annotated terms. This occurs for 64/2,656 partially-annotated genes (2.41%; 113 distinct isoforms, 151 instances). Both rates, taken at face value, could be mistaken for evidence of broad novel-function discovery; two null comparisons show this is not the case. First, the Case A crossing rate within fully-unannotated genes (0.087% of isoform–term pairs) is statistically indistinguishable from — and marginally below — the background false-positive rate across all gene-level-negative pairs genome-wide (0.093%; 1,133,987 pairs), indicating Case A is consistent with ordinary classifier calibration noise rather than an enriched phenomenon. Second, a within-isoform column-shuffle null (permuting each isoform's own 18 term-scores across term labels, 20 trials) predicts 6,394 ± 32 Case B instances by chance alone (because a genuinely high score has an a priori ~89% chance of landing on a non-annotated term slot, given a typical gene has only 1–2 of 18 terms annotated); the observed count of 151 is 197 standard deviations *below* this null, showing that PRISM's scores are overwhelmingly concentrated on each isoform's own gene-annotated terms rather than on foreign terms — the opposite of an inflated novel-discovery signal, and in fact a further confirmation of gene-level calibration.

Because both statistics are consistent with baseline noise rather than a broad effect, we do not interpret Case A/B as evidence that PRISM systematically discovers new gene functions. We instead asked, of the individual instances that do occur, how many correspond to real, independently-documented biology rather than to noise concentrated in this tail. We screened 104 candidate (gene, GO-term) pairs (the 40 highest-scoring Case A instances and all 64 Case B instances) against UniProt/QuickGO GO Biological Process annotations, classifying a pair as literature-supported if the gene carries any BP annotation with direct experimental evidence (IDA/IMP/IPI/IGI/IEP; excluding computational/homology codes IEA/ISS/IBA) that names the same or a closely-matching process. **23/104 (22.1%)** pairs met this criterion (Table S_novelGO), including cases spanning distinct GO categories and mechanisms (Table 1). To characterise what this 22.1% represents, we classified each of the 23 literature-supported pairs by the specificity of the documented gene–process link, using each gene's canonical UniProt function description (independently cross-checked against UniProt REST API records): **Type A** (n=15/23, 65.2%) — the predicted term matches a near-definitional, textbook function of the gene (e.g., CACNA1D, an L-type calcium channel, scoring positive for Ca²⁺ homeostasis; UBQLN4, a ubiquitin-shuttling ubiquilin, for Proteasome-UPS; DNAAF11, a dynein axonemal assembly factor, for MT cytoskeleton organisation) — demonstrating successful zero-shot transfer of a gene's established core function to a GO category absent from the 18-term training panel, a non-trivial capability since PRISM was never exposed to these terms during training; **Type B** (n=5/23, 21.7%) — a genuinely documented but non-headline aspect of the gene's biology that requires knowledge beyond the gene's one-line descriptor (e.g., CTNND2/δ-catenin's structural role in dendritic spine morphogenesis, scored against the related-but-distinct term synaptic transmission; MACF1/spectraplakin's neuronal axon-transport context scored against neuron differentiation, versus its headline multi-tissue cytoskeletal-linker identity) — extending this transfer capability to genuinely non-obvious biology; **Type C** (n=3/23, 13.0%) — a weak or generic mechanistic link lacking gene-specific documented evidence for the predicted process (e.g., CCL2/MCP-1, a chemokine, scored for Ca²⁺ homeostasis via the generic observation that GPCR signalling can mobilise calcium, with no documented calcium-homeostatic role for CCL2 itself). This stratification separates two distinct capabilities within the 22.1% literature-support rate: the majority (Type A) demonstrate that PRISM correctly generalises a gene's established core function across the GO ontology despite training on only 18 terms, while Type B (21.7%) shows this generalisation extending to genuinely non-obvious biology beyond the gene's headline identity; Type C (13.0%) is the weaker tail where the connection to gene-specific biology is not established. Full per-pair classification and rationale: Supplementary Table S_novelGO. The remainder split between genes with no matching experimental annotation in current curation and genes whose real, well-documented function lies in an entirely different domain (e.g., a predicted "MT cytoskeleton organization" call for a gene whose experimentally-confirmed role is Hedgehog-pathway signalling at the primary cilium — a microtubule-based structure the gene acts *at* rather than *organises* — illustrating a recurring pattern in which PRISM's prediction lands in the correct broad biological neighbourhood, such as ciliary/microtubule biology, without matching the specific curated process). This 22.1% literature-support rate, evaluated against a background in which the underlying statistical signal is indistinguishable from noise, indicates that individual high-scoring instances in this tail are enriched for genuine biology relative to what pure noise would produce; the matched-null test below confirms this enrichment is statistically robust (9–14× over background screening from the same gene populations, empirical p < 0.001) rather than an artefact of a small, cherry-picked sample. The phenomenon nonetheless remains numerically bounded (104 candidate pairs, ≈2.4% of all gene-negative isoform–term instances) and is reported as a validated instance of rare, beyond-gene-label true positives — not as a proposal for a new general-purpose isoform-resolution metric, a scope distinction independent of the enrichment's statistical validity. To test whether this 22.1% literature-support rate exceeds chance rather than reflecting the ambient rate at which any gene carries some experimental annotation near any process, we screened matched random (gene, GO-term) pairs — drawn from the same fully-negative (Case A) and partially-annotated (Case B) brain-gene populations, with the same target-term frequencies — against an automated, fully-reproducible version of the same criterion (a pair counts as supported if the gene carries a direct-experimental-evidence BP annotation whose term lies within the GO-DAG closure of the predicted term; gene2go offline, B = 2,000 resamples). Under the strict is_a/part_of closure the PRISM-selected pairs are supported at 6.7% versus a matched-null rate of 0.5 ± 0.7% (14.0× enrichment, z = 9.4, empirical p < 0.001); adding regulates relations to approximate the manual "closely-matching" judgment gives 8.7% versus 0.9 ± 0.9% (9.3×, p < 0.001). Critically, this enrichment is not a study-bias artefact: under a loose floor counting any experimental BP annotation, matched-null genes already score 64.2% (they are themselves well-annotated), and the observed pairs exceed this by only 1.2× — so gene-level annotation richness contributes ≈1.2× while term-specific correspondence contributes ≈10×. The manual 22.1% rate reflects a more inclusive biological-adjacency judgment than the automated closure captures (e.g., regulation-of and act-at relationships), but across all three closeness definitions the PRISM-selected tail is enriched 9–14× over matched random screening from the same populations, directly refuting the concern that the Table 1 cases are a cherry-picked artefact (`reports/novel_go_matched_null/results.json`).

On the true-brain zero-shot test set (63,994 IsoQuant isoforms; 82 valid MF terms; NCBI gene2go ground truth), domain LR degrades further to macro AUPRC **0.0298**, while v17f* — the δ_layer model used elsewhere in this manuscript for true-brain zero-shot comparisons (§6) — reaches **0.6471**, a gap of **+0.6174** (PRISM wins 82/82 MF terms). Domain LR falls 5.45-fold from the muscle-trained baseline (0.1625) to true brain (0.0298), slightly sharper than the 5.1-fold degradation obtained from the mislabelled evaluation above (0.1625 → 0.0319); the qualitative conclusion is unchanged under correction. This degradation is mechanistically expected: the classifier is trained on muscle tissue GO labels where domain-function correspondence is muscle-biology-enriched, yet Pfam domain features are tissue-agnostic; the brain GO annotation space is broader and less muscle-biased, exposing the ceiling imposed by 19.1% domain coverage. PRISM/v17f*, by contrast, operate on continuous ESM-2 embeddings that encode sequence-level structural features independent of training tissue; v17f*'s true-brain AUPRC (0.647) falls only 11.9% from its held-out-muscle value (0.734, §Results), far less than domain LR's 5.45-fold collapse. This tissue-asymmetric behaviour constitutes a direct empirical demonstration that ESM-2-based representation outperforms domain-based annotation not merely by optimising domain→function mappings but by encoding a fundamentally richer and more tissue-transferable feature space.

**Comparative analysis.** To contextualise PRISM and v17f within the broader landscape of sequence-based GO prediction approaches, we evaluated ten methods in two categories: (i) *matched-training* comparisons, where each method trains on the same 31,668 muscle isoforms with the same gene2go MF ground truth (k-NN retrieval, gene-mean retrieval, PRISM D0, fine-tuned D1, v17f, v17f*); and (ii) *pre-trained tool* evaluations, where methods (DeepFRI, DeepGoPlus) are applied without retraining — an out-of-distribution test that provides a realistic deployment scenario but does not control for training set differences. BLAST→GOA and isopretEM constitute a gene-identification oracle upper bound. All evaluations use the same true-brain zero-shot test set (63,994 IsoQuant isoforms; 82 valid MF terms; NCBI gene2go MF ground truth). First, a gene-mean ESM-2 retrieval baseline assigns each brain isoform the GO MF label profile of the k=5 most similar gene centroids from the training set by cosine similarity in ESM-2 L30 embedding space, transferring their gene-level gene2go MF annotations. This approach captures gene-family identity but not isoform-level variation, and achieves macro AUPRC **0.404** (L2_Structural: **0.247**). Second, an isoform-level k-NN retrieval (k=5 nearest individual training isoforms by cosine similarity in ESM-2 L30 space; weighted-average GO label transfer) achieves macro AUPRC **0.561** (95% CI: 0.544–0.572; L2_Structural: **0.458**, 95% CI: 0.434–0.475). Both retrieval baselines remain below direct MF training (D0: **0.593**), establishing that proximity-based label transfer cannot substitute for task-specific learning on the target GO domain, even under genuine cross-tissue transfer. The v17f* δ_layer model substantially exceeds both retrieval baselines: **0.647 All MF** (+0.086 above k-NN) and **0.535 L2_Structural** (+0.077 above k-NN). This gap — present for both All MF and specifically for the structurally complex L2_Structural terms — measures the informational contribution of the inductive layer-contrast analysis: unlike k-NN and PRISM, v17f*'s δ_layer = L30 − L15 captures how deeply each individual isoform's sequence resolves from local structural to global functional encoding, a property that varies within gene families and across isoforms of the same gene, and which is invisible to any sequence-similarity retrieval approach operating on final-layer embeddings alone (Figure S1; Table S2b).

We further benchmarked against DeepFRI (CNN-LM, seq-only mode) [14], a dedicated protein function prediction framework trained on PDB and Swiss-Model structures that combines an LSTM language model with a 1D-CNN for GO term prediction from sequence alone. DeepFRI covers 489 MF GO terms in its vocabulary, of which 65/82 valid test terms (79.3%) overlap with our evaluation panel; predictions for the remaining 17 terms were treated as zero. Applied to the 63,994 true-brain test isoforms — of which 53,825 (84.1%) carry a called protein-coding ORF and thus a usable sequence input, versus near-complete coding coverage in the muscle set — DeepFRI achieves macro AUPRC **0.103** (All MF; 0.116 on covered terms only) and **0.026** (L2_Structural) — substantially below the v17f* true-brain baseline (**0.647**, 6.3× gap). Notably, DeepFRI All MF (0.103) falls below the domain-logistic regression baseline on the muscle training set (0.163 All MF), despite DeepFRI using a far richer sequence representation (LSTM-LM + CNN vs. binary Pfam features); and DeepFRI L2_Structural (0.026) is below v17f*'s true-brain L2_Structural (0.535) by 20×. (DeepFRI's covered-term AUPRC — restricting evaluation to the 65 of 82 valid terms present in DeepFRI's vocabulary — is 0.116, confirming that the zero-padded uncovered terms contribute minimally to the overall deficit.) We ran DeepFRI in sequence-only (CNN-LM) mode; DeepFRI's GCN mode requires protein contact maps, which would necessitate AlphaFold2 structure prediction for all 63,994 isoforms — computationally prohibitive at this scale — and was not evaluated. We further note that retraining DeepFRI or DeepGoPlus on the same skeletal muscle isoform training set as PRISM was computationally and practically infeasible: DeepFRI training requires pre-computed PDB contact maps for all training sequences, and DeepGoPlus training requires full reconstruction of the DIAMOND-indexed Swiss-Prot database with isoform-specific homology labels. The comparison therefore reflects a realistic deployment scenario — applying pre-trained protein function tools to novel long-read isoforms — rather than a matched-training controlled experiment. Under a matched-training scenario, CNN-based approaches would likely improve over the pre-trained baselines, though the fundamental ceiling imposed by 80.9% domain-free isoform coverage would persist. This result traces to the training distribution mismatch: DeepFRI was trained on PDB-resolved proteins and Swiss-Model homology models — predominantly canonical gene products with single, structurally characterised isoforms — whereas our evaluation includes 63,994 novel long-read brain isoforms, the majority of which are expressed from alternative splicing events absent from any structural database. The structural binding and catalytic GO terms that dominate L2_Structural are precisely those most reliably discriminated by structural data, yet without a structural counterpart in training, DeepFRI's LM embedding provides weaker sequence-level signal than ESM-2's evolutionary context. These results collectively establish that isoform-aware single-cell long-read expression context — as exploited by PRISM's ESM-2 training and v17f*'s δ_layer architecture — yields substantially higher performance on brain isoform GO prediction than either general-purpose protein function tools or sequence-similarity retrieval, with v17f* achieving 6.3× the DeepFRI macro AUPRC and 20× the L2_Structural AUPRC.

We further benchmarked DeepGoPlus (Kulmanov and Hoehndorf, Bioinformatics 2020) [15], a dedicated protein GO prediction framework combining a 1D-CNN sequence encoder with DIAMOND-based homology transfer from Swiss-Prot, using an alpha-weighted combination of CNN and homology scores (α_MF = 0.66). Like DeepFRI, DeepGoPlus's CNN component is limited to the 53,825/63,994 (84.1%) true-brain isoforms with a called protein-coding ORF, versus near-complete coding coverage in the muscle set. Applied to the true-brain zero-shot test set, DeepGoPlus achieves macro AUPRC **0.429** (95% CI: 0.419–0.439) and **0.350** L2_Structural (95% CI: 0.338–0.364) — substantially above DeepFRI (0.103, +0.326) due to the DIAMOND homology component, and modestly above the gene-mean ESM-2 retrieval baseline (0.404, +0.025). This ordering — DeepGoPlus (0.429) > Gene-mean (0.404) — is consistent with the DIAMOND component contributing genuine gene-identification signal beyond what pure ESM-2 embedding-centroid retrieval achieves without any homology search; unlike on the muscle-evaluated fold, the α = 0.66 CNN/DIAMOND blend does not suppress this signal below the retrieval baseline on true brain. PRISM v17f* (0.647) exceeds DeepGoPlus by **+0.218** All MF and **+0.185** L2_Structural (1.51× gap), establishing that isoform-aware ESM-2 δ_layer training substantially outperforms dedicated protein function prediction tools on novel long-read isoform data regardless of sequence coverage (Figure S1; Table S2b).

**Sequence homology transfer as a gene-level upper bound.** To establish an upper bound for sequence-based function prediction, we benchmarked BLAST→GOA (DIAMOND blastp [16] against UniProt/Swiss-Prot + GOA MF annotation transfer; max-bitscore per GO term). Applied to all 63,994 true-brain test isoforms, BLAST→GOA achieves macro AUPRC **0.788** (95% CI: 0.777–0.797) and **0.728** L2_Structural (95% CI: 0.712–0.743) — exceeding v17f* by +0.140 and +0.194, respectively. DIAMOND alignment coverage is 81.3% (52,040/63,994 isoforms obtain at least one hit at E<10⁻³ — versus 96.2% on the muscle set, consistent with the true-brain IsoQuant call set's larger fraction of non-coding/ORF-less transcripts); of these, 49,764/63,994 (77.8%) receive a non-zero score for at least one of the 82 study MF terms — the remainder either lack any hit or hit Swiss-Prot proteins whose annotations fall entirely outside this term panel, and receive score 0. This gap is structurally expected: BLAST→GOA performs gene identification rather than isoform-specific function prediction. For a test isoform derived from gene X, the highest-bitscore DIAMOND hit is invariably the canonical X protein in Swiss-Prot (100% identity for most isoforms), whose full set of gene2go MF annotations is then transferred wholesale. Because the evaluation ground truth is also gene-level (NCBI gene2go, shared across all isoforms of gene X), this gene identification → annotation transfer pipeline trivially achieves near-perfect AUPRC for covered sequences; the 0.212-point gap to 1.0 reflects the 22.2% of isoforms with no scoreable BLAST→GOA path (14,230/63,994), GO terms absent from Swiss-Prot annotations, and positive-class isoforms hit by wrong-gene proteins. An EM-based refinement following the isopretEM principle (Ravanmehr et al., Bioinformatics 2023) [17] — redistributing gene-level annotation signal within-gene according to per-isoform BLAST bitscore weights over 10 EM iterations — converges to the same **0.788** AUPRC (CI: 0.777–0.797), confirming that within-gene EM smoothing adds no discriminative signal when evaluation labels are gene-level. (isopretEM was designed for settings where isoform-specific expression data provides within-gene ground truth; the null result under gene-level labels is expected and does not reflect a methodological limitation of the EM approach itself.) BLAST→GOA therefore represents the gene-identification ceiling for sequence-based function transfer on this test set, not an isoform-specific prediction method.

**Isoform discrimination capability.** Gene-level AUPRC rewards methods that correctly identify the gene of a query isoform; it does not measure whether predictions differ between isoforms of the same gene. To quantify isoform discrimination on held-out muscle, we computed three complementary within-gene metrics across 7,459 multi-isoform genes (32,611 isoforms). (i) *Intra-gene CV*: mean coefficient of variation of predicted scores across isoforms within each gene for gene-positive GO terms — PRISM v17f* (0.100) < BLAST→GOA (0.162, normalized), indicating PRISM makes more internally consistent within-gene predictions while still discriminating. (ii) *Length-AUC*: for each gene, we tested whether predicted scores rank longer isoforms above shorter ones (sequence length as a proxy for domain completeness). BLAST→GOA shows strong length bias (within-gene length-AUC = **0.785**), because sequence-coverage-based bitscores directly penalise shorter isoforms. PRISM v17f* shows near-length-agnostic prediction (length-AUC = **0.560**, marginally above random 0.502), demonstrating that PRISM's learned representations are not dominated by sequence-length effects. k-NN ESM-2 (0.550) and gene-mean (0.545) similarly decouple from length. (iii) *Top-isoform BLAST agreement*: the fraction of (gene, GO term) pairs for which a method nominates the same top-ranked isoform as BLAST does. PRISM v17f* agrees with BLAST in only **47.7%** of cases (N=14,657 testable pairs; BLAST ranks isoforms by sequence coverage), near-statistically independent of BLAST's length-based ranking. In contrast, gene-mean agrees with BLAST in 64.2% of cases, and k-NN in 55.5%, confirming that PRISM's isoform-level predictions are qualitatively distinct from coverage-based transfer. Finally, BLAST assigns *identical* scores to all isoforms of the same gene for 30.6% of multi-isoform genes (2,286/7,459) — cases where all isoforms hit the same Swiss-Prot protein with identical bitscore per GO term and BLAST is entirely uninformative for isoform discrimination. These results establish that PRISM and BLAST address complementary aspects of isoform function prediction: BLAST achieves high gene-level AUPRC via reliable gene identification; PRISM captures function-predictive sequence features that are length-independent and discriminate isoforms that BLAST cannot separate (Supplementary Table S3).

Repeating the same three metrics on true brain zero-shot transfer (10,046 multi-isoform genes, 48,779 isoforms; identical methodology) reproduces some contrasts and revises others. (i) *Intra-gene CV*: PRISM v17f* (0.214) < k-NN ESM-2 (0.348) < gene-mean (0.385) < BLAST→GOA (0.465), and BLAST→GOA is now statistically indistinguishable from a label-random baseline (0.484) — in true brain, BLAST's within-gene score spread is essentially noise, while PRISM remains the most internally consistent method without collapsing to zero variance. (ii) *Length-AUC*: every method becomes markedly more length-sensitive in brain — BLAST→GOA rises to 0.945 (from 0.785 on muscle), and PRISM v17f* rises to **0.718** (from 0.560, random = 0.500), with k-NN ESM-2 (0.640) and gene-mean (0.629) intermediate. This is not a metric artifact but tracks an independently established tissue asymmetry (§6): within-gene splice variation is majority domain-affecting in brain (69.8%) versus muscle (53.5%), so sequence length is a better proxy for domain completeness in brain, and any method sensitive to domain content — PRISM included — becomes correspondingly more length-correlated as a downstream consequence. We report this honestly as a revision of the muscle-only near-length-agnostic claim rather than treating it as tissue-invariant. (iii) *Top-isoform BLAST agreement*: PRISM v17f* agrees with BLAST in 46.4% of cases (N=25,445), gene-mean in 45.7% (N=12,661), and k-NN in 42.6% (N=17,997) — the three-way spread that was prominent in muscle (47.7%/64.2%/55.5%) largely collapses in brain, consistent with BLAST's own within-gene ranking losing discriminative content per (i). Finally, BLAST assigns identical scores to all isoforms of the same gene for only 7.6% of multi-isoform genes in brain (versus 30.6% in muscle) — fewer outright ties, but non-identical scores are not necessarily discriminating scores, as (i) shows. Taken together, the tissue comparison sharpens rather than undermines the PRISM/BLAST complementarity argument: BLAST's high gene-level AUPRC continues to rest on gene identification rather than within-gene signal (CV ≈ random in brain), while PRISM's within-gene predictions, though no longer length-agnostic in brain, remain the most internally consistent and the least reducible to BLAST's coverage-based ranking (Supplementary Table S3b; Supplementary Figure S2).

**Functional specialization architecture across multi-isoform genes.** Standard functional annotation assigns all isoforms of a gene identical GO labels, implicitly treating isoform diversity as functionally neutral. To test whether PRISM's isoform-level predictions reveal genuine intra-gene functional specialization — and to characterize its prevalence and mechanism — we analysed GO attribution structure across 2,738 multi-isoform genes with ≥3 isoforms and ≥2 annotated GO MF terms (17,637 isoforms total). For each gene, we computed the Functional Specialization Index (FSI = 1 − mean pairwise Spearman correlation of v17f* predicted scores across GO terms) and top-isoform consistency (fraction of GO terms for which the same isoform receives the highest predicted score). Three distinct architectures emerge: *Canonical-dominant* (Scenario 1, S1; n=926, 33.8%): a single isoform carries all annotated GO functions, with mean pairwise GO-score correlation = 0.978 and top-isoform consistency = 0.995. *Isoform-specialized* (Scenario 2, S2; n=1,161, 42.4%): different isoforms top the predicted score for different GO terms, with mean GO-score correlation = −0.244 (anti-correlated), indicating that the isoform best-suited for one function is systematically the worst for another. *Mixed* (n=651, 23.8%): genes containing both a generalist isoform (topping multiple GO terms) and specialist isoforms (uniquely topping specific functions). S2 genes are thus the numerically dominant class, implying that protein-isoform functional diversification is the rule rather than the exception in multi-isoform genes.

To characterise the mechanistic basis of S2 specialization (excluding GO:0005515 protein binding, which reflects a distinct competition pattern described separately), we identified for each S2 gene the *mode-top isoform* — the isoform that achieves the highest predicted score for the plurality of GO terms — and tabulated the GO terms it specializes in. Among 2,091 S2 mode-top GO-term wins, **74.6% (n=1,559) involve motif-dependent GO terms** (ESM-2 L30 norm ratio of positive to negative isoforms ≥ 0.97, indicating that enriched structural compactness of positive isoforms does not distinguish the term), and only **25.4% (n=532) involve domain-dependent terms** (ratio < 0.97, indicating domain completeness directly enriches positive-isoform representation). The top specialized functions by frequency are RNA binding (GO:0003723; n=153, motif-dependent), identical protein binding (GO:0042802; n=146, motif-dependent), and ATP binding (GO:0005524; n=85, domain-dependent). Transcription factor activity (GO:0003700; n=50), kinase activity (GO:0004674/GO:0004672; n=36/39), and calcium ion binding (GO:0005509; n=39) contribute more domain-dependent cases. Crucially, 71.4% of mode-top isoforms carry the same Pfam domain set as competing isoforms (Type 3 SameDomain classification) — specialization occurs within identical domain architectures through sequence-level binding specificity variation. Domain-dependent GO-term wins show larger within-gene score gaps (mean 0.0334) than motif-dependent wins (0.0208; MWU p = 0.061), consistent with the greater discriminative sharpness expected when domain presence/absence directly determines function.

The mode-top / 5515-winner framework identified for S2 genes extends to Mixed genes. Among 651 Mixed genes, PRISM identifies 524 specialist isoform pairs in which a secondary isoform uniquely tops a specific GO term. In 30.9% of these pairs (162/524), the isoform topping GO:0005515 protein binding is distinct from the generalist (mode-top) isoform — the same 5515-winner divergence pattern observed in S2 genes — confirming that the generalist–specialist architecture is a general property of multi-isoform genes, not an artifact of the S1/S2 classification boundary. Extending the analysis to all 7,459 multi-isoform genes (≥2 isoforms, ≥1 GO term), the S2 rate is 21.8% and the Mixed rate 10.3%; the lower proportions relative to the primary analysis (42.4% / 23.8%) reflect dilution by 2,293 genes with only one annotated GO term (S1\_single; 30.7%), for which functional specialization cannot be assessed. Among genes newly entering the extended analysis (outside the ≥3 iso / ≥2 GO filter), 12.4% (585/4,721) show S2 or Mixed pattern, confirming that the PRISM-revealed functional specialization signal is robust beyond the analytically well-powered gene set.

**Within-gene pos_bias across the full GO ontology.** The muscle-trained PRISM (v15d) shows significant pos_bias — elevated within-gene score variance among positive-class genes relative to randomly selected genes — in 11/13 GO terms tested (B=1,000 gene-level bootstrap; shuffled-label null floor 0.24; §Methods). We extended this analysis to v17f* evaluated zero-shot on the full 278-term expanded GO ontology in true brain (82 MF + 103 BP + 93 CC). For each domain, pos_bias(k) = mean_std_within_gene(positive_genes_k) / global_std_k was computed with B=1,000 bootstrap CIs and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction. v17f* shows pos_bias significantly above the label-shuffled null in **81/82 Molecular Function terms** (mean pos_bias = 1.671; shuffled floor = 0.217; 7.7× ratio), **103/103 Biological Process terms** (mean pos_bias = 1.686; floor = 0.261; 6.5×), and **93/93 Cellular Component terms** (mean pos_bias = 1.273; floor = 0.265; 4.8×). The top-ranking MF terms by pos_bias include cysteine-type deubiquitinase activity (pb = 3.904), cytokine activity (pb = 3.169), and structural constituent of ribosome (pb = 3.145) — all functional classes with known exon-level structural determinants in their protein products. In BP, antimicrobial humoral immune response (pb = 4.421) and small GTPase-mediated signal transduction (pb = 3.002) lead. In CC, ribosome (pb = 2.954) and late endosome membrane (pb = 2.169) show the strongest within-gene discrimination. Near-universal significance (277/278 terms) confirms that the within-gene isoform discrimination mechanism identified in the muscle model is a general property of ESM-2-based PRISM training, independent of GO domain or complexity of the term set, and — evaluated here on genuinely cross-tissue brain isoforms rather than a same-tissue held-out fold — independent of tissue origin of the test data as well.

### 6. Cross-tissue zero-shot transfer to Alzheimer's disease brain

We deployed PRISM zero-shot to 63,994 prefrontal cortex isoforms from the Samsung AD single-cell long-read RNA-seq cohort (13 AD, 8 control donors; 8 resolved cell types) without any retraining. PRISM achieves **macro AUPRC 0.600** across 18 GO terms (range 0.403–0.720), compared to macro AUPRC 0.098 for domain-based annotation on the same brain isoform set (Fig. 4B). The 0.102-point reduction from muscle AUPRC (−14.5% relative) traces to a specific functional subset: GPCR signalling, potassium transport, and immune-related GO terms — lacking functional analogs in PRISM's muscle training set — achieve near-chance performance (0.403–0.467). For GO terms with functional muscle-brain overlap (synaptic transmission, neuron projection development, Ca²⁺ homeostasis, mitochondrial organisation), performance is maintained at 0.567–0.720, consistent with transfer of shared sequence-function associations.

We separately evaluated the layer-contrast **v17f*** architecture (§4) — not the 18-BP PRISM above — directly on the true-brain isoform set. In this genuine zero-shot setting v17f* reaches macro AUPRC **0.647** (95% CI: 0.637–0.658; 82 MF terms), below its 0.734 on the held-out muscle fold. This 0.087 macro drop decomposes cleanly against the gene-mean oracle: the gene-level signal transfers almost losslessly across tissues (oracle 0.803 → 0.795, only −0.008), so the residual ≈0.079 is isoform-level variation that raised macro AUPRC on muscle but cannot on brain — and, tellingly, over the very same transfer the within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC *rises* from 0.630 to 0.775 (+0.145). What zero-shot cross-tissue transfer costs is therefore not gene-level accuracy (near-invariant, matching the oracle) but v17f*'s macro-visible isoform signal, which is simultaneously *recovered* on the orthogonal within-gene ranking axis — the double dissociation of §4b operating under transfer. Critically, on brain the δ_layer layer contrast confers **no advantage over a single ESM-2 layer**: neither on macro AUPRC, nor on the within-gene Domain-Ranking axis. In a controlled layer-ablation on the identical 6,565 within-gene pairs (each configuration re-fit under matched settings), concat[φ_{L30}, δ_layer] reaches DR-AUC 0.745 versus 0.762 for the best single layer φ_{L15} (Δ = −0.018); the production v17f* predictions score 0.775 on this same pair set (§4b), but the contrast feature itself adds nothing over a single well-chosen layer. Three mechanistically distinct interventions — increasing MLP head capacity, adding an explicit within-gene domain-ranking auxiliary loss, and per-GO Fisher-peak layer selection (including a label-leakage oracle that uses brain's own peaks) — all fail to lift the brain within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC above the single-layer ceiling (oracle gain = −0.034). The proximal cause is a cross-tissue layer misalignment: the muscle-optimal (L15, L30) pair is displaced from brain's deeper per-GO Fisher peaks (median MF peak shifts L15 → L20). The distal cause is more fundamental. Brain isoforms diverge **~2.8× more within-gene** in ESM-2 δ space than muscle (raw within-gene ‖δ‖ median 13.1 vs 4.7; between-gene ‖δ‖ comparable at 1.12×, embedding norm identical at 0.97×, so the effect is within-gene-specific, not a scale artefact). This excess is not driven by higher per-residue sensitivity but by **~3.5× larger alternative-splicing events** (median 184 vs 53 changed residues per within-gene isoform pair; at matched splice size the two tissues' embedding differences coincide) — consistent with the brain being the most extensively alternatively-spliced tissue. To locate this divergence relative to the Pfam domain vocabulary directly — rather than inferring it from a magnitude correlation — we mapped every within-gene 2-isoform splice event onto hmmscan Pfam domain envelopes across all 2,179 brain genes carrying exactly two mapped isoforms (one splice event each; distinct from the 6,565 gene×GO-term evaluation pairs of the Domain-Ranking metric). The result refines, and partly corrects, a domain-centric reading: the *majority* of within-gene splice events (69.8%) overlap a Pfam domain, and these are the large events (median 193 changed residues), so most brain within-gene variation *is* domain-describable — consistent with the high brain within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC (0.775) and with a single ESM-2 layer already saturating that domain-completeness signal (single-layer DR 0.762). This is why the layer contrast, added head capacity, and explicit domain-ranking supervision cannot improve it: the ceiling is **representational saturation of an already-encoded signal, not a missing one** — the domain-describable majority is captured about as well as it can be from a single layer, leaving no headroom for the contrast feature. The genuinely label-undescribable component is a substantial *minority*: 30.2% of within-gene splices are domain-preserving (no Pfam domain touched), and these are systematically smaller (median 53 vs 193 changed residues), more disordered (median TOP-IDP 0.138 vs 0.104), and N-terminally enriched (half fall within the first 60 residues, the mitochondrial/secretory targeting-sequence window) — the signature of targeting, NMD-triggering, and disorder-region regulatory splices that the Pfam/GO domain vocabulary cannot score. This label-describability gap is not a fixed constant but scales inversely with splice size, exactly as the divergence mechanism predicts: applying the identical instrument to the held-out muscle fold — where within-gene splices are markedly smaller (median 42 vs 53 non-domain, 105 vs 193 domain-affecting changed residues) — the non-domain rate rises to 46.5% (782/1,683 two-isoform genes) versus brain's 30.2%, and muscle's domain-affecting rate (53.5%) barely clears the random-placement null (49.4%, +4 points), whereas brain's clears it substantially (69.8% vs 49.2%, +21 points). Because the two tissues' random-placement nulls are near-identical (49.4% vs 49.2%, i.e. equal protein-level domain coverage), this contrast is driven purely by splice size, not by a difference in how domain-dense the proteins are: brain's large alternative-splicing events preferentially remodel domains, while muscle's smaller edits more often land in non-domain regulatory sequence, enlarging the describability gap. That these non-domain splices are not merely inert is shown by the study's own statistically-confirmed brain switches, both domain-unchanged: the Tier A-DR NDUFAF5 excitatory-neuron switch is an NMD event (345-aa coding isoform → 267-aa PTC-bearing NMD-sensitive transcript, no Pfam domain lost), and the NDUFS8 inhibitory-neuron switch is an N-terminal mitochondrial-targeting (localization) change we explicitly classify as a mechanistic class distinct from domain loss. Both are functionally consequential yet invisible to the domain vocabulary; BISECT, whose evidence modules are built around detecting Pfam-domain changes, is biased toward the describable majority and undercounts exactly this minority (DDX19A intron-retention/coding-loss is a further instance). The brain within-gene resolution picture is therefore two-sided: the *macro-AUPRC* ceiling is a genuine **label limitation** (annotations are gene-constant, so the gene-identity oracle is perfect and macro cannot test isoforms; §4b), while the *within-gene Domain-Ranking* ceiling reflects **representational saturation** for the domain-describable majority — and the residual ≈30% of within-gene splices that are targeting/NMD/disorder edits are the label-**describability** gap proper: encoded by ESM-2, undescribed by domain vocabularies, and a bounded rather than dominant share of brain isoform variation. We verified the "encoded by ESM-2" half of this claim directly, and in doing so sharpened it. On equal-domain within-gene pairs (the non-domain regime; n=1,505 muscle pairs, median splice 58 aa), the inter-isoform embedding difference decodes whether a splice edits a disordered region at AUROC 0.79 (5-fold gene-disjoint) — the non-domain edit does leave a structurally decodable signature. Critically, unlike the coarse domain-completeness signal that a single ESM-2 layer already saturates (single-layer Domain-Ranking 0.762 ≥ the layer-contrast's 0.745, above), this graded non-domain signature is *not* single-layer-saturated: adding a later layer (L30) significantly improves its decoding in every splice-size bin, including at splice sizes matched to the domain regime (+0.058 [+0.033, +0.084] AUROC over the common 40–175-aa window). The non-domain component therefore carries un-tapped representational headroom, which locates the ceiling precisely: for the non-domain minority the limitation is a *label* one, not a representational one — ESM-2 encodes more about these edits than any gene-level label can supervise toward a function. (This read-out is disorder, a sequence-derived structural property, so it demonstrates representational encoding, not functional describability — the latter is what remains untestable in the absence of isoform-level function labels, and is precisely the describability gap.)

Task-specific training adds substantial functional organisation beyond what the PLM alone provides. PRISM's 18-dimensional output outperforms raw ESM-2 640-dimensional embeddings by **9-fold for neuron projection development** (AUPRC 0.567 vs 0.063) and **10.6-fold for Ca²⁺ homeostasis** (0.447 vs 0.042) (Fig. 4C). A logistic regression on raw ESM-2 features achieves macro AUPRC 0.112 on brain — confirming that the discriminative information is present in ESM-2 embeddings but requires task-specific training to extract. This cross-tissue transfer demonstrates that PRISM's learned representations encode tissue-invariant sequence-function relationships rather than tissue-specific expression patterns.

To dissect the mechanism of the observed AUPRC reduction in true brain zero-shot, we systematically analysed transfer outcomes across 279 GO terms (expanded BP+MF+CC panel; n=61 Transfer_success: brain AUPRC > 0.5 and ΔAUPRC > 0.05 vs. LR baseline; n=185 Transfer_failure: brain AUPRC < 0.4). We tested three hypotheses: H1, tissue bias — PRISM predicts well in muscle but not brain because muscle-specific functional motifs are absent from brain; H2, ESM-2 hard — functions whose positivity depends on molecular context not encoded in protein sequence, failing in both muscle training validation and brain zero-shot; H3, positive-set size — transfer fails primarily due to insufficient training examples. H1 is refuted for 274/279 terms: only 5 terms (1.8%) show the H1 pattern (high muscle AUPRC, low brain AUPRC). H2 — terms where ESM-2 embeddings lack discriminative geometry regardless of tissue — accounts for **203/279 terms (72.8%)**, concentrated in chromatin organisation, PTM-dependent signalling cascades, protein complex assembly, and cell-cycle regulation: functions whose GO-term positivity is determined by molecular context (post-translational modification state, interaction partner availability) not encoded in amino acid sequence alone. H3 contributes modestly (Spearman ρ = 0.250, p = 2.5×10⁻⁵ between positive-class size and brain AUPRC; significant but not determinative). A logistic regression on five predictors (n_pos, muscle AUPRC, category, delta_muscle, n_pos_te) confirms that **muscle AUPRC** is the dominant feature predicting transfer success, consistent with the near-absence of H1 (tissue-bias) failures and H3's modest but significant contribution: GO terms that succeed in muscle transfer well; terms that fail in muscle predict failure in brain. This establishes that the primary bottleneck is ESM-2's intrinsic separability of GO-term-positive embeddings, not tissue specificity of the training distribution.

### 6b. Domain-envelope magnitude and structural encoding of Pfam differences between muscle and brain

To test whether the two tissues differ not only in the *rate* at which alternative-splicing events remodel a Pfam domain (§6) but also in the *magnitude* of representational consequence per edit, we regressed a continuous representational-severity score (a gene-disjoint, length-invariant coherence-direction projection of each pair's embedding difference) on five structural properties of 48,641 canonical-anchored within-gene pairs (15,493 muscle, 33,148 brain): edit size, domain-architecture change, N-terminal overlap, disorder fraction, and alignment-resync failure. All five covariates are significant after gene-clustered correction (pooled R² = 0.119, permutation null R² ≈ 0, P < 0.001; within-gene fixed-effects R² = 0.158, not directly comparable to the pooled value but likewise far above the permutation null), confirming the severity score is isoform-pair-intrinsic rather than a gene-level property. The domain-architecture-change coefficient mirrors the rate-based finding above — larger in brain (β = 2.11) than muscle (β = 1.53) — so brain's larger splices not only more often remodel a domain but register a proportionally larger representational shift when they do. Two further covariates, N-terminal overlap and alignment-resync failure, initially appeared far larger in muscle than brain (β = 1.98 vs. 0.36 and 3.74 vs. 0.98), suggesting a mirror-image muscle-specific story; a mediation follow-up (**Supplementary Note S_severity**) shows this reading does not survive independent, confound-controlled testing. N-terminal overlap collapses to chance-level discrimination in both tissues once edit size is controlled. The resync-failure ordering is real but is a downstream trace of size asymmetry, which is itself 89.2%-explained by edit fragmentation (the number of separate change intervals per edit) — a broad-based effect spanning >2,000 genes per tissue with no detectable functional (GO) enrichment in either tissue. Disorder-region overlap is associated with a smaller representational footprint after adjusting for size (muscle β = −6.69, brain β = −4.07), an encoding/magnitude dissociation complementary to the direction/labelling dissociation established elsewhere in this paper. Full covariate statistics, the three-layer mediation chain (resync → size → fragmentation), and robustness checks (gene-cluster bootstrap, permutation nulls, genome-wide GO enrichment scans) are reported in Supplementary Note S_severity.

The magnitude regression above characterises *how large* a representational shift each edit class produces; a separate, direction-level analysis probed the non-domain residual — the class carrying no common domain-completeness anchor — for finer compositional structure, and its result sharpens rather than fills the describability gap. On the internal-edit subset (non-domain, non-N-terminal pairs: 4,185 muscle, 11,666 brain), we oriented each pair's embedding difference by a median split of four amino-acid-propensity descriptors of the changed residues (mean Chou-Fasman helix and sheet propensity, Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity, physiological net charge) and measured gene-disjoint cross-validated direction-consistency (CV-dir-acc). The oriented differences are internally consistent (CV-dir-acc 0.59–0.65), and this consistency is genuine — a label-permutation null that randomly sign-flips each pair's difference collapses it to chance (brain sheet propensity 0.653 → 0.500) — but it is *gene-independent*: in every case the real value sits at or below its gene-permutation null (muscle sheet 0.628 vs. permuted 0.631; brain sheet 0.653 vs. permuted 0.653; the other three propensities likewise real ≤ permuted in both tissues), so the direction is a dataset-wide long-versus-short regularity rather than the cross-gene-shared anchor that domain completeness provides. Decisively, this compositional direction does not propagate to PRISM's output: applying the identical instrument to the model's predicted GO-score difference (in place of the embedding difference) attenuates the consistency to 0.53–0.58, again indistinguishable from the gene-permutation null in both tissues — the mean-pooled model does not use the compositional axis for functional prediction. The non-domain compositional structure is therefore encoded and weakly surfaced as a dataset-wide orientation, yet is neither a cross-gene anchor, used by the current model, nor attached to any isoform-level label — a precise localisation of where this part of the residual sits relative to the boundary, reinforcing rather than closing the describability gap (Supplementary Note S_severity; `test_covariate_gene_permutation_null.py`, `test_sheet_delta_label_permutation.py`, `b4_compositional_usage.py`).

To further characterise the genome-wide structural encoding, we analysed within-gene isoform pairs across 3,000 multi-isoform genes (5,173 domain-architecture-different pairs versus 20,493 same-domain pairs). Domain-different pairs show 1.80× larger mean |ΔPRISMmean| than same-domain pairs (mean 0.0167 versus 0.0093; Mann-Whitney U = 67,976,178, p = 8.7 × 10⁻²²³), confirming genome-wide that structural domain differences correspond to larger functional score gaps. A linear probe (logistic regression) applied to raw ESM-2 L30 embeddings (φ_{L30}) achieves AUROC = 0.860 for binary domain presence/absence prediction across 5,000 test isoforms, confirming that domain structural information is already encoded in the PLM's penultimate layer before any task-specific training. Partial regression across the 83 BISECT benchmark cases (Ridge regression: ΔPRISMscore ~ β₁·Δdomain_count + β₂·has_MTS_loss + β₃·alt_promoter; R² = 0.186) yields β₁ = 0.114 (Spearman ρ = +0.246, p = 0.025) >> β₂ = 0.081 >> β₃ = 0.021, establishing domain count difference as the dominant structural driver of PRISM's within-gene functional score separation. These genome-wide analyses converge with the BISECT case-level stratification to show that PRISM's isoform discrimination is built on domain-completeness information already embedded in ESM-2, which the task-specific MLP head extracts and maps to GO term predictions.

To directly test whether explicit domain-level supervision could further improve Domain-Ranking AUC beyond the 0.630 baseline, we trained v17f* with a conditional domain-count auxiliary loss — MSE(pred_count, domain_count) applied exclusively to Type 1/2 isoforms (domain_count < gene_max; 1,054/31,668 training isoforms), with Type 3 same-domain isoforms excluded to avoid suppressing motif-level discrimination. All tested λ values (0.05, 0.1, 0.3) produced significant AUPRC degradation (0.734 → 0.601/0.497/0.455 respectively; 3-seed estimates), indicating that explicit domain-count regression conflicts with focal cross-entropy GO-function learning through the shared hidden representation. This negative result confirms that PRISM's domain-ranking capability (DR-AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] on held-out muscle) emerges as an intrinsic consequence of GO-function learning from PLM embeddings — the δ_layer signal that distinguishes domain-complete from domain-truncated isoforms is already present in ESM-2 (probe AUROC = 0.860) and is extracted by the focal loss objective without requiring structural supervision. Direct measurement of Domain-Ranking AUC for the least invasive λ = 0.05 model (3-seed ensemble; correct within-gene domain-count ranking metric) confirms that structural supervision also fails to improve DR-AUC: 0.626 versus the 0.630 PRISM v17f* baseline (Δ = −0.004), remaining below the ESM-2 centroid-similarity baseline of 0.638 and failing to beat the centroid hurdle; DR-AUC thus decreases alongside AUPRC under all tested λ, ruling out any trade-off interpretation. Conditional domain auxiliary training does not improve upon implicit structural encoding and degrades overall GO-function prediction by creating gradient conflicts in the shared representation.

### 7. BISECT identifies a Complex I disruption cascade led by an NDUFAF5–NDUFS7 enzyme–substrate pair in AD brain

BISECT integrates 15 evidence modules — Pfam domain change (M1), PPI disruption (M2/M10), NMD prediction (M3/M15), AlphaFold structural confidence (M4/M11), phyloConservation (M5), localization signal change (M9), exon structure classification (M14), convergence detection (M17), and regulatory context (M6-M8) — to assign a six-tier evidence hierarchy to each isoform switch candidate (Tier A-DR: DRIMSeq genome-wide; Tier A-BPC: Bonferroni-corrected permutation p_adj < 0.010; Tier A-BPP: permutation p < 0.05 uncorrected + ≥1 structural module PASS, replication-required; Tier B: independent replication by independent significance in a second cohort; Tier C: exploratory; Tier D: hypothesis-generating). Applied to 138 candidate switch pairs across 6 cell types, BISECT identifies 101 PASS cases meeting Stage 2 structural criteria.

Donor-level DRIMSeq quasi-Dirichlet-Multinomial regression (13 AD, 8 control donors; stageR two-stage OFDR ≤ 5%) identifies **15 Tier A-DR genes across six cell types**: excitatory neurons (n=5; NDUFAF5, NDUFB10, CNOT11, AZIN1, ZNF582), inhibitory neurons (n=3; SAMHD1, NOL8, DIS3), oligodendrocytes (n=3; USP1, DCAF5, ZCCHC17), microglia (n=2; DOCK10, FANCA), astrocytes (n=2; ZNF268, ERCC6L2). Post-hoc donor consistency filtering (jackknife stability ratio < 0.50, directional consistency ≥ 0.60) is passed by all 15 genes, with SAMHD1, AZIN1, DOCK10, NDUFAF5, and DCAF5 showing perfect directional consistency (1.00) across all donors.

Targeted permutation testing (n=10,000 donor-label permutations) was applied to N=5 pre-specified BISECT-nominated candidates — four Complex I components (NDUFAF5, NDUFS4, NDUFS7, NDUFS8) and DOCK11 (a DOCK-family GEF nominated in inhibitory neurons by BISECT M17 convergence detection) — each nominated by BISECT structural evidence modules (M1–M9/M17) prior to permutation, not selected post-hoc from permutation results. Four additional switches were identified across two Tier A-BP sub-tiers. **Tier A-BPC** (confirmed, Bonferroni-corrected): DOCK11 (inhibitory neurons; perm p=0.0008; Bonferroni p_adj=0.004 for 5 tests; batch-concordant); DOCK11 is the only gene in the pre-specified panel to survive strict Bonferroni correction (p_adj=0.004 < α_corrected=0.010). **Tier A-BPP** (preliminary, replication-required): NDUFS8 (inhibitory neurons; perm p=0.004; Bonferroni p_adj=0.022; 37% effect size; batch-concordant), NDUFS4 (both neuron types; excitatory perm p=0.041, inhibitory perm p=0.024; 5.1% and 32% effect sizes; batch-concordant), and NDUFS7 (excitatory neurons; perm p=0.048; 5.1% effect size; batch-concordant). NDUFS8 does not meet the strict Bonferroni threshold (p_adj=0.022 > α_corrected=0.010) but is classified Tier A-BPP on the basis of: batch concordance across both PO and SMC batches, a 37% effect size among the largest in the permutation panel, and independent BISECT localization switch evidence (MTS-helix disruption; HMMER-confirmed Fer4 domain retention; Fig. 6B) that is mechanistically non-overlapping with the permutation result. **NDUFS7** (excitatory neurons; perm p=0.048; 5.1% effect; batch-concordant) is classified **Tier A-BPP (replication-required)**: nominal permutation significance combined with its direct enzyme–substrate relationship to NDUFAF5 — NDUFS7 is the obligate hydroxylation substrate of NDUFAF5 at Arg73, and both are disrupted in the same excitatory neuron population — constitutes mechanistically non-overlapping convergent evidence. We note explicitly that NDUFS7 was included in the pre-specified permutation panel *because* of this documented enzyme–substrate relationship (established in prior biochemical literature, not discovered in this cohort); its tier classification is therefore hypothesis-confirmatory rather than hypothesis-free, and the permutation p-value alone (p=0.048, uncorrected) would not on its own support Tier A-BPP without this prior mechanistic anchor. This classification does **not** imply Bonferroni-corrected statistical significance; NDUFS7 requires independent cohort replication with isoform-level expression resolution for definitive confirmation. **NDUFS4** (both neuron types; excitatory perm p=0.041, inhibitory perm p=0.024; 5.1% and 32% effects; batch-concordant) carries BISECT-confirmed MTS exon loss (M1 PASS: MTS-bearing exon 1 absent in AD-enriched isoform; M9 PASS: TargetP-2.0 score drop confirmed) and shows directional Ebbert replication, but post-hoc power for the excitatory-neuron effect (5.1%) is 8.6% — substantially below the detectable threshold of this n=13/8 cohort design — and NDUFS4 is classified **Tier C (exploratory)**: the BISECT structural and Ebbert directional evidence constitutes a mechanistically plausible hypothesis, but the Samsung test by itself is underpowered for statistical demonstration, and a post-hoc Fisher combination with the independent Ebbert cohort (§8) does not cross the Bonferroni-corrected threshold for this panel, so the Tier C classification is retained. We note explicitly that NDUFS4's original Stage-1 BISECT candidate-nomination screen (pooled-cell differential transcript usage test, dtu_p = 3.62×10⁻⁶; Supplementary Table S_bisect) reports an apparently strong signal that is not directly comparable to the donor-level permutation result above: the Stage-1 screen tests differential usage across pooled cells without accounting for donor as the unit of biological replication, so its p-value is inflated by cell-level pseudoreplication, whereas the donor-label permutation test (n = 10,000 permutations of the donor group label) treats the donor as the resampling unit and is therefore the conservative, appropriate statistic for cohort-level inference. The two orders of magnitude difference between dtu_p = 3.62×10⁻⁶ and perm p = 0.024–0.041 is expected under this distinction and should not be read as a discrepancy requiring reconciliation; it is the reason NDUFS4 remains Tier C pending independent donor-level replication rather than being promoted to Tier A-BP. Together with Tier A-DR NDUFAF5, these nominate a **Complex I Q-module disruption cascade** with a hierarchical structure — anchored by the genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and its direct NDUFS7 substrate, with NDUFS8 and NDUFS4 as replication-required nominations rather than confirmed disruptions: NDUFAF5 hydroxylates NDUFS7 at Arg73 as an obligate step in early Q-module intermediate formation ([22]); crucially, NDUFS7 is also independently identified here as a Tier A-BPP switch in excitatory neurons. This constitutes a direct enzyme–substrate pair disrupted in concert in the same cell type: NDUFAF5 loss-of-function (NMD switch, Tier A-DR, excitatory neurons) → impaired NDUFS7 Arg73 hydroxylation → defective ~200-kDa Q-module intermediate → downstream assembly failure propagating to NDUFS8 (Q-module TYKY subunit, inhibitory neurons) and NDUFS4 — which is required at the terminal maturation step for NDUFA12 to displace assembly chaperone NDUFAF2 from the NDUFS4-free enzyme; without NDUFS4, NDUFA12-mediated NDUFAF2 replacement cannot occur and the complex is arrested as a NDUFS4-free intermediate incapable of progressing to the mature holoenzyme ([23]) — affecting both neuron types (Fig. 5C). Whether the NDUFS4, NDUFS7, and NDUFS8 switches are direct consequences of NDUFAF5 loss or independent pathological events requires future experimental validation; their mechanistic convergence on NDUFAF5-dependent Q-module biogenesis is consistent with a cascade rather than four parallel disruptions.

In astrocytes, ERCC6L2 (stageR p=0.011; Fig. 6C) exhibits an AD-enriched switch to a shorter isoform that loses six Pfam domains — the DEAD-box helicase core (DEAD), ERCC3 C-terminal ATPase domain, SWI2/SNF2 ATPase module, HDA2-3 chromatin remodelling domain, helicase C-terminal, and a restriction enzyme III fold — collectively comprising the catalytic core of transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair (TC-NER). BISECT classifies this as a domain-loss switch (M1 PASS; delta = 1.0 domain units): the CT-dominant canonical isoform (ERCC6L2-201) retains full TC-NER helicase competence, while the AD-enriched isoform retains only an evolutionarily conserved alternative last exon (ALE; Fig. 6C) with no annotated catalytic function. ERCC6L2 germline truncation mutations cause TC-NER-deficient aplastic anaemia; the astrocyte isoform switch predicts selective loss of helicase-dependent DNA repair capacity in AD glia without disrupting the ALE regulatory output.

BISECT mechanistic analysis reveals two distinct classes of Complex I switch. NDUFAF5 (C20orf7) carries a methyltransferase-like fold but functions biochemically as a **hydroxylase**: it catalyses hydroxylation of NDUFS7 at Arg73, a post-translational modification required for the formation of the early ~200-kDa Q-module intermediate ([22]). A related assembly factor, NDUFAF7, performs the complementary step of symmetric dimethylation of NDUFS2 at Arg85, stabilising the early peripheral arm nucleus ([21]). NDUFAF5 switches from a stable 345-aa CT-dominant isoform to an AD-dominant 267-aa isoform carrying a premature termination codon >2,675 nt upstream of the final exon-junction complex — an NMD-sensitive transcript whose AD-stage accumulation indicates either impaired NMD surveillance or splicing shifts overwhelming NMD clearance. Critically, because NDUFS7 is the direct substrate of NDUFAF5 hydroxylation — and independently shows a Tier A-BPP isoform switch in excitatory neurons in this study — the NDUFAF5 and NDUFS7 disruptions define a direct enzyme–substrate pair, both lost in AD excitatory neurons: NDUFAF5 isoform switch impairs hydroxylation of NDUFS7, which in turn prevents stable formation of the 200-kDa Q-module intermediate and propagates to downstream Q-module assembly including the NDUFS8 subunit switch observed in inhibitory neurons. NDUFS4 presents a third class: BISECT M1 identifies loss of the MTS-bearing exon 1 in the AD-enriched isoform (M9 TargetP-2.0 score drop confirmed), predicting mitochondrial import failure. Critically, NDUFS4 loss has a secondary assembly consequence: NDUFS4 incorporation is the obligate trigger for NDUFA12 to displace assembly chaperone NDUFAF2 in the terminal maturation step, and without NDUFS4, the NDUFS4-free enzyme is arrested before the mature holoenzyme can form ([23]). The AD-enriched NDUFS4 isoform thus disrupts both subunit delivery to the matrix and the final NDUFA12/NDUFAF2 exchange that licenses holoenzymatic competence. NDUFS8 presents a distinct mechanism: HMMER Pfam scan confirms that both CT and AD brain NIC isoforms retain complete Fer4-family [4Fe-4S] binding domains (E < 0.001; 7 coordinating cysteines in shared C-terminal 105-aa core). The functional difference is a **localization switch**: the CT isoform N-terminus contains a TLLWTELFR hydrophobic amphipathic helix compatible with mitochondrial targeting, while the AD isoform N-terminus carries a proline-disrupted segment (PVLPTG) that would impair MTS helix formation. This predicts that the AD isoform produces Fe-S cluster-competent protein stranded in the cytoplasm rather than imported into the mitochondrial matrix — a mechanistic class distinct from the skeletal muscle NDUFS8 switch (Fer4 domain loss), but converging on the same consequence of TYKY subunit depletion in Q-module assembly.

### 7b. Convergent validation: PRISM's independent zero-shot scores corroborate BISECT's structural findings

The convergence of PRISM functional scores with BISECT's multi-evidence pipeline on the same loci provides independent validation of isoform-specific predictions. For NDUFS4, PRISM's 50-fold score differential (above) agrees with BISECT's independent evidence from AlphaFold structural confidence, STRING PPI disruption, and phyloConservation. For DLG1 in brain, PRISM scores DLG1-224 (the PDZ-lacking isoform among 32 DLG1 brain isoforms) at 0.172 versus a median of 0.888 across all other DLG1 isoforms — a 5-fold differential reached by sequence alone, concordant with BISECT's domain-level assessment. These convergent predictions, derived through entirely independent methodological paths, establish the reliability of PRISM's zero-shot functional scores.

A potential alternative explanation for this convergence is that PRISM reproduces gene-level GO labels — assigning high mitochondrial function scores to all isoforms of NDUFS4, NDUFS7, and NDUFS8 by gene identity rather than by sequence-level structural discrimination, and thereby appearing to agree with BISECT without providing independent evidence. Five converging analyses rule out this gene-level classifier hypothesis. First, a gene-mean oracle — the formal gene-level null that assigns each isoform the mean GO score of its parent gene — achieves domain-ranking AUC = 0.500 [0.500, 0.500] by mathematical construction (§Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC); PRISM achieves 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] on held-out muscle and 0.775 directly on these true-brain isoforms (+0.130/+0.275 gap, muscle B=500 bootstrap p < 0.001), which is mathematically inconsistent with gene-level label reproduction. Second, the within-gene CT/AD score ratios (canonical CT isoform / AD-enriched isoform, maximum PRISM GO score across terms) are 4.3× for NDUFS4, 10.3× for NDUFS7, and 3.2× for NDUFS8; under the gene-level classifier null, all three ratios would be exactly 1.00 by definition. Third, LRPPRC — a mitochondria-associated RNA-binding protein directly involved in mitochondrial RNA processing and Complex I-related respiratory chain maintenance — receives identical PRISM scores for its CT and AD isoforms (CT = AD = 0.428; ratio = 1.00), despite belonging to the same mitochondrial gene category as NDUFS4/7/8; PRISM does not elevate scores for mitochondrial genes when domain architecture is unchanged, ruling out categorical gene-function labelling. Fourth, among the 24 Tier-3 BISECT genes — isoform switch candidates lacking structural domain-loss evidence — 23/24 show CT = AD (ratio = 1.00), while BISECT PASS domain-loss genes (n = 41) show a mean CT/AD ratio of 4.96×; this categorical pattern, aligned precisely with structural evidence, is incompatible with gene-level label reproduction, which predicts uniform ratios irrespective of domain status. Fifth, a systematic mechanism-stratified analysis across all 83 BISECT cases with PRISM scores, classified by domain architecture class, reveals a four-category spectrum: Type A (complete domain loss; tier2_functional_loss + tier2_complex_loss; n = 54) yields mean CT/AD ratio = 5.61 with 50/54 cases exceeding 2.0× (MWU vs. Type C: p < 0.001); Type B (domain gain in AD isoform; tier1_functional_switch; n = 3) yields mean ratio < 1.0, indicating PRISM correctly scores the AD isoform higher when it has gained novel functional domains — a direction reversal consistent with gain-of-function switching; Type C (same domain architecture; tier3_gene_median negative controls; n = 23) yields mean ratio = 1.000 (23/23 at ≈ 1.000), the expected negative control result; Type D (partial structural evidence; n = 3) yields intermediate ratios between Type A and C. This four-category spectrum — domain loss → elevated CT/AD ratio, domain gain → inverted ratio, no architectural change → ratio ≈ 1.00 — is the expected quantitative signature of genuine isoform-level structural discrimination and cannot be explained by gene-level label reproduction, which would predict uniform ratios across all four classes. Collectively, these five analyses establish that PRISM's convergence with BISECT reflects isoform-intrinsic sequence discrimination: ESM-2 detects MTS presence/absence, Oxidored_q6 domain completeness, and Fer4 C-terminal truncation from amino acid sequence alone, not gene-level functional category assignment.

### 8. Independent replication and cell-type-resolved validation

To validate the Samsung findings in an independent dataset, we queried the Ebbert et al. bulk long-read RNA-seq cohort (21 donors total; 12 with complete annotation after QC: 6 AD, 6 control; human prefrontal cortex; PRJNA596351). KIF21B — encoding a kinesin-2 motor protein with established roles in axonal transport and AD genetic risk — shows a switch from the full-length isoform (418 aa; kinesin motor domain intact) to a novel NIC isoform in which the motor domain is absent, with the AD-enriched canonical full-length isoform showing significantly reduced fractional abundance in AD donors by Mann-Whitney U test (**MWU p = 0.026**; bootstrap 95% CI confirmed; Fig. 6A). BISECT classifies KIF21B as Tier B: Samsung donor-level permutation is non-significant (perm p=0.472) due to isoform data sparsity in the single-nucleus dataset, but the independent replication direction and significance in Ebbert bulk data support the switch. PRISM scores the motor-domain-containing isoform at 0.472 for microtubule-based movement (GO:0007018) versus 0.113 for the NIC isoform — a directionally correct 4-fold differential consistent with motor domain loss.

We extended the Ebbert replication analysis to three additional BISECT-nominated Complex I genes. For **NDUFS4**, two minor annotated isoforms (ENST00000506765 and ENST00000506974; 6-exon alternative forms with extended C-terminal sequence) both show increased fractional abundance in AD relative to control donors (ENST00000506974: CT=0.0185, AD=0.0433; MWU p=0.041; ENST00000506765: CT=0.0163, AD=0.0391; MWU p=0.041; Bonferroni p_adj=0.082 for 2 isoforms tested; both concordant), directionally consistent with the Samsung finding of AD-enrichment of the novel NIC isoform (tr73243) in excitatory neurons. The identical p-values reflect a discrete MWU distribution with n=6 vs n=6; both isoforms show concordant direction and are reported as directional replication rather than formal significance. The canonical NDUFS4-201 (5-exon; ENST00000296684) shows a complementary decrease (CT=0.927, AD=0.912; p=0.589), consistent with a shift in isoform ratio away from the canonical form. For **NDUFS7**, the longer 5-exon isoform (ENST00000376112) increases in AD (CT=0.583, AD=0.705; Δ=+0.121) while the shorter 4-exon form decreases (CT=0.417, AD=0.296; Δ=−0.121), directionally consistent with Samsung; however, neither reaches significance (MWU p=0.31) — an expected result given that the Samsung effect size was 5.1% in excitatory neurons, which translates to <2% shift in mixed-cell-type bulk tissue, below the detectable threshold at n=6 vs n=6. For **NDUFS8**, the annotated ENST00000690204 (shorter form) decreases in AD (CT=0.242, AD=0.042; MWU p=0.065), while ENST00000377811 increases (CT=0.484, AD=0.661; MWU p=0.026); the Samsung AD-enriched isoform (transcript100761.chr11.nic; localization switch with proline-disrupted MTS) is a novel IsoQuant NIC transcript absent from GENCODE38 and cannot be directly evaluated in Ebbert bulk annotation, limiting interpretability of the annotated-transcript comparison to directional consistency rather than mechanistic replication. Taken together, the Ebbert bulk data provides directional replication for KIF21B (p=0.026), nominal replication for NDUFS4 (p=0.041), directional consistency for NDUFS7 (underpowered by expected effect size), and an isoform proportion shift for NDUFS8 whose mechanistic interpretation is limited by novel transcript annotation coverage. We additionally tested **DOCK11** in Ebbert — not previously attempted, since DOCK11 was nominated by BISECT M17 convergence detection rather than by the original Complex I candidate list. The CT-dominant transcript (ENST00000276204) decreases from 0.853 (CT donors) to 0.691 (AD donors), directionally concordant with the Samsung inhibitory-neuron switch, but the AD-enriched transcript (ENST00000632573) is detected at zero raw reads in 9 of 12 bulk donors, and the resulting proportion shift does not reach significance (MWU p=0.240). This near-total absence of the AD-transcript in bulk tissue is itself informative: it indicates the DOCK11 switch, like the NDUFS7 switch, is diluted below the bulk detection floor by cell-type averaging (inhibitory neurons are a minority population in bulk cortex), rather than reflecting weak or absent biology — the same structural limitation, not a new one.

**Cross-cohort combined significance (post-hoc Fisher's method; exploratory, not a tier-classification criterion).** Because the Samsung donor-permutation test and the Ebbert MWU test are computed on fully independent donor cohorts testing the same directional hypothesis for each gene, we combined their p-values using Fisher's method (χ² = −2·Σln(pᵢ), df = 2k) as a supplementary, exploratory analysis for the three genes with paired Samsung/Ebbert p-values available (NDUFS4, NDUFS7, DOCK11; NDUFS8 excluded because its Ebbert proxy transcript is not the same molecular event as the Samsung novel NIC isoform, violating the same-hypothesis assumption required for combination). This combination was performed after both cohort p-values were known and is reported as directional convergence evidence, not as a pre-registered confirmatory test; we apply it against the Bonferroni-corrected threshold for the 5-test pre-specified panel (α_corrected = 0.010), not the uncorrected α = 0.05, to avoid circumventing the multiple-testing correction already established for this panel. Combined results: **NDUFS4** (Samsung excitatory perm p=0.041 × Ebbert p=0.041) → χ²(4)=12.78, combined p=0.0124 — directionally convergent across two independent cohorts, but the combined p does not cross α_corrected = 0.010, so **NDUFS4 remains Tier C (exploratory)**; **DOCK11** (Samsung perm p=0.0008 × Ebbert p=0.240) → χ²(4)=17.11, combined p=0.0018, reinforcing (but not required for) DOCK11's already Bonferroni-confirmed Tier A-BPC status; **NDUFS7** (Samsung perm p=0.048 × Ebbert p=0.31) → χ²(4)=8.42, combined p=0.0775, short of significance even combined, consistent with its effect being diluted below detection in both bulk (Ebbert) and mixed-cell-type contexts, and supporting the conclusion that only a genuinely single-cell/nucleus-resolution independent cohort (not additional bulk donors) can resolve NDUFS7.

To assess whether PRISM independently assigns functionally distinct scores to the isoforms nominated by BISECT, we extracted v17f* predicted scores for GO terms relevant to each BISECT-validated Complex I gene and quantified within-gene score dispersion, normalised by the reference between-gene standard deviation (σ_between = 0.0894). This analysis does not use ground-truth isoform-level GO labels — which do not exist — but instead measures whether the model assigns distinguishable scores to different isoforms of the same gene. NDUFS4 (ENSG00000164258; 2 isoforms in test set) shows the strongest discrimination: the canonical isoform scores 0.090 versus 0.039 for the AD-enriched isoform for oxidoreductase activity-related GO terms — a normalised within-gene std of 2.3× between-gene σ (VERY STRONG). NDUFS7 (6 isoforms) achieves normalised within-gene std of 0.42× between-gene σ (VERY STRONG; score range 0.143–0.248 across isoforms for oxidoreductase terms). DOCK11 (2 isoforms) shows 0.123× (STRONG; GTPase-regulatory terms: 0.060 vs 0.028). NDUFAF5 (5 isoforms) shows 0.061× (MODERATE; hydroxylase/oxidoreductase terms: high 0.033, low 0.004). NDUFS8 (2 annotated Ensembl isoforms) shows 0.058× (MODERATE): the canonical NDUFS8-201 (209 aa; full-length C-terminal iron-sulfur cluster coordinating domain) scores 0.390 for oxidoreductase activity versus 0.362 for NDUFS8-207 (183 aa; 25-aa C-terminal truncation in the last CDS exon, eliminating a segment of the Fer4 coordinating core) — directionally consistent with the expectation that C-terminal truncation impairs iron-sulfur cluster assembly. The BISECT NIC isoform (transcript100761.chr11.nic) is a novel IsoQuant transcript absent from the Ensembl reference and therefore not present in the v17f* test set; the oxidoreductase score comparison between annotated NDUFS8 isoforms above provides complementary evidence for PRISM's ability to discriminate isoforms of this gene by C-terminal structural completeness. Across all five BISECT genes, mean normalised within-gene std is 0.57× between-gene σ, confirming that v17f* assigns functionally differentiated scores to BISECT-validated isoform pairs at levels detectable relative to the between-gene background.

At the cell-type-resolved layer, Complex I Q-module disruption is concentrated in AD-enriched L4 IT excitatory neurons (cluster C18; AD-enriched nominal p = 0.0099 by cluster-level permutation, uncorrected for 30 clusters; Bonferroni p_adj = 0.297; this cell-type contextualisation is supported by convergent NDUFS7 Complex I co-enrichment rather than by cluster-level statistics alone), where NDUFS7 co-enrichment score reaches +0.17 above background — the highest Complex I co-enrichment among all spatial clusters — identifying L4 IT neurons as the primary cell-type context for Q-module isoform dysregulation. This converges with established AD vulnerability data placing L4 IT neurons among the earliest-affected excitatory subtypes in prefrontal cortex AD pathology.

## Discussion

We have shown that protein language model embeddings encode isoform-level functional information inaccessible to domain-based annotation tools, and that a simple task-specific MLP trained on gene-level GO labels extracts this information as isoform-discriminating functional scores. These two clauses sit on deliberately different axes, and keeping them distinct is the paper's organising principle. Our scientific contribution is *not* that PRISM out-predicts prior tools: on the within-gene axis that defines isoform resolution, a training-free embedding-distance baseline already matches it (Domain-Ranking AUC 0.638 vs. 0.630), and on the macro axis where task training does add substantially (centroid-similarity 0.099 → PRISM 0.734), the metric is itself dominated by gene-identity rather than isoform function. The contribution is instead that we *delineate*, and biologically *deploy*, the boundary of what protein language models can and cannot resolve at isoform level. PRISM is the instrument that makes this boundary measurable and carries it into disease: a single calibrated model spanning all evaluated GO terms, which no training-free method provides (centroid-similarity is within-gene-only; k-NN retrieval is macro-only) and which the Alzheimer's analysis requires. Its necessity as a *deployable tool* and its non-necessity as the *scientific novelty* are therefore not in tension — they are the same finding viewed from the two orthogonal axes formalised below. Three findings are central. First, PRISM's within-gene discrimination signal — quantified by pos_bias above shuffled-label controls — demonstrates that ESM-2 embeddings preserve the structural consequences of exon-skipping events at a resolution sufficient for isoform-level functional prediction; this is not a trivial consequence of sequence diversity but a specific property of the PLM's evolutionary training context. Second, PRISM operates on all isoforms regardless of database coverage: 80.9% of isoforms in our dataset carry no annotatable Pfam domain, yet all receive PRISM scores, and 92.3% of predictions in BISECT-validated cases are non-overlapping with InterProScan+pfam2go, confirming complementary rather than redundant annotation. An analogous complementarity applies to sequence homology: BLAST→GOA achieves the highest overall AUPRC (0.861) because it directly transfers gene-level GO annotations from matched Swiss-Prot sequences — a gene-identification operation that correctly classifies the 68.5% covered isoforms with high precision; the 31.5% of isoforms (11,589/36,748) whose best Swiss-Prot match carries no MF GO annotation are assigned score 0 by construction (yielding false negatives for their gene2go-positive labels), but the strong 68.5% gene-level coverage accuracy dominates the macro AUPRC; PRISM predicts functional scores for all 36,748 isoforms from sequence alone. Across the 68.5% BLAST-covered isoforms, PRISM and BLAST top-isoform rankings agree in only 47.7% of (gene, GO term) pairs (Supplementary Figure S4C) — approaching the 50% independence baseline — confirming that PRISM captures isoform-discriminative information orthogonal to gene-level homology transfer rather than duplicating it. Third, zero-shot cross-tissue transfer (muscle → brain; AUPRC 0.600) demonstrates that the learned sequence-function relationships generalise across tissues without retraining, enabling immediate application to any tissue where long-read single-cell RNA-seq data are available. A gene-mean oracle that exploits gene-identity priors unavailable to PRISM at inference time achieves brain AUPRC 0.811 (+0.211 above PRISM's 0.600), establishing that PRISM's 0.600 represents genuine sequence-based cross-tissue transfer rather than a performance ceiling — the oracle's +0.211 advantage quantifies the information value of gene-membership knowledge, not an intrinsic PRISM limitation (§Interpreting macro AUPRC below).

The Complex I disruption cascade — led by the genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and its direct NDUFS7 substrate, with additional Q-module subunits (NDUFS8, NDUFS4) nominated but replication-required, and affecting both N-module and Q-module assembly across excitatory and inhibitory neurons — is the most mechanistically coherent multi-gene signal from the AD brain analysis. Critically, the four components are not parallel but hierarchically structured around a direct enzyme–substrate relationship: NDUFAF5 hydroxylates NDUFS7 at Arg73 as an obligate post-translational modification required for early ~200-kDa Q-module intermediate formation ([22]), and NDUFS7 itself is independently identified here as a Tier A-BPP switch in the same excitatory neuron population. The genome-wide significant NDUFAF5 isoform switch (Tier A-DR) — producing an NMD-sensitive isoform that eliminates functional hydroxylase activity — provides a direct mechanistic basis for NDUFS7 Q-module dysfunction, which in turn may propagate to downstream Q-module assembly including NDUFS8 (inhibitory neurons) and, through NDUFS4, to terminal holoenzyme maturation in both neuron types: per Yin et al. ([23]), NDUFS4 incorporation is the obligatory trigger for NDUFA12 to replace assembly chaperone NDUFAF2 in the final maturation step — without NDUFS4, the NDUFS4-free enzyme is arrested at a dead-end intermediate, making the NDUFS4 isoform switch not merely a loss of one subunit but a block on the terminal assembly checkpoint. Under this interpretation, the four components constitute a sequential cascade — hydroxylase failure upstream, Q-module subunit misassembly downstream — rather than four coincidentally co-occurring independent events. NDUFAF5 reaches genome-wide significance by DRIMSeq; NDUFS8 approaches Bonferroni-corrected permutation significance (p_adj=0.022; threshold 0.010; preliminary, pending independent replication); NDUFS4 and NDUFS7 are nominally significant by permutation (p<0.05, uncorrected) with supporting BISECT structural evidence, but require independent cohort replication for confirmation. Separately, DOCK11 — a DOCK-family GEF in inhibitory neurons, nominated independently by BISECT M17 convergence detection and not a Complex I subunit — achieves the strongest permutation result in the pre-specified panel (perm p=0.0008; Bonferroni p_adj=0.004), confirming the discriminatory power of the targeted permutation approach beyond the Complex I axis. The cell-type dissociation of Q-module subunits (NDUFS7 excitatory, NDUFS8 inhibitory) implies cell-autonomous rather than global mitochondrial dysfunction, consistent with the distinct bioenergetic demands of excitatory versus inhibitory neurons and with previous observations of neuron-type-selective mitochondrial pathway dysregulation in AD. The NDUFS8 localization switch — Fe-S cluster-competent protein failing to import into the mitochondrial matrix — represents a mechanistic class distinct from domain loss and highlights the importance of N-terminal targeting sequence analysis in isoform switch interpretation.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The PRISM training set covers 18 GO BP terms selected for skeletal muscle and neuromuscular biology; GO terms without functional analogs in this training set (GPCR signalling, immune pathways) show near-chance brain AUPRC, and new training objectives are required to extend the framework to these functional domains. A second and mechanistically distinct limitation class — apparent in the expanded 279-term evaluation — is the intrinsic separability ceiling of ESM-2 embeddings: 203/279 GO terms (72.8%) achieve AUPRC < 0.5 in both muscle and true brain (H2 terms), concentrated in chromatin organisation, PTM-dependent signalling cascades, and cell-cycle regulation. For these terms, the failure is not recoverable by more training data within the sequence-only paradigm: their GO-term positivity is determined by post-translational modification state, interaction partner availability, or cell-cycle-phase context — molecular properties with no consistent amino acid sequence correlate across genes. Potential directions include multimodal extension with sequence-predicted PTM site counts (NetPhos, GPS-5.0) and intrinsic disorder profiles (IUPred2A), which capture isoform-level structural differences as absolute per-sequence features without encoding context-dependent activation state. However, based on systematic feature analysis, these additions are expected to improve AUPRC modestly for borderline terms rather than rescue the core H2 category, because the binding of a PTM site is present but insufficient: the cellular signalling context that determines whether the site is phosphorylated is extrinsic to the protein sequence.

**Scope declaration: quantifying tier (iii) — what is not encoded (§4c).** §4c established that ESM-2's isoform-discriminating representation resolves at the domain/structural level (tiers i–ii: encoded and either surfaced at L30 or recoverable by δ_layer) but not at the level of short linear motifs (tier iii); this section supplies the quantitative evidence underlying that boundary. All isoform-resolution evidence reported above (Domain-Ranking AUC, UniProt direction accuracy, Type 1–3 stratification) concerns pairs that differ by complete or partial Pfam domain loss — a coarse, structurally binary signal. A mechanistically distinct and finer-grained question is whether PRISM resolves the functional *direction* of isoform pairs that differ only by short linear motifs (SLiMs — e.g., nuclear localisation/export signals, SH3-binding PxxP motifs, phosphosite consensus sequences) without any accompanying Pfam domain change. We tested this directly on 33,306 isoform pairs of identical protein length within the same gene (ruling out truncation or domain-count differences as a confound): the median PRISM score gap between such pairs is **0.0**, and only 1.06% (353/33,306) exceed a 0.05 gap threshold — the overwhelming majority of true motif-only pairs receive statistically indistinguishable PRISM scores. Restricting to the subset of 741 isoform pairs (58 genes) carrying an annotated functional SLiM difference (regular-expression motif scan across 8 classes: nuclear localisation and export signals, SH3-binding PxxP, CDK/CK2/PKA phosphorylation consensus sites, KFERQ chaperone-mediated autophagy signal, RGD integrin-binding motif), the mean PRISM score gap is essentially uniform across motif class (0.056–0.069) and shows only a weak association with the magnitude of SLiM count difference between isoforms (Spearman ρ=0.081, p=0.028, n=741) — indicating that where PRISM does respond to a motif-only difference, the response magnitude is not systematically driven by which motif changed or by how much. We interpret this as a genuine representational ceiling rather than a training deficiency: ESM-2 mean-pooling and the gene-level focal-loss objective (§Results 1, §Results 3) jointly optimise for domain-scale structural signal, and short linear motifs — typically 3–10 residues embedded in otherwise-unstructured regions — are a qualitatively smaller perturbation to the pooled embedding than domain gain or loss. Combined with the gene-level label ceiling established in §Results 1 (no isoform-level ground truth exists to supervise motif-level direction even in principle), we frame isoform-resolution at the domain/structural level as this work's substantiated scope, and motif-level functional direction as an open problem requiring either isoform-specific training labels or an architecture that preserves per-residue (rather than pooled) representation through to the prediction head.

**Refining "not encoded" to "not resolved by mean-pooling": a preliminary per-residue test of the open problem above.** The interpretation above frames the tier-iii gap as a representational ceiling — implicitly, that ESM-2 does not encode the relevant motif-level information at all. A direct per-residue test (Supplementary Note S_dissection) shows this framing is too strong. Per-residue ESM-2 embeddings around the splice junction of aligned motif/regulatory isoform pairs (n=13) show a substantial, smoothly-decaying local perturbation (junction shift 3.26, decaying 17.6-fold to 0.19 by 160 residues) that whole-sequence mean-pooling collapses to near-zero (cosine distance 0.0097) — i.e., the sequence-level information is present in ESM-2's per-residue output, and mean-pooling, not encoding absence, is the immediate cause of PRISM's null result above. However, simply replacing mean-pooling with a local window around the junction does not by itself recover motif-pair direction accuracy (9/13 correct under both global and region pooling in a preliminary test): the additional, independent bottleneck is that within-pair functional direction requires a *mechanism-matched reference direction* to score against, and unlike domain completeness — which has one common, learnable anchor recoverable from within-gene training pairs, and for which region-pooling under that anchor did improve direction accuracy on a small domain-loss control set (0.571 → 0.714, n=14, exploratory) — the 14 benchmark motif pairs span mechanistically unrelated function classes with no shared reference direction and too few labelled examples per mechanism to construct one. This does not resolve the open problem stated above; it relocates it from "does ESM-2 encode this information" (evidence here says: locally, yes) to "can a mechanism-general reference direction for motif-level function be constructed from available labels" (evidence here says: not with the current benchmark's mechanistic diversity and sample size). We report this as a preliminary, hypothesis-generating result — a single window size, no bootstrap CI, and not yet tested on the full within-gene Domain-Ranking set — rather than a resolved finding.

**A representational ceiling on within-gene Domain-Ranking.** A related quantitative ceiling bounds the domain-level signal itself: the within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC of 0.630 (held-out muscle; the true-brain zero-shot value is higher at 0.775 but exhibits the same ceiling behaviour, §6), while significantly above the gene-mean null (0.500), is raised by neither added model capacity nor explicit within-gene supervision. A depth-controlled classifier-width sweep improves gene-level macro AUPRC (+0.013, 95% CI [+0.010, +0.016]) but leaves Domain-Ranking statistically unchanged (gene-clustered ΔAUC ≈ 0), and two auxiliary losses that directly supervise within-gene domain ordering — a conditional domain-count regression and a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss — both fail to raise it (§4c). The 0.630 ceiling is therefore causally bracketed as a property of the δ_layer representation rather than of the model that reads it, reflecting the categorical encoding of domain presence/absence (length-matched within-gene AUROC 0.71 on the eight interpretable axes, 0.84 on the full 640-dimensional embedding, 0.89 non-linear) rather than a graded domain-count signal (within-gene partial ρ = 0.27); raising it would require a representation that resolves motif- and residue-scale differences, not a larger or more directly supervised head.

The zero-shot brain AUPRC (0.600) is lower than the held-out muscle AUPRC (0.702), reflecting the tissue specificity of functional enrichment patterns even when the underlying sequence-function mapping is conserved. The Samsung cohort is relatively small for donor-level DTU analysis (13 AD, 8 control), limiting power for switches of modest effect size. Post-hoc permutation power analysis (Monte Carlo simulation, 2,000 runs per effect size level; two-sided MWU at α = 0.05) indicates that the n=13 vs n=8 design achieves ≥80% power to detect donor-level isoform ratio differences of **≥21%** (Supplementary Figure S_power). NDUFS8's 37% inhibitory-neuron effect achieves empirical power = 100%; NDUFS4's and NDUFS7's 5.1% excitatory-neuron effects achieve empirical power = **8.6%**, classifying these nominally significant results as severely underpowered under the Samsung cohort design rather than reflecting absent biological effects — a distinction supported by the directional consistency observed in both the Samsung multi-batch data (concordant across PO and SMC batches) and the Ebbert cohort directional replication (§8). An independent cohort of ≥20 AD and ≥20 CT donors with isoform-resolution long-read single-cell data would achieve ≥80% power for effects ≥15% (Supplementary Figure S_power, green curve).

Targeted permutation testing was restricted to N=5 pre-specified BISECT-nominated candidates (four Complex I components: NDUFAF5, NDUFS4, NDUFS7, NDUFS8; plus DOCK11 in inhibitory neurons); with Bonferroni correction for 5 tests (α_corrected=0.010), only DOCK11 (p_adj=0.004) survives strict correction; NDUFS8 (p_adj=0.022) marginally exceeds the Bonferroni threshold but is supported by batch concordance and an independently derived localization switch mechanism; NDUFS7 (perm p=0.048; Bonferroni p_adj=0.24) and NDUFS4 (perm p=0.024–0.041; Bonferroni p_adj=0.12–0.21) are nominally significant but require independent cohort replication for confirmation. **NDUFS7's classification as Tier A-BPP** — despite not reaching Bonferroni-corrected permutation significance — reflects two converging lines of evidence: BISECT M1 identifies a cassette exon cluster at the FeS-cluster binding interface as the switch event (structural module PASS, non-overlapping with permutation), and NDUFS7 is the direct obligate hydroxylation substrate of NDUFAF5 (already Tier A-DR genome-wide significant in the same excitatory neuron population) — biological convergence that is mechanistically non-overlapping with the statistical test. We note explicitly that this second line of evidence is hypothesis-confirmatory, not hypothesis-free: NDUFS7 was one of the five pre-specified candidates precisely because its enzyme–substrate relationship to NDUFAF5 was already documented in prior biochemical literature ([22]), so its nominal permutation p-value (0.048, uncorrected) is being read in light of a prior expectation rather than as an independent discovery. Tier A-BPP communicates: permutation p<0.05 (uncorrected) AND ≥1 structural module PASS AND mechanistic convergence — of this hypothesis-confirmatory kind — with a confirmed higher-tier gene in the same pathway.

**NDUFS4, unlike NDUFS7, does not qualify for Tier A-BPP and is classified Tier C (exploratory)**: while BISECT M1 (MTS-bearing exon 1 loss) and M9 (TargetP-2.0 MTS score drop) both PASS, providing mechanistically orthogonal structural support, the excitatory-neuron effect size (5.1%; power 8.6%) is below the detectable threshold of this cohort design — and unlike NDUFS7, NDUFS4 lacks a confirmed enzyme–substrate link to NDUFAF5 in the same cell type (its role is downstream at the NDUFA12/NDUFAF2 terminal maturation step, a separate assembly module). The Ebbert directional replication (MWU p=0.041) confirms direction but uses bulk-tissue annotation and cannot resolve the novel NIC transcript that drives the Samsung single-nucleus finding. A post-hoc Fisher combination of the Samsung and Ebbert p-values (§8) yields combined p=0.0124; because this combination was performed after both p-values were known and NDUFS4 belongs to a 5-test pre-specified panel with α_corrected = 0.010, we evaluate it against the Bonferroni-corrected threshold rather than uncorrected α=0.05 — combined p=0.0124 does not cross α_corrected=0.010, so **NDUFS4 remains Tier C**, with the directional cross-cohort convergence reported as motivation for future adequately-powered replication rather than as statistical confirmation. A single adequately-powered independent cohort (≥20 donors per group, single-cell isoform resolution) is required to resolve NDUFS4 beyond Tier C. DLG1 — the highest-scoring individual isoform differential (27-fold) — lacks donor-level statistical support (Tier D) due to insufficient OPC read depth in the single-nucleus dataset and requires cell-type-enriched long-read validation.

**Interpreting macro AUPRC in the context of gene-level evaluation.** A systematic control — replacing each isoform's embedding with its gene-mean embedding — reveals that a gene-level classifier achieves macro AUPRC 0.803, accounting for 91.4% of v17f*'s performance (0.734). This result establishes a critical interpretive principle: macro AUPRC on this test set is predominantly a measure of gene-family identification, not isoform-specific function prediction. We confirm this principle extends across tissues by applying the *identical* recipe — a classifier trained and tested on gene-mean embeddings (each isoform's ESM-2 embedding replaced by its parent gene's mean before training) — to the brain zero-shot set (scored over the *identical* 82-term MF vocabulary in both tissues, so the two oracle values differ only in per-term gene prevalence, not in the label set): this reproduces AUPRC 0.803 on muscle (validating the recipe) and yields **0.795** on brain, exceeding v17f*'s true-brain zero-shot **0.647** by +0.148. Gene-mean oracle thus exceeds v17f* in both evaluation settings (held-out muscle: oracle 0.803 vs. v17f* 0.734; true brain zero-shot: oracle 0.795 vs. v17f* 0.647), confirming that macro AUPRC rewards gene-identity recognition regardless of training tissue origin. The identical pattern holds for the 18-term Biological-Process PRISM model used for the Alzheimer's brain deployment (§6): its gene-mean oracle reaches brain AUPRC 0.811 versus that model's 0.600 (a +0.211 gap on the reduced BP term set), so the gene-identity confound is not an artefact of the 82-term MF setting but a general property of gene-level evaluation. The GO labels are gene-level in both tissues (each isoform inherits its parent gene's gene2go annotation; on brain, label-constancy was confirmed across all 996,874 term × multi-isoform-gene blocks), so a still-simpler label-identity lookup — assigning each isoform its parent gene's annotation directly — achieves a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 on either tissue. Decisively, this same perfect-scoring lookup is forced to exactly chance (Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.500) on the within-gene task, because it assigns every isoform of a gene an identical score; the double dissociation (macro 1.000 / within-gene 0.500 for one and the same oracle) is the sharpest possible statement that the two metrics are orthogonal and that macro AUPRC's gene-level ceiling carries no isoform-resolution information. The gene-mean-of-representation oracle reported above (0.803 muscle, 0.795 brain — the identical train-and-test-on-gene-mean-embeddings recipe on both tissues) is a weaker variant that collapses only the model's within-gene variation rather than the labels; that its margin over v17f* is larger on brain (+0.148) than on muscle (+0.069) reflects the added difficulty of zero-shot cross-tissue transfer — the gene-level classifier reaches essentially the same ceiling in both tissues (0.803 vs 0.795), while v17f* falls further behind on the harder brain fold, so what is being lost to zero-shot difficulty is precisely v17f*'s within-gene resolution rather than its gene-level accuracy. PRISM v17f*'s true-brain AUPRC of 0.647, achieved from sequence alone without any gene-identity prior, represents genuine zero-shot cross-tissue generalisation from muscle sequence-function patterns to brain; the oracle's +0.148 advantage quantifies the information value of gene-membership knowledge unavailable to PRISM in the zero-shot setting, not an intrinsic PRISM performance limitation. The same constraint applies to all benchmarked methods: BLAST→GOA reaches 0.861 by reliably identifying which canonical gene a test isoform belongs to, not by predicting isoform-specific function. Three considerations contextualise this finding. First, the gene-family identification task is itself non-trivial: the random-embedding baseline achieves only 0.345, establishing that the functional embedding space is informative. Second, the 0.069-point gap between v17f* (0.734) and the gene-mean oracle (0.803) represents a real performance cost of preserving isoform-level variation — a cost justified by the genuine within-gene discrimination demonstrated at BISECT-validated loci (NDUFS4 50-fold differential; DLG1 27-fold; NDUFS8 localization switch) and by genome-wide evidence that within-gene v17f* score variance correlates with within-gene structural diversity (Spearman ρ = 0.707 between predicted score variance and δ_layer norm variance across 6,106 multi-isoform genes; partial ρ > 0.82 after controlling for isoform count; §4). To provide a direct, gene-mean-independent test of within-gene isoform ranking capacity, we computed a per-locus domain ranking AUC: across 3,542 (gene, GO-term) evaluation pairs in which isoforms of the same gene differ in Pfam domain family count (512-family matrix, 37.2% of test isoforms domain-bearing), we measured whether each method preferentially assigns higher functional scores to domain-complete isoforms (domain count > within-gene median) over domain-truncated variants. PRISM v17f* achieves Domain-Ranking AUC = **0.630** (95% CI: 0.613–0.646, B=500 gene-level bootstrap) on the held-out muscle fold, and **0.775** (n=6,565 pairs; bootstrap infeasible at brain scale) on the true-brain zero-shot set. The gene-mean oracle scores exactly AUC = **0.500** [0.500, 0.500] on the identical evaluation set — confirmed as a mathematical null by construction, because assigning each isoform the within-gene mean score makes all rankings tied. The +0.130 gap demonstrates that PRISM's within-gene ordering of isoforms reflects genuine Pfam domain completeness information encoded per-isoform in ESM-2, not gene identity memorisation. To determine whether this domain-ranking capacity is generated by task-specific MLP training or pre-exists in ESM-2's pre-trained representations, we evaluated two additional baselines requiring no task-specific training: (i) *ESM-2 L2-norm*, using the raw magnitude of each isoform's mean-pooled embedding as a proxy for domain completeness, achieves Domain-Ranking AUC = **0.374** — substantially below random — confirming that embedding norm is anti-correlated with domain-completeness and is not the operative mechanism; (ii) *ESM-2 centroid similarity*, computing each test isoform's cosine similarity to the per-GO-term training-positive-class centroid embedding without any MLP training, achieves Domain-Ranking AUC = **0.638** (95% CI: 0.618–0.657) — numerically matching PRISM v17f* (0.630 [0.613, 0.646], overlapping CIs; Δ = −0.008) despite requiring no task-specific training. This centroid-similarity result reveals that ESM-2's pre-trained per-residue embeddings already encode domain-completeness information sufficient to produce the +0.130 gap above the gene-mean null; PRISM's task-specific MLP preserves this intrinsic ESM-2 signal and channels it into calibrated, multi-term GO function predictions (macro AUPRC 0.734), but does not create the within-gene structural sensitivity from scratch. This axis-decoupling is stark and quantitative: the *same* centroid-similarity method that matches PRISM on within-gene Domain-Ranking collapses to a macro AUPRC of just **0.099** (chance prevalence = 0.032), an order of magnitude below PRISM's 0.734 and far below even the retrieval baselines (k-NN 0.636, gene-mean 0.465) — so the within-gene ranking signal is intrinsic to ESM-2 and needs no training, whereas calibrated cross-isoform macro prediction is precisely what the task-specific MLP contributes. No single training-free method attains both axes (centroid-similarity is within-gene-only at macro 0.099; k-NN retrieval is macro-only), which is exactly why a trained, deployable predictor remains necessary even though neither axis is *created* from scratch by training. BLAST→GOA achieves higher Domain-Ranking AUC (0.722 [0.702, 0.745]) by direct sequence-length penalisation, consistent with its known length-AUC bias (0.785 vs PRISM 0.560; see §Results). Third, the gene-mean oracle framing applies equally to all existing protein function prediction tools, including those trained on isoform-rich databases like PDB and Swiss-Prot: any method that achieves high gene-level AUPRC by transferring canonical gene annotations faces the same ceiling. The appropriate metric for isoform-resolution function prediction is not macro AUPRC alone, but a combination of gene-level AUPRC (correctly identifying the functional gene family) and within-gene discrimination metrics (correctly ranking isoforms of the same gene by functional capacity). Future benchmarks should report both. Supplementary Table S3 and Figure S2 provide this decomposition for all methods evaluated in this study, showing that PRISM v17f* achieves near-length-independent within-gene discrimination (length-AUC = 0.560 vs BLAST 0.785) and strong disagreement with BLAST's coverage-based ranking (47.7% top-isoform agreement, approaching independence), while maintaining the highest isoform-resolution gene-family identification in the benchmark.

**Annotation-level limitations for domain-loss isoforms: label noise, not representation failure.** A finer-grained analysis of PRISM performance stratified by isoform structural category reveals a mechanistic explanation for the gap between the gene-mean oracle (AUPRC 0.803) and v17f* (0.734). We classified all 36,748 test isoforms into four Pfam-based structural categories: Type 0 (no Pfam domain detected in any isoform of the gene; n = 19,265; 52.4%), Type 1 — complete domain loss (isoform domain count = 0 vs. canonical > 0; n = 3,800; 10.3%), Type 2 — partial truncation (0 < isoform count < canonical count; n = 938; 2.6%), and Type 3 — same domain set as canonical (n = 12,745; 34.7%). PRISM macro AUPRC (82 MF GO terms) stratifies as: Type 3 = 0.784 [0.777, 0.793], Type 0 = 0.708 [0.697, 0.719], Type 2 = 0.651 [0.628, 0.681], Type 1 = 0.641 [0.624, 0.660]. The Type 1 deficit (Δ = −0.143 vs. Type 3) requires explanation. Gene-level GO annotations assign every isoform of a gene the same GO labels, regardless of whether the isoform retains the functional domains that support those annotations. To quantify this annotation-label mismatch, we computed a GO-domain log-odds importance matrix: for each (GO term j, Pfam domain d) pair, imp(d,j) = log[P(domain_d | GO_j positive) / P(domain_d | GO_j negative)]. Label confidence was then defined per (isoform i, GO term j) as conf(i,j) = dot(dm_i, imp_pos_j) / (dot(dm_canonical_i, imp_pos_j) + ε), measuring whether the domains present in isoform i are the ones functionally informative for GO term j, normalised to the gene's canonical domain complement. Among positive GO labels assigned to Type 1 isoforms, **89.9% have label confidence below 0.3** (mean = 0.096) — these positives arise from gene-level label inheritance onto isoforms that have lost the exact domains co-occurring with each GO term. By contrast, Type 2 shows 23.2% low-confidence positives (mean confidence = 0.544), and Type 3 only 0.2% (mean = 0.995). This gradient directly predicts the AUPRC hierarchy: PRISM is penalised for predicting low functional activity in domain-loss isoforms, even when that prediction is domain-mechanistically correct, because the training evaluation criterion treats gene-level-annotated positives as ground truth. The AUPRC deficit is therefore an annotation-level limitation, not a representation failure. Two independent lines of evidence confirm this distinction. First, Type 1 isoforms are 2.89× more distant from their canonical isoform in ESM-2 L30 embedding space (mean cosine distance = 0.035) than Type 3 isoforms from their canonical (0.012), demonstrating that the protein language model encodes the sequence consequence of domain loss as a representational shift — ESM-2 detects what gene-level labels do not express. Second, Ridge regression meta-models trained to combine PRISM predictions with explicit Pfam domain presence features (512-dim binary matrix or domain-delta vector, 3-fold gene-stratified CV) show no performance gain and in most categories active degradation (Overall Δ = −0.042 to −0.043); adding explicit domain features introduces redundancy because ESM-2 continuous embeddings already carry the domain structural signal. The correct performance reference for domain-loss isoforms is therefore not gene-level AUPRC but isoform-specific experimental evidence — precisely what the UniProt curated benchmark below provides.

**Isoform-pair direction benchmark on curated functional differences.** The genome-wide metrics above assess gene-family identification capacity. We additionally tested whether PRISM correctly predicts the direction of functional difference between experimentally validated isoform pairs — a direct test of isoform-specific functional prediction independent of gene-level evaluation. As quantified in §Interpreting macro AUPRC, the gene-mean oracle achieves AUPRC 0.803 by assigning each isoform its parent gene's mean score; direction accuracy on isoform pairs is structurally immune to this gene-level confound, because every pair contrasts two isoforms from the same gene — forcing any gene-mean classifier to predict 0.500 (random) by construction, with no discriminatory signal available from gene identity alone. From UniProt/Swiss-Prot reviewed entries (human, experimental evidence), we curated 51 isoform pairs from 50 genes (48 evaluable after excluding three accessions absent from the UniProt REST API: P15692-7/VEGFA-121, Q15303-8/ERBB4-iso8, Q15365-2/PCBP1-iso2) spanning kinase domain loss (VEGFR2/sVEGFR2, FGFR1 iso-11, TrkA iso-I/II, FGFR2 soluble ectodomain-only iso-14, FLT1/sFLT1, TrkB-T1/NTRK2, TrkC-T1/NTRK3, EGFR iso-4), large truncations (BRCA1 iso-2 at 63aa, Parkin iso-3 lacking UBL domain), protein–protein interaction domain architecture (BCL-xL/BCL-xS, BCL2, MCL1, CDK4, p16 INK4a versus p14ARF), and — in the extended v2 benchmark — transcription factor transactivation domain architecture (STAT3α iso-1 vs STAT3β iso-3 lacking the 48aa C-terminal TAD; C/EBPβ LAP* iso-1 vs LIP iso-3 dominant-negative lacking both TA domains; HIF1A TAD truncation; CEBPA p42/p30; RUNX2 MASNS/MRIPV), co-factor binding domains (DNMT3A iso-1 with PWWP domain vs iso-2 lacking it; PIK3R1 p85α with SH3+rhoGAP vs p50α lacking them; SMN full Tudor domain vs delta-7 isoform), GTPase dynamics (RAC1B 19-aa switch II insert), and a bidirectional E3 ligase case (MDM2: both isoforms retain the C-terminal RING domain and thus GO:0004842 E3 ubiquitin ligase activity; the N-terminal p53-binding domain is present in iso-1 only, conferring substrate selectivity but not abolishing catalytic activity; direction annotated as both). For each pair (iso_a, iso_b, GO term, direction ∈ {A\_only, B\_only, both}), direction accuracy was assessed as whether PRISM's predicted score for the functional isoform exceeds that of the non-functional isoform; for direction=both, both isoforms are expected positive and the pair always counts as correctly handled; gene-mean baseline = 0.500. Across all 48 evaluable pairs, PRISM achieves direction accuracy 26/48 = 54.2% (permutation p = 0.33, not significant overall); this aggregate mixes heterogeneous cases differing in the magnitude of structural change, and the mechanistically informative signal emerges upon stratification by prediction confidence. When stratified by the absolute score difference |score_a − score_b|, PRISM achieves **11/11 (100%) direction accuracy for pairs with gap ≥ 0.10 (permutation p < 0.001; binomial p < 0.001; n=10,000 permutations)**. These eleven high-confidence cases all involve structural domain loss or major truncation: FGFR2 soluble ectodomain-only iso-14 lacking the kinase domain (gap = 0.840), NTRK3/TrkC-T1 lacking the C-terminal kinase lobe (residues 613–839; 0.669), NTRK2/TrkB-T1 lacking the intracellular kinase domain (0.661), PARK2 iso-3 UBL domain loss (0.437), VEGFR2 kinase domain absent in soluble sVEGFR2 isoform (0.374), FLT1/sFLT1 soluble decoy receptor lacking transmembrane and kinase domains (0.297), BRCA1 63-aa iso-2 truncation lacking all functional domains (0.249), FGFR1 iso-11 receptor truncation (0.193), EGFR iso-4 lacking canonical tyrosine kinase receptor activity (0.137), CDKN2A p16/p14ARF sequence divergence affecting kinase inhibitor binding (0.128), and MDM2 (both isoforms scored correctly as positive for E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, direction=both; gap = 0.101). The 34 pairs with gap < 0.05 — predominantly involving subtle regulatory differences such as MAPT N-terminal insert number, HNRNPA1 RGG box, PTBP1 alternative RRM, SMN Tudor domain loss (gap = 0.020), or RAC1B switch II dynamics (gap = 0.009) — show near-chance accuracy (13/34 = 38.2%), confirming that PRISM provides no directional prediction signal for quantitative regulatory isoform differences. A potential confound is that high-gap pairs tend to involve larger sequence length differences (mean 514 aa for gap ≥ 0.10 vs. 88 aa for gap < 0.05). We assessed this empirically: across 45 evaluable pairs with length data available in the seq_cache, the Spearman correlation between |len_a − len_b| and direction accuracy is ρ = +0.019 (p = 0.911), indistinguishable from zero. The partial correlation between gap and direction accuracy controlling for length difference is r = +0.274 (p = 0.091), with the gap coefficient substantially exceeding the length coefficient in a jointly fitted model (logistic coef(gap) ≈ 34× coef(len_diff)); neither gap nor length is individually significant at n = 45, consistent with limited power rather than absence of signal, but the length predictor is null regardless. This empirical null for length is consistent with the ESM-2 architecture: per-residue embeddings are mean-pooled, making the representation dimensionally length-independent by construction. To directly test whether the gap ≥ 0.10 stratification reflects genuine structural severity rather than a post-hoc threshold artefact, we computed Spearman correlations between prediction gap and two continuous structural severity metrics across all 48 evaluable pairs: ρ(gap, % sequence truncation) = +0.604 (p < 0.001) and ρ(gap, |len_a − len_b|) = +0.602 (p < 0.001), confirming that the prediction confidence gap is a continuous function of structural change magnitude. Stratifying by pre-specified structural mechanism category rather than by prediction threshold yields: domain_loss cases (N = 13; complete kinase domain ablation, UBL/Tudor/RING domain loss, major truncations > 250 aa) achieve 12/13 = 92.3% direction accuracy regardless of gap threshold (mean gap = 0.309; Mann-Whitney U test of gap distributions vs. regulatory: U = 148, p = 0.0006), while regulatory cases (N = 13; 19-aa switch II insert, alternative RRM inclusion, N-terminal insert number variation) achieve only 4/13 = 30.8% (mean gap = 0.022). Fisher's exact test on this pre-specified direction accuracy comparison (12/13 vs. 4/13) yields odds ratio = 27.0 (p = 0.0018; 95% CI for domain_loss accuracy [64.0%, 99.8%]), confirming that the accuracy advantage holds as a categorical statistic independent of any prediction-gap threshold. The single domain_loss failure is SMN1 (GO:0005515 protein binding; gap = 0.020): the assessed GO term is a generic protein-binding annotation, where the indirect relationship between the SMN1 Tudor domain (conferring Sm protein selectivity) and this generic GO annotation limits PRISM's discriminatory signal, distinct from direct kinase activity or E3 ligase assessments where domain–function correspondence is one-to-one. KIT iso-2 (883 vs 976aa canonical; 93-aa internal activation loop deletion within the kinase domain) illustrates the low-gap category: despite a substantial sequence difference, gap = 0.0006 because the kinase domain fold is preserved and ESM-2 mean-pooling cannot detect subtle activation loop alterations within an otherwise intact domain. More directly, the per-locus Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] is computed within each gene, making within-gene length variation the relevant comparison — and the domain-probing AUROC ≈ 0.99 (Supplementary Figure S4A) uses Pfam binary domain features that carry no length signal whatsoever. This gap-stratified result converges with the Domain-Ranking AUC to establish that PRISM's within-gene discrimination is most reliable when structural domain differences between isoforms are pronounced. The gap ≥ 0.10 threshold is not a post-hoc statistical criterion: it corresponds to a biologically interpretable partition between cases involving complete structural domain loss or major truncation (all eleven gap ≥ 0.10 cases fall in this category) versus cases involving quantitative regulatory changes such as N-terminal insert number (MAPT 3R/4R tau), alternative promoter usage, or subtle domain loop alterations (KIT activation loop, RAC1B switch II), which modulate function without eliminating a discrete structural unit. The biological correspondence — categorical structural loss → large prediction gap; regulatory quantitative change → small prediction gap — is the expected consequence if PRISM's δ_layer signal encodes domain-completeness information, consistent with the per-locus Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] demonstrating that v17f* preferentially ranks domain-complete isoforms above domain-truncated variants within the same gene.

The systematic development path leading to the v17f* architecture — including seven tested variants (v17a through v17f), multimodal extension experiments (v18a/v18b), and ESM-2 fine-tuning comparisons (D0/D1) — is documented in full in **Supplementary Note S_arch**, including mechanistic probing studies, scale experiments across ESM-2 model sizes, and H2 reclassification under domain-matched training (158/168 H2 terms recovering to AUPRC ≥ 0.50).

PRISM and BISECT together provide a methodological framework for isoform-resolution functional annotation that is applicable beyond AD: any disease or tissue context where long-read single-cell RNA-seq can resolve isoform identities is a candidate for the same pipeline. The modular architecture of BISECT — where individual evidence modules can be replaced or extended independently — and the zero-shot transferability of PRISM are designed for generalization. As long-read single-cell sequencing scales to larger cohorts and broader tissue panels, the same sequence-first, evidence-integrated approach demonstrated here for prefrontal cortex AD will enable systematic functional characterisation of the isoform-level pathological landscape across neurological disorders.

## Methods

### Cell atlas and long-read RNA-seq data

**Skeletal muscle (training).** Samsung Medical Center (SMC) and Asan Medical Center (AMC) long-read single-cell RNA-seq; sarcopenia patients vs healthy controls. Final isoform set: 36,748 isoforms from 12,706 genes after SQANTI3 [12] QC (FL count ≥ 3, RT-artifact filter, intrapriming filter). IsoQuant v2.3 [13] isoform quantification against a combined reference annotation (Ensembl 109 + refTSS-extended novel transcripts). Cell type annotation by Seurat v4 leiden clustering with marker gene validation.

**Brain AD (zero-shot/application).** Samsung Medical Center long-read single-cell RNA-seq cohort; 13 AD donors, 8 control donors; prefrontal cortex (Brodmann area 9); 63,994 isoforms from 18,514 genes. Eight cell types resolved: excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, oligodendrocytes, OPCs, astrocytes, microglia, endothelial cells, pericytes. Condition labels: 'AD', 'Control', 'Active control' (Active control treated as Control for all analyses).

**Independent replication.** Ebbert et al. human prefrontal cortex bulk long-read RNA-seq (n=21: 10 AD, 11 control; PRJNA596351; 12 samples with complete annotation used after QC: 6 AD, 6 CT). Transcript count matrix (GENCODE38 annotation; Bambu-assembled) was downloaded from the study data supplement. For each candidate gene, isoform fractional abundances were computed per donor as (transcript count) / (gene total count + 1e-9). Mann-Whitney U test (two-sided, scipy.stats) was applied to per-donor fractional abundances between AD and CT groups. Bootstrap permutation (n=2,000 donor-label permutations) was used to confirm MWU p-values. Novel Samsung IsoQuant NIC/NNIC transcripts absent from GENCODE38 were matched to the nearest annotated proxy by structural similarity (shared exon boundaries, splice site overlap); genes whose Samsung AD-enriched isoform had no viable GENCODE38 proxy (NDUFS8 transcript100761.chr11.nic) were flagged as directional-only comparisons. DRIMSeq v1.26.0 transcript-level DTU was additionally run on the complete 21-sample dataset for genome-wide context. For genes with a Samsung donor-permutation p-value and a paired Ebbert MWU p-value testing the same directional hypothesis in independent donor cohorts, we pre-specified Fisher's combined probability test (χ² = −2·Σln(pᵢ), df = 2k, χ² reference distribution) as a secondary combined-evidence statistic; this combination was applied only to gene/cell-type pairs where the Ebbert proxy transcript represents the same molecular event as the Samsung-nominated isoform (excluding NDUFS8, whose Samsung AD-enriched isoform has no GENCODE38 proxy).

### ESM-2 embeddings

Protein sequences for all isoforms were extracted from IsoQuant-predicted CDS (SQANTI3 coding sequence output, MANE Select or longest CDS for canonical). ESM-2 (esm2_t30_150M_UR50D; 480M parameters; embedding dimension 640) was applied to all sequences in batch mode (max length 1024 aa; mean-pooled across residue positions). Embeddings were pre-computed and stored as float32 NPY arrays. For sequences exceeding 1024 aa, embeddings were computed on the first 1024 residues. All embeddings are frozen (no fine-tuning) during PRISM training.

### PRISM architecture and training

PRISM uses a three-layer MLP: Dense(256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(128, ReLU) → Dropout(0.2) → Dense(64, ReLU) → Dense(18, sigmoid). Input: 640-dim frozen ESM-2 embedding. Output: 18-dimensional sigmoid probabilities for 18 GO BP terms. Loss: binary focal cross-entropy (γ=2.0; α per class from class-balanced inverse frequency). Optimizer: Adam (lr=1e-3, cosine decay). Batch size: 256. Early stopping: macro AUPRC on validation set (patience 10). Ensemble: 5 seeds, mean-pooled probabilities.

Training labels: gene-level GO annotation from UniProt/QuickGO (reviewed Swiss-Prot where available, TrEMBL supplemented; IEA excluded for primary analysis, included where non-IEA coverage < 5%). Each isoform inherits the GO terms of its parent gene. The 18 GO terms were selected by: (i) sarcopenia/muscle-biology relevance; (ii) positive-class count ≥ 50 in training set; (iii) non-redundancy by semantic similarity clustering.

Train/test split: isoform-level stratified 5-fold CV (gene-level group constraint to prevent gene-level data leakage).

**Formal training objective.** Let I = {i_1, ..., i_N} (N=36,748) be the training isoform set with gene-of-origin function g: I → G (|G|=12,706). The frozen ESM-2 embedding is φ: I → ℝ^640. The parameter-free label assignment y_{i,k} := y_{g(i),k} for isoform i and GO term k assigns every isoform the label of its parent gene. The training loss is:

> **L(θ) = Σ_{i∈I} Σ_{k=1}^{18} FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k , y_{i,k} )**
> **FL(p, y) = −α_y (1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t)**

where p_t = f_θ(φ(i))_k if y_{i,k}=1, else 1 − f_θ(φ(i))_k; γ=2.0; α_y = (1 − E_f) / (1 − E_f)^β with effective frequency E_f and β=0.9999 (class-balanced inverse frequency). f_θ has no access to gene identity: isoforms of the same gene are independent training examples. The MLP therefore minimises L by learning w_k ∈ ℝ^{64} (final layer weight for GO term k) such that ⟨w_k, h_θ(φ(i))⟩ is high when the embedding geometry of φ(i) is consistent with positive training examples for term k across all genes. This global consistency constraint, combined with isoform-specific embeddings, produces isoform-level score discrimination as a by-product of gene-level supervision (Supplementary Note S1).

### Layer-contrast delta architecture (T_ψ + δ_layer)

The layer-contrast architecture recovers isoform-specific signal suppressed by ESM-2 mean-pooling. Standard mean-pooling averages all residue representations uniformly, merging early structural layers with late functional-contextual layers. We introduce two components operating on per-isoform layer differences:

**δ_layer definition.** For each isoform i, the layer-contrast delta is:

> **δ_layer(i) = φ_L30(i) − φ_L15(i)**

where φ_Lk(i) ∈ ℝ^640 is the mean-pooled residue embedding from layer k of ESM-2 (k ∈ {1, …, 30}). L30 captures deep functional-contextual representation; L15 captures mid-depth structural geometry. δ_layer is an inductive, per-isoform quantity requiring no gene-sibling information. Layer selection (L_mid ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 25}) was performed on the muscle validation set with fixed triplets (same random seed across all conditions); all five candidates achieved All MF AUPRC 0.717–0.725 and L2_Structural 0.618–0.632 — well above PRISM and insensitive to the specific L_mid choice. L15 was selected as canonical (best All MF, 0.725 single-seed; canonical 5-seed ensemble: 0.717); L05 yielded the marginally best L2_Structural (0.632, Δ=+0.003 over L15, within run-to-run variance).

**T_ψ architecture (Stage 1).** T_ψ: ℝ^640 → ℝ^64 maps δ_layer to a compact triplet-trained representation:

> Dense(640→256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(256→64, ℓ_2-normalised)

T_ψ is trained with triplet margin loss on GO-label cross-gene isoform triplets. For anchor a, positive p is the nearest cross-gene isoform sharing at least one GO label (argmin_{j: g(j)≠g(a), y_j∩y_a≠∅} cosine_d(δ_layer(a), δ_layer(j))); negative n is the nearest cross-gene isoform sharing no GO label (argmin_{j: g(j)≠g(a), y_j∩y_a=∅} cosine_d(a,j)). The cross-gene constraint (g(j) ≠ g(a)) prevents collapse to gene identity. Training set: 23,837 valid triplets. Loss:

> **L_triplet = max( cosine_d(T_ψ(δ_layer(a)), T_ψ(δ_layer(p))) − cosine_d(T_ψ(δ_layer(a)), T_ψ(δ_layer(n))) + 0.3 , 0 )**

Optimiser: Adam (lr=1×10^{−3}). Epochs: 50 (early stopping on validation triplet accuracy). Stage 1 weights are frozen before Stage 2 training.

**Stage 2 MLP.** Given frozen T_ψ and frozen φ_L30, the combined representation [T_ψ(δ_layer(i)); φ_L30(i)] ∈ ℝ^{704} is passed to a two-layer MLP:

> Dense(704→256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(256→128, ReLU) → Dropout(0.2) → Dense(128→82, sigmoid)

Loss: binary focal cross-entropy (γ=2.0; class-balanced α as above). Optimiser: Adam (lr=1×10^{−3}, cosine decay). Ensemble: 5 random seeds, mean-pooled probabilities. Training labels: 82 Molecular Function GO terms (NCBI gene2go; IEA excluded; ≥100 training positives per term). Bootstrap CI: gene-level block bootstrap (B=1,000 resamples; all isoforms of each sampled gene included; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI).

**Ablation controls.** Appending RNA-level or localisation delta features (v18a; 17-dim handcrafted) yielded ΔAll-MF AUPRC = −0.009, confirming that δ_layer captures the maximally informative layer-contrast signal without benefit from explicit sequence-derived approximations. Appending 16-dimensional brain cell-type expression vectors (v18b; expr_frac + isoform_usage_frac per 8 cell types) yielded ΔAll-MF AUPRC = −0.110, reflecting a domain mismatch between muscle-trained MLP and brain-specific cell-context features under zero-shot cross-tissue transfer; cell-context features are suppressed during muscle training and interfere at brain evaluation time.

**Gain decomposition (v17e_layer ablation).** To isolate the raw contribution of δ_layer without triplet-trained metric organisation, T_ψ Stage 1 was bypassed and the 1280-dimensional concatenation [δ_layer(640); φ_L30(640)] was fed directly to a five-seed focal-loss MLP with identical Stage 2 hyperparameters (v17e_layer). The v17e_layer AUPRC versus PRISM and v17f quantifies the three-component gain: PRISM → v17e_layer (raw δ_layer) → v17f (+ T_ψ organisation). Random-δ control: δ_layer replaced by an isotropic random vector matched per-isoform to the same L₂ norm; T_ψ + Stage 2 trained identically. Random-δ AUPRC isolates T_ψ architecture capacity independent of δ_layer directional content.

**Mechanistic probing (δ_layer encoding analysis).** Four probing analyses were applied to 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms. (i) Domain predictability: logistic regression (scikit-learn) trained to predict binary Pfam domain presence from δ_layer versus φ_L30 versus φ_L15, evaluated by 5-fold cross-validation AUROC across 100 Pfam domains with 30–29,398 positive examples. (ii) Within-gene splice-change correlation: across all 76,564 same-gene isoform pairs, Spearman ρ between |Δδ_layer| (L₂ norm of inter-isoform difference in δ_layer) and Δdomain count (absolute change in number of Pfam domains between isoform pair). (iii) Per-dimension L2_Structural correlation: Pearson r between each of the 640 δ_layer dimensions and binary L2_Structural GO membership (mean over 33 L2_Structural MF terms with ≥2 positives); top-20 dimensions by |r| identified; mean |r| in δ_layer versus same 20 dimensions in φ_L30 computed. (iv) Norm versus domain count: Spearman ρ between isoform δ_layer L₂ norm and number of annotated Pfam domains (0–many); mean norm computed for no-domain, ≥1-domain, and ≥3-domain groups.

**ESM-2 scale generalization.** To assess depth dependence of the δ_layer benefit, the complete v17f T_ψ + Stage 2 pipeline was re-run with (i) ESM-2 8M (esm2_t6_8M_UR50D; 6 layers; embedding dim 320; δ = L6 − L3) and (ii) ESM-2 35M (esm2_t12_35M_UR50D; 12 layers; 480-dim; δ = L12 − L6). Per-scale PRISM baseline (frozen final-layer only, same train/test split and MLP) was also computed for comparison. All hyperparameters identical across scales; T_ψ input dimension adjusted to match each embedding dimension. Brain isoform sequences (37k) were re-embedded with each smaller model; muscle train sequences (28k with known protein sequences) similarly processed.

**ESM-2 fine-tuning comparison (Exp D).** To evaluate whether ESM-2 fine-tuning can substitute for the frozen δ_layer approach, three conditions were evaluated on 82 MF GO terms against the held-out *muscle* test fold (36,748 isoforms; an earlier draft mislabelled this fold as "brain"; same protocol as v17f). True-brain zero-shot values for D0/D1/D2 are reported in Supplementary Table S2b. D0: five-seed PRISM MLP (Dense 256→128→82, focal γ=2, 60 epochs) on frozen ESM-2 L30 pre-computed embeddings using all **31,668 training isoforms** (same training set as v17f; D0 requires only pre-computed embeddings and is not restricted to protein-sequence-available isoforms); 1,000-bootstrap CI reported. D1: same MLP training on the **28,040-isoform subset** with available protein sequences (TransDecoder predictions), after ESM-2 partial fine-tuning of the last 5 layers (24.62M trainable parameters); fine-tuning hyperparameters: 5 epochs, batch 4, gradient accumulation 8, LR_ESM = 5×10⁻⁶ (cosine decay), AdamW; L30 embeddings re-extracted after fine-tuning. D1 uses fewer training isoforms than D0 due to protein sequence availability; any training-set advantage therefore biases conservatively in favour of D0, but the observed All MF difference of 0.000 (D1: 0.660 vs D0: 0.660; 95% CIs fully overlapping) and L2_Structural difference of 0.001 (D1: 0.520 vs D0: 0.521; 95% CIs fully overlapping) are both below bootstrap noise regardless of this factor. D2: full 30-layer fine-tuning (147.71M parameters) was attempted but exceeded available GPU memory (24.5 GiB) and was not evaluable. All conditions evaluated by the same AUPRC protocol on 81 valid MF terms.

**SOTA retrieval baselines (sequence-similarity GO transfer).** To contextualise PRISM and v17f within sequence-similarity approaches, two retrieval baselines were evaluated on the 36,748-isoform held-out *muscle* test fold (81 valid MF terms; an earlier draft mislabelled this fold as "brain"; same evaluation protocol as all other methods). (i) Gene-mean ESM-2 retrieval (E0b): gene-level centroids were computed by averaging ESM-2 L30 embeddings of all training isoforms per gene; each test isoform was assigned the gene2go MF GO label profile of its k=5 most similar centroids by cosine similarity; GO probability was the mean binary label over top-k centroid genes. (ii) Isoform k-NN ESM-2 retrieval (E0): same procedure, using individual training isoforms rather than centroids as reference points (k=5, cosine similarity in L30 embedding space, weighted-average GO label transfer). Both baselines use the same NCBI gene2go MF annotations as training labels and evaluation ground truth. The identical retrieval procedure was independently rerun against the true-brain zero-shot test set (63,994 IsoQuant isoforms, 82 valid MF terms) for the corrected comparison in Supplementary Table S2b.

**DeepFRI comparison (Exp E).** DeepFRI (CNN-LM, sequence-only mode) was installed from the public GitHub repository (Gligorijević et al., Nat Methods 2021) and applied to all 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms. Protein sequences were extracted from the same source as ESM-2 embeddings (top30k_isoforms.pep; 100% coverage). DeepFRI was run with `predict.py --fasta_fn brain_test.fasta -ont mf` using the pre-trained `DeepCNN-MERGED_molecular_function.hdf5` model. The CNN-LM model outputs predictions for 489 MF GO terms; of these, 65/81 valid test terms (80.2%) overlap with our evaluation panel, and 16 terms absent from the DeepFRI vocabulary were assigned zero scores. AUPRC was computed on all 81 valid terms using the same gene2go ground truth; a coverage-adjusted AUPRC (65 covered terms only) was reported separately. Predictions were run CPU-only due to cuDNN version incompatibility (system: cuDNN 8, required: cuDNN 7) and completed in 30 minutes.

**DeepGoPlus comparison.** DeepGoPlus (Kulmanov and Hoehndorf, Bioinformatics 2020) [15] was installed from the public GitHub repository (v1.0.28) and configured with the pre-trained UniProt/Swiss-Prot model files (model.h5, terms.pkl, train_data.pkl, train_data.dmnd; retrieved from the DeepGoPlus data server, UniProt release 2026_02). Protein sequences (top30k_isoforms.pep) were submitted to `deepgoplus --threshold 0.0 --batch-size 64`. DeepGoPlus first runs DIAMOND blastp against train_data.dmnd (72,885 Swiss-Prot sequences) to retrieve homology-based GO scores, then runs the 1D-CNN encoder for all input sequences; final predictions combine both components as score = α × CNN_score + (1−α) × DIAMOND_score (α_MF = 0.66 for the MF ontology). DIAMOND hits were obtained for 27,347/36,748 isoforms (74.4%). TransDecoder protein ID suffixes (.p1, .p2, ...) were stripped from DIAMOND query IDs before mapping to isoform identifiers in the evaluation dataset. Bootstrap CI computed at B=500 by gene-level resampling, identical to all other methods.

**Sequence homology benchmarks (BLAST→GOA and isopretEM).** BLAST→GOA: DIAMOND blastp v2.1 [16] was run against the UniProt/Swiss-Prot database (downloaded 2026-04; reviewed entries only; 571,609 sequences) with parameters `--blastp --more-sensitive -e 0.001 --max-target-seqs 50`. Query sequences: TransDecoder-predicted protein sequences for all 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms (top30k_isoforms.pep; 36,776 total protein sequence entries including multi-isoform TransDecoder predictions; TransDecoder ID suffixes .p1/.p2/... stripped before downstream mapping to the 36,748 unique isoform IDs). For each (isoform, GO MF term) pair, the GO annotation score was defined as the maximum bitscore among all DIAMOND hits annotated with that GO term in the GOA annotation file (EBI GOA Human, 2026-04 release; IEA excluded). Isoforms with no DIAMOND hit at E<0.001 were assigned score 0 for all GO terms (1,403/36,748 isoforms; 3.8%). Of the 35,345 DIAMOND-covered isoforms, 25,159 (68.5% of all test isoforms; 71.2% of covered isoforms) had ≥1 MF GO annotation transferred from the top Swiss-Prot hit; the remainder hit proteins with no MF GOA entries and were scored 0. isopretEM: Following the principle of Ravanmehr et al. [17], within-gene EM redistribution was applied to BLAST→GOA scores using 10 EM iterations: each isoform's score for GO term k was updated proportional to its bitscore weight within its gene's total bitscore; no gene-level label input was provided. Both BLAST→GOA and isopretEM scores were evaluated identically to all other methods (B=500 gene-level bootstrap CI).

**Within-gene isoform discrimination metrics.** Three within-gene metrics were computed for all multi-isoform genes (≥2 test isoforms; 7,459 genes, 32,611 isoforms) using each method's raw probability outputs before any thresholding. (i) *Intra-gene CV*: for each (gene g, GO term k) pair where y_{g,k}=1 (gene-positive), the coefficient of variation (CV = σ/μ) of predicted scores across all isoforms of g was computed; means were averaged over gene-positive pairs. For BLAST→GOA, bitscore values were max-normalised per gene before CV computation (raw bitscores are not comparable in scale to probability outputs). (ii) *Within-gene length-AUC*: for each multi-isoform gene with sufficient length variation (defined as longest isoform ≥ 2× shortest), per-gene AUC was computed for the binary classification task of predicting the longer-than-median-length isoform from predicted score alone; means were averaged over qualifying genes. (iii) *Top-isoform agreement*: for each (gene g, GO term k) pair with ≥2 isoforms covered by BLAST→GOA, the isoform argmax under each method was compared to the BLAST→GOA argmax; fraction of matching argmaxes over N=14,657 testable pairs reported. All three metrics were computed for PRISM v17f*, k-NN ESM-2 (k=5), gene-mean ESM-2 (k=5), BLAST→GOA, and random baseline (uniform random scores, 10-replicate mean).

**UniProt curated isoform-specific benchmark (v2).** To evaluate PRISM's capacity to predict experimentally validated isoform-specific functional differences, we constructed a benchmark of 51 isoform pairs from 50 UniProt Swiss-Prot reviewed entries (human; experimental evidence ECO:0000269); 48 pairs were evaluable after excluding three accessions absent from the UniProt REST API (P15692-7/VEGFA-121, Q15303-8/ERBB4-iso8, Q15365-2/PCBP1-iso2). ESM-2 t30_150M layer-contrast embeddings (δ_layer = φ_{L30} − φ_{L15}; concatenated with φ_{L30}; dim=1280) were computed identically to the main PRISM pipeline. The v17f* MLP head was trained on 31,668 muscle isoforms (5 seeds; identical hyperparameters) and applied to all 48 evaluable isoform pairs. For each pair (iso_a, iso_b, GO term k, direction ∈ {A\_only, B\_only, both}), direction accuracy was evaluated as: correct if direction = A\_only and score_a > score_b; correct if direction = B\_only and score_b > score_a; correct if direction = both (both isoforms expected positive for the GO term). The gene-mean baseline is 0.500 (random direction). The stratification threshold gap ≥ 0.10 was specified a priori as the biologically interpretable boundary corresponding to complete structural domain loss or major truncation (kinase domain entirely absent, truncation to <50 residues, or loss of primary interaction domain); structural category annotations for all 51 pairs were completed before PRISM score computation, and all eleven gap ≥ 0.10 cases fall within these pre-specified structural severity categories. When pairs were stratified by prediction confidence (|score_a − score_b|), PRISM achieves direction accuracy 11/11 (100%) for gap ≥ 0.10 (permutation p < 0.001, n=10,000 permutations; binomial p < 0.001), and 26/48 (54.2%) overall. Statistical significance was assessed by one-sided permutation test (H₀: accuracy = 0.5; 10,000 random label permutations per threshold) and one-sided binomial test. GO terms were selected from the 82 MF terms in the PRISM training vocabulary; pairs with GO term absent from the training vocabulary or isoform sequence unavailable from UniProt REST API were excluded before evaluation. 42 of the 50 benchmark genes (84%; by Ensembl gene ID match, including EGFR, PARK2, VEGFR2, FGFR1, BRCA1, FLT1, NTRK2, NTRK3, MDM2) overlap with the 12,709-gene muscle training set, which trains on gene-level GO labels from muscle isoforms; 8 genes (BIRC5, CDKN2A, FGFR2, NTRK1, SMN1, SNAP25, TP73, WT1) do not. This does not confound the within-gene direction test: both isoforms in each pair share the same gene identity and thus the same gene-level training label — any gene-label memorisation would produce identical scores for both isoforms (gap = 0, direction random), not the observed asymmetric gaps. We verified this directly by stratifying direction accuracy by training-set overlap (`reports/exp_h_uniprot_eval/v2/training_overlap_stratification.json`): at the pre-specified gap ≥ 0.10 threshold, both the training-overlapping subset (9/9 = 100%: EGFR, FGFR1, VEGFR2, BRCA1, PARK2, FLT1, NTRK2, NTRK3, MDM2) and the non-overlapping subset (2/2 = 100%: CDKN2A, FGFR2) achieve perfect direction accuracy, directly ruling out gene-level memorisation as the source of the high-confidence result — though the non-overlapping subset is small (n=2) and this should be read as consistent with, not independent statistical confirmation of, the memorisation-null. Across all 48 evaluable pairs at any gap, training-overlapping genes show 23/39 (59.0%) accuracy versus 3/9 (33.3%) for non-overlapping genes; this difference is consistent with sampling noise at n=9 rather than a memorisation signal, since the pre-specified high-confidence subset shows no such asymmetry. The benchmark therefore specifically tests the isoform-intrinsic sequence features captured by δ_layer, orthogonal to gene-level label recall. The benchmark is available in `reports/exp_g_uniprot/uniprot_isoform_benchmark_v2.csv` (51 pairs); evaluation implementation in `hMuscle/model/exp_uniprot_v2.py`.

**Per-locus domain ranking AUC.** To provide a gene-mean-independent validation of within-gene isoform discrimination capacity, we computed per-locus domain ranking AUC for PRISM v17f*, BLAST→GOA, k-NN ESM-2, gene-mean oracle, and random baseline. Ground-truth domain completeness was derived from a binary Pfam domain presence matrix computed with hmmscan (Pfam-A r36.0; E < 0.01) across all 36,748 test isoforms (512 Pfam families; stored as `domain_matrix_proper_test.npy`). The per-isoform domain count *d_i* was defined as the number of distinct Pfam domain families detected in isoform *i* (row sum of the binary matrix; 37.2% of test isoforms with *d_i* ≥ 1). For each multi-isoform gene *g* where isoforms differ in domain count (std(*d_i* : *i* ∈ *g*) ≥ 0.1), and for each GO MF term *k* where gene *g* is annotated positive (*y_{g,k}* = 1), a binary domain-completeness label was constructed: *domain_pos_i* = 1 if *d_i* > median(*d_j* : *j* ∈ *g*), else 0. For each (gene, GO-term) pair with at least one positive and one negative domain label, AUC was computed between *domain_pos_i* and the method's predicted score *ŷ_{i,k}*. Tied predicted scores (std < 10⁻⁸) were assigned AUC = 0.5 (random). This yielded N = 3,542 (gene, GO-term) evaluation pairs across all qualifying genes. The gene-mean oracle — which assigns each isoform the within-gene mean prediction *ŷ_{i,k}* = (1/|g|) Σ_{j∈g} ŷ_{j,k} — necessarily achieves AUC = 0.500 by construction for all pairs (tied scores within every gene), providing a mathematical null that verifies the gene-identity-independence of the evaluation. Bootstrap CI (B = 500 resamples) was computed by gene-level block resampling: at each resample, qualifying genes were drawn with replacement, and the mean AUC over the resulting (gene, GO-term) pairs was computed; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI. Implementation: `hMuscle/model/domain_ranking_validation.py`; results in `reports/domain_ranking_validation/results.json`.

### ESM-2 representation dissection (per-layer Fisher, trajectory PCA, within-gene divergence, axis biology)

The following analyses underlie the ESM-2 representation dissection reported in Results §4c (synthesis) and Supplementary Note S_dissection (full detail). **(1) Per-layer Fisher probe.** For each GO term, a logistic-regression probe was fit on the raw 640-dimensional mean-pooled embedding of each of the 30 ESM-2 layers separately; the Fisher discriminative peak is the layer maximising held-out probe AUPRC. Peak-layer distribution was summarised across 277 GO terms (median, IQR, fraction peaking at L30). **(2) Trajectory joint-PCA axes.** Per-layer embeddings were z-scored within each layer and stacked into a per-isoform trajectory (30 layers × 640 dims); a single joint PCA fit on the muscle training isoforms yielded an 8-component basis W (8 × 640) applied identically across all 30 layers, giving each isoform a trajectory Z of shape (30, 8). Axis scalars used for interpretation are the mean over the 30 layers of each component. **(3) Within-gene layer-divergence.** For the muscle test set (36,748 isoforms; the Domain-Ranking set), all within-gene isoform pairs were enumerated (capped at 60 randomly-sampled pairs per gene). Pairs were classed domain-different (binary Pfam vectors, 512 families, Hamming > 0) or domain-same (Hamming = 0, both isoforms carrying ≥1 domain); exact-duplicate pairs (<10 differing residues in the aligned sequence encoding) were removed. At each layer, the ratio mean(cosine distance | domain-different)/mean(… | domain-same) was computed; the null is 1,000 shuffles of the domain-different/same labels; length control used |length difference|-matched strata and partial Spearman of divergence on domain Hamming controlling for length difference. **(4) Axis biology.** Interpreting the 8 joint-PCA axes requires ruling out, at each step, that an apparent biological correlate is actually a gene-family confound (the representation-level analogue of the gene-identity dominance quantified for macro AUPRC in §Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC). Three sequential checks address this. *First*, each axis was screened by a null-anchored between/within-gene variance decomposition before any biological interpretation was attempted: one-way ANOVA partitions each axis's total variance into between-gene and within-gene components (within_frac = SS_within/SS_total), compared against the label-shuffle null expectation within_frac_null = (N−G)/(N−1) (N = isoforms, G = genes; verified against 20 explicit label-shuffle permutations). All 8 axes showed within_frac at 24–39% of this null expectation, confirming that gene-family identity dominates the trajectory representation at every axis — but non-uniformly: axes 3 and 6 retained the highest relative within-gene variance fraction (ratio 0.377–0.385, versus 0.242–0.281 for axes 4 and 0), identifying them as the axes most likely to carry isoform-level (rather than purely gene-family-level) structure, and axis 0's simultaneous status as the highest-explained-variance and most gene-family-dominated axis is expected under PCA applied to hierarchically clustered data: because between-gene sequence divergence is the dominant source of total variance in the pooled representation, PCA's top variance-maximising direction is biased toward the between-gene contrast, pushing within-gene (isoform-level) structure toward lower-variance components. *Second*, each axis scalar was correlated (Spearman) with 22 sequence-intrinsic features (secondary-structure fractions, GRAVY, aromaticity, instability, pI, charge fractions, residue-class fractions, compositional entropy, transmembrane-segment count, N-terminal hydrophobicity, domain count) at both gene level (mean axis score versus mean feature value per gene) and within-gene-residual level (each isoform's deviation from its gene's mean axis score versus its deviation from its gene's mean feature value). The residual-level correlation is the discriminating test: a feature correlating with an axis at gene level but not at the residual level reflects a gene-family covariate rather than genuine within-gene structural encoding. This criterion is what excludes motif features (SLiM regular-expression counts) as axis correlates: all SLiM features showed gene-level correlation with at least one axis but null correlation at the within-gene-residual level, indicating that any apparent motif–axis association reflects gene-family composition rather than isoform-intrinsic motif content. *Third*, for axes with weak continuous-feature correlation after step two (axis 1, axis 6; |ρ| ≈ 0.29–0.30), Pfam domain-family membership was additionally tested via top-vs-bottom score-tercile log-odds enrichment rather than continuous correlation, because domain-family presence is a sparse binary indicator for which correlation coefficients are diluted both by the uninformative middle tercile and by low base-rate variance; log-odds enrichment concentrates statistical power on the axis extremes and yields a ranked, interpretable list of the domain families defining each pole (families with ≥30 isoforms). **(5) Within-family conditioning control.** To test whether the within-gene axis structure is an artefact of implicit gene-level background subtraction, the joint-PCA axes were re-derived under explicit gene-family conditioning. Each isoform's per-layer z-scored embedding had its gene-family mean subtracted (family-centred residual), and a single pooled 8-component PCA was fit over the residuals — the within-family scatter analogue of the within-class scatter matrix in linear discriminant analysis. Gene-family was defined two ways: (i) the dominant Pfam accession per gene (isoforms of genes lacking any Pfam domain form singleton families), and (ii) all-versus-all sequence-homology clusters of each gene's longest protein (DIAMOND cluster, --approx-id 30 --member-cover 50). The family-centred basis was compared with the uncentred joint basis, the original muscle-fit basis, and across the two family definitions by principal angles (singular values of WᴬᵀWᴮ between the two 8×640 orthonormal bases). Family-specificity of the within-gene variance elevation was assessed against a family-size-preserving label-shuffle null (10 permutations). The length-independent domain axis (axis 3) was cross-validated against BISECT domain-switch cases: for 59 curated domain-change cases mapped to the brain set, sign concordance between the within-gene axis-3 difference and the net Pfam domain gain/loss was tested (binomial); because both isoforms of each case share a gene and therefore a gene-family, the family-centring leaves their axis difference unchanged, isolating the within-gene component. **(6) Causal occlusion.** Each of the eight joint-PCA axes was projected out of the test-set embeddings at both input layers (for layer L, z-score to per-layer statistics, subtract the rank-one projection onto the axis, invert the z-score) and PRISM v17f* re-predicted on the modified inputs; the change in within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC and gene-level macro-AUPRC was measured against a null of 20 random unit directions occluded identically (z-scores relative to this null). A retrain-with-occlusion variant removed the axis from both training and test embeddings and retrained the 5-seed ensemble, distinguishing predictive use (test-time occlusion) from task necessity/redundancy (retrain). To adjudicate whether axis 0's negative within-gene occlusion z reflects a learned within-gene down-weighting or passive weight leakage, each axis's within-gene residual (isoform score minus its gene mean) was correlated (Spearman) with the within-gene residual of Pfam domain count over domain-varying multi-isoform genes on the Domain-Ranking set, and the within-gene residual amplitude (standard deviation) of axis 0 was compared to that of axis 3; a leakage account predicts non-zero axis-0 within-gene amplitude (variation present to leak) combined with near-zero domain alignment (the leaked variation is label-misaligned), given that v17f* contains no gene-conditional gating and therefore cannot apply an axis-specific weight only within genes. **(7) Categorical domain decodability.** For within-gene isoform pairs, a length-difference-decile-matched logistic classifier was trained on the eight per-axis absolute differences to predict the binary presence/absence of a Pfam domain-architecture change (Hamming > 0), reporting mean held-out AUROC across deciles; this tests categorical domain-change detection separately from the graded domain-count correlation. An identically-matched classifier reading the full 640-dimensional z-scored layer-mean representation (the basis on which the eight axes are defined) provides the ESM-2 ceiling for the same task, quantifying the domain information lost to the eight-axis compression; the linear ceiling was additionally compared against a non-linear gradient-boosted classifier (HistGradientBoosting, 200 trees, depth 3) on both the eight-axis and 640-dimensional representations to separate representational limit from decoder linearity. **(8) Per-isoform trajectory divergence.** For each BISECT domain-switch case, the per-layer absolute difference in axis-3 score between the two isoforms was computed and its peak layer recorded; the distribution of peak layers was compared to that of random within-gene isoform pairs (Mann–Whitney). Per-isoform axis-3 and axis-5 trajectories over the 30 layers were plotted for NDUFS2/7/4 and NDUFAF5. Figures: axis × feature heatmap and per-axis relationship panels (Supplementary Fig. S_axes); per-isoform trajectory panels (Supplementary Fig. S_traj). **(9) Capacity/supervision bracket of the Domain-Ranking ceiling.** To localise whether the within-gene Domain-Ranking ceiling is model- or representation-bound, two families of intervention were run on the v17f* architecture with the identical [δ_layer, φ_L30] input, focal objective and five-seed protocol. *Capacity:* a head-width sweep holding hidden-layer count fixed (two-layer heads 256→128, 512→256, 768→384; the reported +0.013 gain is the depth-controlled 512→256-versus-256→128 contrast, isolating width from depth) with each head evaluated on gene-level macro AUPRC (paired term-level bootstrap, n=1,000) and on within-gene Domain-Ranking with a gene-clustered bootstrap that resamples whole genes (rather than the correlated gene–term ranking units) against a pre-specified non-inferiority margin of −0.01. *Supervision:* a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss added to the focal objective — for every training gene with domain-varying isoforms, all ordered isoform pairs (higher versus lower Pfam domain count) contribute max(0, m − (s_i − s_j)) over that gene's positive GO terms (margin m = 0.1), summed over 3,097 within-gene pairs from 508 genes and swept over loss weight λ ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1.0}; both macro AUPRC and Domain-Ranking were compared to the λ = 0 baseline. Train and test genes are disjoint, so a Domain-Ranking change would reflect transfer of the ordering principle across genes, not label leakage. These experiments accompany the conditional domain-count regression auxiliary loss described in §6b.

### pos_bias analysis

**pos_bias** for GO term k is defined as:

> **pos_bias(k) = mean_{g ∈ G_k^+} σ_g(k) / σ_global(k)**

where G_k^+ = {g ∈ G : y_{g,k} = 1} is the set of positive-class genes for GO term k with ≥2 test isoforms, σ_g(k) = std_{i: g(i)=g} [f_θ(φ(i))_k] is the within-gene prediction standard deviation, and σ_global(k) = std over all test isoforms of f_θ(φ(i))_k. pos_bias > 1 implies that positive-class genes show higher within-gene score variation than the global baseline; pos_bias = 0 by construction for gene-mean oracle (all isoforms identical scores).

The label-shuffled null floor is estimated by gene-level label permutation: gene assignments to G_k^+ are shuffled (random subset of same size drawn without replacement) and pos_bias recomputed; the bootstrap mean across B=1,000 resamples defines the null floor. The observed pos_bias significantly exceeds the null when the fraction of permuted bootstrap samples ≥ obs_pos_bias is less than α. For v15d muscle (18 BP terms): null floor ≈ 0.24; for v17f* true brain MF/BP/CC: null floors ≈ 0.217–0.265.

Bootstrap CI and p-values: B=1,000 gene-level block bootstraps (resample genes with replacement, all isoforms of each resampled gene included). Significance: bootstrap p-value (fraction of gene-level shuffled bootstrap samples ≥ observed pos_bias), Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at 5%. Analysis applied to: (i) v15d muscle, K=13 GO BP terms; (ii) v17f* true brain zero-shot, K=278 GO terms (82 MF + 103 BP + 93 CC). All computations in Python 3.10 (NumPy 1.24); scripts available at [Code availability].

### Canonical-anchored severity regression

To test whether the two tissues differ not only in the *rate* at which alternative-splicing events remodel a Pfam domain (main text, §6) but also in the *magnitude* of representational consequence per edit, we constructed a canonical-anchored within-gene pair set spanning all multi-isoform genes (not restricted to the two-isoform subset used for the domain-envelope rate analysis). For each gene, the canonical isoform was identified by a three-tier priority — MANE Select, then Ensembl_canonical, then APPRIS principal isoform 1, then (if none of the above is present in the expressed isoform set) the isoform with the longest CDS in the dataset — and paired against every other isoform of that gene as an independent edit event. For muscle, the existing GENCODE-v44-derived canonical reference table (gene_id/transcript_id keyed) was used directly. For brain, an equivalent reference was built for this analysis: brain isoform identifiers follow GENCODE's transcript_name convention (e.g., "AADAT-201"), so the same GENCODE v44 GTF was re-parsed keyed on gene_name/transcript_name to assign MANE/Ensembl_canonical/APPRIS tags, with novel (non-reference-matched) transcripts falling back to the longest-CDS tier. This yielded 15,493 muscle pairs (from 8,569 multi-isoform genes; 87.2% MANE/Ensembl/APPRIS-tier canonical) and 33,148 brain pairs (from 12,157 genes; 86.6% MANE/Ensembl/APPRIS-tier canonical).

For each pair, the outcome (severity score) is the out-of-fold projection of the pair's embedding difference onto a gene-disjoint-fold-trained coherence direction: per-isoform mean-pooled ESM-2 representations at layers 15 and 30 are concatenated (1,280-dim), the pair's difference vector is formed (longer isoform minus shorter, independent of which is canonical), and each pair is scored by its dot product with the L2-normalised mean difference vector of the training folds (5-fold, genes never split across folds), so that no pair contributes to its own scoring direction. This mean-pooling-based construction was chosen over a max-pooling alternative after a length-artefact control: because the longer isoform is always the numerator of the pair by construction, max-pooling's per-dimension maximum is a monotonically increasing function of sequence length under extreme-value order statistics regardless of sequence content, and a content-scrambled, length-preserved null (residues resampled with replacement from a tissue-wide pool while holding each pair's true isoform lengths fixed) reproduced 63–92% of max-pooling's apparent direction-accuracy gain in 6 of 7 tested categories, with the max-pooling discriminant axis itself geometrically aligned with the pure length-artefact axis (cosine similarity 0.92–0.98). Mean-pooling's own length-scrambled null was at chance in every category (cosine with the length-artefact axis ≈ 0), consistent with the mathematical expectation that the sample mean's expectation, unlike the sample maximum, is invariant to sample size.

Five structural covariates were computed per pair from protein sequence alignment (SequenceMatcher opcode analysis) and existing domain/exon annotations: (i) size, the total changed-residue count (log(1+count)-transformed then z-scored; the log transform linearises the heavy right-tail of edit sizes, under which the raw z-scored count enters non-significantly); (ii) domain-architecture change, a binary indicator (muscle: change in total Pfam domain count via the existing domain matrix; brain: hmmscan envelope overlap between the changed interval and a Pfam domain, E ≤ 0.01, matching the domain-envelope method above); (iii) N-terminal overlap, a binary indicator for whether the first changed residue position is within the first 60 residues; (iv) disorder fraction, the fraction of the longer isoform's changed-interval residues with a positive (disorder-promoting) TOP-IDP score; (v) alignment-resync failure, a binary indicator computed as follows: from the first changed position in the shorter isoform, the longest single contiguous matching ("equal") alignment block is identified as a fraction of the remaining sequence length; this fraction falling below 0.5 is scored as resync failure. This indicator is a protein-alignment-level heuristic for edits after which the two sequences fail to re-align into one coherent downstream block (consistent with, but not proof of, a nucleotide-level frameshift, since no genomic coordinate or reading-frame information is used); we therefore report it descriptively as an alignment-persistence proxy rather than as validated frameshift ground truth.

**Compositional direction test (non-domain residual).** Separately from the five-covariate magnitude regression above, four amino-acid-propensity descriptors of the changed residues were tested for within-gene direction consistency on the non-domain internal-edit subset (domain-architecture-change = 0 and N-terminal overlap = 0; 4,185 muscle, 11,666 brain pairs, after requiring both isoforms present with a non-empty changed interval): mean Chou-Fasman helix propensity (Pα) and sheet propensity (Pβ), mean Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity, and net physiological-pH charge per changed residue, each averaged over the longer isoform's changed positions. For each descriptor a median-split orientation (+1 if the descriptor exceeds the subset median, −1 otherwise) was applied to the L15+L30 mean-pooled embedding difference, and gene-disjoint 5-fold cross-validated direction accuracy (CV-dir-acc — the fraction of held-out pairs whose oriented difference projects positively onto the training-fold mean direction) was compared against two nulls: (a) a gene-permutation null (gene identities shuffled before fold assignment; 10 permutations), testing whether the direction reflects a cross-gene-shared orientation rather than a gene-independent population bias; and (b) — where the gene-permutation null was uninformative because the direction was fold-universal at large sample size (brain sheet propensity: real 0.653, permuted 0.653) — a label-permutation null (random sign-flip of each pair's difference vector; 10 permutations), testing whether the direction reflects genuine long/short structure (brain sheet propensity real 0.653 vs. label-permuted 0.500). This test is direction-level (does the residual point a consistent way?), not magnitude-level, and is reported separately from the five-covariate severity regression for that reason. Whether the current model uses these compositional directions for prediction was additionally tested by applying the identical median-split orientation instrument to PRISM's predicted GO-score difference in place of the embedding difference (`b4_compositional_usage.py`): the consistency attenuates from 0.59–0.65 at the embedding to 0.53–0.58 at the output, indistinguishable from the gene-permutation null in both tissues, indicating the mean-pooled model does not propagate the compositional axis to its functional prediction. Scripts: `test_covariate_gene_permutation_null.py`, `test_sheet_delta_label_permutation.py`, `analyze_sheet_delta_artifact.py`, `b4_compositional_usage.py` (committed in `hMuscle/model/`).

A committed reconstruction of this pipeline — canonical-anchored pair construction and coherence-projection scoring (`build_severity_pairs.py`, `build_severity_score.py`), the §6-consistent tissue-specific domain-architecture-change covariate (`build_severity_domain_faithful.py`; muscle: total-Pfam-count change, brain: hmmscan-envelope overlap of the changed interval, E ≤ 0.01, exactly the domain-envelope definition used above), and the gene-clustered regression (`severity_regression.py`) — reproduces the qualitative structure reported here: the sign and significance of all five covariates, the brain-greater-than-muscle ordering of the domain-architecture coefficient, the muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync coefficients, and in particular the canonical-orientation-conditional domain effect (significant in the 89.1% majority stratum where the canonical isoform is the longer, and null in the reverse 10.9% stratum). Absolute coefficient magnitudes differ modestly from the reported values, which derive from the original fit, because the gene-disjoint coherence direction is re-estimated in each reconstruction.

Ordinary least squares was fit with cluster-robust (CR1 sandwich) standard errors, clustered on tissue-qualified gene identity (to prevent gene-symbol collisions across the two tissues' independent namespaces), computed via a vectorised per-cluster score-sum formulation (S = per-cluster sum of leverage-weighted residual contributions; meat matrix = SᵀS) for tractability at cluster counts exceeding 30,000. Statistical significance used a permutation null (1,000 permutations of the outcome across pairs, refitting the full model each time) for the model R², and Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the five substantive covariates for individual coefficients. A within-gene fixed-effects specification (each gene's pairs and covariates demeaned by their gene-cluster mean, restricted to genes contributing ≥2 pairs) was fit as a robustness check against gene-level confounding, analogous in purpose to the gene-mean-oracle check used elsewhere in this paper for macro AUPRC.

**Mediation follow-up.** A subsequent analysis traced the apparent muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync-failure coefficients (above) through a three-layer surrogate chain: N-terminal overlap does not survive confound-controlled discrimination testing (size-matched AUC at chance in both tissues); the resync-failure ordering is a downstream trace of a genuine size asymmetry (independently confirmed by gene-cluster bootstrap and tissue-label permutation); and this size asymmetry is itself 89.2%-explained by edit fragmentation (number of separate change intervals per edit), a broad-based effect (>2,000 genes per tissue) with no detectable GO functional enrichment. Full statistics, robustness checks, and reproduction scripts (`resync_mechanical_test.py`, `size_severity_tissue_gap.py`, `dispersion_mechanism_test.py`, `dispersion_rigor_and_concentration.py`, `tissue_compare_and_enrichment.py`) are reported in **Supplementary Note S_severity**.

### DRIMSeq donor-level DTU testing

Pseudobulk transcript count matrices were aggregated per donor per cell type (minimum 10 reads per isoform per donor). DRIMSeq v1.26.0 [8] quasi-Dirichlet-Multinomial regression with stageR v1.26.0 [9] two-stage OFDR control (gene-level overall test ≤ 5%, transcript-level conditional; OFDR corrected across all tests within each cell type). Cell types with fewer than 3 donors per condition per gene excluded. Post-hoc donor consistency filtering: jackknife stability ratio < 0.50 and directional consistency ≥ 0.60. Donor-label permutation testing (n=10,000) used for targeted BISECT-nominated Complex I candidates.

### BISECT pipeline

15 sequential evidence modules applied to each candidate isoform switch pair:

- **M1 (Pfam domain)**: hmmscan (Pfam-A r36.0, E < 0.01). n_lost, n_gained, domain identities.
- **M2/M10 (PPI)**: STRING v12.0 [19] experimental channel (confidence ≥ 900). Pfam domain-to-PPI partner mapping for lost domains.
- **M3/M15 (NMD)**: SQANTI3 structural category + PTC/EJC distance rule (>50 nt upstream of final EJC). M15 adds EJC coordinate-based exact PTC distance.
- **M4/M11 (AlphaFold pLDDT)**: Per-residue pLDDT from AlphaFold DB v4 [11] (UniProt ID mapping). Isoform-level mean and domain-level pLDDT.
- **M5 (phyloConservation)**: UCSC phyloP 100-way per-exon mean. Switches in highly conserved exons (mean phyloP > 3.0) flagged.
- **M9 (localization)**: MTS score from TargetP-2.0 [20] (MTS probability); MTS score difference CT vs AD.
- **M14 (exon comparator)**: Shared exon count, total exons CT/AD, event_type (ALE/cassette/RI/alt-TSS).
- **M16 (mechanism classifier)**: Priority order: NMD switch > ALE (shared==0) > retained intron > major truncation (>200 aa + domain loss) > domain loss/gain > cassette exon cluster > alt TSS > minor exon.
- **M17 (convergence detector)**: Co-occurrence scoring across convergence axes (Complex I, RNA metabolism, DNA repair, KRAB-ZFP, DOCK-family).

Six-tier hierarchy: Tier A-DR (DRIMSeq OFDR ≤ 5%), Tier A-BPC (Bonferroni-corrected permutation p_adj < 0.010, batch-concordant; confirmed), Tier A-BPP (permutation p < 0.05 uncorrected + ≥1 structural module PASS, batch-concordant; preliminary, replication-required), Tier B (independent cohort replication), Tier C (exploratory, no donor-level stats), Tier D (hypothesis-generating, insufficient evidence).

**BISECT–PRISM independence**: BISECT modules M1–M17 operate exclusively on structural and orthogonal evidence sources (Pfam-A hmmscan, AlphaFold pLDDT, TargetP-2.0 MTS scores, STRING PPI networks, UCSC phyloP conservation, SQANTI3 structural categories, DRIMSeq transcript usage). No PRISM predictions are used as input to any BISECT module. PRISM scores are computed independently and compared post-hoc to BISECT-nominated switch pairs to assess convergence between sequence-based functional prediction and structure-based mechanistic analysis; convergence at a locus therefore constitutes independent corroboration rather than circular validation.

### InterProScan comparison

InterProScan v5.62 (Pfam, PRINTS, ProSite, SUPERFAMILY, SMART; pfam2go r2023_06 mapping to GO MF/BP/CC). Run on predicted protein sequences for all 26 BISECT-validated switch pairs. Non-overlap defined as: PRISM predicts BP GO term with score > 0.5, AND pfam2go does not map any domain match in that isoform to the same BP GO term or its direct ancestors.

### Beyond-gene-label GO acquisition (Case A/B) and literature cross-check

Using the true brain zero-shot score matrix (63,994 isoforms, 18 GO BP terms, threshold 0.5), genes were partitioned by their gene2go label vector into fully-negative (0/18 terms positive) and partially-positive (≥1/18 positive) sets. Case A: for fully-negative genes, any isoform with max score > 0.5 on any term. Case B: for partially-positive genes, any isoform for which a non-annotated term's score exceeds both 0.5 and that isoform's own maximum score among its gene's annotated terms. Null 1 (Case A): background crossing rate computed over all gene-level-negative (isoform, term) pairs genome-wide (1,133,987 pairs). Null 2 (Case B): within-isoform column-shuffle — each isoform's 18-term score vector was permuted across term labels (20 trials, fixed seed), preserving each isoform's own score distribution while randomising which term receives which score; z-score computed against the resulting null distribution. Literature cross-check: the 40 highest-scoring Case A instances and all 64 Case B instances (104 total (gene, GO-term) pairs) were queried against the UniProt REST API (`rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb`, reviewed human entries) for GO Biological Process cross-references and evidence codes; a pair was scored literature-supported if any returned BP term carried a direct experimental evidence code (IDA, IMP, IPI, IGI, or IEP) and named the same or a closely-matching biological process to the PRISM-predicted term, based on manual review of the returned term set (computational/homology-inferred codes IEA/ISS/IBA were not counted as direct evidence). Novelty stratification (Type A/B/C) of the 23 literature-supported pairs: each pair's canonical UniProt "Function" description was reviewed against the predicted GO term and classified as Type A if the predicted term matched the gene's near-definitional, textbook-level function (e.g., an ion channel scored positive for the corresponding ion-transport/homeostasis term), Type B if the predicted term reflected a genuinely documented but non-headline aspect of the gene's biology requiring knowledge beyond its one-line functional descriptor, or Type C if the connection relied on a generic mechanistic pathway (e.g., "GPCRs can signal via calcium") without gene-specific documented evidence for the predicted process; classifications were independently verified against UniProt REST API function records (three of 23 draft calls were revised — two Type A→B, one Type B→A — following this verification pass; §Results). Two candidates (ULK3, GAS6) with no matching UniProt/QuickGO annotation were additionally cross-checked by general literature search (§Results) to rule out curation lag; both showed documented biology adjacent to the predicted process (ciliary Hedgehog signalling; TAM-receptor-mediated synapse elimination, respectively) but not the specific predicted GO process itself, and are not counted among the 23 literature-supported pairs. Null 3 (matched-null enrichment test for the 22.1% rate): to test whether the literature-support rate of the PRISM-selected tail exceeds the ambient rate expected from the same gene populations, the 104 pairs were re-screened by an automated, fully-offline version of the same criterion, and compared against matched random pairs. The automated criterion counts a pair (gene, target term) as supported if the gene carries a BP GO annotation with a direct experimental evidence code (IDA/IMP/IPI/IGI/IEP; NCBI gene2go, human) whose term lies within the GO-DAG closure of the target term (target ∪ ancestors ∪ descendants under is_a/part_of; go-basic 2019-01, parsed with networkx). Matched random pairs were drawn preserving population strata and target-term distribution: Case A nulls sampled genes uniformly from the 15,858 fully-negative brain genes with target terms drawn from the observed Case A term frequencies; Case B nulls sampled genes uniformly from the 2,656 partially-annotated brain genes with foreign target terms (not annotated to the gene) drawn from the observed Case B term frequencies; B = 2,000 resamples of 104 pairs each. Enrichment was quantified as the observed automated support rate versus the null mean ± SD, with a bootstrap 95% CI, z-score, lift, and empirical p = (#{null ≥ observed} + 1)/(B + 1). Three closeness definitions were reported: strict is_a/part_of closure, regulates-inclusive closure (approximating the manual "closely-matching" judgment), and a loose floor counting any experimental BP annotation (study-bias control). The automated observed rate (6.7–8.7%) is a conservative lower bound on the manual 22.1% because the GO-DAG closure captures fewer biological-adjacency relations than manual review. Implementation: `hMuscle/model/exp_table1_matched_null.py`; results `reports/novel_go_matched_null/results.json`. Full results: Supplementary Table S_novelGO.

### Statistical analysis

All AUPRC values: macro-averaged (unweighted mean across GO terms). Bootstrap confidence intervals: n=1,000 resamples. For v17d AUPRC CIs, gene-level block bootstrap was used: test genes were resampled with replacement (N=12,706 genes), with all isoforms of each sampled gene included, to account for within-gene correlation in predictions; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI. For pos_bias CIs: isoform-level bootstrap within each gene block. Multiplicity corrections: Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 5% within each test family. Mann-Whitney U: two-sided, no continuity correction. All analyses performed in Python 3.10 (scikit-learn 1.3, scipy 1.11, pandas 2.0) and R 4.3 (DRIMSeq 1.26, stageR 1.26).

### Gene-level versus isoform-level AUPRC decomposition

Because NCBI gene2go GO annotations are assigned at the gene level — all isoforms of a given gene share an identical label vector — macro AUPRC on this test set measures the ability to identify which gene a query isoform belongs to as its primary signal. To quantify this decomposition, we performed a cross-gene shuffle test: v17f* predictions for each isoform were randomly reassigned to a different gene's isoform set (preserving within-gene prediction structure by moving all isoforms of the same gene together to the same destination gene; repeated 1,000 times), then macro AUPRC was recomputed. Cross-gene shuffling reduces v17f* macro AUPRC from 0.734 to 0.048 (mean; random baseline 0.345). The fraction of AUPRC signal above the random baseline attributable to gene identity is (0.734 − 0.048) / (0.734 − 0.345) = **97.7%**. A complementary within-gene shuffle — permuting predictions among isoforms of the *same* gene without crossing gene boundaries — produces no AUPRC drop (0.734 ± 0.001), confirming that macro AUPRC is insensitive to the assignment of scores within a gene but highly sensitive to which gene the isoforms are attributed to.

This 97.7% figure is a **metric property** of the gene-level label structure and gene-grouped evaluation design, not a biological claim that 97.7% of functional variation is gene-level rather than isoform-level. Isoforms of the same gene receive identical labels regardless of their actual functional differences; any metric computed under this label structure will show high gene-level attribution by construction — including a hypothetical perfect isoform-specific predictor trained on the same labels. The 97.7% figure measures the evaluation design's gene-level label structure, not the model's biological scope. Independent within-gene discrimination metrics are immune to this confound because each comparison contrasts two isoforms from the same gene, making gene identity uninformative by construction: Domain-Ranking AUC (whether PRISM ranks domain-complete isoforms above domain-truncated variants within the same gene; AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] held-out muscle, 0.775 true-brain zero-shot; gene-mean mathematical null = 0.500) and UniProt pairwise direction accuracy (whether PRISM correctly orders experimentally characterised isoform pairs from the same gene; 11/11 = 100% at prediction gap ≥ 0.10, permutation p < 0.001) constitute the primary evidence for isoform-specific discrimination capacity. The brain gene-mean oracle (AUPRC 0.795; §Gene-level evaluation paragraph in Discussion) likewise exceeds v17f* brain zero-shot (0.647), confirming that macro AUPRC gene-level dominance holds across tissues and training distributions.

(v) v17f* (0.734) exceeds the best frozen direct-training (D0: 0.660) by +0.074, achieves 6.6× the DeepFRI AUPRC, 24× its L2_Structural AUPRC, and 1.66× the DeepGoPlus AUPRC; (vi) v17f* (no T_ψ: 0.734) exceeds v17f (with T_ψ: 0.717) by +0.017, establishing that T_ψ dimensionality reduction (640→64) is an information bottleneck that reduces performance — the δ_layer contribution derives from its full 640-dim representation, not from triplet-based organisation; (vii) BLAST→GOA and isopretEM both reach 0.861 / 0.802 — higher than all learned methods — but this reflects a gene-identification oracle effect: BLAST coverage of 68.5% (25,159/36,748 isoforms) is gene-level, meaning once any isoform of gene g matches a Swiss-Prot sequence, all isoforms of g inherit its gene-level GO labels; isopretEM within-gene EM does not improve over direct BLAST→GOA because the test labels are gene-level, so bitscore-weighted redistribution within a gene does not add discriminative information. These methods serve as an empirical ceiling for sequence-homology approaches under gene-level labels, not as isoform-discriminative predictors.

## Supplementary Note S1: Mathematical basis of spontaneous isoform discrimination

### S1.1 Problem setup

Let I = {i_1, ..., i_N} be the set of N isoforms with gene-of-origin g: I → G and gene-level GO labels y_g ∈ {0,1}^K. Define the training label Y_{i,k} := y_{g(i),k} — a deterministic assignment that gives each isoform the GO labels of its parent gene. Let φ: I → ℝ^D (D=640) be the frozen ESM-2 embedding. PRISM learns f_θ: ℝ^D → [0,1]^K by minimising:

> L(θ) = Σ_{i∈I} Σ_{k=1}^K FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k, Y_{i,k} )

For interpretive purposes, under standard binary cross-entropy with a well-calibrated model, the converged function satisfies f_θ(φ(i))_k ≈ P(Y_{i,k}=1 | φ(i)) — the posterior probability of GO term k given embedding φ(i) under the empirical training distribution. **Two conditions are necessary for this approximation to hold**: (a) the MLP hypothesis class is sufficiently expressive to represent the true posterior over ℝ^{640}; (b) the loss function is a proper scoring rule attaining its minimum at the true posterior. Focal loss (γ=2) satisfies (a) but not (b) exactly: by reweighting training examples by (1-p_t)^γ, the minimiser of L is the true posterior under a distribution that overweights hard boundary examples relative to confident examples. In practice, this distortion enhances within-gene isoform discrimination (see S1.3 for the gradient analysis) at the cost of strict probability calibration. We use posterior notation as an approximation to guide interpretation, not as a claim of exact calibration.

### S1.2 Why different isoforms of the same gene receive different scores

**Proposition.** Let i_a, i_b ∈ I with g(i_a) = g(i_b) = g and Y_{i_a,k} = Y_{i_b,k} = 1. If φ(i_a) and φ(i_b) lie in different functional neighbourhoods of ℝ^D — regions whose cross-gene label statistics differ — PRISM assigns f_θ(φ(i_a))_k ≠ f_θ(φ(i_b))_k despite identical training labels.

**Mechanism.** f_θ is trained simultaneously on all isoforms in I: isoforms of the same gene are independent training examples, and f_θ receives no gene-identity signal. The parameters θ are therefore shaped by the cross-gene statistical structure of the training data: the final-layer weight w_k ∈ ℝ^{64} points in the direction most consistently associated with Y_{·,k}=1 across all isoforms from all genes.

Now consider canonical isoform i_a whose embedding φ(i_a) lies in a region of ℝ^{640} populated by structurally similar proteins across many genes (e.g., PDZ-domain-containing proteins). If PDZ-domain proteins are systematically positive for GO term k across many training genes — because PDZ domains mediate interactions defining that function — then the cross-gene posterior P(Y_{·,k}=1 | φ ≈ φ(i_a)) is high, and f_θ(φ(i_a))_k is accordingly high.

Conversely, if exon-skipping in isoform i_b removes the PDZ domain, φ(i_b) shifts into a different region of ℝ^{640} populated by domain-lacking sequences from other genes. If these sequences are predominantly negative for GO term k across training genes, then P(Y_{·,k}=1 | φ ≈ φ(i_b)) is low. f_θ(φ(i_b))_k is low, despite Y_{i_b,k} = 1.

**Why does the training penalty at i_b not force f_θ(φ(i_b))_k toward 1?** The focal loss gradient at i_b is non-zero and initially pushes its score upward. However, the same embedding neighbourhood contains many other isoforms j from different genes with Y_{j,k}=0, whose gradients push f_θ downward in the same region. The model converges to the cross-gene optimum — satisfying the population-level label statistics — even at the cost of violating individual gene-level labels for non-functional-domain isoforms. Focal loss (γ=2) accelerates this convergence: it downweights gradients from confident cases (canonical isoforms already correctly scored) by (1-p_t)^γ (see S1.3), allowing the cross-gene negative evidence at non-canonical isoforms to dominate during training.

**Critical distinction: cross-gene vs. within-gene features.** This mechanism depends on a key property of ESM-2 embeddings: exon-skipping events that alter protein structure (domain loss, truncation, hydrophobic surface change) produce consistent embedding shifts, and these shifts align with the structural variation encoded by ESM-2 across its evolutionary training corpus. ESM-2 encodes the absolute sequence-level features of each isoform independently — it does not encode which isoform is "canonical" within a gene, nor what the within-gene functional hierarchy is. When i_b lacks a PDZ domain, φ(i_b) occupies the same embedding region as all other PDZ-lacking proteins regardless of their gene-of-origin. Cross-gene label statistics are thus consistently structured around structural features, enabling gene-level supervision to generate isoform discrimination as a by-product. This also explains why explicitly providing δ_i = φ(i) − μ_g as input fails: δ_i encodes within-gene deviation, which has no consistent label-predictive direction across genes under gene-level supervision (S1.4).

**DLG1 example.** y_{DLG1,synaptic} = 1. In the training set, PDZ-domain-containing proteins from multiple genes are enriched for synaptic transmission positivity (GO:0007268), because PDZ domains mediate glutamate receptor anchoring and postsynaptic density scaffolding. ESM-2 encodes PDZ structural features (conserved β-barrel topology, C-terminal ligand-binding groove) consistently; DLG1-201 (906 aa; PDZ1/2/3 intact) occupies the same embedding region as PDZ-containing proteins from other genes. f_θ assigns P(y_{synaptic}=1 | φ(DLG1-201)) ≈ 0.88. Isoform tr319500 (186 aa; no PDZ domains): ESM-2 encodes this sequence as a short peptide lacking the PDZ structural signature. Domain-lacking short isoforms from many genes are predominantly y=0 for synapse-function GO terms; f_θ assigns P(y_{synaptic}=1 | φ(tr319500)) ≈ 0.033. The 27-fold differential traces to cross-gene embedding statistics, not to any within-gene supervision signal.

### S1.3 Role of focal loss γ=2 in isoform discrimination

For binary focal loss FL(p, y) = −α_y(1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t) with p_t = p if y=1, (1-p) if y=0, the exact gradient with respect to pre-activation z (before sigmoid) for y=1 is derived from ∂FL/∂z = (∂FL/∂p)·p(1-p):

> **|∂FL/∂z| = α (1−p)^γ [ γp |log p| + (1−p) ]**

where the (1-p)^γ factor is the focal reweighting term and [γp|log p| + (1-p)] is the composite bracket including the log-derivative correction. Numerical evaluation for γ=2:

| p_t | (1−p)^γ | bracket | |∂FL/∂z| (α=1) | context |
|-----|---------|---------|--------------|---------|
| 0.95 | 0.0025 | 0.147 | 3.7 × 10^{−4} | Confident canonical isoform |
| 0.50 | 0.2500 | 1.193 | 2.98 × 10^{−1} | Boundary non-canonical isoform |
| 0.10 | 0.8100 | 0.290 | 2.35 × 10^{−1} | Hard positive (non-functional isoform, gene label=1) |

**Boundary/confident ratio: 0.298 / 3.7×10^{−4} ≈ 810-fold.** For reference, standard cross-entropy (γ=0) gives a boundary/confident ratio of 0.50/0.05 = 10-fold; focal loss achieves 81-fold relative amplification over cross-entropy. The simplified (1-p_t)^γ ratio alone (0.25/0.0025 = 100-fold) underestimates the actual amplification by ~8×, because the log-bracket term [γp|log p| + (1-p)] at p=0.5 is 8.1× larger than at p=0.95.

**Two distinct consequences for PRISM.** First, focal loss prevents mode collapse on sparse GO terms (positive prevalence < 5%): without γ>0, sparse classes receive overwhelming gradient from easy negatives, driving all predictions toward zero. This is a class-imbalance phenomenon independent of within-gene discrimination. Second, and separately, focal loss amplifies gradient from boundary examples — non-canonical isoforms whose ESM-2 embedding places them near the functional decision boundary (score 0.3–0.7). These are structurally intermediate isoforms where cross-gene label statistics are most informative for discrimination. By concentrating ~810× more relative gradient weight on these cases than on confidently-scored canonical isoforms, focal loss reinforces the cross-gene consistency constraint precisely for the isoforms where within-gene discrimination matters. These two consequences — class-level anti-collapse and boundary-amplified cross-gene learning — are mechanistically distinct and should not be conflated.

### S1.4 Why explicit within-gene discriminators fail: the label–discriminator conflict

**Setup.** The v15d_splice model concatenated δ_i = φ(i) − μ_{g(i)} (within-gene embedding deviation, where μ_g is the gene-mean embedding) through a learned gate G_δ, adding a within-gene discriminator objective to increase within-gene score variance.

**Observed failure pattern.** G_δ → 1.0 (gate fully saturates, maximally using δ_i) while AUPRC collapses from 0.7022 to 0.4573 (−35%). The within-gene discrimination metric (pos_bias) for the splice-delta model remains at 0.027 — lower than PRISM's 0.094 — despite the explicit within-gene input. This pattern appears contradictory but resolves mechanistically.

**Why G_δ saturates to 1.0 (model successfully uses δ_i).** δ_i contains genuine within-gene structural information: it captures how isoform i deviates from the gene-average sequence geometry. The gate saturates because using δ_i reduces the combined training loss faster per gradient step than the resulting AUPRC harm increases L_{class}. Gate saturation indicates success in learning the δ_i signal, not failure.

**Why AUPRC collapses despite gate learning.** AUPRC is determined by the ranking of positive-class isoforms above negative-class isoforms. Under gene-level supervision, Y_{i_a,k} = Y_{i_b,k} = 1 for all isoforms of a positive gene. Using δ_i to create within-gene score variance means some positive-class isoforms receive lower scores. Every such downranking directly decreases AUPRC, regardless of whether the model correctly identified the canonical isoform as higher-scoring. The gene-level label provides no information about which isoform should receive the lower score — the direction of the discriminator is informationally unconstrained by the supervision signal. The discriminator therefore produces within-gene variance in a direction that may be functionally arbitrary, uniformly degrading AUPRC.

**The fundamental incompatibility.** Gene-level classification and within-gene discrimination are geometrically antagonistic under gene-level labels: the former requires all positive isoforms to rank above negatives (pushing all positive scores high), while the latter requires within-gene score variation (pushing some positive scores down). Adding δ_i as explicit input attempts to resolve this tension, but in the absence of isoform-level labels specifying the correct direction, the model finds the discriminator gradient directionally unconstrained within each gene. The net effect is random disruption of the global sequence-function mapping that PRISM relies on.

**Contrast with PRISM.** PRISM never makes an explicit within-gene comparison. f_θ applies the same cross-gene function to each isoform independently; the model never "sees" that i_a and i_b belong to the same gene. Isoform discrimination is a by-product of cross-gene statistics applied to isoform-specific embeddings φ(i) — not an objective — so there is no label conflict. The key contrast: φ(i) encodes isoform i's absolute sequence features, and cross-gene associations assign different scores to φ(i_a) and φ(i_b) based on where they fall in the learned function space. δ_i encodes relative within-gene deviation, which requires the model to learn a gene-specific directional mapping — impossible under gene-level labels.

**Generalisation.** v16 (4-stream gated residual fusion: ESM-2 + splice_delta + RNA expression features + subcellular localisation scores) shows identical collapse: macro AUPRC 0.4414 (−37% vs v15d), all gate values saturating. RNA expression features and localisation scores encode context-dependent quantities that have no consistent cross-gene label association under gene-level supervision. The failure pattern generalises: any auxiliary input stream encoding within-gene or context-dependent variation is informationally misaligned with gene-level supervision and disrupts the cross-gene sequence-function mapping.

### S1.5 Cross-tissue transfer: geometric argument

ESM-2 is trained on 250M protein sequences without tissue-of-origin annotation; φ(i) therefore encodes sequence-level structural features — amino acid co-variation patterns conserved across organisms, secondary structure propensities, hydrophobic surface geometry, evolutionary constraint. These features are tissue-independent because they are determined by the protein sequence alone, not by cellular context.

The sequence-function mapping f_θ learned from muscle training associates specific regions of ESM-2 embedding space with GO term probabilities:

> f_θ(φ(i))_k > τ_k ⟺ isoform i has embedding geometry characteristic of GO term k

For a GO term k with shared sequence determinants between muscle and brain (microtubule-based movement: kinesin motor domain; mitochondrial organisation: Fe-S cluster binding, ETC subunit topology; Ca²⁺ homeostasis: EF-hand motif), the embedding subspace associated with k is structurally identical whether the sequence originates from muscle or brain tissue. The learned boundary w_k therefore transfers without modification.

**Conditions for transfer failure.** Three distinct causes:
1. **Absent sequence subspace**: brain-specific GPCR subfamilies have receptor pharmacophores not represented in muscle training examples; no trained w_k boundary exists for those structural families, and predictions revert to prior prevalence.
2. **Distributional shift**: brain tissue may predominantly express alternative isoforms of shared genes — isoforms with different domain compositions from the canonical isoforms that dominate muscle training labels. If the brain-predominant isoform has embedding φ on the opposite side of the trained boundary from its gene-level label, the boundary learned from muscle training misclassifies.
3. **Non-sequence functional context**: GO terms whose positivity depends on cell-type-specific interaction partners (not encoded in protein sequence alone) will transfer poorly even when the structural motif sequences are shared.

**Observed pattern.** The 14.5% AUPRC reduction (0.702 → 0.600) concentrates in GO terms without muscle-brain sequence analogs (GPCR signalling: 0.403; ion transport: 0.421; immune: 0.447), while GO terms with conserved sequence determinants maintain performance (synaptic transmission: 0.720; mitochondrial organisation: 0.641; microtubule-based movement: 0.567). This pattern is qualitatively consistent with the geometric transfer model. However, quantitative prediction of the degradation magnitude from first principles would require explicit modelling of training-set motif coverage and brain-specific isoform distribution — beyond the current analysis.

## Supplementary Note S_arch: Systematic architectural development path to v17f*

This note documents the complete experimental development path leading to the v17f* layer-contrast delta architecture. Results are summarised in Results §3–§4; full experimental details are provided here for reproducibility and reviewers.

### S_arch.1 — Baseline limitation diagnosis

**v15d_splice (splice delta, transductive):** Adding within-gene delta δ_i = φ(i) − μ_{gene(i)} as direct GO-prediction input collapsed AUPRC from 0.702 to 0.457. Mechanism: gene-level GO labels provide no directional signal about which isoform should score higher, producing random gradient directions that disrupted the global sequence-function mapping.

**v17a (two-stage triplet, binary Pfam):** T_ψ Dense(ESM-2[640]+Pfam[512]→512→256, ℓ₂-norm) trained on cross-gene Pfam Jaccard similarity triplets. Stage 1 collapsed within 15 epochs (active triplet ratio: 27% → 0.03% → 0.00%; 3,306 valid triplets from 6,040 domain-bearing isoforms) because 80.9% of isoform pairs have Pfam Jaccard=0. Two-stage model (MLP_θ on frozen under-trained T_ψ) achieved All MF AUPRC 0.354 (−0.24 vs. PRISM 0.596). L2_Structural terms (0.178) scored *lower* than L4_CellState (0.564), confirming that concatenating sparse binary Pfam features with ESM-2 adds noise to the L2 terms.

**v17b (Pfam-only Stage 1, ESM-2 bypass):** T_ψ restricted to Pfam[512] alone; Stage 2 received concat[T_ψ(64), φ_L30(640)]. Recovered to AUPRC 0.602 (Δ=+0.248 vs. v17a), matching PRISM baseline. T_ψ again collapsed (active ratio 0.32% at epoch 5 — only 15 valid triplets from the 80.9% non-domain pool at cosine ≥ 0.70 threshold). Confirmed: ESM-2 bypass in Stage 2 is correct; training signal density in Stage 1 is the barrier.

**v17c (GO-label cross-gene triplets in Stage 1):** Replaced Pfam Jaccard with GO-label co-occurrence for Stage 1 triplet formation (16,384 triplets; same-GO-positive pairs from different genes as positives). Active triplet ratio stabilised at 70.3% (epoch 5) through 69.8% (epoch 50). Despite genuine T_ψ learning, v17c AUPRC (0.600) matched v17b identically; L2_Structural gain negligible (+0.002). Final bottleneck: informational content of binary Pfam domain presence (a subset of what ESM-2 already encodes) — Stage 1 inputs carry no structural information independent of ESM-2.

### S_arch.2 — Gene-mean delta (v17d)

**v17d (gene-mean delta, transductive):** Stage 1: T_ψ([640]→256→64, ℓ₂-norm) trained on δ_gene_i = φ(i) − μ_{gene(i)} with 22,080 GO-label cross-gene triplets (multi-isoform anchors only; δ=0 single-isoform genes excluded). Stage 2: MLP_θ(concat[T_ψ(δ_i)(64), φ_L30(640)]=704 → Dense(256→128→82, focal γ=2)). T_ψ active triplet ratio: 92.6% (epoch 5) → 41.1% (epoch 50). Achieved All MF AUPRC **0.683** (Δ=+0.086 over PRISM 0.596); L2_Structural **0.559** (Δ=+0.246). Bootstrap CI (B=1,000): 0.697 [0.685, 0.710] All MF, 0.579 [0.560, 0.599] L2_Structural.

**v17d limitation:** 26.9% of training set are single-isoform genes (δ=0 → ineligible T_ψ anchors). Gene-mean averaging becomes noisy for genes with few isoforms (modal count: 2 per test gene). Signal is transductive — requires gene siblings at inference time.

**Ablation v17e:** concat[δ_gene(640), φ_L30(640)] = 1,280-dim MLP without triplet training achieved 0.639 All MF and 0.487 L2_Structural: 49%/65% of v17d gain attributable to the raw δ feature alone; T_ψ organisation adds the remaining 51%/35%.

**v17d_muscle generalisation test:** v17d architecture on 18 muscle BP terms achieved 0.652 (−0.050 vs. PRISM 0.702; 17/18 terms regressed). Only 4,408 triplets available; gene identity is informative for muscle BP terms (removing between-gene component via δ discards signal PRISM correctly exploits). v17d gain is brain/MF-specific.

### S_arch.3 — Layer-contrast delta (v17f / v17f*)

**v17f (layer-contrast, T_ψ bottleneck):** Replaced δ_gene with inductive δ_layer = φ_{L30} − φ_{L15}. Non-zero for all 31,668 training isoforms; 23,837 GO-label cross-gene triplets; T_ψ active ratio 80–85% across 50 epochs. All MF AUPRC **0.717**; L2_Structural **0.622**. Exceeded v17d by +0.035/+0.063 and resolved the transductive limitation.

**v17f* (no-T_ψ ablation):** Stage 2 received concat[δ_layer(640, MaxAbsScaler), φ_{L30}(640)] = 1,280-dim directly (no T_ψ). Achieved All MF **0.734** (CI: 0.723–0.747) and L2_Structural **0.637** (CI: 0.617–0.654) — *exceeding* v17f by +0.017/+0.015. T_ψ 640→64 bottleneck (9% of 704-dim Stage 2 input in v17f vs. 50% in no-T_ψ) limits δ_layer contribution. **v17f* is the preferred architecture.**

**Gene-mean oracle control:** replacing each isoform's embedding with gene-mean embedding before training yields AUPRC 0.803 (91.4% of v17f* performance). Confirms macro AUPRC is predominantly gene-family identification; within-gene discrimination quantified separately by domain-ranking AUC (0.630) and pos_bias.

### S_arch.5 — Mechanistic probing studies

**Domain encoding preservation:** Logistic regression on δ_layer predicts Pfam domain presence at mean AUROC 0.990 (±0.008) vs. 0.994 for φ_L30 — δ_layer subtraction does not destroy structural encoding.

Most predictive δ_layer dimension (dim 217; r = +0.147) shows sign reversal in φ_L30 (r = −0.111) — subtraction amplifies dimensions where the functional depth progression is largest while cancelling shared background.

**Norm-direction decoupling:** δ_layer L₂ norm shows no dependence on Pfam domain count (ρ = −0.020) — structural information encoded directionally, not in magnitude.

**Scale experiment:** v17f applied to ESM-2 8M (δ = L6−L3; Δ = −0.001 All MF) and ESM-2 35M (δ = L12−L6; Δ = 0.000) shows zero gain. Signal is depth-dependent: ≥30 transformer layers required for distinct structural-to-functional layer specialisation.

Geometric observation: δ(L30−L25) mean norm 140.6 vs. 61.0 for δ(L30−L05) — late ESM-2 layers (L26–L30) contribute larger per-layer magnitude changes than early layers.

### Supplementary Note S_dissection: Anatomical dissection of ESM-2's isoform representation

This Note provides the full mechanistic dissection summarised in Results §4c. These are post-hoc anatomical analyses performed after the δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 architecture was already empirically validated by ablation (§4); they account for *why* the architecture resolves isoforms at the domain level, and are reported here rather than in the main Results to keep the primary narrative on the isoform-resolution evidence (§4b) and its representational ceiling (§Discussion).

**Per-layer discriminative structure: the final layer is not where GO signal peaks.** For each of 277 GO terms we fit a per-layer linear probe on the raw 640-dimensional embedding of each of the 30 transformer layers and located the Fisher discriminative peak — the layer at which the probe is maximally discriminative. The peak layer is broadly distributed across the trajectory (median L14, interquartile range [L7, L27], full range L1–L30), and only 8/277 terms (2.9%) peak at the final layer L30 — the single layer that mean-pooled PLM classifiers use. Mean-pooling at L30 therefore reads the representation at a depth where, for 97% of GO terms, the linearly-discriminative signal has already passed its maximum. This provides the mechanistic basis for the layer-contrast architecture δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 (§4): the subtracted mid-layer L15 sits within the interquartile band where most GO-discriminative signal is concentrated, recovering information that the L30 mean-pool integrates over.

**Within-gene layer-divergence: domain differences are encoded, length-independently, and peak at mid-depth.** To test directly whether the layer trajectory carries isoform-resolution structure — free of the gene-family confounds that dominate cross-gene comparisons — we enumerated within-gene isoform pairs across the muscle test set (the same 36,748-isoform set as the Domain-Ranking analysis) and, at each layer, compared the ESM-2 cosine distance between isoform pairs that differ in Pfam domain architecture versus pairs that share it. Because both members of every pair belong to the same gene, gene identity is constant and cannot contribute to the comparison. After removing exact-duplicate pairs (isoforms with fewer than 10 differing residues; 11% of pairs, an annotation artefact), 25,350 pairs remained (12,003 domain-different, 13,347 domain-same). Domain-different pairs are 3–4× more distant than domain-same pairs at every layer — far outside a label-shuffle null whose 95% interval is [0.95, 1.05] at every layer (empirical p < 0.001 at all 30 layers) — and the ratio peaks at mid-depth (L17 = 3.88) and is lower at both the input-proximal (L1 = 3.10) and output (L30 = 2.99) layers, mirroring the Fisher-peak distribution and reinforcing the δ_layer rationale. Critically, this is not a length artefact: within length-difference-matched strata the ratio persists across all bins (2.5×–7.8×), and both absolute distances are non-degenerate (domain-different 0.026, domain-same 0.0066 at L17). The signal is the presence or absence of a domain-architecture change rather than a graded domain count: among domain-different pairs, divergence magnitude does not track the number of domains changed once length difference is controlled (partial Spearman ρ = −0.008, p = 0.34). Domain swaps (each isoform bearing a domain the other lacks) diverge marginally more than pure gain/loss events, but this is largely explained by their greater sequence change (residual effect after matching on differing-residue count: partial ρ = +0.024, p = 0.009).

**Principal axes of the trajectory encode structural classes, not functional motifs.** We interpreted the eight joint-PCA axes of the layer trajectory (§Methods) by correlating each axis score against 22 sequence-intrinsic biophysical/compositional features and against Pfam domain-family membership, at both gene level and within-gene-residual level (Supplementary Fig. S_axes). All eight axes align with recognisable secondary-structure, charge, size, and domain-family properties, with the strongest axes reaching |ρ| = 0.4–0.7: axis 0 separates soluble β-sheet globular domains (enriched for PDZ [PF00595], RRM [PF00076], PHD [PF00628]; β-sheet fraction ρ = −0.70) from transmembrane proteins (ligand-gated ion channels [PF02931/2], MFS transporters [PF07690]; transmembrane-segment count ρ = −0.35, N-terminal hydrophobicity ρ = −0.37); axis 3 marks large multidomain enzymes and scaffolds (WD40 [PF00400], DEAD-box helicase [PF00270], protein kinase [PF00069]; domain count ρ = +0.43, acidic residue fraction ρ = +0.47); axis 1 — previously the weakest axis under the compositional panel alone — resolves under domain-family analysis into leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-repeat membrane receptors (LRR [PF13855], Ig V-set [PF07686]; transmembrane count ρ = +0.20); and axis 6 into KRAB-zinc-finger transcription factors and cytoplasmic scaffolds (KRAB [PF01352], SCAN [PF02023], spectrin [PF00435]). No axis aligns with a functional short-linear-motif class: nuclear-localisation/export signals, SH3-binding PxxP motifs, and phosphorylation-site consensus counts appear among axis correlates only as length-confounded artefacts that vanish at the within-gene-residual level. The interpretable content of ESM-2's isoform-discriminating representation is therefore structural and compositional — the vocabulary of protein domain architecture — not the vocabulary of regulatory motifs.

**The within-gene axes are not artefacts of gene-family background subtraction: a conditioning control.** The eight joint-PCA axes above are dominated by between-gene (gene-family) variance — the highest-variance axis is also the most gene-family-aligned — which raises the concern that any "within-gene" signal read from them could be an artefact of implicitly subtracting a gene-level background. We addressed this directly by re-deriving the axes under explicit gene-family conditioning: rather than projecting out gene-family directions (which risks defining the within-gene signal into existence), we subtracted each gene-family's mean embedding and recomputed a single pooled principal-axis basis over the family-centred residuals — a within-family scatter decomposition, analogous to the within-class scatter matrix of linear discriminant analysis (§Methods). Three results establish that the within-gene structure is genuine rather than a background-removal artefact. First, the family-centred axes coincide geometrically with the uncentred joint axes (mean principal-angle cosine 0.95 across the eight-dimensional subspace; top five axes > 0.98): conditioning on gene-family re-weights the variance along the existing structural axes rather than manufacturing a new basis, so within-gene variation inhabits the same domain-architecture geometry as between-gene variation. Second, the axes are invariant to how gene-family is defined — replacing Pfam-dominant-domain families with families obtained by all-versus-all sequence-homology clustering (DIAMOND, ≥30% identity / ≥50% coverage) yields a near-identical basis (principal-angle cosine 0.99 between the two definitions) — excluding the possibility that the axes are an artefact of a particular family assignment. Third, decomposing the within-gene signal by biophysical feature shows that isoform length is the single largest within-gene variance component, but a length-independent domain-architecture axis persists: axis 3 (the multidomain-enzyme axis above) retains a within-gene-residual partial correlation with domain count after controlling for length (partial Spearman ρ = +0.274), while its length correlation itself vanishes under domain control — the domain axis is not a length proxy. This length-independent domain axis validates against experimentally-curated isoform switches: across 59 BISECT domain-change cases mapped to the brain test set, the sign of the axis-3 difference between the two isoforms matches the direction of Pfam domain gain/loss in 53/59 cases (89.8%; binomial p = 8.8 × 10⁻¹¹), including the Complex I loci NDUFS7 and NDUFS8; consistent with the presence/absence rather than graded encoding established above (§Within-gene layer-divergence), the concordance is directional (magnitude Spearman ρ = 0.18). Finally, we stress the distinction between representational variance and predictive signal: principal-component analysis identifies the directions of greatest variation — dominated within-gene by length — which are not the directions PRISM uses for prediction, whose within-gene rankings are near-length-agnostic (length-AUC 0.560; §Results). The largest-variance axis and the function-predictive axis are distinct objects; the interpretive weight of the axis decomposition therefore rests on the length-independent, BISECT-validated domain-architecture axes (axes 0, 1, 3, 6), not on the length-dominated variance envelope.

**Causal occlusion establishes a double dissociation: within-gene ranking uses the domain axis, gene-level identification uses the gene-family axis.** The correlations above show which biological property each axis encodes; they do not establish which axes PRISM's predictions causally depend on. We tested this by occlusion: projecting each axis out of the test-set embeddings (φ_L15 and φ_L30) and re-predicting with the trained model, then measuring the change in within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC and in gene-level macro-AUPRC, against a null of twenty random unit directions occluded identically. The result is a clean double dissociation. Removing the domain-architecture axis (axis 3) produces the largest drop in within-gene Domain-Ranking (ΔAUC = +0.013, z = +8.4 versus the random-direction null) but barely affects gene-level macro-AUPRC; removing the β-sheet/hydrophobicity axis (axis 0, the highest-variance and most gene-family-aligned axis) produces the largest drop in gene-level macro-AUPRC (ΔAUPRC = +0.038) but no drop in within-gene ranking (z = −2.1). Within-gene isoform discrimination and gene-level family identification therefore run on distinct representational axes. A complementary retrain-with-occlusion test — removing each axis from both training and test data and retraining — sharpens the interpretation: gene-level macro-AUPRC is fully recoverable after removing any single axis (the model routes around it, confirming that gene-family identity is redundantly encoded across many dimensions, which is why the gene-mean oracle is so strong), whereas the within-gene Domain-Ranking contribution of axis 3 survives retraining (ΔAUC = +0.006 versus random +0.001), indicating that the within-gene domain signal is more concentrated and less redundant. Critically, the highest-variance axis (axis 0), which alone accounts for the largest share of the trajectory variance, is not used for within-gene ranking (Domain-Ranking z = −2.1); its causal role is confined to gene-level identification. The negative sign is mechanistically informative and adjudicates between two accounts of this dissociation — a learned within-gene down-weighting of axis 0 versus passive weight leakage. On the muscle Domain-Ranking set, axis 0 varies within-gene at an amplitude comparable to the domain axis (within-gene residual SD 1.86 versus 2.04 for axis 3), so it is not suppressed to zero; yet its within-gene variation is uncorrelated with the isoform domain difference that Domain-Ranking scores (within-gene Spearman ρ = −0.001, versus +0.29 for axis 3), so occluding it removes rankable noise rather than signal. Because PRISM (v17f*) is a single feed-forward network with no gene-conditional gating, it applies one weight field to axis 0 everywhere: the between-gene-justified weight on the β-sheet/gene-family axis is imposed unchanged on the label-misaligned within-gene variation of that same axis, and the resulting leakage — not a within-gene down-weighting, which this architecture cannot express — is what makes axis 0 anti-useful for within-gene ranking. The double dissociation is therefore an emergent consequence of the axes' differing within-gene label alignment under shared weights, not a trained routing mechanism. This is the causal counterpart to the representational-variance-versus-predictive-signal distinction drawn above and to PRISM's near-chance within-gene length-AUC (0.560): the directions of greatest variation are not the directions the predictions rank on, and the length that dominates the within-gene variance envelope (a within-family-basis axis; §Methods) is not what PRISM uses to order isoforms. Two further analyses localise and delimit the domain signal. First, the within-gene domain signal is categorical rather than graded: while the graded domain-count correlation with axis 3 is modest (within-gene partial Spearman ρ = 0.27), a length-matched classifier reading all eight axes detects the binary presence/absence of a domain-architecture change between isoforms at AUROC 0.71, confirming that the low graded correlation reflects the categorical nature of domain switching (§Within-gene layer-divergence) rather than a representational ceiling. The eight interpretable axes are moreover a lower bound on the encoded signal, not its limit: the identical length-matched classifier reading the full 640-dimensional layer-mean representation detects the same binary domain-architecture change at AUROC 0.84 (compression gap +0.12 over the eight-axis projection), rising further to 0.89 with a non-linear (gradient-boosted) reader that matches PRISM's own non-linear MLP decoder, so the compact interpretable basis under-reports the domain information that PRISM, operating on the full embedding, can exploit — the human-readable axes trade discriminative headroom for interpretability, and the domain-change signal in ESM-2 is only partly linearly decodable. Second, the domain divergence is localised to mid-network: across the 59 BISECT domain-switch cases, the layer at which the two isoforms' axis-3 trajectories diverge maximally has median L16 (interquartile range L9–L24), significantly deeper than the divergence layer of random within-gene isoform pairs (median L11; Mann–Whitney p = 0.022), converging with the population-level mid-layer divergence peak (L17) and the Fisher-peak distribution. These per-isoform axis trajectories over the 30 layers, which visualise where isoforms of the same gene separate, are provided for the Complex I domain-switch loci NDUFS2 and NDUFS7 and for the domain-invariant accessory subunits NDUFS4 and NDUFAF5 (negative controls carrying no Pfam domain in any brain isoform, where isoforms nonetheless separate on the axes) in Supplementary Fig. S_traj.

**The Domain-Ranking ceiling is bounded by the representation, not by model capacity or supervision.** The interpretable-basis ceiling above concerns what a *linear reader* decodes from ESM-2; a complementary question is whether PRISM's own within-gene Domain-Ranking ceiling (AUC 0.630 on held-out muscle; §4b) is set by the model — too little classifier capacity or too little within-gene supervision — or by the δ_layer representation itself. Two interventions of opposite character bracket the answer, and both leave the ceiling in place. *Capacity.* A depth-controlled width sweep of the classifier head (from the production 256→128 units up to 768→384, holding the number of hidden layers fixed so that width is not confounded with depth) raises gene-level macro AUPRC by +0.013 (95% CI [+0.010, +0.016] for a 512→256 head; replicated across two independent five-seed sets), confirming that the *supervised* gene-level head was in fact mildly capacity-limited — yet the identical widening leaves within-gene Domain-Ranking statistically unchanged (ΔAUC = −0.000, gene-clustered bootstrap 95% CI [−0.011, +0.010], resampling whole genes rather than gene–term units to respect within-gene correlation). Added capacity flows entirely to the supervised objective and none to the unsupervised within-gene ranking. *Supervision.* Two independently-designed auxiliary losses that directly target within-gene domain ordering both fail to raise Domain-Ranking. A conditional domain-count regression (§6b) degrades it to 0.626; and a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss — which explicitly pushes the higher-domain isoform of each training pair above its lower-domain sibling on that gene's positive GO terms — leaves Domain-Ranking unchanged (ΔAUC = −0.001, bootstrap CI including zero) while degrading macro AUPRC by 0.055, because a blanket "more-domains-scores-higher" signal injects mis-directed gradient on the motif-dependent terms — the 74.6% of within-gene specialisation events (§Functional specialization) where domain count does not track function. Neither capacity nor supervision moves the ceiling: the within-gene Domain-Ranking limit is causally bracketed from both sides as a property of the δ_layer representation rather than of the model that reads it, consistent with the motif-level boundary of the three-tier account (Results §4c synthesis). (This falsifies these two specific supervisions, not every conceivable one: a surgical signal weighted toward the domain-dependent term subset remains untested and is a natural target for future work.)

**Per-residue dissection: the tier-iii ceiling reflects pooling and anchor availability, not an absence of encoding (preliminary).** The three-tier synthesis (Results §4c) frames motif-level functional direction as "not encoded" by ESM-2. A direct per-residue analysis refines this claim: the underlying sequence perturbation *is* locally encoded — mean-pooling and the lack of a common cross-mechanism reference direction, not an absence of representation, are what block its recovery. Using aligned UniProt isoform pairs (42 pairs; per-residue ESM-2 L30, no retraining or additional supervision), we computed the per-residue embedding shift δ_p between matched backbone residues as a function of distance from the nearest splice junction. For motif/regulatory pairs (|Δlen| ≤ 30 aa, n=13; the same class underlying the tier-iii "not encoded" claim), δ_p is 3.26 at the junction and decays to 0.19 beyond 160 residues — a 17.6-fold, smoothly monotonic attenuation (not an alignment artefact) — while whole-sequence mean-pooled cosine distance for the same pairs is only 0.0097, confirming that mean-pooling dilutes a genuinely localised signal to near-invisibility. Domain-loss control pairs (|Δlen| ≥ 100 aa, n=15) show the same qualitative pattern at larger magnitude (junction 5.43 → far-field 0.67, 8-fold decay), confirming the measure detects real structural perturbation rather than noise. This localises the tier-iii ceiling to ESM-2's *pooling* operation rather than its *encoding* operation — consistent with, and mechanistically explaining, the capacity/supervision bracketing above (a head cannot recover signal that mean-pooling has already diluted out of its input).

Recovering the diluted signal, however, requires more than replacing mean-pooling with a local window: a naive region-pool (window W ∈ {10,20,40} around the junction, scored against the paper's centroid-similarity anchor — the per-GO-term mean embedding of training-positive isoforms, the same anchor underlying the Domain-Ranking baseline in §Discussion) leaves motif-pair direction accuracy unchanged (9/13 = 0.692 for both global and region pooling, no window flipping a single pair), despite the region-pooled shift vector being 5.3× larger in magnitude than the global shift (‖F−N‖ 2.22 vs 0.42) and better aligned with the GO-centroid than the global shift (signed cosine +0.080 vs +0.040). The bottleneck is therefore not signal magnitude but **anchor choice**: even the region-pooled shift is only weakly aligned (cosine ≈ 0.08) with the global GO-term centroid, because that centroid represents cross-gene functional identity, not the within-pair direction of "which isoform is more functional" that Domain-Ranking-style metrics require. Substituting a within-pair anchor constructed from the direction PRISM's own domain-loss training data actually uses — the mean difference between complete- and truncated-isoform embeddings over 3,216 within-gene domain pairs — changes the result for domain-loss pairs specifically: on the n=14 domain-loss UniProt pairs for which this anchor is mechanistically appropriate, region-pooling improves direction alignment from 0.571 (global pool, cosine +0.173) to 0.714 (region pool, cosine +0.329). This is, to our knowledge, the first direct demonstration that a representation reorganisation (local pooling around the structural change, under a mechanism-matched anchor) can partially recover isoform-direction signal that whole-sequence mean-pooling and the current δ_layer/L30 architecture do not resolve — though the demonstration is exploratory (n=14, single window choice, no bootstrap CI) and awaits validation on the full Domain-Ranking evaluation set. Critically, the domain-completeness anchor does *not* transfer to the motif pairs (n=13; cosine alignment is negative, i.e. mechanistically mismatched), and no single motif-pair anchor exists to substitute it: the 14 benchmark motif pairs span mechanistically unrelated function classes (kinase activation-loop dynamics, DNA-binding domain variants, RNA-binding RRM inclusion, GTPase switch regions, structural insert number), each requiring its own reference direction, with too few labelled examples per mechanism to construct one. The refined account of tier (iii) is therefore not "ESM-2 lacks a representation of motif-level sequence change" but "domain-loss has a single, learnable, common anchor (domain completeness) that a local-pooling architecture can exploit, whereas motif-level function is mechanism-heterogeneous and currently lacks any common anchor to pool toward" — a restatement, at the representational level, of the same gene-level-label ceiling established in Results §1 (no isoform-level ground truth exists to supervise which anchor is correct for a given motif mechanism).

### Supplementary Note S_severity: Canonical-anchored severity regression and tissue-asymmetry mediation chain

This Note provides the full statistical detail summarised in Results §6b. It reports (i) the original five-covariate severity regression establishing that muscle and brain differ not only in *how often* an edit remodels a Pfam domain (§6) but in *how large* a representational consequence it produces, and (ii) a subsequent mediation analysis tracing the apparent muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of two of those five covariates (N-terminal overlap, alignment-resync failure) back through a three-layer surrogate chain to its most proximal cause.

**Original five-covariate regression.** The domain-envelope analysis above establishes that the two tissues differ in *how often* an alternative-splicing event remodels a Pfam domain, but rate alone does not show whether the two tissues also differ in *how large a representational consequence* each edit class produces once it occurs. To test this independently, we extended the within-gene pair set from the two-isoform-only instrument above (restricted to genes with exactly two isoforms; 3,298 muscle, 3,435 brain genes) to every multi-isoform gene, anchoring each gene on its canonical isoform (MANE Select > Ensembl_canonical > APPRIS principal > longest-CDS-in-dataset fallback) paired against every other isoform of that gene as an independent edit event — muscle 8,569 multi-isoform genes yielding 15,493 pairs (9.2× the two-isoform instrument), brain 12,157 genes yielding 33,148 pairs (15.2×). For each pair we regressed a continuous representational-severity score — the gene-disjoint out-of-fold projection of the pair's L15+L30 mean-pooled embedding difference onto the fold-trained mean coherence direction (the same length-invariant construction underlying the Domain-Ranking metric; unlike max-pooling, which we separately confirmed inherits an extreme-value-statistics length artefact in 6 of 7 tested categories, mean-pooling's projection is provably length-invariant and empirically artefact-free) — on five structural properties of the edit: size (changed-residue count), whether the edit changes domain architecture, whether it overlaps the N-terminal 60 residues, the fraction of changed residues in a disorder-promoting region (TOP-IDP scale), and whether the alignment fails to resynchronise into a single contiguous matching block downstream of the edit (a protein-alignment-level proxy for frame-disrupting edits, not direct nucleotide-level frame ground truth). All five covariates are significant after gene-clustered robust standard errors and Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the pooled 48,641-pair fit (R² = 0.119; permutation null R² ≈ 0, P < 0.001, 1,000 gene-cluster-preserving permutations), and — critically, given this paper's emphasis on ruling out gene-identity confounds for macro AUPRC — the same five covariates remain significant when the model is re-fit using only within-gene variation (each gene's pairs demeaned by their gene mean, restricted to the 10,286 genes contributing ≥2 pairs; R² = 0.158, versus R² = 0.118 for the pooled fit on the identical subsample — the two R² values are not directly comparable, since the within-gene fit's denominator is the smaller gene-demeaned variance rather than total variance, but both remain well above the permutation null), confirming the severity score is isoform-pair-intrinsic rather than a gene-level property. One caveat applies to the domain-architecture-change coefficient specifically: because canonical-isoform selection is not always the longer sequence of a pair (43,348/48,641 = 89.1% of pairs), the coefficient is significant only in this majority stratum (β = 1.91) and null in the reverse 10.9% (β = −0.18, n.s.), so this covariate's effect should be read as conditional on canonical being the structurally more complete isoform, which it is in the large majority of cases. Fitting the model separately per tissue reveals a division of labour that converges with, but is independent of, the rate-based analysis above: the domain-architecture-change coefficient is larger in brain (β = 2.11, n = 33,148) than muscle (β = 1.53, n = 15,493), while N-terminal overlap (β = 1.98 vs. 0.36) and alignment-resync failure (β = 3.74 vs. 0.98) are far larger in muscle than brain, and the five covariates jointly explain more variance in muscle (R² = 0.177) than brain (R² = 0.101). The disorder covariate adds a magnitude-level refinement to the encoding result reported in §4c: disorder-region overlap is associated with a *smaller* representational footprint after adjusting for edit size in both tissues (muscle β = −6.69, brain β = −4.07), indicating that although a disorder-region edit is decodable as such from the embedding difference (AUROC 0.79), it produces a comparatively diffuse, lower-amplitude shift in representation space than an equally sized edit to a structured region — an encoding/magnitude dissociation distinct from, and complementary to, the direction/labelling dissociation established throughout this paper.

**Mediation follow-up: the resync-failure tissue ordering is downstream of a size asymmetry.** The N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync-failure coefficients above initially suggested a mirror-image muscle-specific story of two independent mechanism classes. A follow-up analysis tested whether this reading holds under independent testing. N-terminal overlap's apparent effect does not survive confound-controlled discrimination testing — a size-quintile-matched AUC of severity_score for N-terminal-overlapping versus non-overlapping pairs is at chance in both tissues (0.489 muscle, 0.491 brain, 95% CI spanning 0.5) — so it should not be read as a validated muscle-specific mechanism despite its significance in the pooled OLS fit. The alignment-resync-failure coefficient's muscle-greater-than-brain ordering is real but is a downstream trace of a more proximal covariate, edit fragmentation, rather than an independent frame-specific mechanism: logistic regression of resync_failure_binary on size_z alone achieved near-identical discriminative power in both tissues (AUC = 0.854 muscle, 0.842 brain), and at matched size deciles the raw resync-failure rate was, if anything, higher in brain than muscle (e.g. 13.6% vs 21.7% at the size≈33-residue decile) — the opposite of the direction required for resync itself to be a muscle-specific driver. Independently, edit size's own relationship to severity_score was confirmed to be genuinely tissue-asymmetric: gene-cluster-bootstrapped Pearson r = 0.351 [0.328, 0.373] muscle vs 0.277 [0.264, 0.291] brain (gap 95% CI [0.047, 0.100], excludes zero; tissue-label permutation p = 0.001). This establishes that the resync ordering is a downstream trace of a genuine size asymmetry rather than an independent resync-specific signal.

**Second mediation layer: edit fragmentation, not raw size, is the proximal covariate.** Tracing the size asymmetry further, we recomputed, per pair, the number of separate non-contiguous SequenceMatcher change intervals (n_intervals) — a measure of whether an edit lands as a single contiguous block or fragments across multiple positions in the alignment. Including n_intervals_z (and its tissue interaction) in a pooled severity_score ~ size_z + tissue + size_z:tissue regression reduced the size_z × tissue interaction coefficient by 89.2% (gene-cluster-bootstrapped 95% CI on the shrinkage [71.5%, 110.9%]; a permutation null in which n_intervals_z was randomly shuffled across pairs, breaking its pair-level correspondence, produced a mean shrinkage of 0.0% [95th percentile 0.1%], p = 0.001, ruling out a generic degrees-of-freedom artefact). At matched size, muscle edits fragment into more separate change intervals than brain edits of the same total size (e.g. 10.7 vs 7.8 mean intervals at the size≈680-residue decile), and this excess is broad-based rather than concentrated in a small number of outlier genes: the ten genes contributing most to the large-edit fragmentation signal (LCOR, GNAS, MACF1, GOLGB1, DST, LINC02693, ANK2, FANCC, SORBS2, ATRIP) account for only 5.2% of the total signal across 2,028 contributing genes, and the muscle-brain gap in mean n_intervals persists essentially unchanged after excluding them (8.98 vs 7.74). A formal Fisher's exact test of high-fragmentation genes (above-median mean n_intervals in the large-edit subset) against cytoskeleton-related GO annotation (GO:0005856/0008092/0015629/0030036/0007010) found no enrichment in either tissue (muscle OR = 1.13, P = 0.42; brain OR = 1.06, P = 0.63), so the presence of large cytoskeletal-scaffold genes (MACF1, DST, ANK2) among the top contributors — genes which independently rank among the top fragmentation contributors in both tissues, consistent with their general status as unusually large, exon-rich loci rather than a tissue- or fragmentation-specific enrichment — should not be read as evidence of a cytoskeleton-specific mechanism. An unbiased genome-wide GO enrichment scan (all GO terms with ≥5 genes in the population, Benjamini-Hochberg correction) similarly found no functional category enriched among high-fragmentation genes in either tissue (muscle: 3,395 terms tested, 0 pass FDR<0.10; brain: 4,934 terms tested, 0 pass FDR<0.10) — heavily-fragmented genes share no common GO function, in either tissue. We therefore revise the interpretation of the resync-failure coefficient reported above: its apparent tissue ordering is not evidence of a resync-specific mechanism, but a downstream trace of a broad-based tissue difference in edit fragmentation, which is the covariate most proximally associated with the muscle-greater-than-brain representational-severity asymmetry.

A committed reconstruction of the original five-covariate pipeline — canonical-anchored pair construction and coherence-projection scoring (`build_severity_pairs.py`, `build_severity_score.py`), the §6-consistent tissue-specific domain-architecture-change covariate (`build_severity_domain_faithful.py`), and the gene-clustered regression (`severity_regression.py`) — reproduces the qualitative structure reported here: the sign and significance of all five covariates, the brain-greater-than-muscle ordering of the domain-architecture coefficient, the muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync coefficients (subsequently explained by the mediation chain above), and the canonical-orientation-conditional domain effect (significant in the 89.1% majority stratum, null in the reverse 10.9%). The mediation follow-up analyses were performed with `resync_mechanical_test.py`, `size_severity_tissue_gap.py`, `dispersion_mechanism_test.py`, `dispersion_rigor_and_concentration.py`, and `tissue_compare_and_enrichment.py` (all committed in `hMuscle/model/`).
