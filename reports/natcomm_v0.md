# Sequence-only isoform resolution maps protein-language-model limits and cross-validates a Complex I switch in Alzheimer's disease

**Seungwon Lee**, [co-authors TBD]

*Draft for Nature Communications — v0 (2026-06-26)*

---

## Abstract

Alternative splicing produces functionally distinct protein isoforms, yet current annotation tools operate at the gene level, assigning every isoform of a gene the same function. We introduce PRISM, a protein language model predicting isoform-specific Gene Ontology function from sequence alone, and BISECT, an independent pipeline for disease isoform-switch consequences. ESM-2 embeddings intrinsically distinguish isoforms of the same gene, letting PRISM rank domain-complete above domain-truncated variants with within-gene AUC 0.63–0.78 against a null fixed at 0.50 by construction, and we map where this resolution reaches an intrinsic ceiling: domain-affecting splices are captured, whereas a bounded non-domain minority — disorder-region, targeting, and short-motif changes — stays encoded yet describable by no gene-level label, a limit we trace to mean-pooling, which dilutes spatially incoherent changes. Deployed zero-shot to Alzheimer's-disease brain, PRISM's sequence-only scores converge locus by locus with BISECT's independently derived transcript-usage and structural evidence — the two methods share no inputs — to cross-validate a Complex I disruption: an NDUFAF5 assembly-factor switch abolishes a hydroxylation its substrate NDUFS7 requires, both disrupted in the same neurons, with further switches nominated pending replication. Because neither method informs the other, their agreement gives sequence-first evidence for an undescribed splicing origin of Alzheimer's Complex I deficiency.

*Keywords*: protein language model, ESM-2, alternative splicing, isoform function prediction, Alzheimer's disease, long-read single-cell RNA-seq, Complex I, BISECT

## Introduction

Alternative splicing affects ~95% of human multi-exon genes and produces protein isoforms with distinct domain compositions, interaction surfaces, and subcellular localisation patterns [1]. The resulting proteomic diversity is functionally consequential: disease-causing mutations are enriched at alternatively spliced exon boundaries [2], splicing factor dysregulation is a hallmark of neurodegeneration [3], and isoform-switching events in Alzheimer's disease (AD) brain have been documented across multiple cohorts using long-read sequencing technologies [4,5]. Yet the tools available to interpret these events remain anchored at the gene level. Pfam domain databases, InterProScan, and pfam2go — the field standard for functional annotation — annotate canonical protein sequences and assign identical Gene Ontology (GO) terms to all isoforms of a gene [6]. When a splicing event removes an interaction domain, alters a mitochondrial targeting sequence, or introduces a premature termination codon, no existing tool propagates this structural change into a revised functional prediction for the resulting isoform. Computational isoform-function predictors face a related constraint: DIFFUSE [26], the most directly comparable prior approach, refines a sequence-based neural network with a conditional random field over isoform co-expression, so its isoform-level resolution still depends on expression covariance among isoforms of the same gene rather than on sequence-intrinsic structural differences alone.

The core difficulty is not that isoform-level differences are biologically subtle; they are often structurally stark. The obstacle has been representational: domain-based tools require annotatable domain matches, which are absent for ~80% of all isoforms [our data: 19.1% carry any Pfam domain] and universally absent for novel isoforms uncatalogued in RefSeq or Ensembl. Protein language models (PLMs) offer a fundamentally different entry point. ESM-2, trained by masked language modelling on evolutionary sequence context across 250 million proteins, generates continuous 640-dimensional embeddings that encode structural and functional features at sub-domain resolution without requiring any database match [7]. We hypothesised that these embeddings encode the isoform-level differences invisible to discrete domain boundaries — that exon-skipping events altering protein hydrophobic cores, interaction interfaces, or disordered linker regions produce measurable embedding shifts, and that a task-specific classifier trained on gene-level GO labels can learn to exploit these shifts for isoform-level prediction.

Here we test this hypothesis systematically. We train PRISM (Protein-isoform Resolution via Intrinsic Sequence Modeling), a lightweight multi-label classifier on top of frozen ESM-2 embeddings, on 36,748 isoforms from long-read single-cell skeletal muscle RNA-seq. We show that, despite training exclusively on gene-level GO labels, PRISM spontaneously learns to discriminate isoforms within the same gene: within-gene prediction variance exceeds between-gene variance, 11 of 13 GO terms show isoform-specific signal significantly above shuffled-label controls (bootstrapped p < 0.05), and individual isoform pairs differ by 27–50-fold in predicted functional score. This isoform discrimination is absent from raw ESM-2 embeddings (where PRISM's 18-dim output outperforms 640-dim ESM-2 by up to 10.6-fold on brain GO terms) and from a splice-feature-only model (within-gene discrimination 0.027 vs 0.094 for PRISM). We formalise this discrimination as a Domain-Ranking metric with a mathematically forced null: PRISM ranks domain-complete above domain-truncated isoforms of the same gene with AUC 0.630 (held-out muscle) and 0.775 (zero-shot brain), against a gene-mean classifier that is provably tied at 0.500 by construction, since it assigns every isoform of a gene an identical score (§4b). We then deploy PRISM zero-shot to 63,994 prefrontal cortex isoforms from an AD single-cell long-read cohort, where it achieves AUPRC 0.600 without any retraining. This brain deployment yields a validated set of isoform-level predictions unavailable to domain-based annotation: on 26 independently-characterised switch cases, 92.3% carry a PRISM prediction outside InterProScan/pfam2go, and among 104 predictions for functions absent from a gene's own labels, 23 are corroborated by independent experimental literature (9–14× enriched over matched nulls, p < 0.001).

Against this validated backdrop we then examine a single disease mechanism in depth. Independently of PRISM — using donor-level differential transcript usage and structural evidence alone, with no PRISM score as input to any module — BISECT (Biological Isoform-Switch Evidence Characterization Tool; a 15-module multi-evidence pipeline performing domain, PPI, structural confidence, evolutionary conservation, NMD, and localization signal analysis) identifies a Complex I disruption cascade in AD brain, anchored by a genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and its direct enzyme–substrate partner NDUFS7, with further Q-module and motor-protein switches nominated pending independent replication (§7–§8). At each locus, PRISM's independently-computed zero-shot score differential converges with BISECT's structural verdict — two methodologically orthogonal routes to the same functional conclusion, providing corroboration rather than a case of one method discovering targets for the other.

To recover isoform-specific information suppressed by ESM-2 mean-pooling, we introduce the layer-contrast delta architecture (v17f*): the difference vector δ_layer = φ_{L30} − φ_{L15} between ESM-2's final and mid transformer layers captures how each individual sequence resolves from local structural to global functional encoding — a per-isoform, inductive signal that requires no gene-sibling sequences at inference time. Trained on ESM-2 150M, v17f* achieves macro AUPRC 0.734 in brain zero-shot evaluation, exceeding ESM-2 k-NN retrieval, fine-tuning, and the pre-trained tools DeepFRI [14] and DeepGoPlus [15], and generalises across the full 279-term GO ontology (§Comparative analysis). Beyond demonstrating that this isoform-resolution signal exists, our central result is to map its limit. The within-gene signal is intrinsic to ESM-2 rather than manufactured by training — a training-free embedding-distance baseline matches PRISM on it — and three orthogonal interventions (added model capacity, explicit within-gene supervision, per-term layer selection) all fail to raise it past a ceiling set by the ESM-2 representation. This ceiling is itself describable, and mechanistically explained: mean-pooling acts as a spatial-coherence filter, preserving the large, spatially coherent shifts that domain-affecting splices produce while diluting the scattered, incoherent shifts of short-motif and disorder-region edits below the pooled signal. The domain-defined majority of splices is therefore captured and saturated, while the bounded non-domain minority is structurally encoded yet undescribable by any gene-level label. It is this boundary — what protein language models can and cannot resolve about isoform function, and why — that our analysis maps, and against which the Alzheimer's-disease application operates.

## Results

### 1. ESM-2 embeddings encode isoform-discriminating sequence features

We first asked whether ESM-2 embeddings intrinsically separate isoforms of the same gene — a prerequisite for any classifier trained on gene-level labels to achieve isoform-specific predictions. For each multi-isoform gene in the training set, we computed the ratio of within-gene embedding variance (variation among isoforms of the same gene) to between-gene embedding variance (variation across genes). Mean-pooled ESM-2 embeddings show a within/between variance ratio of 0.23, consistent with prior evidence that raw PLM embeddings primarily encode gene identity. Following PRISM training, however, the learned 18-dimensional functional representation exhibits within-gene prediction variance of **0.00126**, exceeding between-gene variance of **0.00070** — a reversal demonstrating that task-specific training reorganises the representational space to amplify isoform-level functional differences.

The mechanism by which gene-level supervision produces isoform-level discrimination is informational rather than architectural. Formally, let φ(i) ∈ ℝ^640 denote the frozen ESM-2 embedding of isoform i, and let g(i) denote its gene of origin with gene-level GO label y_{g(i)} ∈ {0,1}^K. PRISM's training objective is:

> **L(θ) = Σ_{i ∈ I} Σ_{k=1}^{K} FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k , y_{g(i),k} )**

where FL(p, y) = −α_y(1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t) is the focal loss with γ=2 and p_t = p if y=1, 1−p otherwise. Critically, f_θ receives no gene-membership signal: isoforms of the same gene are presented as independent examples, and the MLP learns a gene-agnostic mapping from sequence embedding geometry to GO term probability. At convergence, f_θ(φ(i))_k ≈ P(y_k = 1 | φ(i)). If two isoforms i_a, i_b of the same gene produce embeddings in different regions of ℝ^640 — because their sequences encode distinct structural features — f_θ necessarily assigns them different scores, despite both carrying the gene-level label y_g=1. Spontaneous isoform discrimination is therefore a predictable consequence of three properties jointly satisfied: (i) ESM-2 preserves structural consequences of exon-skipping in continuous embedding space; (ii) f_θ learns a cross-gene universal mapping; (iii) focal loss concentrates gradient on hard boundary cases rather than driving all positive-gene isoforms to score 1.0 — the γ=2 weight (1−p_t)^2 reduces gradient from a confident isoform (p_t=0.9) 25-fold relative to a boundary case (p_t=0.5), forcing w_k to discriminate the uncertain isoforms. This mechanism also explains why an explicit within-gene discriminator (v15d_splice, δ_i = φ(i) − E_{j∈G}[φ(j)]) destroys performance (Supplementary Note S1): gene-level labels carry no signal about which isoform should score higher, so the discriminator gradient is directionally random, collapsing AUPRC from 0.7022 to 0.4573.

To quantify this isoform discrimination signal term-by-term, we defined **pos_bias** as the bootstrapped ratio of within-gene positive-class score variance to the label-shuffled null level. Of 13 GO terms tested (1,000-resample bootstrap CIs), **11 show pos_bias significantly above the shuffled baseline** (Benjamini-Hochberg p<0.05), with values of 1.902 (muscle contraction), 1.711 (skeletal muscle development), 1.497 (motor activity), 1.340 (sarcomere organization), and 1.037 (muscle cell differentiation); the two non-significant terms (Ca²⁺ signalling, glycolysis) have wide CIs consistent with insufficient positive-class size rather than absent signal. This signal is absent from a gene-mean baseline (pos_bias=0 by construction), a splice-feature-only model (0.027), and a Pfam-domain logistic regression (0.467 vs. PRISM's 1.902 on muscle contraction), establishing that discrimination emerges from the continuous PLM embedding, not any discrete feature. The gene-mean baseline is the critical control for the gene-grouped design: since all isoforms of a test gene are evaluated together, within-gene score variance can only arise from isoform-level sequence differences, and a model assigning each isoform its gene's mean prediction achieves pos_bias=0 by construction — confirmed across 8,569 multi-isoform test genes (within-gene variance: PRISM 5.0×10⁻⁴, gene-mean 0.0; ratio ~5×10⁸). Non-zero PRISM pos_bias therefore cannot arise from gene identity memorisation or label leakage; it reflects structural information encoded per-isoform in ESM-2.

### 2. PRISM achieves isoform-level GO prediction with substantial gains over domain-based tools

We trained PRISM on 36,748 isoforms from 12,706 human skeletal muscle genes (Samsung long-read single-cell RNA-seq; Samsung Medical Center and Asan Medical Center; sarcopenia vs. healthy controls), using frozen ESM-2 (esm2_t30_150M_UR50D; 640-dim) embeddings as input to a three-layer MLP with binary focal loss (γ=2.0) over 18 GO Biological Process terms. In gene-level held-out evaluation (isoform-level stratified cross-validation), PRISM achieves **macro AUPRC 0.7022** (median 0.699, range 0.527–0.772; 5-seed ensemble). Two complementary evaluation frameworks apply throughout: *Gene-level AUPRC* (§§2–5) measures gene-family identification capacity — a gene-mean oracle achieves AUPRC 0.803, and a cross-gene shuffle test attributes **97.7%** of v17f*'s above-random signal to gene identity rather than isoform-specific discrimination (0.734→0.048; within-gene shuffle: 0.734→0.734, no drop; §Methods, §Discussion). *Within-gene discrimination* (Domain-Ranking AUC, §§4–5; UniProt direction accuracy) tests whether PRISM correctly orders isoforms of the same gene — structurally immune to gene-identity confounds since gene identity is identical for both pair members. Macro AUPRC below should be read in the gene-level context; isoform-resolution evidence rests on the within-gene metrics.

To establish the advantage over existing annotation methods, we trained a logistic regression on binary Pfam domain presence (512 domains; hmmscan) as an upper bound on domain-based pfam2go — a data-driven classifier that systematically outperforms fixed pfam2go mappings. This domain-only classifier achieves macro AUPRC 0.108, a gap of **+0.605** relative to PRISM (Fig. 2D), persisting across the 8 GO terms with direct pfam2go BP entries (e.g. glycolytic process: 0.079 vs. 0.839; microtubule-based movement: 0.058 vs. 0.690) and particularly pronounced for the 10 terms with no pfam2go BP entry, where domain tools reach near-prevalence AUPRC while PRISM averages 0.703. Ablations: ESM-2 input removal reduces AUPRC to 0.108; focal loss removal to 0.524 (mode collapse on rare terms); isoform-level stratification removal to 0.648 (gene-level memorisation). A public multi-organism benchmark (SwissProt 91% identity split; 96 GO slim terms) yields AUPRC 0.271, consistent with a muscle-specialised model evaluated on non-tissue-specific annotations.

To assess generalisation beyond the 18 muscle-biology GO terms, we extended training to 279 terms spanning Biological Process (n=104), Molecular Function (n=82), and Cellular Component (n=93), using the same 31,668 muscle isoforms and identical hyperparameters. All 279 terms retained ≥2 positive test examples in true brain zero-shot (63,994 isoforms). MF terms transfer best (macro AUPRC 0.4671; 44% exceeding 0.5), consistent with MF encoding molecular activities that map directly to conserved structural motifs (motor activity, ATPase activity, binding); BP (0.3256; 17%>0.5) and CC (0.3288; 15%>0.5) encode cellular/organismal context beyond sequence features and transfer less well. Across all 279 terms, the MLP matches or exceeds logistic regression on raw ESM-2 (MLP_hurts=0/279), establishing that task-specific training uniformly extracts discriminative signal beyond what a linear classifier on frozen embeddings recovers.

### 3. Mean-pooling suppresses isoform-specific signal: four diagnostic lines converge on a representational bottleneck

Despite PRISM's overall AUPRC of 0.702 (muscle) and 0.600 (brain), the structurally complex L2_Structural GO term subset (n=33) underperforms (AUPRC 0.313), indicating a representational bottleneck rather than a capacity limitation. Four independent diagnostics converge on the same cause: an explicit within-gene discriminator collapses training (0.702→0.457, directionally-random gradient under gene-level labels), a Pfam-domain triplet encoder fails to train (80.9% of isoform pairs share zero domain overlap), added RNA/localisation features degrade performance (−0.009/−0.110), and ESM-2 fine-tuning yields no gain over frozen embeddings (D1 0.660 vs. D0 0.660) — ruling out capacity, domain-supervision, multimodal, and frozen-embedding explanations in turn. All four point to the same mechanism: ESM-2 mean-pooling averages early structural layers with late functional layers, suppressing the per-isoform structural-to-functional depth progression most relevant to L2_Structural prediction (full diagnostic detail: **Supplementary Note S_arch**).

### 4. Layer-contrast delta architecture (v17f*) overcomes the mean-pooling bottleneck

The diagnostic synthesis yielded explicit design requirements: the differential signal must be (i) inductive — computable per-isoform without gene siblings at inference time; (ii) gradient-conflict-free under gene-level supervision; (iii) directly encoding the structural-to-functional depth progression suppressed by mean-pooling; (iv) independent of Pfam domain annotations, absent for 80.9% of isoforms.

The layer-contrast delta δ_layer(i) = φ_{L30}(i) − φ_{L15}(i) satisfies all four requirements. It is non-zero for every isoform in the training set (compare 26.9% zero-delta isoforms in the transductive gene-mean splice_delta), requires no gene siblings, and captures how each individual sequence resolves from local structural representation (L15) to global functional context (L30) — amplifying dimensions where the depth progression is largest while cancelling background structure shared across both layers. This within-sequence layer contrast encodes the isoform-level structural differences that mean-pooling erases.

We evaluated whether triplet-trained dimensionality reduction of δ_layer (v17f: T_ψ 640→64-dim, 23,837 GO-label cross-gene triplets, active ratio 80–85%) improves performance. Ablation of T_ψ — concatenating δ_layer at full 640-dim directly with φ_{L30} (MaxAbsScaler-normalised) — achieves All MF AUPRC **0.734** (95% CI: 0.723–0.747) and L2_Structural **0.637** (95% CI: 0.617–0.654), *exceeding* v17f by +0.017/+0.015. The T_ψ 640→64 bottleneck reduces δ_layer's Stage 2 input weight from 50% to 9%, limiting its contribution. We therefore report **v17f*** (no-T_ψ: concat[δ_layer(640), φ_{L30}(640)] → Dense(256→128→82, sigmoid); focal γ=2; 5-seed ensemble) as the preferred architecture: highest performance with the simplest training pipeline (+0.138 over PRISM baseline, +0.074 over ESM-2 fine-tuning D1, +0.098 over k-NN retrieval; see §5 for full benchmark comparison).

Three ablations confirm the mechanistic attribution. **(i) Random-δ control**: replacing δ_layer with isotropic random vectors matched in L₂ norm yields 0.642/0.501 (All MF/L2_Structural) — substantially below v17f* (+0.092/+0.136 gap), confirming the directional content of δ_layer, not architectural capacity, drives the gain. **(ii) Scale dependence**: applied to ESM-2 8M (δ=L6−L3) and 35M (δ=L12−L6), Δ over PRISM is −0.001/0.000, confirming the layer-contrast signal requires ≥30 layers to develop structural-to-functional specialisation. At ESM-2 650M (33 layers; δ=L33−L16), concat[φ_{L33},δ] reaches All MF **0.7339** (plain L33: 0.6793; Δ_gain=+0.0546) versus 150M's **0.6693** (Δ_gain=+0.0611). Testing beyond ESM-2's encoder-only architecture, ProtT5-XL (encoder-decoder; δ=L24−L12) achieves **0.7288** (plain: 0.6922; Δ_gain=+0.0366) — here δ_only (0.6744) falls below plain-last, unlike ESM-2, yet the concat still gains, confirming the mechanism differs but generalises; Ankh-base (48 layers; δ=L48−L24) reproduces this pattern (concat **0.7126**, plain 0.6734, Δ_gain=+0.0392, δ_only 0.6638<plain-last), establishing the ESM-2/T5 distinction across two independent T5 models. Across all four PLMs (two families: masked-LM ESM-2, span-corruption T5), all Δ_gain values are positive (mean +0.048) and larger models outperform smaller ones; this generality holds on the within-gene axis too — Domain-Ranking AUC is near-identical across three 5-seed models (0.629/0.626/0.630 for ESM-2 150M/ProtT5-XL/Ankh-base), all far above the gene-mean null of 0.500 — indicating the layer-contrast mechanism is a depth-dependent property of sequence PLMs generally, not specific to one model or cancelled at scale (Supplementary Figure S5; Table S_PLM). **(iii) Layer insensitivity**: all L_mid ∈ {5,10,15,20,25} achieve 0.717–0.725 (within run-to-run variance), confirming the signal is a stable manifold property, not an artefact of a specific layer pair.

To establish that v17f*'s within-gene score variation reflects genuine isoform-level structural differences rather than noise, we computed the genome-wide correlation between within-gene v17f* score variance and within-gene δ_layer norm variance across 6,106 multi-isoform genes in the brain test set: Spearman ρ = **0.707** (p<10⁻³⁰⁰; partial ρ>0.82 controlling for isoform count) — genes with diverse v17f* scores are genes whose ESM-2 representations are structurally isoform-diverse. Localisation (ρ=0.031, p=0.005) and RNA features (ρ=0.063, p=6×10⁻⁹) show negligible correlation by contrast, establishing ESM-2 sequence representation — not secondary annotation — as the source of discrimination.

Extended to the full 279-term GO ontology with identical hyperparameters, v17f* achieves BP 0.664 (95% CI: 0.649–0.680; Δ = +0.292 over PRISM) and CC 0.617 (95% CI: 0.600–0.634; Δ = +0.250), confirming that the layer-contrast depth signal is a domain-general property of ESM-2 150M. Under domain-matched training (279 terms trained within the same GO domain), 158/168 (94%) of previously intractable H2 terms recover to AUPRC ≥ 0.50; the 10 residually hard terms uniformly correspond to functions of rare cell types (olfactory neurons, sperm, endothelial cells) underrepresented in the training corpus, establishing corpus coverage — not fundamental sequence-encodability — as the binding constraint.

### 4b. Within-gene isoform-resolution evidence: the metric structurally immune to gene-identity confounds

Macro AUPRC — the headline metric reported above — is dominated by gene-family identification rather than isoform-specific discrimination: a gene-mean oracle — a classifier trained and tested on gene-mean embeddings (each isoform's ESM-2 embedding replaced by its parent gene's mean before training), the identical recipe applied to both tissues — achieves AUPRC 0.803 (held-out muscle) and 0.795 (true brain zero-shot), exceeding v17f*'s 0.734 (muscle) and 0.647 (brain) respectively (§Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC). These gene-mean-of-representation figures collapse the model's within-gene variation entirely and are near-identical across tissues (0.803 vs 0.795), whereas v17f* falls further behind on the harder zero-shot brain fold. The GO labels themselves are gene-level in both tissues (every isoform inherits its parent gene's gene2go annotation; on brain we confirmed label-constancy across all 996,874 term × multi-isoform-gene blocks), so a still simpler label-identity lookup — assigning each isoform its parent gene's annotation directly — attains a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 on either tissue. Crucially, this same perfect-scoring lookup is forced to exactly chance (Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.500) on the within-gene task below, because it assigns every isoform of a gene the identical score: the two metrics are orthogonal, and macro AUPRC's gene-level ceiling carries no isoform-resolution information. Because a gene-identity oracle is mathematically guaranteed to win on any gene-level-labelled test set, macro AUPRC cannot by itself demonstrate isoform-level discrimination. We therefore report two metrics constructed to be structurally immune to the gene-identity confound, since both contrast isoforms that share the same gene label and so cannot be solved by gene recognition alone.

**Domain-Ranking AUC.** For every multi-isoform gene, we tested whether v17f* ranks the domain-complete isoform above domain-truncated isoforms of the same gene. A gene-mean classifier is mathematically forced to a tie (AUC = 0.500 by construction, since both isoforms receive an identical score) — this is not an empirically weak baseline but a provable null. v17f* achieves Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] (B = 500 gene-level bootstrap) on the held-out muscle fold, a +0.130 gap over the gene-mean null that cannot be produced by any gene-level classifier regardless of its macro AUPRC. The same metric computed directly on the true-brain zero-shot set (n = 6,565 within-gene domain-differentiated pairs) is higher still, Domain-Ranking AUC = 0.775, against the identical 0.500 gene-mean null — so the within-gene domain-ranking signal not only survives but strengthens under genuine cross-tissue transfer. (We report the brain figure as a point estimate: unlike the muscle fold, its bootstrap is computationally prohibitive at brain scale, but the comparison null is 0.500 *exactly and by construction* — a gene-mean classifier assigns tied within-gene scores — not an empirically sampled baseline, so rejecting it does not depend on a sampled interval; the brain estimate moreover rests on more evaluation pairs than muscle's, 6,565 vs 3,542, so is no less stable.)

**UniProt isoform-pair direction accuracy.** On a curated benchmark of 51 UniProt/Swiss-Prot reviewed isoform pairs (48 evaluable) spanning kinase domain loss, large truncations, transcription-factor TAD loss, and E3 ubiquitin ligase domain architecture, v17f* achieves 11/11 (100%) direction accuracy for pairs with a prediction gap ≥ 0.10 (permutation p < 0.001, n = 10,000); all eleven involve complete structural domain loss or major truncation. The 34 pairs with gap < 0.05 show near-chance accuracy, consistent with quantitative regulatory differences that ESM-2 mean-pooling does not resolve rather than with model failure on an arbitrary subset. Sequence-length difference between pair members shows no correlation with direction accuracy (Spearman ρ = +0.019, p = 0.91, n = 45 pairs with length data), ruling out length as a confound for the gap-stratified accuracy pattern.

Together, Domain-Ranking AUC and UniProt direction accuracy constitute the paper's primary isoform-resolution evidence: both are constructed so that a gene-identity classifier is provably at chance, and both show v17f* substantially exceeds that chance level. Macro AUPRC (0.734 muscle / 0.647 brain) should be read as the gene-family-identification context in which this isoform-resolution signal is embedded, not as the isoform-resolution claim itself.

### 4c. Anatomical dissection of ESM-2's isoform representation: what is encoded, what mean-pooling suppresses, and what is absent

The results above establish *that* PRISM resolves isoforms at the domain level; this section dissects *why*, by decomposing where in ESM-2's 30-layer trajectory the isoform-discriminating signal resides, what biological properties its principal axes encode, and where the representation reaches its ceiling. These are post-hoc anatomical analyses performed after the δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 architecture was already empirically validated by ablation (§4): they are offered as a mechanistic account of why the architecture works, not as a prospective derivation that predicted the L15 contrast layer in advance (layer selection was an empirical sweep over L_mid ∈ {5,10,15,20,25}, all of which performed within run-to-run variance; §Methods). Three analyses converge on a three-tier account of ESM-2's isoform representation: information that is encoded and surfaced in the final embedding, information that is encoded but suppressed by mean-pooling, and information that is not encoded at all.

The full anatomical dissection underlying this synthesis — the per-layer Fisher-peak distribution, the within-gene layer-divergence analysis, the eight joint-PCA axis-biology interpretation and its gene-family conditioning control, the causal-occlusion double dissociation and axis-0 leakage account, the capacity/supervision bracketing of the Domain-Ranking ceiling, and a per-residue dissection of the tier-iii motif ceiling — is provided in **Supplementary Note S_dissection**; the three-tier synthesis below states its conclusions.

**Synthesis: a three-tier account of what ESM-2 encodes at isoform resolution.** These analyses, together with the motif-level boundary quantified in the Discussion (§Scope), delineate three tiers of ESM-2's isoform representation. **(i) Encoded and surfaced at the final layer** — domain-level architecture, which PRISM reads directly from φ_L30 to achieve the Domain-Ranking AUC 0.630 and UniProt 11/11 direction accuracy of §4b. **(ii) Encoded but suppressed by mean-pooling** — the mid-layer-concentrated discriminative signal that the L30 mean-pool integrates over, surfaced by the δ_layer layer-contrast (Fisher-peak distribution; within-gene mid-layer divergence peak). **(iii) Not resolved by PRISM's mean-pooled architecture** — the functional direction of short-linear-motif differences (§Scope: same-length isoform pairs differing only by SLiMs receive statistically indistinguishable PRISM scores). A per-residue dissection (Supplementary Note S_dissection) refines the source of this ceiling: the underlying sequence perturbation is locally encoded in per-residue ESM-2 embeddings near the splice junction, but whole-sequence mean-pooling dilutes it, and — unlike domain completeness, which has a single learnable common anchor that a local-pooling architecture can partially recover (preliminary, n=14) — motif-level function is mechanism-heterogeneous and currently lacks any common reference direction to pool toward. The same-length contrast still makes tiers (i)/(ii) and (iii) complementary for PRISM's current architecture: at fixed sequence length, isoforms differing in domain content diverge strongly in the pooled ESM-2 trajectory, whereas isoforms differing only in motif content do not — but tier (iii)'s ceiling is now attributed to pooling-and-anchor availability rather than to an absence of encoding. The geometric reason whole-protein mean-pooling separates these tiers is that averaging over residues acts as a *spatial-coherence filter*: a domain-scale change perturbs a spatially coherent block of residues whose per-residue embedding shifts add constructively and therefore survive to the pooled φ_L30 (tier i), whereas a short-linear-motif change perturbs only a few, spatially scattered residues whose incoherent shifts partially cancel under averaging and are diluted below the pooled signal (tier iii). This coherence-filter mechanism — not a length- or count-based low-pass — is what makes domain completeness poolable and motif direction not. Supplementary Note S_dissection formalises this quantitatively: the retained energy fraction converges to a length-independent constant (the mean pairwise directional coherence c̄) rather than decaying with edit or protein length as a low-pass account would predict.

### 4d. The representational boundary of isoform resolution: an intrinsic ESM-2 ceiling, and where it lies

The preceding sections establish that ESM-2 encodes isoform-discriminating signal (§1, §4c) and that PRISM extracts it into calibrated GO predictions (§2, §4) with genuine within-gene resolution (§4b). We now state the paper's central result, which the remaining sections and the Discussion substantiate in detail: this isoform-resolution capability is bounded by a ceiling intrinsic to the ESM-2 representation, and that ceiling is itself describable.

Five convergent lines fix its nature. **First, the within-gene signal is intrinsic to ESM-2, not manufactured by training:** a training-free per-GO-term embedding-centroid baseline attains the same within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC as the trained model (0.638 vs. 0.630), while the identical centroid method collapses to macro AUPRC 0.099 (chance prevalence 0.032) — an order of magnitude below PRISM's 0.734. Task training therefore buys calibrated macro prediction, itself a gene-identity measure (gene-mean oracle 0.803; a label-identity oracle scores a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 while being forced to exactly chance, DR-AUC 0.500, on within-gene ranking; §4b), not isoform resolution. **Second, the ceiling cannot be lifted by modelling:** three mechanistically distinct interventions — added MLP head capacity, an explicit within-gene domain-ranking auxiliary loss, and per-GO Fisher-peak layer selection (including a label-leakage oracle) — each fail to raise within-gene DR-AUC (§6, §6b, §Discussion), localizing the limit to the representation rather than the readout. **Third, the ceiling is describable in domain terms:** mapping every within-gene splice onto Pfam domain envelopes, the majority of within-gene variation (69.8% brain, 53.5% muscle) overlaps a structured domain and is already saturated by a single ESM-2 layer, which is why the layer contrast and the interventions above add nothing to it. **Fourth, the residual is a label limit, not a representational one:** the non-domain minority (30.2% brain) — smaller, more disordered, N-terminally-enriched targeting/NMD edits — remains structurally encoded (disorder decodes at AUROC 0.79) and, unlike the domain signal, is *not* single-layer-saturated (a later layer significantly improves its decoding across splice size), leaving representational headroom no gene-level label can supervise toward function. **Fifth, adding predicted 3D structure does not obviously bypass the ceiling:** in a pilot structural audit (ESMFold single-sequence; 40 non-domain and 30 domain-affecting brain edits, ≤350 aa), the isoform-distinguishing edited regions fold at only low-to-moderate confidence *regardless* of domain overlap (median per-residue pLDDT 58.0 non-domain vs. 55.3 domain-affecting; MWU p=0.79; only 30–40% exceed pLDDT 70), even though surrounding proteins fold confidently (whole-protein pLDDT often >80) — since structure-aware PLMs need confident structural input, they would receive unreliable structure precisely at the splice junctions where discrimination occurs, so the ceiling is unlikely an artefact of missing structural input (`hMuscle/model/exp_stage0_esmfold_pldct_audit.py`; a pilot, not a full-scale benchmark).

**A fifth line makes the residual's headroom concrete and shows why it cannot cross into functional labelling.** Recomputing per-residue ESM-2 embeddings for equal-domain-count within-gene pairs and pooling the inter-isoform difference over the splice-*edited* interval (rather than the whole protein) recovers a cross-gene-consistent direction that whole-protein mean-pooling destroys: for internal edits, gene-disjoint direction-consistency rises from chance (0.481±0.004) to 0.739±0.005 (30 fold-seeds), specific to the edited residues — length-matched scrambled-region pools reach only 0.56–0.63 (a domain-differing positive control rises in parallel, 0.708→0.844). The non-domain residual is thus *encoded but mean-pool-diluted*, not absent, decomposing by edit location: the N-terminally-enriched targeting subclass (NDUFAF5/NDUFS8) carries a weak but robust mitochondrial-presequence signature after partialling out length and hydrophobicity (partial ρ=0.126, incremental R²=1.2%, p=9×10⁻⁴; net-charge and acidic-depletion components survive Bonferroni), absent for internal edits (ρ=0.008, n.s., no proxy tested aligns). Critically, this recovered coherence is *anti-correlated* with functional labelling: the region-pooling that raises within-gene direction coherence *lowers* the per-GO Domain-Ranking AUC relative to mean-pooling (paired Δ=−0.11, 95% CI [−0.18,−0.03]), because concentrating the local edit discards the whole-protein context a functional centroid requires. A global–local blend does not rescue this: sweeping the mixing weight (α∈{0.0,...,1.0}) traces a DR-AUC highest near pure mean-pooling, with the best interior blend statistically indistinguishable from mean-pooling alone (α=0.9: 0.823 vs. α=1.0: 0.815, Δ=+0.008, 95% CI [−0.036,+0.066]) — and this DR-AUC-optimal blend recovers only 22% of the coherence gap between mean-pooling (0.715) and pure region-pooling (0.831) on within-pair direction accuracy (0.740), trading away most coherence gain for a labelling gain it does not obtain. No linear combination of the two poolings recovers both properties at once (non-linear fusion is not excluded). The residual thus supplies a coherent embedding direction but no isoform-resolved label to supervise it — a concrete pooling recipe (splice-interval region-pool) that retrieves within-gene *direction* where curated *function* remains out of reach.

The boundary is therefore two-sided: macro AUPRC is capped by gene-constant labels, and within-gene resolution is capped by representational saturation of the domain-describable signal, leaving a bounded, label-undescribable non-domain remainder. §6 shows that this boundary transfers across tissues — the splice-size mechanism predicts the muscle-to-brain non-domain contrast — and §§6–8 show that the biologically consequential isoform switches in Alzheimer's disease brain fall squarely in the label-undescribable class the boundary identifies, which is precisely why a sequence-first framework is required to detect them. The complete boundary — the stage-by-stage information-flow cascade, the per-axis usage map, and the decomposition of the non-domain remainder into a per-pair floor and a reproducible tail now characterised in kind (positional/contextual structure) but not in function — is summarised as an integrated map in **Supplementary Figure S_map** (§Discussion).

### 5. PRISM predicts non-overlapping functional space: 92.3% of predictions unavailable from InterProScan

A critical question is whether PRISM simply recapitulates what domain-based tools already annotate. We compared PRISM predictions with InterProScan+pfam2go across 26 BISECT-validated isoform switch cases. InterProScan+pfam2go maps domain matches (Pfam, PRINTS, ProSite) to **Molecular Function (MF)** GO terms: motor activity (kinesin domain), cytoskeletal binding (spectrin domain), PDZ-domain binding. PRISM predicts **Biological Process (BP)** terms: microtubule-based movement, synaptic transmission, actin-based movement. These two annotation layers address complementary facets of protein function (domain-level activity vs. cellular process context), and **24 of 26 BISECT-validated cases (92.3%)** carry a PRISM BP prediction not covered by any pfam2go BP mapping for that isoform, establishing non-redundant rather than competing annotation. This comparison is not confounded by ontological differences: we tested pfam2go BP entries specifically (not only MF), and 10 of the 18 PRISM training GO terms lack any pfam2go BP entry, confirming that the non-overlap reflects a genuine coverage gap rather than an ontological mismatch. Furthermore, PRISM covers all isoforms including the 7,899 brain NIC/NNIC sequences (12.3%) that carry no recognisable Pfam domains and thus receive no InterProScan annotation; among these, 541 (6.8%) score >0.5 for at least one of 73 brain-relevant BP GO terms. These findings motivate a three-layer annotation framework: InterProScan (domain-level MF) → PRISM (sequence-encoded BP) → BISECT (multi-evidence causal support).

**Beyond gene-level annotation: isoforms acquiring GO terms absent from their parent gene's label set.** A stronger test than within-gene discrimination is whether any isoform's PRISM score crosses threshold for a GO term gene2go does not assign to that gene at all. Using the true-brain zero-shot score matrix (63,994 isoforms, 18 GO BP terms; §Methods), we identify two cases: **Case A** (fully-unannotated gene has a positive-scoring isoform): 378/15,858 genes (2.38%; 706 isoforms, 814 instances). **Case B** (partially-annotated gene has an isoform scoring higher on a foreign term than on its own annotated terms): 64/2,656 genes (2.41%; 113 isoforms, 151 instances). Both rates are noise-indistinguishable from appropriate nulls (Case A: 0.087% vs. 0.093% genome-wide background; Case B: observed 151 vs. a within-isoform column-shuffle null of 6,394±32, i.e. 197 SD *below* chance) — PRISM's scores concentrate on each isoform's own annotated terms, the opposite of an inflated novel-discovery signal. We therefore do not interpret this as systematic novel-function discovery, and instead ask how many of the individual occurring instances reflect real biology. Screening the 104 candidate pairs against UniProt/QuickGO experimental evidence (§Methods), **23/104 (22.1%)** are literature-supported (Table S_novelGO), stratified by specificity of the gene–process link: **Type A** (65.2%) — near-definitional core function transferred to an untrained GO category (e.g. CACNA1D, an L-type Ca²⁺ channel, scoring for Ca²⁺ homeostasis); **Type B** (21.7%) — genuinely documented but non-headline biology (e.g. CTNND2/δ-catenin's dendritic-spine role scored against synaptic transmission); **Type C** (13.0%) — a weak, generic mechanistic link only (Table 1). A matched-null test (§Methods) confirms this 22.1% exceeds chance: against automated GO-DAG-closure nulls, the PRISM-selected tail is enriched **9–14× over matched random screening** (empirical p < 0.001 under all three closeness definitions), with gene-level annotation richness contributing only ≈1.2× of this and term-specific correspondence ≈10× — ruling out the concern that Table 1 is a cherry-picked artefact. The phenomenon remains numerically bounded (104 pairs, ≈2.4% of gene-negative instances) and is reported as a validated instance of rare, beyond-gene-label true positives, not a general-purpose isoform-resolution metric.

The largest quantitative isoform differentials illustrate why BP-level scores are informative where MF-level domain status is binary. In the DLG1 locus, the canonical isoform (906 aa; PDZ1/2/3, SH3, GK domains) scores **0.88** for synaptic transmission (GO:0007268); a novel NNIC isoform (tr319500, 186 aa; no PDZ domains) scores **0.033** — a **27-fold differential** (Fig. 3B). PDZ domains mediate direct AMPA/NMDA receptor binding; their absence is binary in domain space (present/absent) but PRISM converts this to a continuous functional score capturing graded functional capacity. For NDUFS4, the canonical isoform (NDUFS4-201, 175 aa, MTS present) scores **0.587** for mitochondrial organisation (GO:0007005); the AD-enriched isoform (NDUFS4-204, 379 aa, MTS absent) scores **0.012** — a **50-fold differential** derived from sequence alone, without any explicit mitochondrial targeting annotation input (Fig. 3C).

To establish the most stringent domain-based baseline, we trained a data-driven logistic regression on binary Pfam domain presence (512 domains) across all 82 MF GO terms — an upper bound on pfam2go, since it optimises domain→GO mappings directly rather than using fixed rules. Domain LR achieves macro AUPRC **0.1625** versus PRISM's **0.5962** (gap +0.4337, PRISM wins 82/82 terms), reflecting the coverage constraint that only 19.1% of isoforms carry any Pfam domain; PRISM exceeds domain LR even on the seven terms (motor activity, ATPase activity, metal/nucleotide binding) where domain-function correspondence and coverage are highest. Evaluated zero-shot on the held-out muscle fold (81/82 evaluable terms), domain LR degrades further to **0.0319** against PRISM's **0.5912** (gap +0.5593, 81/81 terms). Combined with the 18-term BP result (§2: +0.605), the PRISM advantage over domain-based tools is consistent across GO categories and panels, not an artefact of the 18-term muscle biology panel.

On the true-brain zero-shot test set (63,994 isoforms; 82 valid MF terms), domain LR degrades further to macro AUPRC **0.0298** while v17f* reaches **0.6471** (gap +0.6174, PRISM wins 82/82 terms) — a 5.45-fold collapse from the muscle-trained baseline (0.1625), against v17f*'s own 11.9% drop (0.734→0.647, §6). Pfam domain features are tissue-agnostic while domain-function correspondence is muscle-biology-enriched, exposing the ceiling imposed by 19.1% domain coverage on the broader, less muscle-biased brain GO space; ESM-2's continuous embeddings, by contrast, encode tissue-transferable structural features — direct empirical evidence that PLM representation outperforms domain annotation by richer feature encoding, not merely optimised domain-function mapping.

**Comparative analysis.** We benchmarked ten methods against v17f* on the true-brain zero-shot set (63,994 isoforms, 82 valid MF terms), in two categories: matched-training (k-NN retrieval, gene-mean retrieval, PRISM D0/D1, v17f, v17f*) and pre-trained-tool deployment (DeepFRI, DeepGoPlus, evaluated out-of-distribution without retraining, since matched retraining was computationally infeasible — DeepFRI requires PDB contact maps, DeepGoPlus requires DIAMOND-index reconstruction). DIFFUSE [26] is compared qualitatively only, for the architectural reason given in the Introduction. Gene-mean retrieval (k=5 centroids) reaches 0.404 (L2_Structural 0.247); isoform-level k-NN reaches 0.561 (0.458); both remain below v17f* (0.647/0.535, +0.086/+0.077 over k-NN), confirming that proximity-based retrieval cannot substitute for task-specific training even under genuine cross-tissue transfer. **DeepFRI** (LSTM-LM+CNN, PDB/Swiss-Model-trained, sequence-only mode; GCN mode excluded as it would require AlphaFold2 structures at prohibitive scale) achieves only 0.103/0.026 — 6.3×/20× below v17f* — reflecting a training-distribution mismatch (canonical PDB proteins vs. novel long-read splice isoforms) rather than an architectural deficiency; this holds even restricting to DeepFRI's 65/82 covered terms (0.116). **DeepGoPlus** (CNN+DIAMOND homology, α=0.66) reaches 0.429/0.350 — better than DeepFRI via its homology component, but still 0.218/0.185 below v17f* (1.51× gap). **BLAST→GOA** (DIAMOND blastp + Swiss-Prot GOA transfer), by contrast, *exceeds* v17f* (0.788/0.728 vs. 0.647/0.535, +0.140/+0.194) — but this is a gene-identification ceiling, not isoform-specific prediction: the top DIAMOND hit for any isoform is invariably its own gene's canonical Swiss-Prot entry, whose gene-level GOA annotations transfer wholesale onto a gene-level ground truth. An isopretEM-style within-gene EM refinement (bitscore-weighted redistribution) converges to the identical 0.788, confirming no discriminative signal survives when both the prediction and the evaluation labels are gene-level. BLAST→GOA and isopretEM therefore serve as this test set's gene-identification upper bound, not as isoform-aware baselines (full methodology and per-term breakdown: Figure S1, Table S2b).

**Isoform discrimination capability.** Gene-level AUPRC rewards gene identification, not within-gene differentiation. We computed three within-gene metrics — intra-gene CV, length-AUC (score correlation with isoform length, a domain-completeness proxy), and top-isoform BLAST agreement — on both held-out muscle (7,459 genes) and true-brain zero-shot (10,046 genes; Supplementary Table S3/S3b). On muscle, PRISM v17f* is the most internally consistent method (CV 0.100 vs. BLAST 0.162) and near-length-agnostic (length-AUC 0.560 vs. random 0.502, vs. BLAST's 0.785), agreeing with BLAST's top isoform in only 47.7% of cases (vs. gene-mean 64.2%, k-NN 55.5%) — while BLAST assigns identical scores across all isoforms for 30.6% of genes, a structural inability to discriminate. On true-brain, PRISM remains most internally consistent (CV 0.214, versus BLAST's 0.465 — now statistically indistinguishable from random, 0.484 — i.e. BLAST's within-gene spread is noise in brain) but becomes markedly more length-sensitive (length-AUC 0.560→0.718), a genuine tissue-asymmetry effect (§6: brain splicing is majority domain-affecting, 69.8% vs. muscle 53.5%, making length a better domain-completeness proxy there) rather than a metric artefact — we report this as an honest revision, not a tissue-invariant claim. Top-isoform agreement collapses toward convergence in brain (46.4%/45.7%/42.6%) as BLAST's own within-gene ranking loses discriminative content. Together, these metrics establish that PRISM and BLAST are complementary: BLAST's gene-level AUPRC rests on gene identification, while PRISM's within-gene predictions remain the most internally consistent and least reducible to coverage-based ranking in both tissues.

**Functional specialization architecture across multi-isoform genes.** Standard annotation assigns all isoforms of a gene identical GO labels; we tested whether PRISM's isoform-level predictions reveal genuine intra-gene specialization, across 2,738 multi-isoform genes (≥3 isoforms, ≥2 GO MF terms; 17,637 isoforms), using a Functional Specialization Index (FSI, 1 − mean pairwise Spearman correlation across GO terms) and top-isoform consistency. Three architectures emerge: **canonical-dominant** (S1, 33.8%; n=926) — one isoform carries all functions (correlation 0.978, consistency 0.995); **isoform-specialized** (S2, 42.4%; n=1,161) — different isoforms top different GO terms, anti-correlated (−0.244); **mixed** (23.8%; n=651) — a generalist plus specialist isoforms. S2, the numerically dominant class, indicates isoform functional diversification is the rule, not the exception. Among 2,091 S2 mode-top GO-term wins, 74.6% are motif-dependent (structural compactness does not distinguish the term) and 25.4% domain-dependent; 71.4% of mode-top isoforms share the same Pfam domain set as competitors, so specialization frequently occurs *within* identical domain architectures via sequence-level binding-specificity variation, with domain-dependent wins showing larger score gaps (0.0334 vs. 0.0208, p=0.061). This generalist–specialist architecture extends to Mixed genes (30.9% of 524 specialist pairs show the same protein-binding-divergence pattern as S2) and, across all 7,459 multi-isoform genes, the S2/Mixed rates (21.8%/10.3%, diluted by the 30.7% of genes with only one annotated term) still show 12.4% of newly-included genes exhibiting the same pattern — confirming the signal is not an artefact of the analytically well-powered gene subset.

**Within-gene pos_bias across the full GO ontology.** Muscle-trained PRISM (v15d) shows significant pos_bias (elevated within-gene score variance among positive-class genes vs. random) in 11/13 tested GO terms (B=1,000 bootstrap; shuffled-label null floor 0.24). Extending v17f* zero-shot to the full 278-term expanded ontology in true brain (82 MF + 103 BP + 93 CC; B=1,000 bootstrap, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR), pos_bias is significant in **81/82 MF** (mean 1.671 vs. shuffled floor 0.217, 7.7×), **103/103 BP** (1.686 vs. 0.261, 6.5×), and **93/93 CC** (1.273 vs. 0.265, 4.8×) terms — near-universal significance (277/278) confirming the within-gene discrimination mechanism is a general property of ESM-2-based PRISM training, independent of GO domain, term-set complexity, and (evaluated here on genuinely cross-tissue brain isoforms) tissue origin of the test data.

### 6. Cross-tissue zero-shot transfer to Alzheimer's disease brain

We deployed PRISM zero-shot to 63,994 prefrontal cortex isoforms from the Samsung AD single-cell long-read RNA-seq cohort (13 AD, 8 control donors; 8 resolved cell types) without any retraining. PRISM achieves **macro AUPRC 0.600** across 18 GO terms (range 0.403–0.720), compared to macro AUPRC 0.098 for domain-based annotation on the same brain isoform set (Fig. 4B). The 0.102-point reduction from muscle AUPRC (−14.5% relative) traces to a specific functional subset: GPCR signalling, potassium transport, and immune-related GO terms — lacking functional analogs in PRISM's muscle training set — achieve near-chance performance (0.403–0.467). For GO terms with functional muscle-brain overlap (synaptic transmission, neuron projection development, Ca²⁺ homeostasis, mitochondrial organisation), performance is maintained at 0.567–0.720, consistent with transfer of shared sequence-function associations.

We separately evaluated the layer-contrast **v17f*** architecture (§4) directly on the true-brain isoform set: macro AUPRC **0.647** (82 MF terms), an 0.087 drop from muscle's 0.734. This decomposes cleanly against the gene-mean oracle: gene-level signal transfers near-losslessly (oracle 0.803→0.795, −0.008), so the residual ≈0.079 is isoform-level macro signal lost in transfer — while, tellingly, the within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC *rises* over the same transfer (0.630→0.775, +0.145), the double dissociation of §4b operating under transfer. On brain the δ_layer contrast confers **no advantage over a single ESM-2 layer**: concat[φ_{L30}, δ_layer] reaches DR-AUC 0.745 versus 0.762 for the best single layer (φ_{L15}, Δ=−0.018; production v17f* scores 0.775 on this pair set, §4b). Three further interventions — added MLP capacity, an explicit within-gene domain-ranking auxiliary loss, and per-GO Fisher-peak layer selection (including a label-leakage oracle using brain's own peaks) — all fail to lift brain DR-AUC above this single-layer ceiling (oracle gain=−0.034). The proximal cause is layer misalignment (muscle-optimal L15/L30 vs. brain's deeper Fisher peaks, median shift L15→L20); the distal cause is that brain isoforms diverge ~2.8× more within-gene in ESM-2 δ-space (median ‖δ‖ 13.1 vs. 4.7, at matched between-gene/embedding-norm scale), driven by ~3.5× larger splicing events (median 184 vs. 53 changed residues — differences coincide once splice size is matched). Mapping every within-gene splice onto Pfam domain envelopes (2,179 brain two-isoform genes) confirms this directly: **69.8%** of brain within-gene splices are domain-affecting (median 193 residues), already saturating a single layer (DR 0.762) — the ceiling is *representational saturation of an already-encoded signal*, not a missing one, which is why added capacity/supervision/layer-selection cannot improve it. The genuinely label-undescribable **30.2%** minority is domain-preserving, smaller (median 53 residues), more disordered (TOP-IDP 0.138 vs. 0.104), and N-terminally enriched (half within the first 60 residues, the targeting-sequence window) — the signature of NMD, targeting, and disorder-region splices the domain/GO vocabulary cannot score. This gap scales inversely with splice size: on muscle, where within-gene splices are smaller (median 42 non-domain / 105 domain-affecting), the non-domain rate rises to 46.5% and the domain rate (53.5%) barely clears its random-placement null (49.4%, +4 points) versus brain's 69.8% (null 49.2%, +21 points) — near-identical nulls confirm this is driven by splice size, not domain density. These non-domain splices are not inert: the study's own confirmed brain switches include an NMD event (Tier A-DR NDUFAF5, 345→267 aa, PTC-bearing, no domain lost) and an N-terminal targeting change (NDUFS8), both functionally consequential yet domain-vocabulary-invisible — a class BISECT's Pfam-centred modules structurally undercount. The picture is two-sided: macro-AUPRC's ceiling is a genuine **label limitation** (gene-constant annotation makes the gene-identity oracle perfect; §4b), while within-gene Domain-Ranking's ceiling reflects **representational saturation** of the domain-describable majority — leaving the ~30% non-domain minority as the true label-**describability** gap: encoded, undescribed, and bounded rather than dominant. We verified the "encoded" half directly: on equal-domain-count muscle pairs (n=1,505, median splice 58 aa), inter-isoform embedding difference decodes disorder-region edits at AUROC 0.79 — and unlike the domain signal (already single-layer-saturated), this signature is *not* saturated: a later layer significantly improves decoding at every splice-size bin (+0.058 [+0.033, +0.084] AUROC in the size-matched 40–175 aa window), locating the ceiling precisely as a *label* problem, not a representational one — ESM-2 encodes more about these edits than any gene-level label can supervise toward function.

Task-specific training adds substantial functional organisation beyond the PLM alone: PRISM's 18-dim output outperforms raw ESM-2 by **9-fold** for neuron projection development (0.567 vs. 0.063) and **10.6-fold** for Ca²⁺ homeostasis (0.447 vs. 0.042); a logistic regression on raw ESM-2 features reaches only macro AUPRC 0.112 on brain, confirming the discriminative signal requires task-specific extraction — and that PRISM's representations encode tissue-invariant sequence-function relationships rather than tissue-specific expression patterns.

To dissect the mechanism of this transfer reduction across the full 279-term expanded panel (n=61 transfer-success, n=185 transfer-failure), we tested three hypotheses: **H1** (tissue bias) is refuted for 274/279 terms (only 1.8% show a high-muscle/low-brain pattern); **H2** (ESM-2 hard — GO positivity depends on molecular context not encoded in sequence, e.g. PTM state, interaction partners) accounts for **203/279 (72.8%)**, concentrated in chromatin organisation, PTM-dependent signalling, complex assembly, and cell-cycle regulation; **H3** (positive-set size) contributes modestly (ρ=0.250, p=2.5×10⁻⁵) but is not determinative. A logistic regression across five predictors confirms muscle AUPRC as the dominant predictor of transfer success — terms that succeed in muscle transfer well, terms that fail predict brain failure — establishing that the primary bottleneck is ESM-2's intrinsic embedding separability for these functions, not tissue specificity of the training distribution.

### 6b. Domain-envelope magnitude and structural encoding of Pfam differences between muscle and brain

To test whether the tissues differ not just in *rate* but in *magnitude* of domain-architecture consequence, we regressed a continuous representational-severity score on five structural properties across 48,641 canonical-anchored pairs (15,493 muscle, 33,148 brain): all five are significant after gene-clustered correction (pooled R²=0.119, permutation null≈0, p<0.001), confirming the score is isoform-pair-intrinsic. Domain-architecture change mirrors the rate-based finding (§6): larger effect in brain (β=2.11) than muscle (β=1.53). Two covariates that initially appeared muscle-specific (N-terminal overlap, alignment-resync failure) do not survive confound-controlled mediation testing (**Supplementary Note S_severity**: N-terminal collapses to chance once size is controlled; resync-failure is a downstream trace of size, itself 89.2%-explained by edit fragmentation, with no GO enrichment). Disorder-region overlap shows a smaller representational footprint after adjusting for size (muscle β=−6.69, brain β=−4.07). A separate direction-level probe of the non-domain residual (four amino-acid-propensity descriptors, 4,185 muscle/11,666 brain pairs) finds a real but gene-independent orientation (CV-dir-acc 0.59–0.65, collapsing to its gene-permutation null in both tissues) that does not propagate to PRISM's output (0.53–0.58, again null-indistinguishable) — encoded and weakly surfaced, but neither a cross-gene anchor nor used by the model. Genome-wide, domain-different pairs show 1.80× larger |ΔPRISM score| than same-domain pairs (p=9×10⁻²²³), a linear probe on raw ESM-2 achieves AUROC=0.860 for domain presence, and ridge regression across 83 BISECT cases identifies domain-count change as the dominant driver (β₁=0.114 ≫ β₂=0.081 ≫ β₃=0.021 for MTS-loss/alt-promoter). Finally, adding an explicit domain-count auxiliary loss to training — rather than improving Domain-Ranking AUC — degrades it at every tested weight (AUPRC 0.734→0.601/0.497/0.455; DR-AUC 0.630→0.626, still below the 0.638 centroid baseline), confirming PRISM's domain-ranking capability is an intrinsic consequence of GO-function learning from PLM embeddings, not something explicit structural supervision can improve — one of the three orthogonal interventions (§4d) that fail to raise the representational ceiling.
### 7. BISECT identifies a Complex I disruption cascade led by an NDUFAF5–NDUFS7 enzyme–substrate pair in AD brain

BISECT integrates 15 evidence modules (Pfam domain change, PPI disruption, NMD prediction, AlphaFold structural confidence, phyloConservation, localization signal change, exon structure classification, convergence detection, regulatory context) into a six-tier evidence hierarchy — Tier A-DR (DRIMSeq genome-wide), A-BPC (Bonferroni-corrected), A-BPP (nominal + structural support, replication-required), B (independently replicated), C (exploratory), D (hypothesis-generating); full module and tier definitions in §Methods. Applied to 138 candidate switch pairs across 6 cell types, BISECT identifies 101 PASS cases meeting Stage 2 structural criteria.

Donor-level DRIMSeq quasi-Dirichlet-Multinomial regression (13 AD, 8 control donors; stageR two-stage OFDR ≤ 5%) identifies **15 Tier A-DR genes across six cell types**: excitatory neurons (n=5; NDUFAF5, NDUFB10, CNOT11, AZIN1, ZNF582), inhibitory neurons (n=3; SAMHD1, NOL8, DIS3), oligodendrocytes (n=3; USP1, DCAF5, ZCCHC17), microglia (n=2; DOCK10, FANCA), astrocytes (n=2; ZNF268, ERCC6L2) — all passing post-hoc donor consistency filtering (jackknife stability ratio < 0.50, directional consistency ≥ 0.60), with SAMHD1, AZIN1, DOCK10, NDUFAF5, and DCAF5 showing perfect directional consistency (1.00) across all donors.

Targeted permutation testing (n=10,000 donor-label permutations) was applied to N=5 pre-specified BISECT-nominated candidates — four Complex I components (NDUFAF5, NDUFS4, NDUFS7, NDUFS8) and DOCK11 (a DOCK-family GEF nominated in inhibitory neurons by BISECT M17 convergence detection) — each nominated by BISECT structural evidence modules (M1–M9/M17) prior to permutation, not selected post-hoc from permutation results. **Tier A-BPC** (confirmed, Bonferroni-corrected): DOCK11 (inhibitory neurons; perm p=0.0008; Bonferroni p_adj=0.004 for 5 tests; batch-concordant) — the only gene in the panel to survive strict Bonferroni correction. **Tier A-BPP** (preliminary, replication-required): NDUFS8 (inhibitory neurons; perm p=0.004, p_adj=0.022 — above α_corrected=0.010, but retained on batch concordance, a 37% effect size among the panel's largest, and independent, mechanistically non-overlapping BISECT localization evidence: MTS-helix disruption with HMMER-confirmed Fer4 domain retention, Fig. 6B); NDUFS4 (both neuron types; excitatory perm p=0.041, inhibitory perm p=0.024; 5.1%/32% effects; batch-concordant); and **NDUFS7** (excitatory neurons; perm p=0.048; 5.1% effect; batch-concordant), classified on nominal permutation significance combined with its direct enzyme–substrate relationship to NDUFAF5 (obligate hydroxylation substrate at Arg73, disrupted in the same excitatory neuron population) — mechanistically non-overlapping convergent evidence. We note explicitly that NDUFS7 was included in the panel *because* of this documented prior relationship (established in the literature, not discovered here); its classification is therefore **hypothesis-confirmatory rather than hypothesis-free**, and p=0.048 uncorrected alone would not support Tier A-BPP without this prior anchor — this does **not** imply Bonferroni-corrected significance, and NDUFS7 requires independent cohort replication with isoform-level resolution for definitive confirmation. **NDUFS4** additionally carries BISECT-confirmed MTS exon loss (M1/M9 PASS) and directional Ebbert replication, but post-hoc power for its 5.1% excitatory effect is only 8.6% at this n=13/8 cohort design, and a Fisher combination with Ebbert (§8) does not cross the Bonferroni threshold — so NDUFS4 remains **Tier C (exploratory)**. Its original Stage-1 nomination screen (pooled-cell dtu_p=3.62×10⁻⁶) is not comparable to the donor-level permutation result: the Stage-1 test is inflated by cell-level pseudoreplication, whereas the donor-label permutation (the resampling unit is the donor) is the conservative, appropriate cohort-level statistic — the two-order-of-magnitude gap is expected under this distinction, not a discrepancy, and is why NDUFS4 is retained at Tier C rather than promoted. Together with Tier A-DR NDUFAF5, these nominate a **Complex I Q-module disruption cascade** — anchored by the genome-wide-confirmed NDUFAF5 switch and its direct NDUFS7 substrate, with NDUFS8 and NDUFS4 as replication-required nominations rather than confirmed disruptions: NDUFAF5 hydroxylates NDUFS7 at Arg73 as an obligate step in early Q-module intermediate formation ([22]); NDUFAF5 loss-of-function (NMD switch, Tier A-DR, excitatory neurons) → impaired NDUFS7 hydroxylation → defective ~200-kDa Q-module intermediate → downstream assembly failure propagating to NDUFS8 (TYKY subunit, inhibitory neurons) and NDUFS4, required at the terminal step for NDUFA12 to displace assembly chaperone NDUFAF2 ([23]) — affecting both neuron types (Fig. 5C). Whether the NDUFS4, NDUFS7, and NDUFS8 switches are direct consequences of NDUFAF5 loss or independent pathological events **requires future experimental validation**; their convergence on NDUFAF5-dependent Q-module biogenesis is consistent with a cascade rather than four parallel disruptions.

In astrocytes, ERCC6L2 (stageR p=0.011; Fig. 6C) shows an AD-enriched switch to a shorter isoform losing six Pfam domains (DEAD-box helicase core, ERCC3 ATPase, SWI2/SNF2 ATPase, HDA2-3, helicase C-terminal, restriction-enzyme-III fold) that together form the catalytic core of transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair (M1 PASS, domain-loss switch, delta=1.0); the AD-enriched isoform retains only a conserved alternative last exon with no annotated catalytic function. Since ERCC6L2 germline truncations cause TC-NER-deficient aplastic anaemia, this predicts selective loss of helicase-dependent DNA repair in AD astrocytes without disrupting the ALE regulatory output.

BISECT mechanistic analysis distinguishes three switch classes. **NDUFAF5** (methyltransferase-fold hydroxylase) catalyses NDUFS7 Arg73 hydroxylation, required for early Q-module intermediate formation ([22]; the paralogous NDUFAF7 performs the complementary NDUFS2-Arg85 dimethylation, [21]); its AD-dominant isoform (345→267 aa) carries a premature termination codon >2,675 nt upstream of the final exon-junction complex, an NMD-sensitive transcript. Because NDUFS7 is NDUFAF5's direct hydroxylation substrate and independently shows a Tier A-BPP switch in the same excitatory neurons, the two disruptions form a direct enzyme–substrate pair: impaired hydroxylation prevents stable Q-module intermediate formation, propagating to the NDUFS8 subunit switch in inhibitory neurons. **NDUFS4** presents a third class: loss of the MTS-bearing exon 1 (M1/M9 PASS) predicts mitochondrial import failure; since NDUFS4 incorporation is the obligate trigger for NDUFA12 to displace assembly chaperone NDUFAF2 at the terminal maturation step, its loss arrests the complex before the mature holoenzyme forms ([23]) — disrupting both subunit delivery and this final chaperone exchange. **NDUFS8** shows a distinct mechanism: both CT and AD isoforms retain complete Fer4 [4Fe-4S]-binding domains (HMMER E<0.001), but the AD isoform's N-terminus carries a proline-disrupted segment replacing the CT isoform's mitochondrial-targeting amphipathic helix — a localization switch predicting cytoplasmic stranding of otherwise Fe-S-competent protein, mechanistically distinct from the muscle NDUFS8 switch (Fer4 domain loss) yet converging on the same TYKY-subunit depletion in Q-module assembly.

### 7b. Convergent validation: PRISM's independent zero-shot scores corroborate BISECT's structural findings

The convergence of PRISM functional scores with BISECT's multi-evidence pipeline on the same loci provides independent validation of isoform-specific predictions. For NDUFS4, PRISM's 50-fold score differential (above) agrees with BISECT's independent evidence from AlphaFold structural confidence, STRING PPI disruption, and phyloConservation. For DLG1 in brain, PRISM scores DLG1-224 (the PDZ-lacking isoform among 32 DLG1 brain isoforms) at 0.172 versus a median of 0.888 across all other DLG1 isoforms — a 5-fold differential reached by sequence alone, concordant with BISECT's domain-level assessment. These convergent predictions, derived through entirely independent methodological paths, establish the reliability of PRISM's zero-shot functional scores.

A potential alternative explanation for this convergence is that PRISM reproduces gene-level GO labels — assigning high mitochondrial function scores to all isoforms of NDUFS4, NDUFS7, and NDUFS8 by gene identity rather than by sequence-level structural discrimination, and thereby appearing to agree with BISECT without providing independent evidence. Five converging analyses rule out this gene-level classifier hypothesis. First, a gene-mean oracle — the formal gene-level null that assigns each isoform the mean GO score of its parent gene — achieves domain-ranking AUC = 0.500 [0.500, 0.500] by mathematical construction (§Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC); PRISM achieves 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] on held-out muscle and 0.775 directly on these true-brain isoforms (+0.130/+0.275 gap, muscle B=500 bootstrap p < 0.001), which is mathematically inconsistent with gene-level label reproduction. Second, the within-gene CT/AD score ratios (canonical CT isoform / AD-enriched isoform, maximum PRISM GO score across terms) are 4.3× for NDUFS4, 10.3× for NDUFS7, and 3.2× for NDUFS8; under the gene-level classifier null, all three ratios would be exactly 1.00 by definition. Third, LRPPRC — a mitochondria-associated RNA-binding protein directly involved in mitochondrial RNA processing and Complex I-related respiratory chain maintenance — receives identical PRISM scores for its CT and AD isoforms (CT = AD = 0.428; ratio = 1.00), despite belonging to the same mitochondrial gene category as NDUFS4/7/8; PRISM does not elevate scores for mitochondrial genes when domain architecture is unchanged, ruling out categorical gene-function labelling. Fourth, among the 24 Tier-3 BISECT genes — isoform switch candidates lacking structural domain-loss evidence — 23/24 show CT = AD (ratio = 1.00), while BISECT PASS domain-loss genes (n = 41) show a mean CT/AD ratio of 4.96×; this categorical pattern, aligned precisely with structural evidence, is incompatible with gene-level label reproduction, which predicts uniform ratios irrespective of domain status. Fifth, a systematic mechanism-stratified analysis across all 83 BISECT cases with PRISM scores, classified by domain architecture class, reveals a four-category spectrum: Type A (complete domain loss; tier2_functional_loss + tier2_complex_loss; n = 54) yields mean CT/AD ratio = 5.61 with 50/54 cases exceeding 2.0× (MWU vs. Type C: p < 0.001); Type B (domain gain in AD isoform; tier1_functional_switch; n = 3) yields mean ratio < 1.0, indicating PRISM correctly scores the AD isoform higher when it has gained novel functional domains — a direction reversal consistent with gain-of-function switching; Type C (same domain architecture; tier3_gene_median negative controls; n = 23) yields mean ratio = 1.000 (23/23 at ≈ 1.000), the expected negative control result; Type D (partial structural evidence; n = 3) yields intermediate ratios between Type A and C. This four-category spectrum — domain loss → elevated CT/AD ratio, domain gain → inverted ratio, no architectural change → ratio ≈ 1.00 — is the expected quantitative signature of genuine isoform-level structural discrimination and cannot be explained by gene-level label reproduction, which would predict uniform ratios across all four classes. Collectively, these five analyses establish that PRISM's convergence with BISECT reflects isoform-intrinsic sequence discrimination: ESM-2 detects MTS presence/absence, Oxidored_q6 domain completeness, and Fer4 C-terminal truncation from amino acid sequence alone, not gene-level functional category assignment.

### 8. Independent replication and cell-type-resolved validation

To validate Samsung findings independently, we queried the Ebbert et al. bulk long-read cohort (12 donors post-QC: 6 AD, 6 control; PRJNA596351). **KIF21B** shows significantly reduced full-length-isoform abundance in AD (MWU p=0.026; Tier B, Samsung non-significant at n=13/8, perm p=0.472, due to single-nucleus sparsity), with PRISM scoring the motor-domain-intact isoform 4-fold higher (0.472 vs. 0.113) for microtubule-based movement. Extending to three further Complex I candidates: **NDUFS4**'s two minor isoforms both show AD-enriched abundance (MWU p=0.041 each, Bonferroni p_adj=0.082, directionally concordant with Samsung); **NDUFS7** shows the same direction but does not reach significance (p=0.31), consistent with its 5.1% excitatory-neuron effect diluting below <2% in mixed-cell-type bulk tissue; **NDUFS8** shows mixed significance (p=0.065/0.026) with its Samsung-nominated NIC transcript absent from GENCODE38, limiting interpretation to directional consistency. **DOCK11**, tested for the first time in Ebbert, shows concordant direction but its AD-enriched transcript is near-undetectable in bulk (zero reads in 9/12 donors, p=0.240) — itself informative, since inhibitory neurons (like NDUFS7's excitatory-neuron effect) are diluted below the bulk detection floor by cell-type averaging, not evidence of absent biology. A post-hoc, exploratory Fisher combination of Samsung × Ebbert p-values (evaluated against the pre-specified Bonferroni threshold α_corrected=0.010, not uncorrected α=0.05) gives NDUFS4 combined p=0.0124 (still short of threshold, **remains Tier C**), DOCK11 combined p=0.0018 (reinforcing its already-confirmed Tier A-BPC), and NDUFS7 combined p=0.0775 (not significant even combined — only a genuinely single-cell/nucleus-resolved independent cohort, not more bulk donors, can resolve it). Independently, v17f*'s predicted-score dispersion across isoforms of the five BISECT Complex I genes (normalised to between-gene σ=0.0894) shows detectable within-gene functional discrimination in every case — NDUFS4 2.3×, NDUFS7 0.42×, DOCK11 0.123×, NDUFAF5 0.061×, NDUFS8 0.058× (mean 0.57×) — confirming PRISM assigns functionally differentiated scores to BISECT-validated isoform pairs without access to isoform-level ground truth. At the cell-type-resolved layer, Q-module disruption concentrates in AD-enriched L4 IT excitatory neurons (cluster C18; uncorrected p=0.0099, Bonferroni p_adj=0.297 across 30 clusters), where NDUFS7 co-enrichment (+0.17 above background) is the highest among all spatial clusters — consistent with independent AD-vulnerability data placing L4 IT among the earliest-affected excitatory subtypes.

## Discussion

We have shown that protein language model embeddings encode isoform-level functional information inaccessible to domain-based annotation tools, and that a simple task-specific MLP trained on gene-level GO labels extracts this information as isoform-discriminating functional scores. These two clauses sit on deliberately different axes, and keeping them distinct is the paper's organising principle. Our scientific contribution is *not* that PRISM out-predicts prior tools: on the within-gene axis that defines isoform resolution, a training-free embedding-distance baseline already matches it (Domain-Ranking AUC 0.638 vs. 0.630), and on the macro axis where task training does add substantially (centroid-similarity 0.099 → PRISM 0.734), the metric is dominated by gene-identity rather than isoform function. The contribution is instead that we *delineate*, and biologically *deploy*, the boundary of what protein language models can and cannot resolve at isoform level. PRISM is the instrument that makes this boundary measurable and carries it into disease: a single calibrated model spanning all evaluated GO terms, which no training-free method provides (centroid-similarity is within-gene-only; k-NN is macro-only) and which the Alzheimer's analysis requires — its necessity as a *deployable tool* and non-necessity as the *scientific novelty* are the same finding viewed from two orthogonal axes. Three findings are central. First, PRISM's within-gene discrimination signal (pos_bias above shuffled-label controls) demonstrates that ESM-2 embeddings preserve exon-skipping's structural consequences at isoform-level resolution — a specific property of the PLM's evolutionary training context, not a trivial consequence of sequence diversity. Second, PRISM operates on all isoforms regardless of database coverage: 80.9% carry no annotatable Pfam domain yet all receive PRISM scores, and 92.3% of BISECT-validated predictions are non-overlapping with InterProScan+pfam2go, confirming complementary rather than redundant annotation. Sequence homology shows an analogous complementarity: BLAST→GOA reaches the highest overall AUPRC (0.861) via gene-identification transfer from matched Swiss-Prot sequences (68.5% covered isoforms; the 31.5% uncovered score 0 by construction), yet PRISM and BLAST's top-isoform rankings agree in only 47.7% of pairs — approaching the 50% independence baseline — confirming PRISM captures information orthogonal to gene-level homology transfer. Third, zero-shot cross-tissue transfer (muscle→brain, AUPRC 0.600) demonstrates that learned sequence-function relationships generalise across tissues without retraining. A gene-mean oracle achieves brain AUPRC 0.811 (+0.211 above PRISM's 0.600), establishing that 0.600 is genuine sequence-based transfer, not a performance ceiling — the oracle's advantage quantifies gene-membership information unavailable to PRISM at inference, not an intrinsic limitation (§Interpreting macro AUPRC).

The Complex I disruption cascade (§7) — NDUFAF5→NDUFS7→NDUFS8/NDUFS4, hierarchically structured around the NDUFAF5–NDUFS7 enzyme–substrate relationship — is the most mechanistically coherent multi-gene signal from the AD brain analysis, evidenced across statistical tiers: NDUFAF5 genome-wide significant (DRIMSeq), NDUFS8 approaching the Bonferroni threshold (p_adj=0.022), NDUFS4/NDUFS7 nominally significant with structural support but replication-required. The cell-type dissociation of Q-module subunits (NDUFS7 excitatory, NDUFS8 inhibitory) implies cell-autonomous rather than global mitochondrial dysfunction, consistent with distinct bioenergetic demands and prior reports of neuron-type-selective mitochondrial dysregulation in AD. Separately, DOCK11 (a non-Complex-I DOCK-family GEF, nominated by BISECT M17 convergence detection) achieves the panel's strongest result (perm p=0.0008, p_adj=0.004), confirming the targeted permutation approach's discriminatory power extends beyond the Complex I axis.

**PRISM's contribution to the disease finding is an independent, domain-transcending functional-evidence layer whose concordance cross-validates the cascade.** The value PRISM adds to the Complex I result is best stated against the two things it is deliberately *not*. It is not a domain-annotation tool: on the BISECT-validated switches, 24/26 carry a PRISM Biological-Process prediction that no pfam2go mapping supplies (§5), and PRISM scores every isoform — including the 7,899 brain NIC/NNIC sequences (12.3%) that carry no recognisable Pfam domain and receive nothing from InterProScan. That this beyond-domain layer reflects genuine biology rather than hallucination is established independently of the AD cohort and against a matched null: across gene-negative predictions genome-wide, the high-scoring beyond-gene-label tail is enriched 9–14× over matched random screening for independent experimental-literature support (empirical p < 0.001; §5, Table 1), with the enrichment attributable to term-specific correspondence (≈10×) rather than to ambient annotation richness (≈1.2×). And it is not the source of the disease statistics: donor-level DRIMSeq and donor-label permutation testing (§7, §8) take no PRISM score as input, so the two evidence streams are methodologically disjoint by construction. That disjointness is the point rather than a caveat — it is precisely what makes PRISM's locus-by-locus concordance with BISECT a genuine cross-validation rather than a circular restatement of the same signal. Critically, that concordance is shown *not* to be gene-label reproduction: a gene-mean oracle scores exactly chance on the within-gene ranking these cases turn on (Domain-Ranking AUC 0.500 vs. PRISM 0.630/0.775), the within-gene CT/AD score ratios are 4.3×/10.3×/3.2× for NDUFS4/7/8 where a gene-level classifier is fixed at 1.00, the LRPPRC mitochondrial control sits at exactly 1.00 when its domain architecture is unchanged, and the CT/AD ratio tracks domain loss/gain/none across all 83 BISECT cases in a four-category pattern no gene-level classifier can produce (§7b). The cascade thus rests on three methodologically independent legs — the transcript-usage statistics, BISECT's structural modules, and PRISM's sequence-only functional scores — that converge on the same loci; PRISM contributes the functional interpretation and the independent corroboration, and its very independence from the disease statistics is what gives that corroboration evidential weight. This disjointness rules out computational circularity but not every shared confound: because Q-module and other Complex I genes are transcriptionally co-regulated as part of mitochondrial biogenesis programmes, a general, mechanism-nonspecific disturbance of this pathway in excitatory neurons could in principle drive both PRISM's score differential and BISECT's transcript-usage statistic without the specific NDUFAF5-to-NDUFS7 hydroxylation link being causally operative — a possibility the present data do not exclude, and a further reason, beyond statistical power, that the cascade is presented as nominated rather than established. Distinguishing pathway-level vulnerability from a mechanism-specific cascade will require testing whether structurally unrelated Complex I/OXPHOS genes outside the Q-module show comparable switch rates in the same cell type.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The PRISM training set covers 18 GO BP terms selected for muscle/neuromuscular biology; GO terms without functional analogs (GPCR signalling, immune pathways) show near-chance brain AUPRC, requiring new training objectives to extend the framework. A second, mechanistically distinct limitation is ESM-2's intrinsic separability ceiling for H2-category terms (§6: 203/279, 72.8%, concentrated in chromatin organisation, PTM-dependent signalling, cell-cycle regulation) — not recoverable by more training data within the sequence-only paradigm, since these terms' positivity depends on PTM state, interaction-partner availability, or cell-cycle context with no consistent sequence correlate across genes. Multimodal extension with sequence-predicted PTM sites (NetPhos, GPS-5.0) or disorder profiles (IUPred2A) could add per-sequence structural features, but systematic feature analysis suggests only modest gains for borderline terms rather than rescuing the core H2 category, because the cellular signalling context determining whether a PTM site is actually activated is extrinsic to sequence.

**Scope declaration: quantifying tier (iii) — what is not encoded (§4c).** §4c established that ESM-2's isoform-discriminating representation resolves at the domain/structural level but not for short linear motifs (SLiMs); this section quantifies that boundary directly. On 33,306 same-length within-gene isoform pairs (ruling out truncation/domain-count confounds), the median PRISM score gap is **0.0**, with only 1.06% (353/33,306) exceeding a 0.05 threshold. Restricting to 741 pairs (58 genes) carrying an annotated SLiM difference (8 motif classes: NLS/NES, SH3-binding PxxP, CDK/CK2/PKA phosphosites, KFERQ, RGD), the mean gap is uniform across motif class (0.056–0.069) and only weakly associates with motif-count difference (ρ=0.081, p=0.028) — indicating no systematic response to which or how many motifs changed. We interpret this as a genuine representational ceiling: mean-pooling and the gene-level focal-loss objective jointly optimise for domain-scale signal, and SLiMs (3–10 residues) are a qualitatively smaller pooled-embedding perturbation than domain gain/loss. Combined with the gene-level label ceiling (§1), we frame domain/structural-level resolution as this work's substantiated scope, with motif-level functional direction an open problem requiring isoform-specific labels or a per-residue (not pooled) architecture.

**Refining "not encoded" to "not resolved by mean-pooling": a preliminary per-residue test.** The tier-iii framing above implicitly assumes ESM-2 does not encode motif-level information at all; a direct per-residue test (Supplementary Note S_dissection) shows this is too strong. Per-residue embeddings around the splice junction of aligned motif/regulatory pairs (n=13) show a substantial, smoothly-decaying local perturbation (junction shift 3.26, decaying 17.6-fold by 160 residues) that mean-pooling collapses to near-zero (cosine distance 0.0097) — the information is present per-residue; pooling, not encoding absence, is the immediate cause of the null result. However, simply replacing mean-pooling with a local window does not itself recover direction accuracy (9/13 correct under both poolings, preliminary): unlike domain completeness, which has one common learnable anchor (region-pooling improved a small domain-loss control set 0.571→0.714, n=14, exploratory), the 14 benchmark motif pairs span mechanistically unrelated function classes with no shared reference direction and too few examples per mechanism to construct one. This relocates rather than resolves the open problem — from "does ESM-2 encode this" (locally, yes) to "can a mechanism-general reference direction be constructed from available labels" (not with current benchmark diversity/size) — reported as preliminary and hypothesis-generating (single window size, no bootstrap CI), not a resolved finding.

**A systematic search independently reinforces the "not with available labels" half of this conclusion.** We tested six alternative isoform-level supervision sources (GO Biological Process, APPRIS alternative-isoform status, two ELM motif-instance granularities, UniProt/HPA localisation, and an IDR boundary-shift label) as candidate targets for the mechanism-general reference direction above. All six fail to provide editcore-specific supervision distinguishable from gene-level, domain-bias, database-sparsity, or positional confounds, each via a distinct mechanism (Supplementary Note S_label_search). This establishes, across a wider net than the 14-pair benchmark above, that the tier-iii gap's obstacle is annotation scarcity in the public label landscape, not an unexplored corner of it.

**A representational ceiling on within-gene Domain-Ranking.** A related ceiling bounds the domain-level signal itself: Domain-Ranking AUC of 0.630 (muscle; 0.775 true-brain, same ceiling behaviour, §6), while significantly above the gene-mean null (0.500), is raised by neither added capacity nor explicit within-gene supervision. A depth-controlled width sweep improves gene-level macro AUPRC (+0.013 [+0.010,+0.016]) but leaves Domain-Ranking unchanged (ΔAUC≈0), and three auxiliary losses directly supervising within-gene domain ordering — conditional domain-count regression, a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss, and a surgical domain-dependent-subset variant — all fail to raise it (§4c). The ceiling is thus a property of the δ_layer representation, not the readout: it reflects categorical domain presence/absence encoding (within-gene AUROC 0.71 on 8 interpretable axes, 0.84 full-640-dim, 0.89 non-linear) rather than a graded domain-count signal (partial ρ=0.27); raising it needs a representation resolving motif/residue-scale differences, not a larger or more supervised head.

**Decomposing PRISM's within-gene ceiling into architectural, representational, and labelling bottlenecks.** The boundary results across §4c, §4d, and §6b cohere into a single interpretive map locating, for each isoform-difference class, the exact stage at which it is lost along PRISM's sequence→score pipeline: (i) physical sequence change, (ii) ESM-2 per-residue encoding, (iii) survival through mean-pooling, (iv) a common learnable *anchor* direction to score against, and (v) an isoform-level label to reward it. Domain-architecture edits traverse all five (junction shift 3.26, domain-probe AUROC≈0.99, common anchor in domain completeness, Domain-Ranking AUC 0.630/0.775) and fail only at the label stage (89.9% of complete-domain-loss positives are low-confidence gene-level inheritances) — *solved to the representation, capped by annotation*. Non-domain edits fracture across different stages instead of one: short linear motifs are encoded but averaged out by pooling; disorder-region edits survive encoding (AUROC 0.79) but register no scorable direction; compositional propensities carry a real but gene-independent direction that does not propagate to output (§6b) — encoded, un-anchored, unused, unlabelled. Decomposing the non-domain variance by gene-disjoint reproducibility: roughly half is gene-reproducible structure, of which most is amino-acid composition (gene-independent); the residual tail survives removal of the full 20-dim composition subspace and is structural/positional rather than compositional (Supplementary Note S_dissection identifies it as within-class positional residue identity) — attributable-in-kind but functionally unlabelled outside a targeting/degron subset. The 8 interpretable structural axes recover only ~two-thirds of the linearly-decodable domain signal (AUROC 0.71 vs. 0.84 full-640-dim probe; §4c), so even the best-understood class retains an unnarratable residual. This reframes "PRISM's within-gene ceiling" from a scalar into a structured diagnosis separating failures that are annotation-recoverable (domain labels), pooling-recoverable in principle (per-residue architectures), and intrinsic to any sequence-only model (post-translational-state-dependent function) — noting the pooling (B2) and usage (B4) stages are architecture-conditional (a differently-pooled or within-gene-supervised model could shift where signal is lost), while encoding (B1, ESM-2) and label (B5, annotation) generalise beyond PRISM. The full map — cascade, axis-usage assignments, variance decomposition, and case-study projection — is summarised in **Supplementary Figure S_map**.

**Tissue asymmetry in domain-remodelling severity is one-directional, not a reciprocal division of labour.** Brain's splicing is the more domain-consequential of the two tissues on both rate (§6) and magnitude (§6b: domain-architecture-change β=2.11 brain vs. 1.53 muscle) axes. A raw per-tissue fit initially suggested a *reciprocal* muscle-specific story (N-terminal overlap and resync-failure coefficients far larger in muscle, plus a steeper size-severity slope: r=0.351 muscle vs. 0.277 brain, gap 95% CI [0.047,0.100], p=0.001) that would read as a mirror-image mechanism — but this does not survive independent testing (§6b): N-terminal overlap collapses to chance once size is controlled, resync-failure is a downstream trace of the size slope, and the slope itself is 89.2%-mediated by edit fragmentation (muscle edits fragment into more intervals than brain edits of equal size), a broad property with no GO enrichment in either tissue. The muscle-side asymmetry is thus real edit geometry, not a distinct biological programme; only the one-directional, brain-greater domain-remodelling severity survives as functionally meaningful — the within-tissue analogue of the gene-identity-confound discipline applied to macro AUPRC below.

The zero-shot brain AUPRC (0.600) is lower than the held-out muscle AUPRC (0.702), reflecting tissue-specific functional enrichment even when the underlying sequence-function mapping is conserved. The Samsung cohort (13 AD, 8 control) is relatively small for donor-level DTU analysis, limiting power for modest-effect switches. Post-hoc permutation power analysis (Monte Carlo, 2,000 runs per effect level; two-sided MWU α=0.05) indicates the n=13 vs. n=8 design achieves ≥80% power for donor-level isoform-ratio differences **≥21%** (Supplementary Figure S_power). NDUFS8's 37% inhibitory-neuron effect reaches empirical power=100%; NDUFS4's and NDUFS7's 5.1% excitatory-neuron effects reach only **8.6%** — severely underpowered rather than absent, a distinction supported by directional consistency across Samsung batches and Ebbert replication (§8). An independent cohort of ≥20 AD/≥20 CT donors with isoform-resolution long-read single-cell data would achieve ≥80% power for effects ≥15%.

With Bonferroni correction for the 5-test panel (α_corrected=0.010), NDUFS7's p_adj=0.24 (NDUFS4: 0.12–0.21) confirms neither reaches strict significance (§7). NDUFS7's Tier A-BPP status rests on two mechanistically non-overlapping lines beyond the nominal permutation p=0.048: a BISECT-identified cassette-exon cluster at the FeS-cluster binding interface (M1 PASS), and its hypothesis-confirmatory enzyme–substrate link to NDUFAF5 (§7) — a classification we do not claim is hypothesis-free.

NDUFS4 remains Tier C (§7–§8): despite orthogonal BISECT structural support (MTS exon-1 loss, M1/M9 PASS) and directional Ebbert replication, its excitatory-neuron effect (5.1%, power 8.6%) is underpowered, and — unlike NDUFS7 — it lacks a confirmed enzyme–substrate link to NDUFAF5 in the same cell type, acting downstream at the separate NDUFA12/NDUFAF2 maturation step. The Fisher-combined p=0.0124 (§8) does not cross α_corrected=0.010. DLG1, the highest single isoform differential (27-fold), lacks donor-level statistical support (Tier D) due to insufficient OPC read depth and requires cell-type-enriched validation.

**Interpreting macro AUPRC in the context of gene-level evaluation.** Macro AUPRC on this test set predominantly measures gene-family identification, not isoform-specific function prediction: a gene-mean oracle reaches 0.803 (muscle)/0.795 (brain), exceeding v17f* (0.734/0.647), and this generalises to the 18-term BP deployment model (oracle 0.811 vs. deployed 0.600; Abstract, §6); a still-simpler label-identity lookup achieves a perfect macro AUPRC of 1.000 while scoring exactly chance (Domain-Ranking AUC=0.500) on within-gene ranking — the sharpest possible statement that the two metrics are orthogonal (§4b). Two training-free controls isolate where this within-gene signal originates: raw embedding-norm magnitude (a domain-completeness proxy) achieves only DR-AUC=0.374 — anti-correlated with domain completeness, ruling out magnitude as the mechanism — while per-GO-term centroid-similarity, with no MLP training at all, matches v17f* on DR-AUC (0.638 vs. 0.630) yet collapses to macro AUPRC 0.099, an order of magnitude below v17f*'s 0.734 (§4d). This axis-decoupling — no training-free method attains both axes — is exactly why a trained, deployable predictor remains necessary even though neither axis is created from scratch by training. A gene-family-identification random-embedding floor of 0.345 confirms the functional embedding space itself is informative beyond chance. The appropriate evaluation for isoform-resolution function prediction is therefore not macro AUPRC alone but the combination of gene-level AUPRC and within-gene discrimination (Domain-Ranking AUC, UniProt direction accuracy; §4b, §5); Supplementary Table S3/Figure S2 report this decomposition for every benchmarked method.

**Annotation-level limitations for domain-loss isoforms: label noise, not representation failure.** Stratifying all 36,748 test isoforms by Pfam-based structural category — Type 0 (no domain in gene; 52.4%), Type 1 (complete domain loss; 10.3%), Type 2 (partial truncation; 2.6%), Type 3 (same domain set as canonical; 34.7%) — PRISM macro AUPRC stratifies as Type 3=0.784, Type 0=0.708, Type 2=0.651, Type 1=0.641 (Δ=−0.143 Type 1 vs. Type 3), explaining much of the gene-mean-oracle/v17f* gap. A GO-domain log-odds label-confidence score (whether an isoform's remaining domains are the ones functionally informative for a given GO term, normalised to its gene's canonical complement) shows Type 1 positives are 89.9% low-confidence (mean 0.096) — gene-level label inheritance onto isoforms that lost the domains co-occurring with the annotation — versus 23.2% for Type 2 and 0.2% for Type 3, directly predicting the AUPRC hierarchy: PRISM is penalised for domain-mechanistically-correct low-activity predictions because the evaluation treats inherited gene-level positives as ground truth. Two controls confirm this is a label, not representational, limitation: Type 1 isoforms sit 2.89× farther from their canonical isoform in ESM-2 embedding space (0.035 vs. 0.012 for Type 3) — the model encodes what the label does not express — and ridge meta-models combining PRISM with explicit Pfam domain features show no gain, mostly active degradation (Δ=−0.042 to −0.043), since ESM-2 embeddings already carry the domain signal. The correct performance reference for domain-loss isoforms is therefore isoform-specific experimental evidence, not gene-level AUPRC — precisely what the UniProt benchmark below provides.

**Isoform-pair direction benchmark on curated functional differences.** Beyond gene-family identification, we tested whether PRISM predicts the direction of functional difference between experimentally validated isoform pairs — structurally immune to gene-level confounds since a gene-mean classifier is forced to 0.500 on any within-gene contrast. From 51 UniProt/Swiss-Prot pairs (48 evaluable; kinase-domain loss, large truncations, PPI-domain architecture, TF transactivation-domain loss, co-factor-binding domains, GTPase dynamics, and a bidirectional E3-ligase case), raw aggregate direction accuracy is 26/48=54.2% (permutation p=0.33, NS) — an uninformative average masking a sharp confidence-stratified signal. Stratifying by |score_a−score_b|, PRISM achieves 11/11 (100%) accuracy for gap≥0.10 (permutation p<0.001), all involving complete domain loss or major truncation, versus 13/34=38.2% for gap<0.05 (subtle regulatory differences: MAPT N-terminal inserts, HNRNPA1 RGG box, RAC1B switch-II dynamics). Length is not a confound: length-difference vs. accuracy ρ=+0.019 (p=0.911); gap and length jointly predict accuracy with the gap coefficient ~34× the length coefficient. The gap threshold reflects genuine structural severity, not a post-hoc artefact: gap correlates with % sequence truncation (ρ=0.604) and length difference (ρ=0.602), both p<0.001. Stratifying by pre-specified mechanism category (not gap) gives the same result independent of any threshold: domain-loss pairs (n=13) reach 92.3% versus regulatory pairs (n=13) at 30.8% (Fisher OR=27, p=0.0018; Abstract). The single domain-loss failure (SMN1, generic protein-binding GO term with only indirect Tudor-domain relevance) and the lowest-gap domain-preserving case (KIT iso-2, activation-loop deletion within an otherwise intact kinase fold, gap=0.0006) illustrate the boundary: mean-pooling detects complete domain loss reliably but not subtle intra-domain perturbations. This converges with the Domain-Ranking AUC (0.630) and domain-probing AUROC (~0.99, no length signal) to establish that PRISM's within-gene discrimination is most reliable precisely where structural domain differences are pronounced (full pair list, examples, and stratification methodology: §Methods, Supplementary Table).

The systematic development path leading to the v17f* architecture — including seven tested variants (v17a through v17f), multimodal extension experiments (v18a/v18b), and ESM-2 fine-tuning comparisons (D0/D1) — is documented in full in **Supplementary Note S_arch**, including mechanistic probing studies, scale experiments across ESM-2 model sizes, and H2 reclassification under domain-matched training (158/168 H2 terms recovering to AUPRC ≥ 0.50).

PRISM and BISECT together provide a methodological framework for isoform-resolution functional annotation that is applicable beyond AD: any disease or tissue context where long-read single-cell RNA-seq can resolve isoform identities is a candidate for the same pipeline. The modular architecture of BISECT — where individual evidence modules can be replaced or extended independently — and the zero-shot transferability of PRISM are designed for generalization. As long-read single-cell sequencing scales to larger cohorts and broader tissue panels, the same sequence-first, evidence-integrated approach demonstrated here for prefrontal cortex AD will enable systematic functional characterisation of the isoform-level pathological landscape across neurological disorders.

## Methods

### Cell atlas and long-read RNA-seq data

**Skeletal muscle (training).** Samsung Medical Center (SMC) and Asan Medical Center (AMC) long-read single-cell RNA-seq; sarcopenia patients vs healthy controls. Final isoform set: 36,748 isoforms from 12,706 genes after SQANTI3 [12] QC (FL count ≥ 3, RT-artifact filter, intrapriming filter). IsoQuant v2.3 [13] isoform quantification against a combined reference annotation (Ensembl 109 + refTSS-extended novel transcripts). Cell type annotation by Seurat v4 leiden clustering with marker gene validation.

**Brain AD (zero-shot/application).** Samsung Medical Center long-read single-cell RNA-seq cohort; 13 AD donors, 8 control donors; prefrontal cortex (Brodmann area 9); 63,994 isoforms from 18,514 genes. Eight cell types resolved: excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, oligodendrocytes, OPCs, astrocytes, microglia, endothelial cells, pericytes. Condition labels: 'AD', 'Control', 'Active control' (Active control treated as Control for all analyses).

**Independent replication.** Ebbert et al. human prefrontal cortex bulk long-read RNA-seq (n=21: 10 AD, 11 control; PRJNA596351; 12 samples with complete annotation used after QC: 6 AD, 6 CT). Transcript count matrix (GENCODE38 annotation; Bambu-assembled) was downloaded from the study data supplement. For each candidate gene, isoform fractional abundances were computed per donor as (transcript count) / (gene total count + 1e-9). Mann-Whitney U test (two-sided, scipy.stats) was applied to per-donor fractional abundances between AD and CT groups. Bootstrap permutation (n=2,000 donor-label permutations) was used to confirm MWU p-values. Novel Samsung IsoQuant NIC/NNIC transcripts absent from GENCODE38 were matched to the nearest annotated proxy by structural similarity (shared exon boundaries, splice site overlap); genes whose Samsung AD-enriched isoform had no viable GENCODE38 proxy (NDUFS8 transcript100761.chr11.nic) were flagged as directional-only comparisons. DRIMSeq v1.26.0 transcript-level DTU was additionally run on the complete 21-sample dataset for genome-wide context. For genes with a Samsung donor-permutation p-value and a paired Ebbert MWU p-value testing the same directional hypothesis in independent donor cohorts, we pre-specified Fisher's combined probability test (χ² = −2·Σln(pᵢ), df = 2k, χ² reference distribution) as a secondary combined-evidence statistic; this combination was applied only to gene/cell-type pairs where the Ebbert proxy transcript represents the same molecular event as the Samsung-nominated isoform (excluding NDUFS8, whose Samsung AD-enriched isoform has no GENCODE38 proxy).

### ESM-2 embeddings

Protein sequences for all isoforms were extracted from IsoQuant-predicted CDS (SQANTI3 coding sequence output, MANE Select or longest CDS for canonical). ESM-2 (esm2_t30_150M_UR50D; 480M parameters; embedding dimension 640) was applied to all sequences in batch mode (max length 1024 aa; mean-pooled across residue positions). Embeddings were pre-computed and stored as float32 NPY arrays. For sequences exceeding 1024 aa, embeddings were computed on the first 1024 residues. All embeddings are frozen (no fine-tuning) during PRISM training.

### PRISM architecture and training

PRISM uses a three-layer MLP: Dense(256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(128, ReLU) → Dropout(0.2) → Dense(64, ReLU) → Dense(18, sigmoid). Input: 640-dim frozen ESM-2 embedding. Output: 18-dimensional sigmoid probabilities for 18 GO BP terms. Loss: binary focal cross-entropy (γ=2.0; α per class from class-balanced inverse frequency). Optimizer: Adam (lr=1e-3, cosine decay). Batch size: 256. Early stopping: macro AUPRC on validation set (patience 10). Ensemble: 5 seeds, mean-pooled probabilities.

Training labels: gene-level GO annotation from UniProt/QuickGO (reviewed Swiss-Prot where available, TrEMBL supplemented; IEA excluded for primary analysis, included where non-IEA coverage < 5%). Each isoform inherits the GO terms of its parent gene. The 18 GO terms were selected by: (i) sarcopenia/muscle-biology relevance; (ii) positive-class count ≥ 50 in training set; (iii) non-redundancy by semantic similarity clustering.

Train/test split: isoform-level stratified 5-fold CV (gene-level group constraint to prevent gene-level data leakage).

**Formal training objective.** Let I = {i_1, ..., i_N} (N=36,748) be the training isoform set with gene-of-origin function g: I → G (|G|=12,706). The frozen ESM-2 embedding is φ: I → ℝ^640. The parameter-free label assignment y_{i,k} := y_{g(i),k} for isoform i and GO term k assigns every isoform the label of its parent gene. The training loss is:

> **L(θ) = Σ_{i∈I} Σ_{k=1}^{18} FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k , y_{i,k} )**
> **FL(p, y) = −α_y (1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t)**

where p_t = f_θ(φ(i))_k if y_{i,k}=1, else 1 − f_θ(φ(i))_k; γ=2.0; α_y = (1 − E_f) / (1 − E_f)^β with effective frequency E_f and β=0.9999 (class-balanced inverse frequency). f_θ has no access to gene identity: isoforms of the same gene are independent training examples. The MLP therefore minimises L by learning w_k ∈ ℝ^{64} (final layer weight for GO term k) such that ⟨w_k, h_θ(φ(i))⟩ is high when the embedding geometry of φ(i) is consistent with positive training examples for term k across all genes. This global consistency constraint, combined with isoform-specific embeddings, produces isoform-level score discrimination as a by-product of gene-level supervision (Supplementary Note S1).

### Layer-contrast delta architecture (T_ψ + δ_layer)

The layer-contrast architecture recovers isoform-specific signal suppressed by ESM-2 mean-pooling. Standard mean-pooling averages all residue representations uniformly, merging early structural layers with late functional-contextual layers. We introduce two components operating on per-isoform layer differences:

**δ_layer definition.** For each isoform i, the layer-contrast delta is:

> **δ_layer(i) = φ_L30(i) − φ_L15(i)**

where φ_Lk(i) ∈ ℝ^640 is the mean-pooled residue embedding from layer k of ESM-2 (k ∈ {1, …, 30}). L30 captures deep functional-contextual representation; L15 captures mid-depth structural geometry. δ_layer is an inductive, per-isoform quantity requiring no gene-sibling information. Layer selection (L_mid ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 25}) was performed on the muscle validation set with fixed triplets (same random seed across all conditions); all five candidates achieved All MF AUPRC 0.717–0.725 and L2_Structural 0.618–0.632 — well above PRISM and insensitive to the specific L_mid choice. L15 was selected as canonical (best All MF, 0.725 single-seed; canonical 5-seed ensemble: 0.717); L05 yielded the marginally best L2_Structural (0.632, Δ=+0.003 over L15, within run-to-run variance).

**T_ψ architecture (Stage 1).** T_ψ: ℝ^640 → ℝ^64 maps δ_layer to a compact triplet-trained representation:

> Dense(640→256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(256→64, ℓ_2-normalised)

T_ψ is trained with triplet margin loss on GO-label cross-gene isoform triplets. For anchor a, positive p is the nearest cross-gene isoform sharing at least one GO label (argmin_{j: g(j)≠g(a), y_j∩y_a≠∅} cosine_d(δ_layer(a), δ_layer(j))); negative n is the nearest cross-gene isoform sharing no GO label (argmin_{j: g(j)≠g(a), y_j∩y_a=∅} cosine_d(a,j)). The cross-gene constraint (g(j) ≠ g(a)) prevents collapse to gene identity. Training set: 23,837 valid triplets. Loss:

> **L_triplet = max( cosine_d(T_ψ(δ_layer(a)), T_ψ(δ_layer(p))) − cosine_d(T_ψ(δ_layer(a)), T_ψ(δ_layer(n))) + 0.3 , 0 )**

Optimiser: Adam (lr=1×10^{−3}). Epochs: 50 (early stopping on validation triplet accuracy). Stage 1 weights are frozen before Stage 2 training.

**Stage 2 MLP.** Given frozen T_ψ and frozen φ_L30, the combined representation [T_ψ(δ_layer(i)); φ_L30(i)] ∈ ℝ^{704} is passed to a two-layer MLP:

> Dense(704→256, ReLU) → BatchNorm → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(256→128, ReLU) → Dropout(0.2) → Dense(128→82, sigmoid)

Loss: binary focal cross-entropy (γ=2.0; class-balanced α as above). Optimiser: Adam (lr=1×10^{−3}, cosine decay). Ensemble: 5 random seeds, mean-pooled probabilities. Training labels: 82 Molecular Function GO terms (NCBI gene2go; IEA excluded; ≥100 training positives per term). Bootstrap CI: gene-level block bootstrap (B=1,000 resamples; all isoforms of each sampled gene included; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI).

**Ablation controls.** Appending RNA-level or localisation delta features (v18a; 17-dim handcrafted) yielded ΔAll-MF AUPRC = −0.009, confirming that δ_layer captures the maximally informative layer-contrast signal without benefit from explicit sequence-derived approximations. Appending 16-dimensional brain cell-type expression vectors (v18b; expr_frac + isoform_usage_frac per 8 cell types) yielded ΔAll-MF AUPRC = −0.110, reflecting a domain mismatch between muscle-trained MLP and brain-specific cell-context features under zero-shot cross-tissue transfer; cell-context features are suppressed during muscle training and interfere at brain evaluation time.

**Gain decomposition (v17e_layer ablation).** To isolate the raw contribution of δ_layer without triplet-trained metric organisation, T_ψ Stage 1 was bypassed and the 1280-dimensional concatenation [δ_layer(640); φ_L30(640)] was fed directly to a five-seed focal-loss MLP with identical Stage 2 hyperparameters (v17e_layer). The v17e_layer AUPRC versus PRISM and v17f quantifies the three-component gain: PRISM → v17e_layer (raw δ_layer) → v17f (+ T_ψ organisation). Random-δ control: δ_layer replaced by an isotropic random vector matched per-isoform to the same L₂ norm; T_ψ + Stage 2 trained identically. Random-δ AUPRC isolates T_ψ architecture capacity independent of δ_layer directional content.

**Mechanistic probing (δ_layer encoding analysis).** Four probing analyses were applied to 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms. (i) Domain predictability: logistic regression (scikit-learn) trained to predict binary Pfam domain presence from δ_layer versus φ_L30 versus φ_L15, evaluated by 5-fold cross-validation AUROC across 100 Pfam domains with 30–29,398 positive examples. (ii) Within-gene splice-change correlation: across all 76,564 same-gene isoform pairs, Spearman ρ between |Δδ_layer| (L₂ norm of inter-isoform difference in δ_layer) and Δdomain count (absolute change in number of Pfam domains between isoform pair). (iii) Per-dimension L2_Structural correlation: Pearson r between each of the 640 δ_layer dimensions and binary L2_Structural GO membership (mean over 33 L2_Structural MF terms with ≥2 positives); top-20 dimensions by |r| identified; mean |r| in δ_layer versus same 20 dimensions in φ_L30 computed. (iv) Norm versus domain count: Spearman ρ between isoform δ_layer L₂ norm and number of annotated Pfam domains (0–many); mean norm computed for no-domain, ≥1-domain, and ≥3-domain groups.

**ESM-2 scale generalization.** To assess depth dependence of the δ_layer benefit, the complete v17f T_ψ + Stage 2 pipeline was re-run with (i) ESM-2 8M (esm2_t6_8M_UR50D; 6 layers; embedding dim 320; δ = L6 − L3) and (ii) ESM-2 35M (esm2_t12_35M_UR50D; 12 layers; 480-dim; δ = L12 − L6). Per-scale PRISM baseline (frozen final-layer only, same train/test split and MLP) was also computed for comparison. All hyperparameters identical across scales; T_ψ input dimension adjusted to match each embedding dimension. Brain isoform sequences (37k) were re-embedded with each smaller model; muscle train sequences (28k with known protein sequences) similarly processed.

**ESM-2 fine-tuning comparison (Exp D).** To evaluate whether ESM-2 fine-tuning can substitute for the frozen δ_layer approach, three conditions were evaluated on 82 MF GO terms against the held-out *muscle* test fold (36,748 isoforms; an earlier draft mislabelled this fold as "brain"; same protocol as v17f). True-brain zero-shot values for D0/D1/D2 are reported in Supplementary Table S2b. D0: five-seed PRISM MLP (Dense 256→128→82, focal γ=2, 60 epochs) on frozen ESM-2 L30 pre-computed embeddings using all **31,668 training isoforms** (same training set as v17f; D0 requires only pre-computed embeddings and is not restricted to protein-sequence-available isoforms); 1,000-bootstrap CI reported. D1: same MLP training on the **28,040-isoform subset** with available protein sequences (TransDecoder predictions), after ESM-2 partial fine-tuning of the last 5 layers (24.62M trainable parameters); fine-tuning hyperparameters: 5 epochs, batch 4, gradient accumulation 8, LR_ESM = 5×10⁻⁶ (cosine decay), AdamW; L30 embeddings re-extracted after fine-tuning. D1 uses fewer training isoforms than D0 due to protein sequence availability; any training-set advantage therefore biases conservatively in favour of D0, but the observed All MF difference of 0.000 (D1: 0.660 vs D0: 0.660; 95% CIs fully overlapping) and L2_Structural difference of 0.001 (D1: 0.520 vs D0: 0.521; 95% CIs fully overlapping) are both below bootstrap noise regardless of this factor. D2: full 30-layer fine-tuning (147.71M parameters) was attempted but exceeded available GPU memory (24.5 GiB) and was not evaluable. All conditions evaluated by the same AUPRC protocol on 81 valid MF terms.

**SOTA retrieval baselines (sequence-similarity GO transfer).** To contextualise PRISM and v17f within sequence-similarity approaches, two retrieval baselines were evaluated on the 36,748-isoform held-out *muscle* test fold (81 valid MF terms; an earlier draft mislabelled this fold as "brain"; same evaluation protocol as all other methods). (i) Gene-mean ESM-2 retrieval (E0b): gene-level centroids were computed by averaging ESM-2 L30 embeddings of all training isoforms per gene; each test isoform was assigned the gene2go MF GO label profile of its k=5 most similar centroids by cosine similarity; GO probability was the mean binary label over top-k centroid genes. (ii) Isoform k-NN ESM-2 retrieval (E0): same procedure, using individual training isoforms rather than centroids as reference points (k=5, cosine similarity in L30 embedding space, weighted-average GO label transfer). Both baselines use the same NCBI gene2go MF annotations as training labels and evaluation ground truth. The identical retrieval procedure was independently rerun against the true-brain zero-shot test set (63,994 IsoQuant isoforms, 82 valid MF terms) for the corrected comparison in Supplementary Table S2b.

**DeepFRI comparison (Exp E).** DeepFRI (CNN-LM, sequence-only mode) was installed from the public GitHub repository (Gligorijević et al., Nat Methods 2021) and applied to all 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms. Protein sequences were extracted from the same source as ESM-2 embeddings (top30k_isoforms.pep; 100% coverage). DeepFRI was run with `predict.py --fasta_fn brain_test.fasta -ont mf` using the pre-trained `DeepCNN-MERGED_molecular_function.hdf5` model. The CNN-LM model outputs predictions for 489 MF GO terms; of these, 65/81 valid test terms (80.2%) overlap with our evaluation panel, and 16 terms absent from the DeepFRI vocabulary were assigned zero scores. AUPRC was computed on all 81 valid terms using the same gene2go ground truth; a coverage-adjusted AUPRC (65 covered terms only) was reported separately. Predictions were run CPU-only due to cuDNN version incompatibility (system: cuDNN 8, required: cuDNN 7) and completed in 30 minutes.

**DeepGoPlus comparison.** DeepGoPlus (Kulmanov and Hoehndorf, Bioinformatics 2020) [15] was installed from the public GitHub repository (v1.0.28) and configured with the pre-trained UniProt/Swiss-Prot model files (model.h5, terms.pkl, train_data.pkl, train_data.dmnd; retrieved from the DeepGoPlus data server, UniProt release 2026_02). Protein sequences (top30k_isoforms.pep) were submitted to `deepgoplus --threshold 0.0 --batch-size 64`. DeepGoPlus first runs DIAMOND blastp against train_data.dmnd (72,885 Swiss-Prot sequences) to retrieve homology-based GO scores, then runs the 1D-CNN encoder for all input sequences; final predictions combine both components as score = α × CNN_score + (1−α) × DIAMOND_score (α_MF = 0.66 for the MF ontology). DIAMOND hits were obtained for 27,347/36,748 isoforms (74.4%). TransDecoder protein ID suffixes (.p1, .p2, ...) were stripped from DIAMOND query IDs before mapping to isoform identifiers in the evaluation dataset. Bootstrap CI computed at B=500 by gene-level resampling, identical to all other methods.

**Sequence homology benchmarks (BLAST→GOA and isopretEM).** BLAST→GOA: DIAMOND blastp v2.1 [16] was run against the UniProt/Swiss-Prot database (downloaded 2026-04; reviewed entries only; 571,609 sequences) with parameters `--blastp --more-sensitive -e 0.001 --max-target-seqs 50`. Query sequences: TransDecoder-predicted protein sequences for all 36,748 held-out muscle test isoforms (top30k_isoforms.pep; 36,776 total protein sequence entries including multi-isoform TransDecoder predictions; TransDecoder ID suffixes .p1/.p2/... stripped before downstream mapping to the 36,748 unique isoform IDs). For each (isoform, GO MF term) pair, the GO annotation score was defined as the maximum bitscore among all DIAMOND hits annotated with that GO term in the GOA annotation file (EBI GOA Human, 2026-04 release; IEA excluded). Isoforms with no DIAMOND hit at E<0.001 were assigned score 0 for all GO terms (1,403/36,748 isoforms; 3.8%). Of the 35,345 DIAMOND-covered isoforms, 25,159 (68.5% of all test isoforms; 71.2% of covered isoforms) had ≥1 MF GO annotation transferred from the top Swiss-Prot hit; the remainder hit proteins with no MF GOA entries and were scored 0. isopretEM: Following the principle of Ravanmehr et al. [17], within-gene EM redistribution was applied to BLAST→GOA scores using 10 EM iterations: each isoform's score for GO term k was updated proportional to its bitscore weight within its gene's total bitscore; no gene-level label input was provided. Both BLAST→GOA and isopretEM scores were evaluated identically to all other methods (B=500 gene-level bootstrap CI).

**Within-gene isoform discrimination metrics.** Three within-gene metrics were computed for all multi-isoform genes (≥2 test isoforms; 7,459 genes, 32,611 isoforms) using each method's raw probability outputs before any thresholding. (i) *Intra-gene CV*: for each (gene g, GO term k) pair where y_{g,k}=1 (gene-positive), the coefficient of variation (CV = σ/μ) of predicted scores across all isoforms of g was computed; means were averaged over gene-positive pairs. For BLAST→GOA, bitscore values were max-normalised per gene before CV computation (raw bitscores are not comparable in scale to probability outputs). (ii) *Within-gene length-AUC*: for each multi-isoform gene with sufficient length variation (defined as longest isoform ≥ 2× shortest), per-gene AUC was computed for the binary classification task of predicting the longer-than-median-length isoform from predicted score alone; means were averaged over qualifying genes. (iii) *Top-isoform agreement*: for each (gene g, GO term k) pair with ≥2 isoforms covered by BLAST→GOA, the isoform argmax under each method was compared to the BLAST→GOA argmax; fraction of matching argmaxes over N=14,657 testable pairs reported. All three metrics were computed for PRISM v17f*, k-NN ESM-2 (k=5), gene-mean ESM-2 (k=5), BLAST→GOA, and random baseline (uniform random scores, 10-replicate mean).

**UniProt curated isoform-specific benchmark (v2).** To evaluate PRISM's capacity to predict experimentally validated isoform-specific functional differences, we constructed a benchmark of 51 isoform pairs from 50 UniProt Swiss-Prot reviewed entries (human; experimental evidence ECO:0000269); 48 pairs were evaluable after excluding three accessions absent from the UniProt REST API (P15692-7/VEGFA-121, Q15303-8/ERBB4-iso8, Q15365-2/PCBP1-iso2). Pairs were selected from the literature to span structurally well-characterised isoform differences: receptor tyrosine kinase domain loss (VEGFR2/sVEGFR2, FGFR1 iso-11, TrkA splice variants, FGFR2 soluble iso-14, FLT1/sFLT1, NTRK2/TrkB-T1, NTRK3/TrkC-T1, EGFR iso-4, ERBB4 iso-8), large truncations (BRCA1 63-aa iso-2 lacking all functional domains; Parkin iso-3 lacking the UBL domain), anti-apoptotic/pro-apoptotic switching (BCL-xL vs BCL-xS; MCL1-L vs MCL1-S), tumour suppressor divergence (p16 INK4a vs p14ARF from the CDKN2A locus), cytoskeletal binding (ACTN1 muscle vs non-muscle, FLNB hinge exon), transcription factor transactivation domain architecture (CEBPA p42/p30, RUNX2 MASNS/MRIPV, HIF1A TAD truncation; STAT3α iso-1 vs STAT3β iso-3 lacking 48aa C-terminal TAD; C/EBPβ LAP* iso-1 vs LIP iso-3 dominant-negative lacking both TA domains), co-factor and nucleotide binding domains (DNMT3A iso-1 PWWP domain vs iso-2 lacking it; PIK3R1 p85α with SH3+rhoGAP vs p50α lacking both), RNA binding (TIA1 RRM3 loss, PTBP1 RRM2 loss, SMN full Tudor domain vs delta-7 isoform), GTPase dynamics (RAC1B 19-aa switch II insert reducing intrinsic GTPase rate), neurodegeneration-relevant splicing (MAPT 4R/3R tau, APP neuronal/ubiquitous, PARK2 E3 ligase), and a bidirectional E3 ligase case (MDM2: both iso-1 and iso-2 retain the C-terminal RING domain and GO:0004842 activity; iso-2 lacks the N-terminal p53-binding domain but not the catalytic domain — direction annotated as both). For each pair (iso_a, iso_b), the canonical isoform sequence was retrieved from UniProt REST API and the non-canonical isoform from the per-isoform FASTA endpoint. ESM-2 t30_150M layer-contrast embeddings (δ_layer = φ_{L30} − φ_{L15}; concatenated with φ_{L30}; dim=1280) were computed identically to the main PRISM pipeline. The v17f* MLP head was trained on 31,668 muscle isoforms (5 seeds; identical hyperparameters) and applied to all 48 evaluable isoform pairs. For each pair (iso_a, iso_b, GO term k, direction ∈ {A\_only, B\_only, both}), direction accuracy was evaluated as: correct if direction = A\_only and score_a > score_b; correct if direction = B\_only and score_b > score_a; correct if direction = both (both isoforms expected positive for the GO term). The gene-mean baseline is 0.500 (random direction). The stratification threshold gap ≥ 0.10 was specified a priori as the biologically interpretable boundary corresponding to complete structural domain loss or major truncation (kinase domain entirely absent, truncation to <50 residues, or loss of primary interaction domain); structural category annotations for all 51 pairs were completed before PRISM score computation, and all eleven gap ≥ 0.10 cases fall within these pre-specified structural severity categories. When pairs were stratified by prediction confidence (|score_a − score_b|), PRISM achieves direction accuracy 11/11 (100%) for gap ≥ 0.10 (permutation p < 0.001, n=10,000 permutations; binomial p < 0.001), and 26/48 (54.2%) overall. Statistical significance was assessed by one-sided permutation test (H₀: accuracy = 0.5; 10,000 random label permutations per threshold) and one-sided binomial test. GO terms were selected from the 82 MF terms in the PRISM training vocabulary; pairs with GO term absent from the training vocabulary or isoform sequence unavailable from UniProt REST API were excluded before evaluation. 42 of the 50 benchmark genes (84%; by Ensembl gene ID match, including EGFR, PARK2, VEGFR2, FGFR1, BRCA1, FLT1, NTRK2, NTRK3, MDM2) overlap with the 12,709-gene muscle training set, which trains on gene-level GO labels from muscle isoforms; 8 genes (BIRC5, CDKN2A, FGFR2, NTRK1, SMN1, SNAP25, TP73, WT1) do not. This does not confound the within-gene direction test: both isoforms in each pair share the same gene identity and thus the same gene-level training label — any gene-label memorisation would produce identical scores for both isoforms (gap = 0, direction random), not the observed asymmetric gaps. We verified this directly by stratifying direction accuracy by training-set overlap (`reports/exp_h_uniprot_eval/v2/training_overlap_stratification.json`): at the pre-specified gap ≥ 0.10 threshold, both the training-overlapping subset (9/9 = 100%: EGFR, FGFR1, VEGFR2, BRCA1, PARK2, FLT1, NTRK2, NTRK3, MDM2) and the non-overlapping subset (2/2 = 100%: CDKN2A, FGFR2) achieve perfect direction accuracy, directly ruling out gene-level memorisation as the source of the high-confidence result — though the non-overlapping subset is small (n=2) and this should be read as consistent with, not independent statistical confirmation of, the memorisation-null. Across all 48 evaluable pairs at any gap, training-overlapping genes show 23/39 (59.0%) accuracy versus 3/9 (33.3%) for non-overlapping genes; this difference is consistent with sampling noise at n=9 rather than a memorisation signal, since the pre-specified high-confidence subset shows no such asymmetry. The benchmark therefore specifically tests the isoform-intrinsic sequence features captured by δ_layer, orthogonal to gene-level label recall. The benchmark is available in `reports/exp_g_uniprot/uniprot_isoform_benchmark_v2.csv` (51 pairs); evaluation implementation in `hMuscle/model/exp_uniprot_v2.py`.

**Per-locus domain ranking AUC.** To provide a gene-mean-independent validation of within-gene isoform discrimination capacity, we computed per-locus domain ranking AUC for PRISM v17f*, BLAST→GOA, k-NN ESM-2, gene-mean oracle, and random baseline. Ground-truth domain completeness was derived from a binary Pfam domain presence matrix computed with hmmscan (Pfam-A r36.0; E < 0.01) across all 36,748 test isoforms (512 Pfam families; stored as `domain_matrix_proper_test.npy`). The per-isoform domain count *d_i* was defined as the number of distinct Pfam domain families detected in isoform *i* (row sum of the binary matrix; 37.2% of test isoforms with *d_i* ≥ 1). For each multi-isoform gene *g* where isoforms differ in domain count (std(*d_i* : *i* ∈ *g*) ≥ 0.1), and for each GO MF term *k* where gene *g* is annotated positive (*y_{g,k}* = 1), a binary domain-completeness label was constructed: *domain_pos_i* = 1 if *d_i* > median(*d_j* : *j* ∈ *g*), else 0. For each (gene, GO-term) pair with at least one positive and one negative domain label, AUC was computed between *domain_pos_i* and the method's predicted score *ŷ_{i,k}*. Tied predicted scores (std < 10⁻⁸) were assigned AUC = 0.5 (random). This yielded N = 3,542 (gene, GO-term) evaluation pairs across all qualifying genes. The gene-mean oracle — which assigns each isoform the within-gene mean prediction *ŷ_{i,k}* = (1/|g|) Σ_{j∈g} ŷ_{j,k} — necessarily achieves AUC = 0.500 by construction for all pairs (tied scores within every gene), providing a mathematical null that verifies the gene-identity-independence of the evaluation. Bootstrap CI (B = 500 resamples) was computed by gene-level block resampling: at each resample, qualifying genes were drawn with replacement, and the mean AUC over the resulting (gene, GO-term) pairs was computed; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI. Implementation: `hMuscle/model/domain_ranking_validation.py`; results in `reports/domain_ranking_validation/results.json`.

### ESM-2 representation dissection (per-layer Fisher, trajectory PCA, within-gene divergence, axis biology)

The following analyses underlie the ESM-2 representation dissection reported in Results §4c (synthesis) and Supplementary Note S_dissection (full detail). **(1) Per-layer Fisher probe.** For each GO term, a logistic-regression probe was fit on the raw 640-dimensional mean-pooled embedding of each of the 30 ESM-2 layers separately; the Fisher discriminative peak is the layer maximising held-out probe AUPRC. Peak-layer distribution was summarised across 277 GO terms (median, IQR, fraction peaking at L30). **(2) Trajectory joint-PCA axes.** Per-layer embeddings were z-scored within each layer and stacked into a per-isoform trajectory (30 layers × 640 dims); a single joint PCA fit on the muscle training isoforms yielded an 8-component basis W (8 × 640) applied identically across all 30 layers, giving each isoform a trajectory Z of shape (30, 8). Axis scalars used for interpretation are the mean over the 30 layers of each component. **(3) Within-gene layer-divergence.** For the muscle test set (36,748 isoforms; the Domain-Ranking set), all within-gene isoform pairs were enumerated (capped at 60 randomly-sampled pairs per gene). Pairs were classed domain-different (binary Pfam vectors, 512 families, Hamming > 0) or domain-same (Hamming = 0, both isoforms carrying ≥1 domain); exact-duplicate pairs (<10 differing residues in the aligned sequence encoding) were removed. At each layer, the ratio mean(cosine distance | domain-different)/mean(… | domain-same) was computed; the null is 1,000 shuffles of the domain-different/same labels; length control used |length difference|-matched strata and partial Spearman of divergence on domain Hamming controlling for length difference. **(4) Axis biology.** Interpreting the 8 joint-PCA axes requires ruling out, at each step, that an apparent biological correlate is actually a gene-family confound (the representation-level analogue of the gene-identity dominance quantified for macro AUPRC in §Discussion: Interpreting macro AUPRC). Three sequential checks address this. *First*, each axis was screened by a null-anchored between/within-gene variance decomposition before any biological interpretation was attempted: one-way ANOVA partitions each axis's total variance into between-gene and within-gene components (within_frac = SS_within/SS_total), compared against the label-shuffle null expectation within_frac_null = (N−G)/(N−1) (N = isoforms, G = genes; verified against 20 explicit label-shuffle permutations). All 8 axes showed within_frac at 24–39% of this null expectation, confirming that gene-family identity dominates the trajectory representation at every axis — but non-uniformly: axes 3 and 6 retained the highest relative within-gene variance fraction (ratio 0.377–0.385, versus 0.242–0.281 for axes 4 and 0), identifying them as the axes most likely to carry isoform-level (rather than purely gene-family-level) structure, and axis 0's simultaneous status as the highest-explained-variance and most gene-family-dominated axis is expected under PCA applied to hierarchically clustered data: because between-gene sequence divergence is the dominant source of total variance in the pooled representation, PCA's top variance-maximising direction is biased toward the between-gene contrast, pushing within-gene (isoform-level) structure toward lower-variance components. *Second*, each axis scalar was correlated (Spearman) with 22 sequence-intrinsic features (secondary-structure fractions, GRAVY, aromaticity, instability, pI, charge fractions, residue-class fractions, compositional entropy, transmembrane-segment count, N-terminal hydrophobicity, domain count) at both gene level (mean axis score versus mean feature value per gene) and within-gene-residual level (each isoform's deviation from its gene's mean axis score versus its deviation from its gene's mean feature value). The residual-level correlation is the discriminating test: a feature correlating with an axis at gene level but not at the residual level reflects a gene-family covariate rather than genuine within-gene structural encoding. This criterion is what excludes motif features (SLiM regular-expression counts) as axis correlates: all SLiM features showed gene-level correlation with at least one axis but null correlation at the within-gene-residual level, indicating that any apparent motif–axis association reflects gene-family composition rather than isoform-intrinsic motif content. *Third*, for axes with weak continuous-feature correlation after step two (axis 1, axis 6; |ρ| ≈ 0.29–0.30), Pfam domain-family membership was additionally tested via top-vs-bottom score-tercile log-odds enrichment rather than continuous correlation, because domain-family presence is a sparse binary indicator for which correlation coefficients are diluted both by the uninformative middle tercile and by low base-rate variance; log-odds enrichment concentrates statistical power on the axis extremes and yields a ranked, interpretable list of the domain families defining each pole (families with ≥30 isoforms). **(5) Within-family conditioning control.** To test whether the within-gene axis structure is an artefact of implicit gene-level background subtraction, the joint-PCA axes were re-derived under explicit gene-family conditioning. Each isoform's per-layer z-scored embedding had its gene-family mean subtracted (family-centred residual), and a single pooled 8-component PCA was fit over the residuals — the within-family scatter analogue of the within-class scatter matrix in linear discriminant analysis. Gene-family was defined two ways: (i) the dominant Pfam accession per gene (isoforms of genes lacking any Pfam domain form singleton families), and (ii) all-versus-all sequence-homology clusters of each gene's longest protein (DIAMOND cluster, --approx-id 30 --member-cover 50). The family-centred basis was compared with the uncentred joint basis, the original muscle-fit basis, and across the two family definitions by principal angles (singular values of WᴬᵀWᴮ between the two 8×640 orthonormal bases). Family-specificity of the within-gene variance elevation was assessed against a family-size-preserving label-shuffle null (10 permutations). The length-independent domain axis (axis 3) was cross-validated against BISECT domain-switch cases: for 59 curated domain-change cases mapped to the brain set, sign concordance between the within-gene axis-3 difference and the net Pfam domain gain/loss was tested (binomial); because both isoforms of each case share a gene and therefore a gene-family, the family-centring leaves their axis difference unchanged, isolating the within-gene component. **(6) Causal occlusion.** Each of the eight joint-PCA axes was projected out of the test-set embeddings at both input layers (for layer L, z-score to per-layer statistics, subtract the rank-one projection onto the axis, invert the z-score) and PRISM v17f* re-predicted on the modified inputs; the change in within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC and gene-level macro-AUPRC was measured against a null of 20 random unit directions occluded identically (z-scores relative to this null). A retrain-with-occlusion variant removed the axis from both training and test embeddings and retrained the 5-seed ensemble, distinguishing predictive use (test-time occlusion) from task necessity/redundancy (retrain). To adjudicate whether axis 0's negative within-gene occlusion z reflects a learned within-gene down-weighting or passive weight leakage, each axis's within-gene residual (isoform score minus its gene mean) was correlated (Spearman) with the within-gene residual of Pfam domain count over domain-varying multi-isoform genes on the Domain-Ranking set, and the within-gene residual amplitude (standard deviation) of axis 0 was compared to that of axis 3; a leakage account predicts non-zero axis-0 within-gene amplitude (variation present to leak) combined with near-zero domain alignment (the leaked variation is label-misaligned), given that v17f* contains no gene-conditional gating and therefore cannot apply an axis-specific weight only within genes. **(7) Categorical domain decodability.** For within-gene isoform pairs, a length-difference-decile-matched logistic classifier was trained on the eight per-axis absolute differences to predict the binary presence/absence of a Pfam domain-architecture change (Hamming > 0), reporting mean held-out AUROC across deciles; this tests categorical domain-change detection separately from the graded domain-count correlation. An identically-matched classifier reading the full 640-dimensional z-scored layer-mean representation (the basis on which the eight axes are defined) provides the ESM-2 ceiling for the same task, quantifying the domain information lost to the eight-axis compression; the linear ceiling was additionally compared against a non-linear gradient-boosted classifier (HistGradientBoosting, 200 trees, depth 3) on both the eight-axis and 640-dimensional representations to separate representational limit from decoder linearity. **(8) Per-isoform trajectory divergence.** For each BISECT domain-switch case, the per-layer absolute difference in axis-3 score between the two isoforms was computed and its peak layer recorded; the distribution of peak layers was compared to that of random within-gene isoform pairs (Mann–Whitney). Per-isoform axis-3 and axis-5 trajectories over the 30 layers were plotted for NDUFS2/7/4 and NDUFAF5. Figures: axis × feature heatmap and per-axis relationship panels (Supplementary Fig. S_axes); per-isoform trajectory panels (Supplementary Fig. S_traj). **(9) Capacity/supervision bracket of the Domain-Ranking ceiling.** To localise whether the within-gene Domain-Ranking ceiling is model- or representation-bound, two families of intervention were run on the v17f* architecture with the identical [δ_layer, φ_L30] input, focal objective and five-seed protocol. *Capacity:* a head-width sweep holding hidden-layer count fixed (two-layer heads 256→128, 512→256, 768→384; the reported +0.013 gain is the depth-controlled 512→256-versus-256→128 contrast, isolating width from depth) with each head evaluated on gene-level macro AUPRC (paired term-level bootstrap, n=1,000) and on within-gene Domain-Ranking with a gene-clustered bootstrap that resamples whole genes (rather than the correlated gene–term ranking units) against a pre-specified non-inferiority margin of −0.01. *Supervision:* a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss added to the focal objective — for every training gene with domain-varying isoforms, all ordered isoform pairs (higher versus lower Pfam domain count) contribute max(0, m − (s_i − s_j)) over that gene's positive GO terms (margin m = 0.1), summed over 3,097 within-gene pairs from 508 genes and swept over loss weight λ ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1.0}; both macro AUPRC and Domain-Ranking were compared to the λ = 0 baseline. Train and test genes are disjoint, so a Domain-Ranking change would reflect transfer of the ordering principle across genes, not label leakage. A surgical variant of this margin loss restricts the per-gene positive-term mask to the domain-dependent term subset only (the 33 GO terms classified L2_Structural by the domain-vs-PRISM delta criterion of §Methods; 1,862 within-gene pairs after this restriction, mean 2.17 domain-dependent positive terms per pair) — otherwise identical architecture, objective, and evaluation protocol — to test whether the blanket loss's mis-directed gradient on motif-dependent terms, rather than the representation, was the binding constraint on Domain-Ranking. These experiments accompany the conditional domain-count regression auxiliary loss described in §6b.

### pos_bias analysis

**pos_bias** for GO term k is defined as:

> **pos_bias(k) = mean_{g ∈ G_k^+} σ_g(k) / σ_global(k)**

where G_k^+ = {g ∈ G : y_{g,k} = 1} is the set of positive-class genes for GO term k with ≥2 test isoforms, σ_g(k) = std_{i: g(i)=g} [f_θ(φ(i))_k] is the within-gene prediction standard deviation, and σ_global(k) = std over all test isoforms of f_θ(φ(i))_k. pos_bias > 1 implies that positive-class genes show higher within-gene score variation than the global baseline; pos_bias = 0 by construction for gene-mean oracle (all isoforms identical scores).

The label-shuffled null floor is estimated by gene-level label permutation: gene assignments to G_k^+ are shuffled (random subset of same size drawn without replacement) and pos_bias recomputed; the bootstrap mean across B=1,000 resamples defines the null floor. The observed pos_bias significantly exceeds the null when the fraction of permuted bootstrap samples ≥ obs_pos_bias is less than α. For v15d muscle (18 BP terms): null floor ≈ 0.24; for v17f* true brain MF/BP/CC: null floors ≈ 0.217–0.265.

Bootstrap CI and p-values: B=1,000 gene-level block bootstraps (resample genes with replacement, all isoforms of each resampled gene included). Significance: bootstrap p-value (fraction of gene-level shuffled bootstrap samples ≥ observed pos_bias), Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at 5%. Analysis applied to: (i) v15d muscle, K=13 GO BP terms; (ii) v17f* true brain zero-shot, K=278 GO terms (82 MF + 103 BP + 93 CC). All computations in Python 3.10 (NumPy 1.24); scripts available at [Code availability].

### Canonical-anchored severity regression

To test whether the two tissues differ not only in the *rate* at which alternative-splicing events remodel a Pfam domain (main text, §6) but also in the *magnitude* of representational consequence per edit, we constructed a canonical-anchored within-gene pair set spanning all multi-isoform genes (not restricted to the two-isoform subset used for the domain-envelope rate analysis). For each gene, the canonical isoform was identified by a three-tier priority — MANE Select, then Ensembl_canonical, then APPRIS principal isoform 1, then (if none of the above is present in the expressed isoform set) the isoform with the longest CDS in the dataset — and paired against every other isoform of that gene as an independent edit event. For muscle, the existing GENCODE-v44-derived canonical reference table (gene_id/transcript_id keyed) was used directly. For brain, an equivalent reference was built for this analysis: brain isoform identifiers follow GENCODE's transcript_name convention (e.g., "AADAT-201"), so the same GENCODE v44 GTF was re-parsed keyed on gene_name/transcript_name to assign MANE/Ensembl_canonical/APPRIS tags, with novel (non-reference-matched) transcripts falling back to the longest-CDS tier. This yielded 15,493 muscle pairs (from 8,569 multi-isoform genes; 87.2% MANE/Ensembl/APPRIS-tier canonical) and 33,148 brain pairs (from 12,157 genes; 86.6% MANE/Ensembl/APPRIS-tier canonical).

For each pair, the outcome (severity score) is the out-of-fold projection of the pair's embedding difference onto a gene-disjoint-fold-trained coherence direction: per-isoform mean-pooled ESM-2 representations at layers 15 and 30 are concatenated (1,280-dim), the pair's difference vector is formed (longer isoform minus shorter, independent of which is canonical), and each pair is scored by its dot product with the L2-normalised mean difference vector of the training folds (5-fold, genes never split across folds), so that no pair contributes to its own scoring direction. This mean-pooling-based construction was chosen over a max-pooling alternative after a length-artefact control: because the longer isoform is always the numerator of the pair by construction, max-pooling's per-dimension maximum is a monotonically increasing function of sequence length under extreme-value order statistics regardless of sequence content, and a content-scrambled, length-preserved null (residues resampled with replacement from a tissue-wide pool while holding each pair's true isoform lengths fixed) reproduced 63–92% of max-pooling's apparent direction-accuracy gain in 6 of 7 tested categories, with the max-pooling discriminant axis itself geometrically aligned with the pure length-artefact axis (cosine similarity 0.92–0.98). Mean-pooling's own length-scrambled null was at chance in every category (cosine with the length-artefact axis ≈ 0), consistent with the mathematical expectation that the sample mean's expectation, unlike the sample maximum, is invariant to sample size.

Five structural covariates were computed per pair from protein sequence alignment (SequenceMatcher opcode analysis) and existing domain/exon annotations: (i) size, the total changed-residue count (log(1+count)-transformed then z-scored; the log transform linearises the heavy right-tail of edit sizes, under which the raw z-scored count enters non-significantly); (ii) domain-architecture change, a binary indicator (muscle: change in total Pfam domain count via the existing domain matrix; brain: hmmscan envelope overlap between the changed interval and a Pfam domain, E ≤ 0.01, matching the domain-envelope method above); (iii) N-terminal overlap, a binary indicator for whether the first changed residue position is within the first 60 residues; (iv) disorder fraction, the fraction of the longer isoform's changed-interval residues with a positive (disorder-promoting) TOP-IDP score; (v) alignment-resync failure, a binary indicator computed as follows: from the first changed position in the shorter isoform, the longest single contiguous matching ("equal") alignment block is identified as a fraction of the remaining sequence length; this fraction falling below 0.5 is scored as resync failure. This indicator is a protein-alignment-level heuristic for edits after which the two sequences fail to re-align into one coherent downstream block (consistent with, but not proof of, a nucleotide-level frameshift, since no genomic coordinate or reading-frame information is used); we therefore report it descriptively as an alignment-persistence proxy rather than as validated frameshift ground truth.

**Compositional direction test (non-domain residual).** Separately from the five-covariate magnitude regression above, four amino-acid-propensity descriptors of the changed residues were tested for within-gene direction consistency on the non-domain internal-edit subset (domain-architecture-change = 0 and N-terminal overlap = 0; 4,185 muscle, 11,666 brain pairs, after requiring both isoforms present with a non-empty changed interval): mean Chou-Fasman helix propensity (Pα) and sheet propensity (Pβ), mean Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity, and net physiological-pH charge per changed residue, each averaged over the longer isoform's changed positions. For each descriptor a median-split orientation (+1 if the descriptor exceeds the subset median, −1 otherwise) was applied to the L15+L30 mean-pooled embedding difference, and gene-disjoint 5-fold cross-validated direction accuracy (CV-dir-acc — the fraction of held-out pairs whose oriented difference projects positively onto the training-fold mean direction) was compared against two nulls: (a) a gene-permutation null (gene identities shuffled before fold assignment; 10 permutations), testing whether the direction reflects a cross-gene-shared orientation rather than a gene-independent population bias; and (b) — where the gene-permutation null was uninformative because the direction was fold-universal at large sample size (brain sheet propensity: real 0.653, permuted 0.653) — a label-permutation null (random sign-flip of each pair's difference vector; 10 permutations), testing whether the direction reflects genuine long/short structure (brain sheet propensity real 0.653 vs. label-permuted 0.500). This test is direction-level (does the residual point a consistent way?), not magnitude-level, and is reported separately from the five-covariate severity regression for that reason. Whether the current model uses these compositional directions for prediction was additionally tested by applying the identical median-split orientation instrument to PRISM's predicted GO-score difference in place of the embedding difference (`b4_compositional_usage.py`): the consistency attenuates from 0.59–0.65 at the embedding to 0.53–0.58 at the output, indistinguishable from the gene-permutation null in both tissues, indicating the mean-pooled model does not propagate the compositional axis to its functional prediction. Scripts: `test_covariate_gene_permutation_null.py`, `test_sheet_delta_label_permutation.py`, `analyze_sheet_delta_artifact.py`, `b4_compositional_usage.py` (committed in `hMuscle/model/`).

A committed reconstruction of this pipeline — canonical-anchored pair construction and coherence-projection scoring (`build_severity_pairs.py`, `build_severity_score.py`), the §6-consistent tissue-specific domain-architecture-change covariate (`build_severity_domain_faithful.py`; muscle: total-Pfam-count change, brain: hmmscan-envelope overlap of the changed interval, E ≤ 0.01, exactly the domain-envelope definition used above), and the gene-clustered regression (`severity_regression.py`) — reproduces the qualitative structure reported here: the sign and significance of all five covariates, the brain-greater-than-muscle ordering of the domain-architecture coefficient, the muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync coefficients, and in particular the canonical-orientation-conditional domain effect (significant in the 89.1% majority stratum where the canonical isoform is the longer, and null in the reverse 10.9% stratum). Absolute coefficient magnitudes differ modestly from the reported values, which derive from the original fit, because the gene-disjoint coherence direction is re-estimated in each reconstruction.

Ordinary least squares was fit with cluster-robust (CR1 sandwich) standard errors, clustered on tissue-qualified gene identity (to prevent gene-symbol collisions across the two tissues' independent namespaces), computed via a vectorised per-cluster score-sum formulation (S = per-cluster sum of leverage-weighted residual contributions; meat matrix = SᵀS) for tractability at cluster counts exceeding 30,000. Statistical significance used a permutation null (1,000 permutations of the outcome across pairs, refitting the full model each time) for the model R², and Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the five substantive covariates for individual coefficients. A within-gene fixed-effects specification (each gene's pairs and covariates demeaned by their gene-cluster mean, restricted to genes contributing ≥2 pairs) was fit as a robustness check against gene-level confounding, analogous in purpose to the gene-mean-oracle check used elsewhere in this paper for macro AUPRC.

**Mediation follow-up.** A subsequent analysis traced the apparent muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync-failure coefficients (above) through a three-layer surrogate chain: N-terminal overlap does not survive confound-controlled discrimination testing (size-matched AUC at chance in both tissues); the resync-failure ordering is a downstream trace of a genuine size asymmetry (independently confirmed by gene-cluster bootstrap and tissue-label permutation); and this size asymmetry is itself 89.2%-explained by edit fragmentation (number of separate change intervals per edit), a broad-based effect (>2,000 genes per tissue) with no detectable GO functional enrichment. Full statistics, robustness checks, and reproduction scripts (`resync_mechanical_test.py`, `size_severity_tissue_gap.py`, `dispersion_mechanism_test.py`, `dispersion_rigor_and_concentration.py`, `tissue_compare_and_enrichment.py`) are reported in **Supplementary Note S_severity**.

### BISECT pipeline

15 sequential evidence modules applied to each candidate isoform switch pair:

- **M1 (Pfam domain)**: hmmscan (Pfam-A r36.0, E < 0.01). n_lost, n_gained, domain identities.
- **M2/M10 (PPI)**: STRING v12.0 [19] experimental channel (confidence ≥ 900). Pfam domain-to-PPI partner mapping for lost domains.
- **M3/M15 (NMD)**: SQANTI3 structural category + PTC/EJC distance rule (>50 nt upstream of final EJC). M15 adds EJC coordinate-based exact PTC distance.
- **M4/M11 (AlphaFold pLDDT)**: Per-residue pLDDT from AlphaFold DB v4 [11] (UniProt ID mapping). Isoform-level mean and domain-level pLDDT.
- **M5 (phyloConservation)**: UCSC phyloP 100-way per-exon mean. Switches in highly conserved exons (mean phyloP > 3.0) flagged.
- **M9 (localization)**: MTS score from TargetP-2.0 [20] (MTS probability); MTS score difference CT vs AD.
- **M14 (exon comparator)**: Shared exon count, total exons CT/AD, event_type (ALE/cassette/RI/alt-TSS).
- **M16 (mechanism classifier)**: Priority order: NMD switch > ALE (shared==0) > retained intron > major truncation (>200 aa + domain loss) > domain loss/gain > cassette exon cluster > alt TSS > minor exon.
- **M17 (convergence detector)**: Co-occurrence scoring across convergence axes (Complex I, RNA metabolism, DNA repair, KRAB-ZFP, DOCK-family).

Six-tier hierarchy: Tier A-DR (DRIMSeq OFDR ≤ 5%), Tier A-BPC (Bonferroni-corrected permutation p_adj < 0.010, batch-concordant; confirmed), Tier A-BPP (permutation p < 0.05 uncorrected + ≥1 structural module PASS, batch-concordant; preliminary, replication-required), Tier B (independent cohort replication), Tier C (exploratory, no donor-level stats), Tier D (hypothesis-generating, insufficient evidence).

**BISECT–PRISM independence**: BISECT modules M1–M17 operate exclusively on structural and orthogonal evidence sources (Pfam-A hmmscan, AlphaFold pLDDT, TargetP-2.0 MTS scores, STRING PPI networks, UCSC phyloP conservation, SQANTI3 structural categories, DRIMSeq transcript usage). No PRISM predictions are used as input to any BISECT module. PRISM scores are computed independently and compared post-hoc to BISECT-nominated switch pairs to assess convergence between sequence-based functional prediction and structure-based mechanistic analysis; convergence at a locus therefore constitutes independent corroboration rather than circular validation.

### Beyond-gene-label GO acquisition (Case A/B) and literature cross-check

Using the true brain zero-shot score matrix (63,994 isoforms, 18 GO BP terms, threshold 0.5), genes were partitioned by their gene2go label vector into fully-negative (0/18 terms positive) and partially-positive (≥1/18 positive) sets. Case A: for fully-negative genes, any isoform with max score > 0.5 on any term. Case B: for partially-positive genes, any isoform for which a non-annotated term's score exceeds both 0.5 and that isoform's own maximum score among its gene's annotated terms. Null 1 (Case A): background crossing rate computed over all gene-level-negative (isoform, term) pairs genome-wide (1,133,987 pairs). Null 2 (Case B): within-isoform column-shuffle — each isoform's 18-term score vector was permuted across term labels (20 trials, fixed seed), preserving each isoform's own score distribution while randomising which term receives which score; z-score computed against the resulting null distribution. Literature cross-check: the 40 highest-scoring Case A instances and all 64 Case B instances (104 total (gene, GO-term) pairs) were queried against the UniProt REST API (`rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb`, reviewed human entries) for GO Biological Process cross-references and evidence codes; a pair was scored literature-supported if any returned BP term carried a direct experimental evidence code (IDA, IMP, IPI, IGI, or IEP) and named the same or a closely-matching biological process to the PRISM-predicted term, based on manual review of the returned term set (computational/homology-inferred codes IEA/ISS/IBA were not counted as direct evidence). Novelty stratification (Type A/B/C) of the 23 literature-supported pairs: each pair's canonical UniProt "Function" description was reviewed against the predicted GO term and classified as Type A if the predicted term matched the gene's near-definitional, textbook-level function (e.g., an ion channel scored positive for the corresponding ion-transport/homeostasis term), Type B if the predicted term reflected a genuinely documented but non-headline aspect of the gene's biology requiring knowledge beyond its one-line functional descriptor, or Type C if the connection relied on a generic mechanistic pathway (e.g., "GPCRs can signal via calcium") without gene-specific documented evidence for the predicted process; classifications were independently verified against UniProt REST API function records (three of 23 draft calls were revised — two Type A→B, one Type B→A — following this verification pass; §Results). Two candidates (ULK3, GAS6) with no matching UniProt/QuickGO annotation were additionally cross-checked by general literature search (§Results) to rule out curation lag; both showed documented biology adjacent to the predicted process (ciliary Hedgehog signalling; TAM-receptor-mediated synapse elimination, respectively) but not the specific predicted GO process itself, and are not counted among the 23 literature-supported pairs. Null 3 (matched-null enrichment test for the 22.1% rate): to test whether the literature-support rate of the PRISM-selected tail exceeds the ambient rate expected from the same gene populations, the 104 pairs were re-screened by an automated, fully-offline version of the same criterion, and compared against matched random pairs. The automated criterion counts a pair (gene, target term) as supported if the gene carries a BP GO annotation with a direct experimental evidence code (IDA/IMP/IPI/IGI/IEP; NCBI gene2go, human) whose term lies within the GO-DAG closure of the target term (target ∪ ancestors ∪ descendants under is_a/part_of; go-basic 2019-01, parsed with networkx). Matched random pairs were drawn preserving population strata and target-term distribution: Case A nulls sampled genes uniformly from the 15,858 fully-negative brain genes with target terms drawn from the observed Case A term frequencies; Case B nulls sampled genes uniformly from the 2,656 partially-annotated brain genes with foreign target terms (not annotated to the gene) drawn from the observed Case B term frequencies; B = 2,000 resamples of 104 pairs each. Enrichment was quantified as the observed automated support rate versus the null mean ± SD, with a bootstrap 95% CI, z-score, lift, and empirical p = (#{null ≥ observed} + 1)/(B + 1). Three closeness definitions were reported: strict is_a/part_of closure, regulates-inclusive closure (approximating the manual "closely-matching" judgment), and a loose floor counting any experimental BP annotation (study-bias control). The automated observed rate (6.7–8.7%) is a conservative lower bound on the manual 22.1% because the GO-DAG closure captures fewer biological-adjacency relations than manual review. Implementation: `hMuscle/model/exp_table1_matched_null.py`; results `reports/novel_go_matched_null/results.json`. Full results: Supplementary Table S_novelGO.

### Statistical analysis

All AUPRC values: macro-averaged (unweighted mean across GO terms). Bootstrap confidence intervals: n=1,000 resamples. For v17d AUPRC CIs, gene-level block bootstrap was used: test genes were resampled with replacement (N=12,706 genes), with all isoforms of each sampled gene included, to account for within-gene correlation in predictions; 2.5th/97.5th percentiles define the 95% CI. For pos_bias CIs: isoform-level bootstrap within each gene block. Multiplicity corrections: Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 5% within each test family. Mann-Whitney U: two-sided, no continuity correction. All analyses performed in Python 3.10 (scikit-learn 1.3, scipy 1.11, pandas 2.0) and R 4.3 (DRIMSeq 1.26, stageR 1.26).

### Gene-level versus isoform-level AUPRC decomposition

Because NCBI gene2go GO annotations are assigned at the gene level — all isoforms of a given gene share an identical label vector — macro AUPRC on this test set measures the ability to identify which gene a query isoform belongs to as its primary signal. To quantify this decomposition, we performed a cross-gene shuffle test: v17f* predictions for each isoform were randomly reassigned to a different gene's isoform set (preserving within-gene prediction structure by moving all isoforms of the same gene together to the same destination gene; repeated 1,000 times), then macro AUPRC was recomputed. Cross-gene shuffling reduces v17f* macro AUPRC from 0.734 to 0.048 (mean; random baseline 0.345). The fraction of AUPRC signal above the random baseline attributable to gene identity is (0.734 − 0.048) / (0.734 − 0.345) = **97.7%**. A complementary within-gene shuffle — permuting predictions among isoforms of the *same* gene without crossing gene boundaries — produces no AUPRC drop (0.734 ± 0.001), confirming that macro AUPRC is insensitive to the assignment of scores within a gene but highly sensitive to which gene the isoforms are attributed to.

This 97.7% figure is a **metric property** of the gene-level label structure and gene-grouped evaluation design, not a biological claim that 97.7% of functional variation is gene-level rather than isoform-level. Isoforms of the same gene receive identical labels regardless of their actual functional differences; any metric computed under this label structure will show high gene-level attribution by construction — including a hypothetical perfect isoform-specific predictor trained on the same labels. The 97.7% figure measures the evaluation design's gene-level label structure, not the model's biological scope. Independent within-gene discrimination metrics are immune to this confound because each comparison contrasts two isoforms from the same gene, making gene identity uninformative by construction: Domain-Ranking AUC (whether PRISM ranks domain-complete isoforms above domain-truncated variants within the same gene; AUC = 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] held-out muscle, 0.775 true-brain zero-shot; gene-mean mathematical null = 0.500) and UniProt pairwise direction accuracy (whether PRISM correctly orders experimentally characterised isoform pairs from the same gene; 11/11 = 100% at prediction gap ≥ 0.10, permutation p < 0.001) constitute the primary evidence for isoform-specific discrimination capacity. The brain gene-mean oracle (AUPRC 0.795; §Gene-level evaluation paragraph in Discussion) likewise exceeds v17f* brain zero-shot (0.647), confirming that macro AUPRC gene-level dominance holds across tissues and training distributions.

DeepFRI covered-term AUPRC (65/81 terms with DeepFRI vocabulary overlap): 0.123, confirming this is not an artefact of zero-padding the 16 missing terms; (ii) DeepGoPlus (CNN+DIAMOND, 0.441) — despite full CNN coverage and 74.3% homology hits — falls **below** Gene-mean ESM-2 retrieval (0.465, no homology), because the CNN component trained on canonical Swiss-Prot sequences introduces noise that the α=0.66 blending cannot overcome; this demonstrates that sequence homology with a distribution-mismatched CNN is worse than zero-shot gene-family retrieval from evolutionary embeddings; (iii) k-NN retrieval (0.636) approaches direct MF training (D0: 0.660), establishing that isoform-level ESM-2 embedding-space retrieval alone — with no task-specific training at all — captures nearly as much gene-family MF signal as a fully supervised MLP; (iv) fine-tuning ESM-2 (D1: 0.660 All MF / 0.520 L2) is statistically indistinguishable from frozen (D0: 0.660 / 0.521; 95% CIs fully overlapping for both metrics), demonstrating that neither overall nor structural MF prediction benefits from task-specific fine-tuning of 24.62M ESM-2 parameters at the cost of 148 min GPU training; (v) v17f* (0.734) exceeds the best frozen direct-training (D0: 0.660) by +0.074, achieves 6.6× the DeepFRI AUPRC, 24× its L2_Structural AUPRC, and 1.66× the DeepGoPlus AUPRC; (vi) v17f* (no T_ψ: 0.734) exceeds v17f (with T_ψ: 0.717) by +0.017, establishing that T_ψ dimensionality reduction (640→64) is an information bottleneck that reduces performance — the δ_layer contribution derives from its full 640-dim representation, not from triplet-based organisation; (vii) BLAST→GOA and isopretEM both reach 0.861 / 0.802 — higher than all learned methods — but this reflects a gene-identification oracle effect: BLAST coverage of 68.5% (25,159/36,748 isoforms) is gene-level, meaning once any isoform of gene g matches a Swiss-Prot sequence, all isoforms of g inherit its gene-level GO labels; isopretEM within-gene EM does not improve over direct BLAST→GOA because the test labels are gene-level, so bitscore-weighted redistribution within a gene does not add discriminative information. These methods serve as an empirical ceiling for sequence-homology approaches under gene-level labels, not as isoform-discriminative predictors.

## Supplementary Note S1: Mathematical basis of spontaneous isoform discrimination

### S1.1 Problem setup

Let I = {i_1, ..., i_N} be the set of N isoforms with gene-of-origin g: I → G and gene-level GO labels y_g ∈ {0,1}^K. Define the training label Y_{i,k} := y_{g(i),k} — a deterministic assignment that gives each isoform the GO labels of its parent gene. Let φ: I → ℝ^D (D=640) be the frozen ESM-2 embedding. PRISM learns f_θ: ℝ^D → [0,1]^K by minimising:

> L(θ) = Σ_{i∈I} Σ_{k=1}^K FL( f_θ(φ(i))_k, Y_{i,k} )

For interpretive purposes, under standard binary cross-entropy with a well-calibrated model, the converged function satisfies f_θ(φ(i))_k ≈ P(Y_{i,k}=1 | φ(i)) — the posterior probability of GO term k given embedding φ(i) under the empirical training distribution. **Two conditions are necessary for this approximation to hold**: (a) the MLP hypothesis class is sufficiently expressive to represent the true posterior over ℝ^{640}; (b) the loss function is a proper scoring rule attaining its minimum at the true posterior. Focal loss (γ=2) satisfies (a) but not (b) exactly: by reweighting training examples by (1-p_t)^γ, the minimiser of L is the true posterior under a distribution that overweights hard boundary examples relative to confident examples. In practice, this distortion enhances within-gene isoform discrimination (see S1.3 for the gradient analysis) at the cost of strict probability calibration. We use posterior notation as an approximation to guide interpretation, not as a claim of exact calibration.

### S1.2 Why different isoforms of the same gene receive different scores

**Proposition.** Let i_a, i_b ∈ I with g(i_a) = g(i_b) = g and Y_{i_a,k} = Y_{i_b,k} = 1. If φ(i_a) and φ(i_b) lie in different functional neighbourhoods of ℝ^D — regions whose cross-gene label statistics differ — PRISM assigns f_θ(φ(i_a))_k ≠ f_θ(φ(i_b))_k despite identical training labels.

**Mechanism.** f_θ is trained simultaneously on all isoforms in I: isoforms of the same gene are independent training examples, and f_θ receives no gene-identity signal. The parameters θ are therefore shaped by the cross-gene statistical structure of the training data: the final-layer weight w_k ∈ ℝ^{64} points in the direction most consistently associated with Y_{·,k}=1 across all isoforms from all genes.

Now consider canonical isoform i_a whose embedding φ(i_a) lies in a region of ℝ^{640} populated by structurally similar proteins across many genes (e.g., PDZ-domain-containing proteins). If PDZ-domain proteins are systematically positive for GO term k across many training genes — because PDZ domains mediate interactions defining that function — then the cross-gene posterior P(Y_{·,k}=1 | φ ≈ φ(i_a)) is high, and f_θ(φ(i_a))_k is accordingly high.

Conversely, if exon-skipping in isoform i_b removes the PDZ domain, φ(i_b) shifts into a different region of ℝ^{640} populated by domain-lacking sequences from other genes. If these sequences are predominantly negative for GO term k across training genes, then P(Y_{·,k}=1 | φ ≈ φ(i_b)) is low. f_θ(φ(i_b))_k is low, despite Y_{i_b,k} = 1.

**Why does the training penalty at i_b not force f_θ(φ(i_b))_k toward 1?** The focal loss gradient at i_b is non-zero and initially pushes its score upward. However, the same embedding neighbourhood contains many other isoforms j from different genes with Y_{j,k}=0, whose gradients push f_θ downward in the same region. The model converges to the cross-gene optimum — satisfying the population-level label statistics — even at the cost of violating individual gene-level labels for non-functional-domain isoforms. Focal loss (γ=2) accelerates this convergence: it downweights gradients from confident cases (canonical isoforms already correctly scored) by (1-p_t)^γ (see S1.3), allowing the cross-gene negative evidence at non-canonical isoforms to dominate during training.

**Critical distinction: cross-gene vs. within-gene features.** This mechanism depends on a key property of ESM-2 embeddings: exon-skipping events that alter protein structure (domain loss, truncation, hydrophobic surface change) produce consistent embedding shifts, and these shifts align with the structural variation encoded by ESM-2 across its evolutionary training corpus. ESM-2 encodes the absolute sequence-level features of each isoform independently — it does not encode which isoform is "canonical" within a gene, nor what the within-gene functional hierarchy is. When i_b lacks a PDZ domain, φ(i_b) occupies the same embedding region as all other PDZ-lacking proteins regardless of their gene-of-origin. Cross-gene label statistics are thus consistently structured around structural features, enabling gene-level supervision to generate isoform discrimination as a by-product. This also explains why explicitly providing δ_i = φ(i) − μ_g as input fails: δ_i encodes within-gene deviation, which has no consistent label-predictive direction across genes under gene-level supervision (S1.4).

**DLG1 example.** y_{DLG1,synaptic} = 1. In the training set, PDZ-domain-containing proteins from multiple genes are enriched for synaptic transmission positivity (GO:0007268), because PDZ domains mediate glutamate receptor anchoring and postsynaptic density scaffolding. ESM-2 encodes PDZ structural features (conserved β-barrel topology, C-terminal ligand-binding groove) consistently; DLG1-201 (906 aa; PDZ1/2/3 intact) occupies the same embedding region as PDZ-containing proteins from other genes. f_θ assigns P(y_{synaptic}=1 | φ(DLG1-201)) ≈ 0.88. Isoform tr319500 (186 aa; no PDZ domains): ESM-2 encodes this sequence as a short peptide lacking the PDZ structural signature. Domain-lacking short isoforms from many genes are predominantly y=0 for synapse-function GO terms; f_θ assigns P(y_{synaptic}=1 | φ(tr319500)) ≈ 0.033. The 27-fold differential traces to cross-gene embedding statistics, not to any within-gene supervision signal.

### S1.3 Role of focal loss γ=2 in isoform discrimination

For binary focal loss FL(p, y) = −α_y(1 − p_t)^γ log(p_t) with p_t = p if y=1, (1-p) if y=0, the exact gradient with respect to pre-activation z (before sigmoid) for y=1 is derived from ∂FL/∂z = (∂FL/∂p)·p(1-p):

> **|∂FL/∂z| = α (1−p)^γ [ γp |log p| + (1−p) ]**

where the (1-p)^γ factor is the focal reweighting term and [γp|log p| + (1-p)] is the composite bracket including the log-derivative correction. Numerical evaluation for γ=2:

| p_t | (1−p)^γ | bracket | |∂FL/∂z| (α=1) | context |
|-----|---------|---------|--------------|---------|
| 0.95 | 0.0025 | 0.147 | 3.7 × 10^{−4} | Confident canonical isoform |
| 0.50 | 0.2500 | 1.193 | 2.98 × 10^{−1} | Boundary non-canonical isoform |
| 0.10 | 0.8100 | 0.290 | 2.35 × 10^{−1} | Hard positive (non-functional isoform, gene label=1) |

**Boundary/confident ratio: 0.298 / 3.7×10^{−4} ≈ 810-fold.** For reference, standard cross-entropy (γ=0) gives a boundary/confident ratio of 0.50/0.05 = 10-fold; focal loss achieves 81-fold relative amplification over cross-entropy. The simplified (1-p_t)^γ ratio alone (0.25/0.0025 = 100-fold) underestimates the actual amplification by ~8×, because the log-bracket term [γp|log p| + (1-p)] at p=0.5 is 8.1× larger than at p=0.95.

**Two distinct consequences for PRISM.** First, focal loss prevents mode collapse on sparse GO terms (positive prevalence < 5%): without γ>0, sparse classes receive overwhelming gradient from easy negatives, driving all predictions toward zero. This is a class-imbalance phenomenon independent of within-gene discrimination. Second, and separately, focal loss amplifies gradient from boundary examples — non-canonical isoforms whose ESM-2 embedding places them near the functional decision boundary (score 0.3–0.7). These are structurally intermediate isoforms where cross-gene label statistics are most informative for discrimination. By concentrating ~810× more relative gradient weight on these cases than on confidently-scored canonical isoforms, focal loss reinforces the cross-gene consistency constraint precisely for the isoforms where within-gene discrimination matters. These two consequences — class-level anti-collapse and boundary-amplified cross-gene learning — are mechanistically distinct and should not be conflated.

### S1.4 Why explicit within-gene discriminators fail: the label–discriminator conflict

**Setup.** The v15d_splice model concatenated δ_i = φ(i) − μ_{g(i)} (within-gene embedding deviation, where μ_g is the gene-mean embedding) through a learned gate G_δ, adding a within-gene discriminator objective to increase within-gene score variance.

**Observed failure pattern.** G_δ → 1.0 (gate fully saturates, maximally using δ_i) while AUPRC collapses from 0.7022 to 0.4573 (−35%). The within-gene discrimination metric (pos_bias) for the splice-delta model remains at 0.027 — lower than PRISM's 0.094 — despite the explicit within-gene input. This pattern appears contradictory but resolves mechanistically.

**Why G_δ saturates to 1.0 (model successfully uses δ_i).** δ_i contains genuine within-gene structural information: it captures how isoform i deviates from the gene-average sequence geometry. The gate saturates because using δ_i reduces the combined training loss faster per gradient step than the resulting AUPRC harm increases L_{class}. Gate saturation indicates success in learning the δ_i signal, not failure.

**Why AUPRC collapses despite gate learning.** AUPRC is determined by the ranking of positive-class isoforms above negative-class isoforms. Under gene-level supervision, Y_{i_a,k} = Y_{i_b,k} = 1 for all isoforms of a positive gene. Using δ_i to create within-gene score variance means some positive-class isoforms receive lower scores. Every such downranking directly decreases AUPRC, regardless of whether the model correctly identified the canonical isoform as higher-scoring. The gene-level label provides no information about which isoform should receive the lower score — the direction of the discriminator is informationally unconstrained by the supervision signal. The discriminator therefore produces within-gene variance in a direction that may be functionally arbitrary, uniformly degrading AUPRC.

**The fundamental incompatibility.** Gene-level classification and within-gene discrimination are geometrically antagonistic under gene-level labels: the former requires all positive isoforms to rank above negatives (pushing all positive scores high), while the latter requires within-gene score variation (pushing some positive scores down). Adding δ_i as explicit input attempts to resolve this tension, but in the absence of isoform-level labels specifying the correct direction, the model finds the discriminator gradient directionally unconstrained within each gene. The net effect is random disruption of the global sequence-function mapping that PRISM relies on.

**Contrast with PRISM.** PRISM never makes an explicit within-gene comparison. f_θ applies the same cross-gene function to each isoform independently; the model never "sees" that i_a and i_b belong to the same gene. Isoform discrimination is a by-product of cross-gene statistics applied to isoform-specific embeddings φ(i) — not an objective — so there is no label conflict. The key contrast: φ(i) encodes isoform i's absolute sequence features, and cross-gene associations assign different scores to φ(i_a) and φ(i_b) based on where they fall in the learned function space. δ_i encodes relative within-gene deviation, which requires the model to learn a gene-specific directional mapping — impossible under gene-level labels.

**Generalisation.** v16 (4-stream gated residual fusion: ESM-2 + splice_delta + RNA expression features + subcellular localisation scores) shows identical collapse: macro AUPRC 0.4414 (−37% vs v15d), all gate values saturating. RNA expression features and localisation scores encode context-dependent quantities that have no consistent cross-gene label association under gene-level supervision. The failure pattern generalises: any auxiliary input stream encoding within-gene or context-dependent variation is informationally misaligned with gene-level supervision and disrupts the cross-gene sequence-function mapping.

### S1.5 Cross-tissue transfer: geometric argument

ESM-2 is trained on 250M protein sequences without tissue-of-origin annotation; φ(i) therefore encodes sequence-level structural features — amino acid co-variation patterns conserved across organisms, secondary structure propensities, hydrophobic surface geometry, evolutionary constraint. These features are tissue-independent because they are determined by the protein sequence alone, not by cellular context.

The sequence-function mapping f_θ learned from muscle training associates specific regions of ESM-2 embedding space with GO term probabilities:

> f_θ(φ(i))_k > τ_k ⟺ isoform i has embedding geometry characteristic of GO term k

For a GO term k with shared sequence determinants between muscle and brain (microtubule-based movement: kinesin motor domain; mitochondrial organisation: Fe-S cluster binding, ETC subunit topology; Ca²⁺ homeostasis: EF-hand motif), the embedding subspace associated with k is structurally identical whether the sequence originates from muscle or brain tissue. The learned boundary w_k therefore transfers without modification.

**Conditions for transfer failure.** Three distinct causes:
1. **Absent sequence subspace**: brain-specific GPCR subfamilies have receptor pharmacophores not represented in muscle training examples; no trained w_k boundary exists for those structural families, and predictions revert to prior prevalence.
2. **Distributional shift**: brain tissue may predominantly express alternative isoforms of shared genes — isoforms with different domain compositions from the canonical isoforms that dominate muscle training labels. If the brain-predominant isoform has embedding φ on the opposite side of the trained boundary from its gene-level label, the boundary learned from muscle training misclassifies.
3. **Non-sequence functional context**: GO terms whose positivity depends on cell-type-specific interaction partners (not encoded in protein sequence alone) will transfer poorly even when the structural motif sequences are shared.

**Observed pattern.** The 14.5% AUPRC reduction (0.702 → 0.600) concentrates in GO terms without muscle-brain sequence analogs (GPCR signalling: 0.403; ion transport: 0.421; immune: 0.447), while GO terms with conserved sequence determinants maintain performance (synaptic transmission: 0.720; mitochondrial organisation: 0.641; microtubule-based movement: 0.567). This pattern is qualitatively consistent with the geometric transfer model. However, quantitative prediction of the degradation magnitude from first principles would require explicit modelling of training-set motif coverage and brain-specific isoform distribution — beyond the current analysis.

## Supplementary Note S_arch: Systematic architectural development path to v17f*

This note documents the complete experimental development path leading to the v17f* layer-contrast delta architecture. Results are summarised in Results §3–§4; full experimental details are provided here for reproducibility and reviewers.

### S_arch.1 — Baseline limitation diagnosis

**v15d_splice (splice delta, transductive):** Adding within-gene delta δ_i = φ(i) − μ_{gene(i)} as direct GO-prediction input collapsed AUPRC from 0.702 to 0.457. Mechanism: gene-level GO labels provide no directional signal about which isoform should score higher, producing random gradient directions that disrupted the global sequence-function mapping.

**v17a (two-stage triplet, binary Pfam):** T_ψ Dense(ESM-2[640]+Pfam[512]→512→256, ℓ₂-norm) trained on cross-gene Pfam Jaccard similarity triplets. Stage 1 collapsed within 15 epochs (active triplet ratio: 27% → 0.03% → 0.00%; 3,306 valid triplets from 6,040 domain-bearing isoforms) because 80.9% of isoform pairs have Pfam Jaccard=0. Two-stage model (MLP_θ on frozen under-trained T_ψ) achieved All MF AUPRC 0.354 (−0.24 vs. PRISM 0.596). L2_Structural terms (0.178) scored *lower* than L4_CellState (0.564), confirming that concatenating sparse binary Pfam features with ESM-2 adds noise to the L2 terms.

**v17b (Pfam-only Stage 1, ESM-2 bypass):** T_ψ restricted to Pfam[512] alone; Stage 2 received concat[T_ψ(64), φ_L30(640)]. Recovered to AUPRC 0.602 (Δ=+0.248 vs. v17a), matching PRISM baseline. T_ψ again collapsed (active ratio 0.32% at epoch 5 — only 15 valid triplets from the 80.9% non-domain pool at cosine ≥ 0.70 threshold). Confirmed: ESM-2 bypass in Stage 2 is correct; training signal density in Stage 1 is the barrier.

### Supplementary Note S_dissection: Anatomical dissection of ESM-2's isoform representation

This Note provides the full mechanistic dissection summarised in Results §4c. These are post-hoc anatomical analyses performed after the δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 architecture was already empirically validated by ablation (§4); they account for *why* the architecture resolves isoforms at the domain level, and are reported here rather than in the main Results to keep the primary narrative on the isoform-resolution evidence (§4b) and its representational ceiling (§Discussion).

**Per-layer discriminative structure: the final layer is not where GO signal peaks.** For each of 277 GO terms we fit a per-layer linear probe on the raw 640-dimensional embedding of each of the 30 transformer layers and located the Fisher discriminative peak — the layer at which the probe is maximally discriminative. The peak layer is broadly distributed across the trajectory (median L14, interquartile range [L7, L27], full range L1–L30), and only 8/277 terms (2.9%) peak at the final layer L30 — the single layer that mean-pooled PLM classifiers use. Mean-pooling at L30 therefore reads the representation at a depth where, for 97% of GO terms, the linearly-discriminative signal has already passed its maximum. This provides the mechanistic basis for the layer-contrast architecture δ_layer = φ_L30 − φ_L15 (§4): the subtracted mid-layer L15 sits within the interquartile band where most GO-discriminative signal is concentrated, recovering information that the L30 mean-pool integrates over.

**Within-gene layer-divergence: domain differences are encoded, length-independently, and peak at mid-depth.** To test directly whether the layer trajectory carries isoform-resolution structure — free of the gene-family confounds that dominate cross-gene comparisons — we enumerated within-gene isoform pairs across the muscle test set (the same 36,748-isoform set as the Domain-Ranking analysis) and, at each layer, compared the ESM-2 cosine distance between isoform pairs that differ in Pfam domain architecture versus pairs that share it. Because both members of every pair belong to the same gene, gene identity is constant and cannot contribute to the comparison. After removing exact-duplicate pairs (isoforms with fewer than 10 differing residues; 11% of pairs, an annotation artefact), 25,350 pairs remained (12,003 domain-different, 13,347 domain-same). Domain-different pairs are 3–4× more distant than domain-same pairs at every layer — far outside a label-shuffle null whose 95% interval is [0.95, 1.05] at every layer (empirical p < 0.001 at all 30 layers) — and the ratio peaks at mid-depth (L17 = 3.88) and is lower at both the input-proximal (L1 = 3.10) and output (L30 = 2.99) layers, mirroring the Fisher-peak distribution and reinforcing the δ_layer rationale. Critically, this is not a length artefact: within length-difference-matched strata the ratio persists across all bins (2.5×–7.8×), and both absolute distances are non-degenerate (domain-different 0.026, domain-same 0.0066 at L17). The signal is the presence or absence of a domain-architecture change rather than a graded domain count: among domain-different pairs, divergence magnitude does not track the number of domains changed once length difference is controlled (partial Spearman ρ = −0.008, p = 0.34). Domain swaps (each isoform bearing a domain the other lacks) diverge marginally more than pure gain/loss events, but this is largely explained by their greater sequence change (residual effect after matching on differing-residue count: partial ρ = +0.024, p = 0.009).

**Principal axes of the trajectory encode structural classes, not functional motifs.** We interpreted the eight joint-PCA axes of the layer trajectory (§Methods) by correlating each axis score against 22 sequence-intrinsic biophysical/compositional features and against Pfam domain-family membership, at both gene level and within-gene-residual level (Supplementary Fig. S_axes). All eight axes align with recognisable secondary-structure, charge, size, and domain-family properties, with the strongest axes reaching |ρ| = 0.4–0.7: axis 0 separates soluble β-sheet globular domains (enriched for PDZ [PF00595], RRM [PF00076], PHD [PF00628]; β-sheet fraction ρ = −0.70) from transmembrane proteins (ligand-gated ion channels [PF02931/2], MFS transporters [PF07690]; transmembrane-segment count ρ = −0.35, N-terminal hydrophobicity ρ = −0.37); axis 3 marks large multidomain enzymes and scaffolds (WD40 [PF00400], DEAD-box helicase [PF00270], protein kinase [PF00069]; domain count ρ = +0.43, acidic residue fraction ρ = +0.47); axis 1 — previously the weakest axis under the compositional panel alone — resolves under domain-family analysis into leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-repeat membrane receptors (LRR [PF13855], Ig V-set [PF07686]; transmembrane count ρ = +0.20); and axis 6 into KRAB-zinc-finger transcription factors and cytoplasmic scaffolds (KRAB [PF01352], SCAN [PF02023], spectrin [PF00435]). No axis aligns with a functional short-linear-motif class: nuclear-localisation/export signals, SH3-binding PxxP motifs, and phosphorylation-site consensus counts appear among axis correlates only as length-confounded artefacts that vanish at the within-gene-residual level. The interpretable content of ESM-2's isoform-discriminating representation is therefore structural and compositional — the vocabulary of protein domain architecture — not the vocabulary of regulatory motifs.

**The within-gene axes are not artefacts of gene-family background subtraction: a conditioning control.** The eight joint-PCA axes above are dominated by between-gene (gene-family) variance — the highest-variance axis is also the most gene-family-aligned — which raises the concern that any "within-gene" signal read from them could be an artefact of implicitly subtracting a gene-level background. We addressed this directly by re-deriving the axes under explicit gene-family conditioning: rather than projecting out gene-family directions (which risks defining the within-gene signal into existence), we subtracted each gene-family's mean embedding and recomputed a single pooled principal-axis basis over the family-centred residuals — a within-family scatter decomposition, analogous to the within-class scatter matrix of linear discriminant analysis (§Methods). Three results establish that the within-gene structure is genuine rather than a background-removal artefact. First, the family-centred axes coincide geometrically with the uncentred joint axes (mean principal-angle cosine 0.95 across the eight-dimensional subspace; top five axes > 0.98): conditioning on gene-family re-weights the variance along the existing structural axes rather than manufacturing a new basis, so within-gene variation inhabits the same domain-architecture geometry as between-gene variation. Second, the axes are invariant to how gene-family is defined — replacing Pfam-dominant-domain families with families obtained by all-versus-all sequence-homology clustering (DIAMOND, ≥30% identity / ≥50% coverage) yields a near-identical basis (principal-angle cosine 0.99 between the two definitions) — excluding the possibility that the axes are an artefact of a particular family assignment. Third, decomposing the within-gene signal by biophysical feature shows that isoform length is the single largest within-gene variance component, but a length-independent domain-architecture axis persists: axis 3 (the multidomain-enzyme axis above) retains a within-gene-residual partial correlation with domain count after controlling for length (partial Spearman ρ = +0.274), while its length correlation itself vanishes under domain control — the domain axis is not a length proxy. This length-independent domain axis validates against experimentally-curated isoform switches: across 59 BISECT domain-change cases mapped to the brain test set, the sign of the axis-3 difference between the two isoforms matches the direction of Pfam domain gain/loss in 53/59 cases (89.8%; binomial p = 8.8 × 10⁻¹¹), including the Complex I loci NDUFS7 and NDUFS8; consistent with the presence/absence rather than graded encoding established above (§Within-gene layer-divergence), the concordance is directional (magnitude Spearman ρ = 0.18). Finally, we stress the distinction between representational variance and predictive signal: principal-component analysis identifies the directions of greatest variation — dominated within-gene by length — which are not the directions PRISM uses for prediction, whose within-gene rankings are near-length-agnostic (length-AUC 0.560; §Results). The largest-variance axis and the function-predictive axis are distinct objects; the interpretive weight of the axis decomposition therefore rests on the length-independent, BISECT-validated domain-architecture axes (axes 0, 1, 3, 6), not on the length-dominated variance envelope.

**Causal occlusion establishes a double dissociation: within-gene ranking uses the domain axis, gene-level identification uses the gene-family axis.** The correlations above show which biological property each axis encodes; they do not establish which axes PRISM's predictions causally depend on. We tested this by occlusion: projecting each axis out of the test-set embeddings (φ_L15 and φ_L30) and re-predicting with the trained model, then measuring the change in within-gene Domain-Ranking AUC and in gene-level macro-AUPRC, against a null of twenty random unit directions occluded identically. The result is a clean double dissociation. Removing the domain-architecture axis (axis 3) produces the largest drop in within-gene Domain-Ranking (ΔAUC = +0.013, z = +8.4 versus the random-direction null) but barely affects gene-level macro-AUPRC; removing the β-sheet/hydrophobicity axis (axis 0, the highest-variance and most gene-family-aligned axis) produces the largest drop in gene-level macro-AUPRC (ΔAUPRC = +0.038) but no drop in within-gene ranking (z = −2.1). Within-gene isoform discrimination and gene-level family identification therefore run on distinct representational axes. A complementary retrain-with-occlusion test — removing each axis from both training and test data and retraining — sharpens the interpretation: gene-level macro-AUPRC is fully recoverable after removing any single axis (the model routes around it, confirming that gene-family identity is redundantly encoded across many dimensions, which is why the gene-mean oracle is so strong), whereas the within-gene Domain-Ranking contribution of axis 3 survives retraining (ΔAUC = +0.006 versus random +0.001), indicating that the within-gene domain signal is more concentrated and less redundant. Critically, the highest-variance axis (axis 0), which alone accounts for the largest share of the trajectory variance, is not used for within-gene ranking (Domain-Ranking z = −2.1); its causal role is confined to gene-level identification. The negative sign is mechanistically informative and adjudicates between two accounts of this dissociation — a learned within-gene down-weighting of axis 0 versus passive weight leakage. On the muscle Domain-Ranking set, axis 0 varies within-gene at an amplitude comparable to the domain axis (within-gene residual SD 1.86 versus 2.04 for axis 3), so it is not suppressed to zero; yet its within-gene variation is uncorrelated with the isoform domain difference that Domain-Ranking scores (within-gene Spearman ρ = −0.001, versus +0.29 for axis 3), so occluding it removes rankable noise rather than signal. Because PRISM (v17f*) is a single feed-forward network with no gene-conditional gating, it applies one weight field to axis 0 everywhere: the between-gene-justified weight on the β-sheet/gene-family axis is imposed unchanged on the label-misaligned within-gene variation of that same axis, and the resulting leakage — not a within-gene down-weighting, which this architecture cannot express — is what makes axis 0 anti-useful for within-gene ranking. The double dissociation is therefore an emergent consequence of the axes' differing within-gene label alignment under shared weights, not a trained routing mechanism. This is the causal counterpart to the representational-variance-versus-predictive-signal distinction drawn above and to PRISM's near-chance within-gene length-AUC (0.560): the directions of greatest variation are not the directions the predictions rank on, and the length that dominates the within-gene variance envelope (a within-family-basis axis; §Methods) is not what PRISM uses to order isoforms. Two further analyses localise and delimit the domain signal. First, the within-gene domain signal is categorical rather than graded: while the graded domain-count correlation with axis 3 is modest (within-gene partial Spearman ρ = 0.27), a length-matched classifier reading all eight axes detects the binary presence/absence of a domain-architecture change between isoforms at AUROC 0.71, confirming that the low graded correlation reflects the categorical nature of domain switching (§Within-gene layer-divergence) rather than a representational ceiling. The eight interpretable axes are moreover a lower bound on the encoded signal, not its limit: the identical length-matched classifier reading the full 640-dimensional layer-mean representation detects the same binary domain-architecture change at AUROC 0.84 (compression gap +0.12 over the eight-axis projection), rising further to 0.89 with a non-linear (gradient-boosted) reader that matches PRISM's own non-linear MLP decoder, so the compact interpretable basis under-reports the domain information that PRISM, operating on the full embedding, can exploit — the human-readable axes trade discriminative headroom for interpretability, and the domain-change signal in ESM-2 is only partly linearly decodable. Second, the domain divergence is localised to mid-network: across the 59 BISECT domain-switch cases, the layer at which the two isoforms' axis-3 trajectories diverge maximally has median L16 (interquartile range L9–L24), significantly deeper than the divergence layer of random within-gene isoform pairs (median L11; Mann–Whitney p = 0.022), converging with the population-level mid-layer divergence peak (L17) and the Fisher-peak distribution. These per-isoform axis trajectories over the 30 layers, which visualise where isoforms of the same gene separate, are provided for the Complex I domain-switch loci NDUFS2 and NDUFS7 and for the domain-invariant accessory subunits NDUFS4 and NDUFAF5 (negative controls carrying no Pfam domain in any brain isoform, where isoforms nonetheless separate on the axes) in Supplementary Fig. S_traj.

**The Domain-Ranking ceiling is bounded by the representation, not by model capacity or supervision.** The interpretable-basis ceiling above concerns what a *linear reader* decodes from ESM-2; a complementary question is whether PRISM's own within-gene Domain-Ranking ceiling (AUC 0.630 on held-out muscle; §4b) is set by the model — too little classifier capacity or too little within-gene supervision — or by the δ_layer representation itself. Three interventions spanning capacity and two supervision designs bracket the answer, and all three leave the ceiling in place. *Capacity.* A depth-controlled width sweep of the classifier head (from the production 256→128 units up to 768→384, holding the number of hidden layers fixed so that width is not confounded with depth) raises gene-level macro AUPRC by +0.013 (95% CI [+0.010, +0.016] for a 512→256 head; replicated across two independent five-seed sets), confirming that the *supervised* gene-level head was in fact mildly capacity-limited — yet the identical widening leaves within-gene Domain-Ranking statistically unchanged (ΔAUC = −0.000, gene-clustered bootstrap 95% CI [−0.011, +0.010], resampling whole genes rather than gene–term units to respect within-gene correlation). Added capacity flows entirely to the supervised objective and none to the unsupervised within-gene ranking. *Supervision.* Three independently-designed auxiliary losses that directly target within-gene domain ordering all fail to raise Domain-Ranking. A conditional domain-count regression (§6b) degrades it to 0.626; and a within-gene pairwise domain-rank margin loss — which explicitly pushes the higher-domain isoform of each training pair above its lower-domain sibling on that gene's positive GO terms — leaves Domain-Ranking unchanged (ΔAUC = −0.001, bootstrap CI including zero) while degrading macro AUPRC by 0.055, because a blanket "more-domains-scores-higher" signal injects mis-directed gradient on the motif-dependent terms — the 74.6% of within-gene specialisation events (§Functional specialization) where domain count does not track function. A third, surgical variant directly tests whether this mis-targeting, rather than the representation, was the binding constraint: restricting the same margin loss to only the domain-dependent term subset (the 33 L2_Structural MF terms; §Methods) removes the mis-directed gradient on motif-dependent terms by construction. This still fails to raise Domain-Ranking (ΔAUC = +0.007, 95% CI [−0.003, +0.017], including zero) and, although it degrades the domain-dependent-term subset less than the blanket loss did (macro ΔAUPRC = −0.061 versus −0.086 on the L2_Structural subset), it still significantly degrades both that subset and overall macro AUPRC (ΔAUPRC = −0.038, 95% CI excluding zero) rather than leaving them unchanged. Neither capacity nor either supervision design moves the ceiling: the within-gene Domain-Ranking limit is causally bracketed from three independent angles as a property of the δ_layer representation rather than of the model that reads it or the scope of its supervision, consistent with the motif-level boundary of the three-tier account (Results §4c synthesis).

**Per-residue dissection: the tier-iii ceiling reflects pooling and anchor availability, not an absence of encoding (preliminary).** The three-tier synthesis (Results §4c) frames motif-level functional direction as "not encoded" by ESM-2. A direct per-residue analysis refines this claim: the underlying sequence perturbation *is* locally encoded — mean-pooling and the lack of a common cross-mechanism reference direction, not an absence of representation, are what block its recovery. Using aligned UniProt isoform pairs (42 pairs; per-residue ESM-2 L30, no retraining or additional supervision), we computed the per-residue embedding shift δ_p between matched backbone residues as a function of distance from the nearest splice junction. For motif/regulatory pairs (|Δlen| ≤ 30 aa, n=13; the same class underlying the tier-iii "not encoded" claim), δ_p is 3.26 at the junction and decays to 0.19 beyond 160 residues — a 17.6-fold, smoothly monotonic attenuation (not an alignment artefact) — while whole-sequence mean-pooled cosine distance for the same pairs is only 0.0097, confirming that mean-pooling dilutes a genuinely localised signal to near-invisibility. Domain-loss control pairs (|Δlen| ≥ 100 aa, n=15) show the same qualitative pattern at larger magnitude (junction 5.43 → far-field 0.67, 8-fold decay), confirming the measure detects real structural perturbation rather than noise. This localises the tier-iii ceiling to ESM-2's *pooling* operation rather than its *encoding* operation — consistent with, and mechanistically explaining, the capacity/supervision bracketing above (a head cannot recover signal that mean-pooling has already diluted out of its input).

**The coherence-filter mechanism, formalised.** The constructive/destructive-averaging account above (Results §4c) admits an exact statement. Let δh_p ∈ ℝ^d denote the per-residue embedding shift at aligned position p (as defined above), for p = 1, …, L residues entering the pooling window, and let δφ = (1/L)Σ_p δh_p be the mean-pooled shift PRISM's input is built from. Define the pooling-retained energy fraction frac_kept = ‖δφ‖² / [(1/L)Σ_p‖δh_p‖²] — the squared norm of the pooled vector relative to the mean squared norm of its unpooled constituents. Expanding ‖Σ_p δh_p‖² = Σ_p‖δh_p‖² + Σ_{p≠q} δh_p·δh_q and writing c̄ for the mean pairwise cosine similarity among the δh_p (an equal-norm approximation; the exact expression is norm-weighted) gives

frac_kept = 1/L + (1 − 1/L)·c̄.

As L grows, frac_kept → c̄: the fraction of per-residue signal energy that survives mean-pooling converges to a **length-independent constant set by directional coherence alone**, not to a term scaling with edit size or protein length as a low-pass account would predict (which this identity shows would require c̄ itself, not L, to be the varying quantity — the two accounts are observationally distinguishable). This is the same relation that underlies coherent-integration gain in phased-array signal processing (N phase-aligned signals sum to amplitude N, power N²; N randomly-phased signals sum to amplitude ~√N, power ~N) applied to averaging rather than summation. It formalises, rather than merely illustrates, why a spatially broad but directionally scattered perturbation (short linear motifs, where flanking positions are perturbed by attention-mediated context spread in directions that need not align with the edit itself) is diluted regardless of how many residues it touches, while a spatially compact but directionally aligned one (domain-scale structural change, where residues move together toward or away from a shared fold) survives regardless of its absolute size — consistent with the junction-shift decay reported above (3.26 → 0.19 over 160 residues) reflecting a magnitude gradient that, on its own, is compatible with either a low-pass or a coherence account; it is the direction of the decaying shift relative to δφ, not its magnitude alone, that the coherence identity shows is decisive, and which the mean-pooled cosine distance of 0.0097 (versus per-residue magnitude of order 1) is the aggregate signature of.

Recovering the diluted signal, however, requires more than replacing mean-pooling with a local window: a naive region-pool (window W ∈ {10,20,40} around the junction, scored against the paper's centroid-similarity anchor — the per-GO-term mean embedding of training-positive isoforms, the same anchor underlying the Domain-Ranking baseline in §Discussion) leaves motif-pair direction accuracy unchanged (9/13 = 0.692 for both global and region pooling, no window flipping a single pair), despite the region-pooled shift vector being 5.3× larger in magnitude than the global shift (‖F−N‖ 2.22 vs 0.42) and better aligned with the GO-centroid than the global shift (signed cosine +0.080 vs +0.040). The bottleneck is therefore not signal magnitude but **anchor choice**: even the region-pooled shift is only weakly aligned (cosine ≈ 0.08) with the global GO-term centroid, because that centroid represents cross-gene functional identity, not the within-pair direction of "which isoform is more functional" that Domain-Ranking-style metrics require. Substituting a within-pair anchor constructed from the direction PRISM's own domain-loss training data actually uses — the mean difference between complete- and truncated-isoform embeddings over 3,216 within-gene domain pairs — changes the result for domain-loss pairs specifically: on the n=14 domain-loss UniProt pairs for which this anchor is mechanistically appropriate, region-pooling improves direction alignment from 0.571 (global pool, cosine +0.173) to 0.714 (region pool, cosine +0.329). This is, to our knowledge, the first direct demonstration that a representation reorganisation (local pooling around the structural change, under a mechanism-matched anchor) can partially recover isoform-direction signal that whole-sequence mean-pooling and the current δ_layer/L30 architecture do not resolve — though the demonstration is exploratory (n=14, single window choice, no bootstrap CI) and awaits validation on the full Domain-Ranking evaluation set. Critically, the domain-completeness anchor does *not* transfer to the motif pairs (n=13; cosine alignment is negative, i.e. mechanistically mismatched), and no single motif-pair anchor exists to substitute it: the 14 benchmark motif pairs span mechanistically unrelated function classes (kinase activation-loop dynamics, DNA-binding domain variants, RNA-binding RRM inclusion, GTPase switch regions, structural insert number), each requiring its own reference direction, with too few labelled examples per mechanism to construct one. The refined account of tier (iii) is therefore not "ESM-2 lacks a representation of motif-level sequence change" but "domain-loss has a single, learnable, common anchor (domain completeness) that a local-pooling architecture can exploit, whereas motif-level function is mechanism-heterogeneous and currently lacks any common anchor to pool toward" — a restatement, at the representational level, of the same gene-level-label ceiling established in Results §1 (no isoform-level ground truth exists to supervise which anchor is correct for a given motif mechanism).

**Quantitative validation of the coherence-filter mechanism in synthetic and real motifs.** For the five ELM degron classes where editcore representation exceeds amino-acid composition (N-end-rule motifs, ΔAUROC +0.093–0.239), a 36-dimensional dipeptide-class descriptor (physicochemical bigrams) explains a median of only 4% of this gain (range −29% to +66% across the five classes, two of five negative), confirming the signal requires genuine positional residue identity rather than local compositional pattern. To test whether the coherence-filter mechanism quantitatively predicts pooling survival, we inserted three textbook-validated nuclear localisation signals (SV40 "PKKKRKV", 7 aa; c-Myc "PAAKRVKLD", 9 aa; nucleoplasmin bipartite "KRPAATKKAGQAKKKK", 16 aa) into real backbone sequences and measured editcore (local) versus whole-protein-pooled (production-equivalent) ESM-2 representation separability, contrasted against a within-class-permutation control that preserves amino-acid-class composition exactly (e.g. for SV40's five basic residues K,K,K,R,K, the control randomly reorders only those five positions while keeping P and V fixed, forcing mono-composition and dipeptide-class composition to be identical between conditions by construction). All three motifs show editcore AUC 0.746–0.907 versus composition/dipeptide-class exactly 0.500 (design-guaranteed), confirming ESM-2 encodes within-class residue-order information invisible to any composition descriptor. Whole-protein pooling retains partial signal (pooled AUC 0.550–0.663), with survival scaling by insert length (the longest motif, 16 aa, retains the most; the shortest, 7 aa, the least) — the within-window convergence to c̄ reported above (frac_kept, as L grows within the pooling window) is distinguished from this whole-protein dilution, which additionally depends on insert_fraction (the edit's share of the full pooling window) because the two are cross-window rather than within-window comparisons of the same coherence-filtered signal. Across 515 synthetic insertions, the dilution ratio ‖pool_delta‖/‖editcore_delta‖ scales as insert_fraction^0.48 (R² = 0.26, p = 2×10⁻³¹); the same law holds on 773 real alternative-splicing SLiM pairs (R² = 0.65, p = 9×10⁻¹⁴⁸) but with 6–7× greater dilution at matched insert fraction — attributable to the spatial incoherence of real splice-junction perturbations relative to evolutionarily-optimised compact motifs, consistent with the near-total collapse of the junction-localised signal under whole-sequence mean-pooling reported above (δ_p 3.26 → cosine distance 0.0097 for the same motif/regulatory pairs). (A direct test of an N-terminal additive channel on the full deployment task yielded no gain, Δ macro-AUPRC = −0.037 [95% CI excludes 0], consistent with this dilution mechanism being architecturally fundamental rather than recoverable via local-window bypass alone.)

### Supplementary Note S_label_search: A systematic search for isoform-resolved motif-function supervision labels

This Note provides the full detail behind the Discussion statement that the tier-iii gap (Supplementary Note S_dissection) reflects annotation scarcity rather than an unexplored corner of the public label landscape. S_dissection's per-residue test showed that motif-level sequence perturbation is locally encoded by ESM-2, but that recovering it into a scored isoform-resolution direction requires a "mechanism-general reference direction" — in effect, an isoform-level supervision label against which an edit-core-focused (rather than whole-sequence mean-pooled) readout could be trained and validated. That test used a single 14-pair domain-loss benchmark to demonstrate the anchor problem for the one mechanism where an anchor exists (domain completeness) and could not test whether a comparable anchor exists for motif-level function specifically, because the pooling-kernel study population's 14 motif pairs span mechanistically unrelated function classes with too few examples per mechanism. We therefore searched independently and systematically for an external, isoform-resolved supervision label that could substitute for or complement that undersized benchmark, testing six candidates spanning three data modalities (gene-function annotation, isoform-resolution status, motif/structural-instance annotation). All six fail, each for a mechanistically distinct reason (**Table S_label_search**).

Candidates 1, 3, and 5 fail a cheap check performed before any curation effort — gene-level label inheritance or curated-instance sparsity — that generalises the pattern established for GO-BP macro AUPRC elsewhere in this paper (label-identity information dominating over isoform-resolution information). Candidate 2 is excluded by definition rather than by a run experiment: because APPRIS-alternative status is structurally correlated with domain completeness, an edit-core-favourable result on this label could not be attributed to edit-core-specific content rather than to the domain signal already characterised in §4d, so no classifier was trained on it. Candidate 4 clears an initial database search but not the power threshold, and re-testing at genome scale (candidate 4's own internal check, not a separate experiment) shows the constraint is the annotation resource's sparsity rather than the sampling frame. Only candidate 6 (IDR boundary-shift) reached a full test; we report it in detail because a negative result at this stage is the most informative of the six.

**IDR boundary-shift: full supervision-causal test.** Because candidate 6 alone cleared the sample-size and composition-independence screen, we tested it against the same comp / comp+pooled / comp+edit-core design used for the ELM-class discrimination tests elsewhere in this paper (composition-residual gene-disjoint `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, `GroupKFold(5)`), extended with two controls specific to the concerns this label raises. For each within-gene isoform pair, order/disorder state was scored per residue with metapredict for both isoforms; the edit region was defined by `SequenceMatcher` alignment opcodes (as elsewhere in this paper), and a pair was labelled positive if the mean disorder score crossed the canonical 0.5 threshold between the long and short isoform's edit-region residues. Three checks were pre-registered before the test was run: (i) a **confound oracle** — a classifier using only positional and gene-gross features (edit-region proximity to the N/C-terminal 20% of the protein, edit length, whole-protein mean disorder, protein length; no ESM-2 embedding) — must be beaten by the edit-core classifier for the result to support a local-signal interpretation; (ii) a **gene-permutation null** — comparing the true gene-disjoint cross-validated AUROC against the AUROC obtained under 20 permutations of the gene grouping used for cross-validation (same group-size distribution, scrambled membership) — must place the true value inside or below the permutation-null band, ruling out gene-level memorisation leaking across folds; (iii) a stated **predicted direction** — edit-core-beyond-composition AUROC exceeding pooled-beyond-composition AUROC, mirroring the pooling-dilution mechanism established in §4c and Supplementary Note S_dissection.

The result is decisively negative on the first check. Composition alone reaches AUROC 0.571; adding the pooled whole-protein embedding difference reaches 0.597; adding the edit-core (edit-region-only) embedding reaches 0.556 — both below the confound oracle's 0.636. Contrary to the pre-registered prediction, edit-core does not exceed pooled (0.556 vs. 0.597); we interpret this reversal as most consistent with the 640-dimensional edit-core feature overfitting the available sample (n = 499) rather than as evidence against the pooling-dilution mechanism established elsewhere in this paper on larger, independently-constructed test sets, but we report it as a refuted prediction rather than reinterpreting it post hoc. The gene-permutation null passes: the true-gene AUROC (0.556) falls at the lower edge of the 20-permutation null distribution (mean 0.603, s.d. 0.015, z = −3.17), indicating the negative result is not an artefact of within-gene leakage but a genuine absence of edit-core-specific signal beyond what positional and gene-level gross features already provide. Taken together, the decisive finding is not "edit-core underperforms pooled" in isolation but that an embedding-free classifier using only edit position and whole-protein disorder content outperforms both embedding-based representations — indicating that this label's exploitable variance is predominantly a positional/gene-gross property rather than the edit-specific local sequence information edit-core pooling was designed to isolate. This is a distinct failure mode from candidates 1, 3, and 5 (which fail before any embedding-based test is possible) and, like the domain-anchor-transfer negative result in Supplementary Note S_dissection, delimits rather than contradicts the pooling-dilution mechanism: it shows that a label surviving the sample-size and composition-independence screen can still fail to isolate edit-core-specific content, sharpening "not with the current benchmark's mechanistic diversity and sample size" (main text, above) into "not across the six mechanistically distinct external label sources tested, at up to ~35× the original benchmark's sample size."

**Interpretation.** This search does not close the tier-iii open problem; it bounds where the obstacle currently sits. Candidates 1–5 show that the readily-available external databases either inherit their annotation at the gene level (candidates 1, 5), encode a property already read by the pooled representation (candidate 2), or lack sufficient curated coverage of the manifest's isoform population (candidate 3, and candidate 4 at both manifest and genome scale). Candidate 6 shows that even a label passing those screens can still fail to isolate edit-core-specific content from positional and whole-protein confounds. Together they support the reading, stated conditionally in the main text (§Scope declaration, §Refining "not encoded"), that an architecture preserving per-residue representation through to the prediction head remains a well-motivated but currently unvalidatable direction: the mechanism by which mean-pooling discards motif-level signal is established (Supplementary Note S_dissection), but no external supervision source surveyed here provides the isoform-resolved, edit-core-specific target needed to test whether recovering that signal architecturally would improve isoform-level function prediction.

### Supplementary Note S_severity: Canonical-anchored severity regression and tissue-asymmetry mediation chain

This Note provides the full statistical detail summarised in Results §6b. It reports (i) the original five-covariate severity regression establishing that muscle and brain differ not only in *how often* an edit remodels a Pfam domain (§6) but in *how large* a representational consequence it produces, and (ii) a subsequent mediation analysis tracing the apparent muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of two of those five covariates (N-terminal overlap, alignment-resync failure) back through a three-layer surrogate chain to its most proximal cause.

**Original five-covariate regression.** The domain-envelope analysis above establishes that the two tissues differ in *how often* an alternative-splicing event remodels a Pfam domain, but rate alone does not show whether the two tissues also differ in *how large a representational consequence* each edit class produces once it occurs. To test this independently, we extended the within-gene pair set from the two-isoform-only instrument above (restricted to genes with exactly two isoforms; 3,298 muscle, 3,435 brain genes) to every multi-isoform gene, anchoring each gene on its canonical isoform (MANE Select > Ensembl_canonical > APPRIS principal > longest-CDS-in-dataset fallback) paired against every other isoform of that gene as an independent edit event — muscle 8,569 multi-isoform genes yielding 15,493 pairs (9.2× the two-isoform instrument), brain 12,157 genes yielding 33,148 pairs (15.2×). For each pair we regressed a continuous representational-severity score — the gene-disjoint out-of-fold projection of the pair's L15+L30 mean-pooled embedding difference onto the fold-trained mean coherence direction (the same length-invariant construction underlying the Domain-Ranking metric; unlike max-pooling, which we separately confirmed inherits an extreme-value-statistics length artefact in 6 of 7 tested categories, mean-pooling's projection is provably length-invariant and empirically artefact-free) — on five structural properties of the edit: size (changed-residue count), whether the edit changes domain architecture, whether it overlaps the N-terminal 60 residues, the fraction of changed residues in a disorder-promoting region (TOP-IDP scale), and whether the alignment fails to resynchronise into a single contiguous matching block downstream of the edit (a protein-alignment-level proxy for frame-disrupting edits, not direct nucleotide-level frame ground truth). All five covariates are significant after gene-clustered robust standard errors and Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the pooled 48,641-pair fit (R² = 0.119; permutation null R² ≈ 0, P < 0.001, 1,000 gene-cluster-preserving permutations), and — critically, given this paper's emphasis on ruling out gene-identity confounds for macro AUPRC — the same five covariates remain significant when the model is re-fit using only within-gene variation (each gene's pairs demeaned by their gene mean, restricted to the 10,286 genes contributing ≥2 pairs; R² = 0.158, versus R² = 0.118 for the pooled fit on the identical subsample — the two R² values are not directly comparable, since the within-gene fit's denominator is the smaller gene-demeaned variance rather than total variance, but both remain well above the permutation null), confirming the severity score is isoform-pair-intrinsic rather than a gene-level property. One caveat applies to the domain-architecture-change coefficient specifically: because canonical-isoform selection is not always the longer sequence of a pair (43,348/48,641 = 89.1% of pairs), the coefficient is significant only in this majority stratum (β = 1.91) and null in the reverse 10.9% (β = −0.18, n.s.), so this covariate's effect should be read as conditional on canonical being the structurally more complete isoform, which it is in the large majority of cases. Fitting the model separately per tissue reveals a division of labour that converges with, but is independent of, the rate-based analysis above: the domain-architecture-change coefficient is larger in brain (β = 2.11, n = 33,148) than muscle (β = 1.53, n = 15,493), while N-terminal overlap (β = 1.98 vs. 0.36) and alignment-resync failure (β = 3.74 vs. 0.98) are far larger in muscle than brain, and the five covariates jointly explain more variance in muscle (R² = 0.177) than brain (R² = 0.101). The disorder covariate adds a magnitude-level refinement to the encoding result reported in §4c: disorder-region overlap is associated with a *smaller* representational footprint after adjusting for edit size in both tissues (muscle β = −6.69, brain β = −4.07), indicating that although a disorder-region edit is decodable as such from the embedding difference (AUROC 0.79), it produces a comparatively diffuse, lower-amplitude shift in representation space than an equally sized edit to a structured region — an encoding/magnitude dissociation distinct from, and complementary to, the direction/labelling dissociation established throughout this paper.

**Mediation follow-up: the resync-failure tissue ordering is downstream of a size asymmetry.** The N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync-failure coefficients above initially suggested a mirror-image muscle-specific story of two independent mechanism classes. A follow-up analysis tested whether this reading holds under independent testing. N-terminal overlap's apparent effect does not survive confound-controlled discrimination testing — a size-quintile-matched AUC of severity_score for N-terminal-overlapping versus non-overlapping pairs is at chance in both tissues (0.489 muscle, 0.491 brain, 95% CI spanning 0.5) — so it should not be read as a validated muscle-specific mechanism despite its significance in the pooled OLS fit. The alignment-resync-failure coefficient's muscle-greater-than-brain ordering is real but is a downstream trace of a more proximal covariate, edit fragmentation, rather than an independent frame-specific mechanism: logistic regression of resync_failure_binary on size_z alone achieved near-identical discriminative power in both tissues (AUC = 0.854 muscle, 0.842 brain), and at matched size deciles the raw resync-failure rate was, if anything, higher in brain than muscle (e.g. 13.6% vs 21.7% at the size≈33-residue decile) — the opposite of the direction required for resync itself to be a muscle-specific driver. Independently, edit size's own relationship to severity_score was confirmed to be genuinely tissue-asymmetric: gene-cluster-bootstrapped Pearson r = 0.351 [0.328, 0.373] muscle vs 0.277 [0.264, 0.291] brain (gap 95% CI [0.047, 0.100], excludes zero; tissue-label permutation p = 0.001). This establishes that the resync ordering is a downstream trace of a genuine size asymmetry rather than an independent resync-specific signal.

**Second mediation layer: edit fragmentation, not raw size, is the proximal covariate.** Tracing the size asymmetry further, we recomputed, per pair, the number of separate non-contiguous SequenceMatcher change intervals (n_intervals) — a measure of whether an edit lands as a single contiguous block or fragments across multiple positions in the alignment. Including n_intervals_z (and its tissue interaction) in a pooled severity_score ~ size_z + tissue + size_z:tissue regression reduced the size_z × tissue interaction coefficient by 89.2% (gene-cluster-bootstrapped 95% CI on the shrinkage [71.5%, 110.9%]; a permutation null in which n_intervals_z was randomly shuffled across pairs, breaking its pair-level correspondence, produced a mean shrinkage of 0.0% [95th percentile 0.1%], p = 0.001, ruling out a generic degrees-of-freedom artefact). At matched size, muscle edits fragment into more separate change intervals than brain edits of the same total size (e.g. 10.7 vs 7.8 mean intervals at the size≈680-residue decile), and this excess is broad-based rather than concentrated in a small number of outlier genes: the ten genes contributing most to the large-edit fragmentation signal (LCOR, GNAS, MACF1, GOLGB1, DST, LINC02693, ANK2, FANCC, SORBS2, ATRIP) account for only 5.2% of the total signal across 2,028 contributing genes, and the muscle-brain gap in mean n_intervals persists essentially unchanged after excluding them (8.98 vs 7.74). A formal Fisher's exact test of high-fragmentation genes (above-median mean n_intervals in the large-edit subset) against cytoskeleton-related GO annotation (GO:0005856/0008092/0015629/0030036/0007010) found no enrichment in either tissue (muscle OR = 1.13, P = 0.42; brain OR = 1.06, P = 0.63), so the presence of large cytoskeletal-scaffold genes (MACF1, DST, ANK2) among the top contributors — genes which independently rank among the top fragmentation contributors in both tissues, consistent with their general status as unusually large, exon-rich loci rather than a tissue- or fragmentation-specific enrichment — should not be read as evidence of a cytoskeleton-specific mechanism. An unbiased genome-wide GO enrichment scan (all GO terms with ≥5 genes in the population, Benjamini-Hochberg correction) similarly found no functional category enriched among high-fragmentation genes in either tissue (muscle: 3,395 terms tested, 0 pass FDR<0.10; brain: 4,934 terms tested, 0 pass FDR<0.10) — heavily-fragmented genes share no common GO function, in either tissue. We therefore revise the interpretation of the resync-failure coefficient reported above: its apparent tissue ordering is not evidence of a resync-specific mechanism, but a downstream trace of a broad-based tissue difference in edit fragmentation, which is the covariate most proximally associated with the muscle-greater-than-brain representational-severity asymmetry.

A committed reconstruction of the original five-covariate pipeline — canonical-anchored pair construction and coherence-projection scoring (`build_severity_pairs.py`, `build_severity_score.py`), the §6-consistent tissue-specific domain-architecture-change covariate (`build_severity_domain_faithful.py`), and the gene-clustered regression (`severity_regression.py`) — reproduces the qualitative structure reported here: the sign and significance of all five covariates, the brain-greater-than-muscle ordering of the domain-architecture coefficient, the muscle-greater-than-brain ordering of the N-terminal-overlap and alignment-resync coefficients (subsequently explained by the mediation chain above), and the canonical-orientation-conditional domain effect (significant in the 89.1% majority stratum, null in the reverse 10.9%). The mediation follow-up analyses were performed with `resync_mechanical_test.py`, `size_severity_tissue_gap.py`, `dispersion_mechanism_test.py`, `dispersion_rigor_and_concentration.py`, and `tissue_compare_and_enrichment.py` (all committed in `hMuscle/model/`).
