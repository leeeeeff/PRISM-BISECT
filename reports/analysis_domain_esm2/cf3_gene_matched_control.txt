[8] Reviewer Response 초안 (CF3 대응 문구)

  "The reviewer raises a valid concern about whether PRISM's convergence with BISECT
  reflects isoform-intrinsic sequence discrimination or merely gene-level GO label
  reproduction. We provide four lines of evidence that distinguish these hypotheses.

  First, a gene-mean oracle classifier — which assigns all isoforms of the same gene
  identical gene-level GO scores — is mathematically guaranteed to produce a
  domain-ranking AUC of exactly 0.500, since it cannot assign higher scores to
  domain-complete isoforms versus domain-truncated variants within the same gene.
  PRISM achieves 0.630 [0.613, 0.646] (B=500 bootstrap; +0.130 above null; p<0.001),
  which is inconsistent with the gene-level classifier hypothesis.

  Second, LRPPRC — a mitochondria-associated RNA-binding protein involved in
  Complex I-related processes — shows identical PRISM scores for CT and AD
  isoforms (both 0.428), despite the gene being functionally related to
  Complex I. PRISM does not elevate scores for a Complex I-related gene when the
  isoforms lack structural domain differences, ruling out categorical gene-function
  labelling.

  Third, 23 of 24 Tier-3 BISECT genes (no domain-loss evidence) show CT=AD PRISM
  scores (ratio=1.00), while all 41 Tier-2 domain-loss genes show CT>AD (mean ratio
  4.96×). This pattern is inconsistent with gene-level label reproduction, which would
  predict uniform ratios regardless of domain evidence.

  Fourth, for NDUFS4 specifically, the AD-enriched isoform (tr73243) encodes a
  LINE-1 reverse transcriptase domain (RVT_1) and lacks the mitochondrial targeting
  sequence. PRISM assigns this isoform a score of 0.012 versus 0.587 for canonical
  NDUFS4-201 — a 49-fold differential derived from sequence alone, without any
  explicit domain or targeting signal annotation provided as input. A gene-level
  classifier would return 0.587 for both."
